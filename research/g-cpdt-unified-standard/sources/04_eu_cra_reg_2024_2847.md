## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Framework
Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (CRA) establishes mandatory cybersecurity requirements across the entire lifecycle of "products with digital elements" (PDEs) placed on the EU single market. It mandates that manufacturers maintain a machine-readable Software Bill of Materials (SBOM), document component supply chains, report actively exploited vulnerabilities (Article 14), and undergo conformity assessments based on product classification.

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 sets out technical descriptions for product categories under Annexes III and IV.

## 2. Verbatim Key Passages & Data Points

### On Classification & Conformity Routes (Annexes III & IV):
> "The Cyber Resilience Act defines four distinct risk tiers:
> 1. Default (Not listed in Annex III or IV): Module A (internal control).
> 2. Important Class I (Annex III - 19 categories): Module A only where harmonised standards are fully applied; otherwise third-party Module B+C or H.
> 3. Important Class II (Annex III - 4 categories): Mandatory third-party conformity assessment (Module B+C or H). The 4 categories are: hypervisors and container runtime systems; firewalls, IDS, and IPS; tamper-resistant microprocessors; and tamper-resistant microcontrollers.
> 4. Critical (Annex IV - 3 categories): Mandatory European Cybersecurity Certificate (EUCC) at assurance level 'substantial' or above where available, or Module B+C/H. The 3 categories are: hardware devices with security boxes; smart meter gateways and secure cryptoprocessing devices; smartcards and secure elements."
> *(Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 Articles 6, 7, 32 & Annexes III, IV; Implementing Reg (EU) 2025/2392)*

### On Essential Cybersecurity Requirements (Annex I):
> "Annex I Part I: Products with digital elements shall be designed, developed and produced in such a way that they ensure an appropriate level of cybersecurity based on the risks... delivered without any known exploitable vulnerabilities."
> *(Source: Annex I Part I Essential Requirements)*

> "Annex I Part II (Vulnerability handling): Manufacturers shall identify and document components and vulnerabilities contained in the product, including by drawing up a software bill of materials in a commonly used and machine-readable format covering at the very least the top-level dependencies of the product."
> *(Source: Annex I Part II, Clause 1)*

### On Article 14 Reporting Obligations:
> "A manufacturer shall notify CSIRTs and ENISA within 24 hours of becoming aware of any actively exploited vulnerability contained in the product with digital elements, followed by an initial incident report within 72 hours."
> *(Source: Article 14 Notification Obligations)*

## 3. Evaluative Analysis for G_CPDT
- **The Compliance Vacuum**: CRA requires tracking software dependencies in hardware/devices, but industrial control operators cannot evaluate the physical blast radius of a component vulnerability from an SBOM alone.
- **The Machine-Speed Solution**: G_CPDT directly provides the machine-readable data bridge required by Annex I Part II. By tying CycloneDX SBOMs and VEX to DEXPI/CIM equipment, manufacturers and asset owners can compute whether a reported CVE (Article 14) actually reaches an actuator or breaker, proving exploitability or non-exploitability at machine speed.
