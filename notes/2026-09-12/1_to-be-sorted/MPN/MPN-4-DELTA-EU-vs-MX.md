## 1. Purpose and method

This report records, for the author, everything that differs between the two editions of the deployment paper and everything that does not, so that the two files can be maintained as one design with two jurisdictional profiles rather than as two designs. It was produced by reading both files from the first line to the last and then aligning them in six passes.

The first pass aligned the two papers section by section on the shared numbering 1 to 10, which the Mexico edition adopted deliberately (MX 7: "whose section numbering it mirrors so that a delta can be read section by section"). Each of the ten sections is treated in section 3 below with the line ranges of both files, what each edition says, and the difference and its reason, classified as legal, scientific or editorial. Sections that are unchanged are still listed and the claim of no change is proved by line reference.

The second pass mapped the invariants one to one. The Mexico edition labels each carried invariant with its D-number and a status word (MX 133: "each carried invariant names its D-number and says whether it is unchanged or modified"); this report checks every such label against the two texts and quotes the operative clause of each so that the reader can see the textual difference rather than take the label on trust. Where the label "unchanged" is attached to an invariant whose text nevertheless differs, the difference is recorded.

The third pass inventoried every legal instrument cited in either edition, article by article, recital by recital and numeral by numeral, and states for each what it does to the design in each edition and what the net effect on the system is. Instruments cited in one edition only are marked "not cited" in the other with the reason.

The fourth pass inventoried the modes: the three modes shared by both editions and the fourth, the operator-state overlay, that only the Mexico edition admits. For each mode the admitted channels, the outputs, the listeners, the consent or legal gate, the log rules and the differences between editions are stated. The profile-MX relaxations of N-1, N-2, N-3, N-14, E-9 and E-3 are quoted in the exact words of MPN-2 and MPN-3 and in the exact words of the relaxation in MPN-4-MX, and the six requirements the Mexico edition says it does not relax (N-19, N-25, E-5, E-16, E-17, E-21) are quoted in full.

The fifth pass compared the validation stacks: the instruments, the acceptance criteria in full, the staged rollouts stage by stage with their gates, and the milestones with the claim and the instrument each carries.

The sixth pass diffed the two reference lists entry by entry, producing three complete lists (shared, EU only, MX only) with counts, and noting bibliographic differences within shared entries. The open items were then collected from the two legal ledgers, the two QA reports, the two citation ledgers and the papers' own "not read", "not verified" and "working group's own" flags. Section 10 closes with the consequences for MPN-1, MPN-2 and MPN-3 and a registry recommendation.

No row in any table says "see above", no list ends in "and others", and no invariant is paraphrased where the task is to quote it.

## 2. What does not change (the jurisdiction-independent core)

Both editions rest on a core that neither depends on a statute nor moves with the border. The Mexico edition says so in its own words: "The scientific limits do not move with the border" (MX 17), "Every invariant that rests on science or safety is carried unchanged in substance" (MX 133), and "the science does not change at the border: the review the European legislator cited is as true in Monterrey as in Rotterdam" (MX 168). The core has four parts.

The scientific limits. Physiology indexes arousal and load, not emotion (EU 17, citing Barrett et al. as [15]; MX 17 and MX 226, citing the same review as [14]). No facial configuration is reliably diagnostic of an emotional state, and the working group extends that finding to the voice on its own responsibility (EU 17; MX 17, MX 156 in DM-22, MX 226). No controlled study of continuous sonification of plant state in an industrial control room exists (EU 239; MX 293, "That statement is unchanged from the EU edition and is not softened by the jurisdiction"). Every performance claim is a transfer from anaesthesia, the Loeb and Fitch two-stream study and the Watson and Sanderson respiratory sonification (EU 15, 194, 239 [6] [7]; MX 17, 240, 293 [15] [16]). No lead time, no reduction in incident rate and no insurance premium delta is claimed in either edition (EU 15, D-17 at 119, D-20 at 122, 251, 261, 302; MX DM-15 at 149, DM-18 at 152, 303, 315, 351). The invariants that carry these limits are D-2 and DM-2 (no emotion label), D-17 and DM-15 (early-warning observables are annotations and never a lead time), and D-20 and DM-18 (no lead time, incident-rate or premium claim).

The safety invariants. The display is not an alarm and is entered in the alarm philosophy as a non-alarm auditory display requiring no response (D-11 at EU 113; DM-9 at MX 143; both cite N-20 and E-18). The annunciated alarm rate, measured by the IEC 62682 rate metrics before and after commissioning, must not rise (D-14 at EU 116; DM-12 at MX 146). The flood behaviour of N-22 and E-8 is implemented in both: at ten alarms in ten minutes the alarm-rate and interaction streams fall silent and the plant stream reduces to a tonic drone at pianissimo (EU 162, 271; MX 210, 325), and under profile MX the personal and aggregate streams fall silent with them (MX 325). Audibility of the site's danger signals is verified under ISO 7731 before commissioning and after any change to the instrument set (D-15 at EU 117; DM-13 at MX 147). The operator has one silence control, without penalty and without any record of who used it (D-13 at EU 115; DM-11 at MX 145; both cite N-24 and E-11). The Milford Haven lesson, that an overloaded operator needs less sound and an overview, is stated in both section 8s in the same words from the same HSE sources (EU 269 to 271 [54] [55] [56]; MX 323 to 325 [77] [78] [79]), and both editions draw the same three consequences: the flood rule, the overview finding, and management of change.

The validation stack. Both editions use ISO 11064-7 with ISO 11064-1 and ISO 9241-210 for the evaluation frame, Endsley's three-level situation-awareness model with SAGAT as the method, NASA-TLX for workload, multiple-resource theory as the prediction under test, the two-stream detection task of Loeb and Fitch adapted per Paper 2 section 9, and magnitude estimation of mapping polarity after Walker (EU 192 to 194 [36] to [43]; MX 240 [61] to [68]). The Mexico edition says why: "The instruments are the EU edition's, because they were chosen for the science and not for the jurisdiction" (MX 240). The first four rollout stages (simulator, shadow mode, single console, crew) and the first five milestones (M1 conformance, M2 listener study, M3 simulator trial, M4 shadow-mode field trial, M5 learned renderer in simulation) are identical in substance (EU 209 to 235, 275 to 302; MX 258 to 289, 329 to 351).

The three shared modes. Operational mode, adversary mode and simulation and training mode are declared "unchanged from the EU edition" in the Mexico edition (MX 172, 210, 214, 218). Person-level vectors appear only in simulation and adversary mode and any learned component is confined to those modes and physically separable (D-4 at EU 106; DM-4 at MX 138, identical text). Adversary mode takes its discourse estimate from threat intelligence about a threat actor and never applies it to a member of staff, a suspected insider included (D-5 at EU 107; DM-5 at MX 139). Simulation mode keeps real participants' interaction data inside the simulator and out of any per-person record (D-18 at EU 120; DM-16 at MX 150, identical text). The fatigue annotation is a crew-level text annotation from schedule-predicted sleep opportunity, advisory, never a go/no-go criterion, not rendered for a crew of one (D-6 at EU 108; DM-6 at MX 140; MX 234 carries it "unchanged").

What does change is stated equally plainly by the Mexico edition: "Mexico has no prohibition, and ... the difference between the two is the whole of the paper" (MX 355). The difference is one overlay mode, the legal anchors under every carried invariant that had a European statute beneath it, and the fourteen new invariants that condition the overlay.

## 3. Section-by-section delta

### 3.1 Section 1, Executive Summary and Scope

(a) The EU edition (EU 11 to 23) states that the paper closes the series and says how an MPN system is put into a European control room (EU 13); numbers its invariants D-1 through D-20 (EU 13); states the narrow outcome claim, detection and orientation transferred from anaesthesia, with no lead time, incident-rate or premium claim (EU 15); sets out the lawful-by-design position on Article 5(1)(f), the Commission's wide reading of "emotions", Recital 35 health data and works-council consent, and confines person-level modelling to adversary, simulation and the crew-level fatigue annotation (EU 17); recommends the subtitle "the state of the socio-technical link made audible" in place of "operator state made audible" (EU 19); and has one subsection, 1.1 "What this paper does not do" (EU 21 to 23).

(b) The Mexico edition (MX 11 to 25) states that it is the Mexico edition, says what changes relative to the EU edition and what does not, and numbers its invariants DM-1 through DM-32 "so that they do not collide with the D-numbers of the EU edition" (MX 13). It answers the author's brief ("Mexico is not in the European Union and there are no restrictions") with "That is half right, and the wrong half matters more", lists what Mexico lacks (an AI statute, a rule naming emotion recognition, a workplace-surveillance statute, a works-council veto) and what it has (the LFPDPPP of 20 March 2025 with sensitive data on an open-ended definition, express written consent, purpose and proportionality, minimum retention, revocable consent, an objection right, fines to 320,000 UMA about 37.5 million pesos; NOM-035; the LFT dignity and non-discrimination clauses; the public-sector law at CENACE, CFE and PEMEX) (MX 15). It restates the scientific limits and announces the operator-state channel with its four sensors and its exclusions (MX 17). It has two subsections: 1.1 "The jurisdictional-profile concept" (MX 19 to 21), which defines profile EU and profile MX, the overlay, the five relaxed requirements and the six not relaxed, and 1.2 "What this paper does not do" (MX 23 to 25), which adds that the Reglamento of the new law was unpublished as of July 2026, that the authority has issued no guidance, and that no Mexican court has ruled on physiological or affective monitoring at work.

(c) Differences and reasons. Legal: the whole of MX 15 replaces EU 17, because the three European grounds (Article 5(1)(f), Recital 35, WOR Article 27) do not reach a Mexican site and the Mexican grounds (LFPDPPP Articles 2, 7, 8, 11, 12, 26, 59; NOM-035; LFT Articles 2, 3, 134; LGPDPPSO) take their place. Scientific: MX 17 carries EU 15 and EU 17's science unchanged and adds the admission of an arousal and load index from HRV, EDA, pupillometry and eye tracking, which is lawful in Mexico and not in the EU. Editorial: MX 1.1 (the profile concept) is new and has no EU counterpart because the EU edition was written for one jurisdiction; MX renumbers "What this paper does not do" to 1.2; MX drops the subtitle recommendation of EU 19 (the recommendation stands in the EU edition and is not contradicted); MX 13 cites the EU edition as [6] alongside Papers 1 to 3; MX 15 gives the UMA figure and the fine ceiling, which the EU edition has no counterpart for (the EU edition never states the AI Act's Article 99 fine, which q9 line 33 marks UNVERIFIED); MX omits the EU 15 statement of the outcome hypothesis from section 1 and carries it instead at MX 293 and MX 357.

### 3.8 Section 8, The Milford Haven lesson

(a) The EU edition (EU 267 to 271) gives the date, injuries and damage; quotes CHIS6 on 275 alarms in 11 minutes; quotes four causes from the COMAH case study (excessive alarms, no process overviews, a control valve shut when indicated open, plant modified without assessment); states the EEMUA 191, IEC 62682 and ISA-18.2 benchmarks; and draws three consequences: the flood rule at ten alarms in ten minutes, the overview finding, and management of change with D-11.

Scientific and design: the sentence that the personal and aggregate streams fall silent in a flood (MX 325) is new and is required by the overlay's design ("the base mode's streams, indices, alert rule and flood machine run unchanged", MX 222).

### 3.9 Section 9, Roadmap

(a) The EU edition, "Programme roadmap" (EU 273 to 302), maps the four papers to the working group's phases, has a diagram with four paper nodes P1 to P4 feeding five milestone nodes M1 to M5 and a "Licensed by no milestone" node (lead time; incident-rate reduction; premium delta; any statement about a person), and describes each milestone with the claim it licenses: M1 conformance and remediation (implementation reproduces the notation); M2 listener study (polarity and scaling; laboratory detection versus visual baseline); M3 simulator trial with SAGAT and TLX (the first milestone at which "benefit" may be used, qualified "in a simulator"); M4 shadow-mode field trial after works-council consent, DPIA, alarm-philosophy entry and security coverage (D-9 to D-12), where the deferred Annex III obligations would bite from 2 December 2027; M5 the two-dimensional learned renderer in simulation mode only.

(c) Differences and reasons. Legal: M4's instrument changes from works-council consent and DPIA to reglamento interior, policy and STPS readiness; M6 exists only under profile MX; the Reglamento gap is named at M6. Editorial: the MX diagram drops the four paper nodes and the phase mapping paragraph (EU 275) and adds an instrument line to every milestone; the MX diagram's edges are a single chain M1 to M6 where the EU's run P4 to M1 and then M1 to M5. Scientific: the "Licensed by no milestone" node gains "any emotion label" because an emotion label is now the thing most at risk of being claimed under an overlay that measures physiology; the closing sentence on a lead time adds "or, under DM-15, on consented operator-state series, a research programme beyond M6 with no date attached" (MX 351).

### 3.10 Section 10, Conclusion

(a) The EU edition (EU 304 to 308) states that the legal frame is quoted rather than summarised, that twenty invariants turn the readings into obligations a checker can test, that three modes keep the person-level layer where it is lawful, that the validation stack is ordinary control-centre practice, that the early-warning observables are numbers in a margin and that the outcome is a list of artefacts the working group does not price (EU 306); and that what the audible control room delivers is a regulated auditory channel that falls silent in a flood, and what it does not deliver "is any reading of the people in the room" (EU 308).

(b) The Mexico edition (MX 353 to 357) states that "Mexico has no prohibition, and that the difference between the two is the whole of the paper"; counts thirty-two invariants, "eighteen are the European invariants carried across, eight unchanged because they rested on the alarm standards, the acoustics and the science, ten modified where a Mexican article now supplies the anchor; two European invariants, the works-council gate and the AI Act classification record, are replaced by the consent instrument and the reglamento interior adoption; fourteen are new, and every new one is a condition on the one thing Mexico adds, an overlay in which a consenting operator hears their own arousal and load and a supervisor hears at most a mean over three" (MX 355); and that what the room delivers in Mexico is what it delivers in Europe, that what it may add under consent is "a mirror", and that what it does not deliver in either jurisdiction is any reading of what anyone feels, "because no sensor measures that and no statute can make one do so" (MX 357).

(c) Differences and reasons. Editorial and arithmetical: the invariant counts (20 versus 32, with the 8 plus 10 plus 2 plus 14 decomposition) are verified in section 5 of this report; the four carried-by-label "unchanged" invariants with small textual differences (DM-5, DM-6, DM-12, DM-18) do not disturb the count, since the differences are citation or clause-order changes and not changes of obligation. Legal: "no prohibition" versus "no restrictions" is the MX edition's own thesis and has no EU counterpart. Scientific: the closing sentence of each edition says the same thing; the MX one adds the reason.

## 10. Consequences for the series

### 10.1 What MPN-1, MPN-2 and MPN-3 must say about jurisdictional profiles

Each of the three earlier papers states the lawful-by-design position in European terms as if it were the only frame (MPN-1 line 16: "The programme is lawful by design ... The legal grounds are three. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Article 5(1)(f) ... GDPR Article 9 and Recital 35 ... the Dutch Works Councils Act"; MPN-2 line 14: "The notation is lawful by design. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Article 5(1)(f) ..."; MPN-3 line 17: "The engine is lawful by design, as every paper in the series is. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 Article 5(1)(f) ..."). MPN-2 line 46 calls the biometric rule "absolute", which the Mexico edition overrides for the overlay (MX 21), and MPN-2 line 44 says "Paper 4 requires consent before any is connected [10]" with the Works Councils Act as the basis. MPN-3 line 200 describes the log in terms the overlay respects but does not mention the operator-state store. None of the three names the profile concept. The following insertions are proposed.

Proposed insertion for MPN-1, after the lawful-by-design paragraph at line 16 (or as a closing paragraph of section 6.7 at line 273):

"The legal grounds above are those of the European Union and the Netherlands, and the deployment paper of this series is written for them as profile EU. Paper 4 also has a Mexico edition, profile MX, for a jurisdiction with no artificial-intelligence statute, no rule naming emotion recognition and no works-council veto, where sensitive data may be processed on express written consent under the Ley Federal de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de los Particulares of 2025. Under that profile one channel the European frame forbids regardless of consent, an arousal and load index computed from a consenting operator's heart-rate variability, electrodermal activity, pupillometry and eye tracking, may be admitted as an overlay heard by that operator alone, with a supervisor hearing at most a mean over three. What does not move with the border is the science of this paper: physiology indexes arousal and load and not emotion, no facial or vocal configuration is reliably diagnostic of an emotional state, and no lead time has been measured for any operator metric; F-8, F-11 and F-13 bind every profile, and no profile admits an emotion label, a per-person fatigue value, a decision about an individual or a claim about a person."

Proposed insertion for MPN-2, after the sentence at line 46 ("The rule is absolute and is stated once here and again as N-2 ...") and cross-referenced from the requirements preamble at line 320:

"The rule is stated for profile EU, the jurisdiction the state vector's personal-data and biometric columns were written for. Paper 4 defines jurisdictional profiles by difference: profile MX, the Mexico edition, relaxes N-1 to admit a header that declares a base mode and an optional operator-state overlay, lifts N-2's prohibition of biometric channels and of inferred arousal for that overlay only while keeping its prohibition of inferred emotion, mood and stress in every mode, relaxes N-3 so that a score carrying the overlay holds per-operator indices in the consenting operator's own view and a mean over at least three consenting operators in the supervisor's view while the room-wide score and the persistent log carry neither, and admits under N-14 two further streams, a personal stream and an aggregate stream, each with a timbre family, register, rhythmic figure and pan position overlapping no other stream's. N-19, N-25 and every other requirement of section 7 bind every profile. A score that declares no overlay is a profile EU score wherever it is rendered, and the four remarks on this table apply to it; the consent that Paper 4 requires before the interaction, latency and fatigue channels are connected is the works council's under profile EU and the reglamento interior de trabajo's under profile MX."

Proposed insertion for MPN-3, after the lawful-by-design paragraph at line 17 and cross-referenced from section 4.2 (inputs by mode) and the requirements preamble at line 204:

"The engine is specified for profile EU. Paper 4's Mexico edition defines profile MX, under which E-3's schema boundary admits four operator-state channels, heart-rate variability, electrodermal activity, pupillometry and eye tracking, only while a header flag declares the operator-state overlay, and rejects them otherwise with the same hard failure; E-9 admits two further streams under the overlay, a personal stream at the consenting operator's position and an aggregate at the supervisor's, with timbre family, register, rhythmic figure and pan position fixed at configuration time; and the computation from sensor to index is a second fixed, declared function with no learned component, whose form and version identifier alone enter the header, its per-operator baseline constants living in a separate operator-state store outside the header and outside the log of E-16. E-5, E-16, E-17 and E-21 are not relaxed under any profile: the voice, speech and personality heads are absent from every build, the log carries no user identity, there is no export path, and early-warning observables and the fatigue index remain text annotations. An engine built for profile EU is conformant under profile MX with the overlay disabled; an engine that enables the overlay must also satisfy the operator-state invariants of the Mexico edition and must not be placed on the Union market with the overlay enabled."

### 10.2 Registry implications

The choice is between two slugs (one registry entry per edition) and one slug carrying two editions. This report recommends two slugs, registered as two papers in WG-09 with the Mexico edition marked as a jurisdictional profile of Paper 4, for the following reasons.

First, the invariant sets differ in numbering and in count, D-1 to D-20 against DM-1 to DM-32, and the Mexico edition chose the DM prefix precisely "so that they do not collide with the D-numbers of the EU edition" (MX 13). A checker citing "D-9" or "DM-19" needs a single URL to resolve it against; one page with two invariant lists would make every cross-reference from MPN-2 and MPN-3 ambiguous.

Second, the Mexico edition cites the EU edition as a reference in its own list (MX [6], "MPN-4: Deployment and outcome, the audible control room (EU edition)"), and the series plan's rule that "each paper cites the previous by slug" (MPN-SERIES-PLAN.md line 52) requires a slug for the cited document. The same plan records that MPN-2's reference to MPN-1 waits on a slug; the MX edition's reference [6] is in the same position.

Third, the reference lists differ by 12 EU-only and 34 MX-only entries (section 8), and the corpus convention of one reference list per paper under "## 11. References" cannot hold two numberings on one page without renumbering one edition, which would break every "[n]" in one of the two QA reports and citation ledgers, both of which are per-edition artefacts (QA-MPN-4.md, QA-MPN-4-MX.md, CITATION-LEDGER-MPN-4.md, CITATION-LEDGER-MPN-4-MX.md).

Fourth, the profile concept is defined "by difference" (MX 21) and anticipates further profiles; one paper per profile scales, and this delta report is the instrument by which the profiles are kept aligned. A single page with two editions would need a third artefact to say which paragraphs belong to which edition, which is what the shared section numbering already does across two files.

Fifth, the series arithmetic can absorb it: the plan's Phase 3 count of 63 and the "WG-09 count rises to 9 in four later PRs" (MPN-SERIES-PLAN.md line 48) become a WG-09 count of 10 in five later PRs, one PR per paper as the plan already requires, with the census re-baseline of plus one document, plus N characters and plus 4 mermaid diagrams for the Mexico edition. The metadata table of the Mexico edition already says "Paper 4 of 4 (Mexico edition; the EU edition is MPN-4)" (MX 8), so the series stays a series of four with one paper in two profiles.

Suggested registry values, following the plan's table at MPN-SERIES-PLAN.md lines 56 to 61: id `WG-09-MPN-Deployment-MX`, slug `audible-control-room-mx`, badge "Deployment (MX)", with the EU edition keeping `WG-09-MPN-Deployment` and `audible-control-room`. Both metadata tables should carry a "Profile" row (EU; MX) and a "Sibling edition" row with the other slug's URL once registered, and the MX reference [6] and the EU edition's "Extends" row should point at each other by URL. If the author prefers one slug regardless, the least damaging form is a single page whose body is the EU edition and whose section 12 is the full Mexico edition with its own invariant list and its own reference list under a second heading, accepting that "[n]" citations must then be prefixed by edition throughout; this report advises against it for the four reasons above.
