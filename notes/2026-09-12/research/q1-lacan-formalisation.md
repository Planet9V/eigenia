Scope note. Web budget was 8 searches / 6 fetches. ## Findings

### 1. The mathemes S1, S2, $, a

- Lacan's term "matheme" (mathème) is coined c. 1971–73, modelled on Lévi-Strauss's "mytheme"; the stated ambition is "integral transmission" of psychoanalytic knowledge ("L'Étourdit", 1972, in *Autres écrits*, Seuil 2001; Seminar XX, 1972–73). (Evans 1996, entry "matheme" — UNVERIFIED).
- The four letters as used in the discourses (Seminar XVII, 1969–70): **S1** = the master signifier (signifier without signified; the unary, commanding signifier); **S2** = knowledge (savoir), the battery/chain of signifiers; **$** = the barred (divided) subject, the subject as effect of the signifier; **a** = objet petit a, surplus-jouissance (plus-de-jouir), cause of desire, remainder/product of the signifying operation. (Lacan 1969-70/2007; Fink 1995 ch. 9; Evans 1996 entries "master signifier", "objet petit a", "subject" — UNVERIFIED as to page.)
- Genealogy of the symbols: **$** and **a** first stabilise in the graph of desire (Seminars V–VI, 1957–59) and in the fantasy formula ($ ◊ a); **a** is elaborated as object-cause of desire in Seminar X (*Anxiety*, 1962–63, trans. A. R. Price, Polity 2014); **S1/S2** as a pair derive from the "binary signifier" of Seminar XI (1964) and are fixed as the master/knowledge pair in Seminars XVI–XVII (1968–70). (Standard secondary account: Evans 1996; Fink 1995; Chiesa 2007 — UNVERIFIED as to page.)
- Lacan explicitly says in Seminar XVII that plus-de-jouir (surplus-jouissance, **a**) is modelled on Marx's *Mehrwert* (surplus-value) — the point Zupančič's essay in Clemens & Grigg 2006 works from. (UNVERIFIED as to page.)

### 2. The four discourses (Seminar XVII, 1969–70)

Primary: Lacan, J. (1969–70). *Le Séminaire, livre XVII: L'envers de la psychanalyse*, ed. J.-A. Miller, Seuil 1991. English: *The Seminar of Jacques Lacan, Book XVII: The Other Side of Psychoanalysis*, trans. Russell Grigg, W. W. Norton, 2007 (ISBN 978-0-393-33040-3 seen in a search-result title — UNVERIFIED on a publisher page). The discourses are set out in the opening sessions (Nov–Dec 1969); the schema also appears in "Radiophonie" (1970, *Autres écrits* 2001), and the fifth "capitalist discourse" is drawn in the Milan lecture "Du discours psychanalytique" (12 May 1972) — UNVERIFIED for editions.

Structure (confirmed on the nosubject.com summary, matching Fink 1995 and Evans 1996):

- Four **positions**, fixed:
  ```
  agent   →  other
  -----      -----
  truth      product (production)
  ```
  agent = the dominant term that "speaks"/sets the discourse going; other = what it addresses/puts to work; truth = what underlies and supports the agent, hidden; product = what the operation yields (also "loss", the remainder).
- Four **terms** S1, S2, $, a in a fixed cyclic order; each discourse is a **quarter-turn** (rotation by 90°) of the previous. Standard layouts (agent/truth → other/product):
  - **Master**: S1/$ → S2/a
  - **Hysteric**: $/a → S1/S2
  - **University**: S2/S1 → a/$
  - **Analyst**: a/S2 → $/S1
- Standard relational reading (Fink 1995; Verhaeghe 1995; Bracher 1993): the upper arrow (agent → other) is the impossibility relation; the lower relation (product ↔ truth) is barred ("impotence"/inability): the product never reaches the truth. Lacan pairs this with Freud's three impossible professions (governing, educating, analysing) plus "causing desire" — UNVERIFIED as to page.
- The rotation is a cyclic permutation of one step; it is NOT a full symmetric group on four letters — only the four cyclic rotations are Lacanian discourses (the "capitalist discourse" breaks this by swapping S1 and $ on the left, which is why Lacan calls it not a real discourse).

Secondary (authoritative):
- Fink, B. (1995). *The Lacanian Subject: Between Language and Jouissance*. Princeton UP. Ch. 9 "The Four Discourses" — the most-cited English exposition. UNVERIFIED as to page.
- Bracher, M. (1993). *Lacan, Discourse, and Social Change: A Psychoanalytic Cultural Criticism*. Cornell UP. Ch. 3 applies the discourses to cultural criticism. And Bracher, M., Alcorn, M., Corthell, R., Massardier-Kenney, F. (eds.) (1994). *Lacanian Theory of Discourse: Subject, Structure, and Society*. NYU Press — contains Bracher's "On the Psychological and Social Functions of Language: Lacan's Theory of the Four Discourses". UNVERIFIED.
- Clemens, J. & Grigg, R. (eds.) (2006). *Jacques Lacan and the Other Side of Psychoanalysis: Reflections on Seminar XVII* (sic vi). Duke UP. ISBN 9780822337195 (seen in Duke UP/Amazon result titles). Contains Zupančič, A., "When Surplus Enjoyment Meets Surplus Value"; Žižek, "Objet a in Social Links"; Grigg, "Absolute Freedom and Major Structural Change". UNVERIFIED as to contents pages.
- Verhaeghe, P. (1995). "From Impossibility to Inability: Lacan's Theory of the Four Discourses". *The Letter* 3, 91–108. UNVERIFIED.
- Evans, D. (1996). *An Introductory Dictionary of Lacanian Psychoanalysis*. Routledge. Entry "discourse". UNVERIFIED.

### 3. RSI and the Borromean knot (Seminars XIX–XXIII; Seminar XXII "R.S.I.", 1974–75)

- The three registers are first set out in the lecture "Le symbolique, l'imaginaire et le réel" (8 July 1953; in *Des Noms-du-Père*, Seuil 2005; English in *On the Names-of-the-Father*, trans. Fink, Polity 2013). UNVERIFIED.
- The Borromean knot enters in Seminar XIX *…ou pire* (session of 9 Feb 1972), is used in Seminar XX *Encore* (1972–73), Seminar XXI *Les non-dupes errent* (1973–74), and becomes the whole object of Seminar XXII *R.S.I.* (1974–75). Seminar XXII has NO official Seuil edition; portions were published in *Ornicar?* nos. 2–5 (1975–76); the only English text is the unofficial translation by Cormac Gallagher (Karnac/Lacan in Ireland). Seminar XXIII *Le sinthome* (1975–76) is official: Seuil 2005; English trans. A. R. Price, Polity 2016. UNVERIFIED as to volume/pages.
- The formal claim: R, S and I are three rings linked so that no two are linked to each other, yet the three hold together — cut any one and the other two fall apart (Borromean property; in knot theory the Borromean rings are a 3-component Brunnian link). Lacan attaches jouissance types to the overlaps (e.g., phallic jouissance between S and R, meaning (sens) between S and I, jouissance of the Other/"J(A)" between R and I; objet a at the central triple overlap) and in Seminar XXIII adds a fourth ring — the sinthome (Name-of-the-Father as a fourth term) — to repair a failed knotting (the Joyce case). Secondary: Evans 1996 entries "Borromean knot", "real", "symbolic", "imaginary"; Ragland & Milovanovic 2004; Chiesa 2007 ch. 5; Wikipedia "Borromean clinic" (tertiary). UNVERIFIED as to page.
- Lacan insists the knot is "not a model" or metaphor but a "writing" (écriture) of the structure — that is itself a contestable claim (see §7).

### 4. The graph of desire

- Developed across Seminar V *Les formations de l'inconscient* (1957–58; Seuil 1998; trans. Grigg, Polity 2017) and Seminar VI *Le désir et son interprétation* (1958–59; Seuil 2013; trans. Fink, Polity 2019); its completed form is published in "The Subversion of the Subject and the Dialectic of Desire in the Freudian Unconscious" (Royaumont, 1960), in *Écrits* (Seuil 1966); English in *Écrits: The First Complete Edition in English*, trans. Bruce Fink, Norton 2006. UNVERIFIED as to pages.
- Four stages in "Subversion": (i) the elementary cell — a vector of the signifying chain S→S' crossed by the subject's intention Δ→$, the two crossing points forming the *point de capiton* (quilting point) with its retroactive effect (signification is produced *après coup*); (ii) Graph 2 adds the Other (A), s(A) signified of the Other, the ego m and the ideal-ego i(a), the ego-ideal I(A); (iii) Graph 3 adds "Che vuoi?", desire d and fantasy ($ ◊ a); (iv) the complete graph adds the upper level of enunciation with S(Ⱥ) (signifier of the lack in the Other), the drive ($ ◊ D), and the vector of jouissance/castration. UNVERIFIED as to page; the structure is standard (Evans 1996 entry "graph of desire"; Fink 1995; Žižek 1989 *The Sublime Object of Ideology* ch. 3; Chiesa 2007 ch. 4).

### 5. The formulas of sexuation

- Primary: Seminar XX *Encore* (1972–73; Seuil 1975; English *On Feminine Sexuality, the Limits of Love and Knowledge*, trans. Bruce Fink, Norton 1998), chapter VII "A Love Letter" (session of 13 March 1973), where the table is drawn; also "L'Étourdit" (1972; *Autres écrits* 2001) and Seminar XIX *…ou pire*. UNVERIFIED as to page.
- The table (upper part, using Φx = "x is subject to the phallic function"):
  - Masculine side: **∃x ¬Φx** (there exists at least one x not subject to the phallic function — the exception/primal father) and **∀x Φx** (all x are subject to it).
  - Feminine side: **¬∃x ¬Φx** (there is no x not subject to the phallic function) and **¬∀x Φx** (not-all — *pas-tout* — x are subject to it).
  - Lower part: $ and Φ on the male side; barred La (Ⱥ-woman: "The woman does not exist"), with arrows to Φ and to S(Ⱥ), on the female side; the male $ aims at objet a placed on the female side.
- The logic is deliberately non-classical: the two negations on the feminine side are not simple contradictories (a negated universal is read not as "some x is not Φ" but as an indeterminate "not-all"), which is why serious commentators read it as a revision of Aristotle's square of opposition rather than first-order logic. Secondary: Fink 1995 ch. 8; Copjec, J. (1994) *Read My Desire*, MIT Press, ch. 8 "Sex and the Euthanasia of Reason" (reads the formulas through Kant's antinomies); Zupančič, A. (2017) *What Is Sex?*, MIT Press; Chiesa, L. (2016) *The Not-Two: Logic and God in Lacan*, MIT Press; Le Gaufey, G. (2006) *Le Pastout de Lacan*, EPEL (English: *Lacan's Notall*, 2020). UNVERIFIED as to page.

### 6. Miller, "Suture (elements of the logic of the signifier)" — VERIFIED

- Original: Miller, J.-A. (1966). "La Suture: Éléments de la logique du signifiant". *Cahiers pour l'Analyse* 1(3), pp. 37–49; first delivered 24 February 1965 at Lacan's Seminar XII. (Kingston *Cahiers* electronic edition, VERIFIED.)
- English: "Suture (Elements of the Logic of the Signifier)", trans. Jacqueline Rose, *Screen* 18(4), Winter 1977–78, pp. 24–34 (Kingston, VERIFIED; the OUP *Screen* record academic.oup.com/screen/article/18/4/24/1616000 was seen only in search results).
- Content (Kingston synopsis, VERIFIED): Miller works from Frege's *Grundlagen der Arithmetik* (1884), §§74–79, on the construction of the number series. Frege assigns the number **0** to the concept "not identical with itself", a concept which as a concept has an extension that subsumes no object. Miller's claim: "the concept of not-identical-with-itself is assigned by the number zero which sutures logical discourse" (Miller, p. 44 Fr. / p. 29 Eng.). The successor operation (0 → 1 → …) is read as an "evocation and revocation" of the non-identical: the impossible object (the subject) is summoned and then excluded so that the truth-preserving logic of the series can run. The zero is thus the mark of the *lack* that the numeric chain both depends on and effaces — the "suture" being the subject's relation to the chain of its discourse: it figures there as the element that is lacking, in the form of a stand-in.
- Reception: Badiou, A. (1990/2008). *Number and Numbers* (Le Nombre et les nombres, Seuil 1990; trans. Robin Mackay, Polity 2008) discusses Frege's construction and includes a note on Miller's "contemporary usage of Frege" — Badiou's objection is that the zero is not the mark of a subject's lack but a point of decision/axiom (ontological, not subjective). UNVERIFIED as to chapter. Also lacanonline.com "Reading Miller's 'Suture'" (secondary, seen in search).

### 7. Lacan on cybernetics (Seminar II)

- Primary: Lacan, J. (1954–55). *Le Séminaire, livre II: Le moi dans la théorie de Freud et dans la technique de la psychanalyse*, ed. Miller, Seuil 1978. English: *The Seminar of Jacques Lacan, Book II: The Ego in Freud's Theory and in the Technique of Psychoanalysis, 1954–1955*, trans. Sylvana Tomaselli, notes by John Forrester, Cambridge UP (UK) / W. W. Norton (US) 1988 (paperback 1991). The lecture "Psychoanalysis and Cybernetics, or on the Nature of Language" (22 June 1955) is the final chapter (XXIII) — UNVERIFIED as to chapter number/page; the title and year are stable across editions.
- Relevant content: (a) the preceding sessions "Odd or Even? Beyond Intersubjectivity" (ch. XV) and "The Purloined Letter" (ch. XVI) introduce the +/– game and the 1–3 "network" (the α β γ δ syntax) in which certain successions of symbols become impossible after a few steps — Lacan's demonstration that a memory-like law can arise from pure symbolic combinatorics without any subject; this is expanded in the 1966 "Introduction" and "Parenthèse des parenthèses" appended to the "Seminar on 'The Purloined Letter'" in *Écrits* (Norton 2006). (b) In the cybernetics lecture, Lacan says cybernetics is the science of "the conjectural sciences" (contrasted with the "exact sciences"), that the machine embodies the symbolic order's binary alternation (the door as the archetype of the symbol: open/closed, 0/1), that the symbolic order is the order of the machine, and that the imaginary (the ego, gestalt) is what the machine lacks — "the machine is the structure detached from the activity of the subject." UNVERIFIED as to wording; the paraphrase follows the standard accounts.
- Secondary: Liu, L. H. (2010). *The Freudian Robot: Digital Media and the Future of the Unconscious*. U Chicago Press (ch. 5 on Lacan and the "Purloined Letter" game). Geoghegan, B. D. (2011). "From Information Theory to French Theory: Jakobson, Lévi-Strauss, and the Cybernetic Apparatus". *Critical Inquiry* 38(1), 96–126; and Geoghegan (2023). *Code: From Information Theory to French Theory*. Duke UP. Johnston, J. (2008). *The Allure of Machinic Life*. MIT Press. Forrester, J. (1990). *The Seductions of Psychoanalysis*. Cambridge UP (ch. on Lacan and the cybernetic subject). All UNVERIFIED.

### 8. (a) Sokal & Bricmont on Lacan, and the serious responses

Publication (Wikipedia summary, VERIFIED): Sokal, A. & Bricmont, J. (1997). *Impostures intellectuelles*. Odile Jacob. English: *Fashionable Nonsense: Postmodern Intellectuals' Abuse of Science*, Picador 1998; UK *Intellectual Impostures*, Profile 1998/1999. Lacan is chapter 2.

What they actually criticise (Wikipedia summary VERIFIED for the first three; the rest is standard knowledge of the chapter, UNVERIFIED):
1. **Topology**: Lacan asserts an analogy between topology (torus, Möbius strip, cross-cap, Klein bottle) and the structure of mental illness/the subject — e.g. "this torus really exists and it is exactly the structure of the neurotic" — "unsupported by any argument"; in their judgement "not just false: it is gibberish". Their point is that Lacan never says which properties of the topological objects are supposed to carry over, so no analogy can be assessed.
2. **Compactness** (Seminar XX, *Encore*, ch. I): Lacan describes the "space of jouissance" as compact and gestures at the Heine–Borel/bounded-closed characterisation; S&B argue the passage garbles the definition (covering by open sets, closed and bounded) and uses "compact" with no discernible content.
3. **The imaginary number**: in "The Subversion of the Subject" (Écrits, 1960) Lacan writes that the erectile organ "is equivalent to the √−1 of the signification produced above"; S&B treat this as meaningless mathematics-as-ornament, and they also charge that Lacan (in Seminar of 1959 and elsewhere) confuses irrational and imaginary numbers.
4. **Set theory / logic**: passages from Seminars XX and XIX using quantifiers and set-theoretic language are, they say, deployed without the required definitions.
5. Their general thesis: the mathematics is (i) often plain wrong, (ii) irrelevant to the psychoanalytic claim, and (iii) functions to impress a non-mathematical audience; they explicitly disclaim any judgement on psychoanalysis as such.

Serious responses:
- **Plotnitsky, A. (2002).** *The Knowable and the Unknowable: Modern Science, Nonclassical Thought, and the "Two Cultures"*. U Michigan Press. (Wikipedia VERIFIED for content, book record UNVERIFIED.) Plotnitsky disputes S&B's claim that irrational and imaginary numbers "have nothing to do with one another" (both arise as extensions of the rationals under closure operations) and defends Lacan's conceptual linkage while agreeing that the √−1 is not literally the erectile organ — i.e., the mathematics is used as a "nonclassical" model of the unrepresentable, and the correct reading is as structure-analogy rather than literal identity.
- **Fink, B. (2004).** *Lacan to the Letter: Reading Écrits Closely*. U Minnesota Press. (Wikipedia VERIFIED for content.) Fink charges S&B with "linguistic reductionism": Lacan uses mathematical terms metaphorically/rhetorically and for a specific audience; he reads the √−1 passage in the context of the graph of desire's signification of castration.
- **Badiou, A. (1990/2008).** *Number and Numbers*. Polity. Not a response to Sokal (it predates it) but the most rigorous mathematically-literate engagement with the Miller/Frege "suture" argument; Badiou (a mathematician-philosopher) rejects Miller's reading of the zero. UNVERIFIED as to chapter.
- **Burgoyne, B. (ed.) (2000).** *Drawing the Soul: Schemas and Models in Psychoanalysis*. Rebus Press, London. ISBN 1-85575-903-9 (seen in AbeBooks URL — UNVERIFIED). Contains chapters on Lacan's schemas, graphs and the status of models in analysis (Burgoyne's own chapter on Lacan's use of mathematics). UNVERIFIED as to contents.
- **Ragland, E. & Milovanovic, D. (eds.) (2004).** *Lacan: Topologically Speaking*. Other Press, New York. Essays by Lacanian analysts and mathematically-trained authors (incl. Ragland's on the discourse of science; Vappereau-influenced work) arguing the topology is a "writing" of structure, not analogy. UNVERIFIED (Goodreads record seen only).
- **Glynos, J. & Stavrakakis, Y. (eds.) (2002).** *Lacan and Science*. Karnac. Contains direct discussion of the Sokal affair. UNVERIFIED.
- Note: no response I found claims Lacan's compactness passage is mathematically correct; the defences are (i) that the usage is analogical/metaphorical (Fink), (ii) that the analogies are conceptually motivated (Plotnitsky), or (iii) that the topology is a non-metaphorical "writing" of psychical structure (Ragland/Milovanovic, Vappereau) — position (iii) is the one S&B would say is unfalsifiable.

## What an engineering treatise can legitimately say

- **Legitimate**: the four discourses are a *finite, exactly specifiable combinatorial object* — four ordered terms {S1, S2, $, a} placed in four fixed slots (agent, other, truth, product) under the cyclic group Z4 of quarter-turns — and this structure is stated by Lacan himself (Seminar XVII, 1969–70) and confirmed by every standard commentator (Fink 1995; Bracher 1993; Evans 1996). Representing it as a 4-cycle/rotation table is faithful, and it is fair to say Lacan intended it as transmissible in that form (the matheme programme).
- **Legitimate**: the formulas of sexuation are written in quantifier notation by Lacan himself (Seminar XX, ch. VII); one may transcribe them, provided one states that the negation on the "not-all" side is *not* classical first-order negation and that Lacan says so. Treating them as ordinary predicate logic is a misreading (Copjec 1994; Chiesa 2016; Le Gaufey 2006).
- **Legitimate**: Lacan's Seminar II material (the +/– game, the 1–3 network, the cybernetics lecture) is an explicit, documented engagement with information theory and finite-state combinatorics; an engineering text can cite it as historical precedent for reading "the symbolic order" as a machine-like syntax with memory constraints, with Liu 2010 and Geoghegan 2011/2023 as the scholarly bridges. Claiming Lacan anticipated Markov models or cybernetic control theory in any technical sense is a misreading — his point is that a syntax alone produces "memory" and impossibility without a subject.
- **Legitimate with care**: Miller's "Suture" is a rigorous-looking but *philosophical* reading of Frege §§74–79; it is citable (Cahiers 1(3), 1966; Screen 18(4), 1977–78) as the source of the "the subject is the lack the chain sutures over" thesis. It is not a mathematical result, and Badiou (1990) contests it; do not present it as "Frege proves…".
- **Legitimate with care**: the Borromean knot has a precise knot-theoretic meaning (a 3-component Brunnian link), and Lacan uses exactly that property (remove one, all fall apart). An engineering text can use the Borromean property as a *structural constraint* metaphor (three coupled registers, no pairwise binding) if it says so. Saying that RSI "is" a knot in any measurable sense, or extracting quantitative predictions from the knot, is exactly what Sokal & Bricmont called gibberish and what no serious defender has rescued.
- **Misreading to avoid**: treating S1/S2/$/a as variables with values, or the discourses as state machines with transition probabilities. The Lacanian literature contains no such semantics; any such model is the treatise's own construction and must be labelled as such (Gadalla et al. 2023/2026 do label theirs as empirical operationalisation, not as Lacan's).
- **Misreading to avoid**: claiming Lacan's topology (torus, Möbius, cross-cap, compactness) is mathematically sound; Sokal & Bricmont's specific objections on compactness and √−1 stand unrefuted on the mathematics, and the best defences (Plotnitsky 2002; Fink 2004) concede the point on literal content while defending analogical or rhetorical use.
- **Defensible framing for a control-room text**: "Lacan supplies a discrete vocabulary (four positions, four terms, a rotation rule) for describing who is put to work by whom in a socio-technical link; we adopt that vocabulary as an engineering ontology and do not inherit its metapsychological claims." This is how Bracher (1993) and the *British Journal of Guidance & Counselling* applications also proceed.

## Gaps

- Page numbers for the Norton/Polity/Cambridge editions could not be verified within budget (no publisher page fetched); all page references are omitted except the Miller 44/29 pair seen on the Kingston synopsis.
- The ScienceDirect record for Gadalla et al. 2023 could not be fetched (HTTP 400); its DOI and article number remain unverified. Follow up: doi.org lookup on "Concepts and experiments on psychoanalysis driven computing".
- The ResearchGate item "On Lacan's neglected computational model and the Oedipal structure" (429) — author, venue, and whether it is peer-reviewed are unknown. Follow up via Google Scholar.
- Exact wording of Lacan's cybernetics lecture and the chapter numbering in the Tomaselli translation were not checked against the text.
- Seminar XXII *R.S.I.* has no official edition; any citation must be to *Ornicar?* extracts or the Gallagher unofficial translation, and should say so.
- Contradiction to flag: the Lacanian-topology school (Ragland & Milovanovic; Vappereau) says the knot is a non-metaphorical "writing"; Fink's Sokal-response says the mathematics is metaphorical. A treatise cannot cite both as supporting the same claim.
- Contradiction to flag: Zupančič and Bracher read the discourses politically (surplus-value, social bonds); Fink and Verhaeghe read them clinically; the engineering ontology should say which it draws on.
- No peer-reviewed mathematical model of RSI was found at all; the field for the four discourses consists essentially of one research group (Gadalla/Nikoletseas/Amazonas, 2023–26) plus interpretive applications.
- Not checked: Ehresmann/Vanbremeersch or Lawvere-style category-theoretic readings of Lacan sometimes circulated in the "mathematics of psychoanalysis" literature — no peer-reviewed instance surfaced in the searches run.
