# q4 — Psychometric constructs and real-time operator state measurement

Research note for the control-room operator/team-state treatise series. Written 2026-09-11.
Method: 8 web searches, 6 fetches (PubMed and PMC pages were partly blocked by bot-checks; the
Barrett 2019 APS page, the SD4 paper PDF, the DISC Wikipedia article and the PubMed record pointing

## Findings

### 1. Big Five / Five-Factor Model (FFM)

**Lineage and instruments.** The lexical tradition (Goldberg 1990 "An alternative 'description of
personality': The Big-Five factor structure"; Goldberg 1993 "The structure of phenotypic
personality traits") and the questionnaire tradition (Costa & McCrae 1992 NEO-PI-R manual; the
NEO-FFI short form; McCrae & Costa 2004 contemplated revision of the NEO-FFI [McCraeCosta2004,
found in search, content UNVERIFIED]) converged on five broad domains: Neuroticism, Extraversion,
Openness, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness. John & Srivastava (1999) published the 44-item Big Five
Inventory (BFI) in the *Handbook of Personality* and the chapter is the standard review of history,
measurement and theoretical status [JohnSrivastava1999, UNVERIFIED]. The TIPI (Gosling, Rentfrow &
Swann 2003) is a very brief 10-item alternative [Gosling2003, search hit, UNVERIFIED].

**Reliability.** The NEO-PI-R manual and subsequent literature report domain-level internal
consistencies and multi-year test-retest stabilities that are generally regarded as high for
personality domains and lower for facets [UNVERIFIED — no coefficient was seen on a fetched page;
do not cite a number without pulling the manual or a meta-analysis such as Roberts & DelVecchio
2000 on rank-order consistency]. What can be said safely: the FFM domains are among the most
stable self-report constructs in psychology, with stability increasing with age.

**Cross-cultural replication.** McCrae & Costa (1997, *American Psychologist*, "Personality trait
structure as a human universal") and Piedmont & Chae (1997, *J. Cross-Cultural Psychology*,
"Cross-cultural generalizability of the Five-Factor Model of personality") report factor-structure
replication of the NEO-PI-R in translated samples [Piedmont1997 VERIFIED as existing via SAGE
search hit; content UNVERIFIED]. The general finding across many later studies is that the
five-factor structure replicates well in literate, industrialised samples using translated
questionnaires, less well with indigenous lexical studies (where fewer or different factors
sometimes emerge) and in some small-scale societies [UNVERIFIED background].

**Critiques.** Block (1995, *Psychological Bulletin*, "A contrarian view of the five-factor
approach to personality description") argued that (a) the lexical hypothesis is a weak
foundation, (b) the factors depend on the item pool and rotation choices, (c) five is not
compelled by the data, and (d) the model is descriptive rather than explanatory and says nothing
about within-person dynamics [Block1995, UNVERIFIED — well known but not fetched]. Other
standing critiques: the Big Five are broad and atheoretical; facets carry most predictive
validity; the model is a taxonomy of between-person differences, not a model of the person
[UNVERIFIED].

**Standing.** The FFM is the mainstream scientific model of trait personality. It is a
between-person trait taxonomy with strong replication and stability evidence; it is NOT a
state model and does not by itself predict what a person is doing at 03:14 in a control room.

### 2. DISC

**Lineage.** Marston's *Emotions of Normal People* (1928) proposed four primary emotions/behaviour
tendencies (Dominance, Inducement, Submission, Compliance). Marston did not build a test. Walter
Clarke built the first instrument from the theory (Activity Vector Analysis, 1956); Clarke &
Merenda published in the *Journal of Clinical Psychology* (Jan 1965); John Geier developed the
"Self Description" instrument into the Personal Profile System in the 1970s, which became the
Wiley "Everything DiSC" line. TTI Success Insights and Persolog sell other DISC-derived
instruments. [DISC-Wiki VERIFIED as summary of the Wikipedia article; primary sources UNVERIFIED.]

**Evidence.** The Wikipedia article (fetched) states plainly: "The scientific validity of the DISC
assessment has not been demonstrated, and psychologists question its predictive validity" and
"The DISC assessments have demonstrated no ability to predict job performance, as this is not
part of their designed functionality." It also reports: high test-retest reliability (a Russian
pilot study reporting .89 after one week); a 2013 German evaluation of the Persolog version
finding that it "largely" met reliability requirements "but not at all in terms of validity";
and Scandinavian research finding the DISC dimensions were not psychometrically independent and
were better explained by Big Five traits. [DISC-Wiki VERIFIED; underlying studies UNVERIFIED.]

**Independent validation?** The validation evidence that exists is overwhelmingly publisher
technical reports (Wiley/"Everything DiSC" research reports, TTI white papers, the 2015 IML
validation study found in search) rather than independent peer-reviewed construct- or
criterion-validity studies [VERIFIED that these are vendor documents from URLs; contents
UNVERIFIED]. I found no independent peer-reviewed study demonstrating criterion validity of DISC
for job performance, and no meta-analysis. The commercial instruments typically report acceptable
internal consistency and short-interval test-retest, which is the least demanding standard.

**Standing.** DISC is a commercial typology with reasonable reliability but weak, largely
non-independent validity evidence. Its four dimensions are not orthogonal and largely collapse
into Big Five Extraversion/Agreeableness space. It is not an accepted scientific personality
model. A treatise that uses DISC labels must treat them as a *communication vocabulary*, not as
measured constructs.

### 3. Dark Triad and Dark Tetrad

**Constructs.** Paulhus & Williams (2002, *J. Research in Personality*) introduced the Dark Triad
(narcissism, Machiavellianism, subclinical psychopathy) [UNVERIFIED — not fetched]. Buckels,
Jones & Paulhus (2013, *Psychological Science*, "Behavioral confirmation of everyday sadism") added
everyday sadism; Paulhus (2014, *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, "Toward a taxonomy
of dark personalities") named the Dark Tetrad [both UNVERIFIED, cited in the SD4 paper].

**Instruments.** Dirty Dozen (Jonason & Webster 2010) and Short Dark Triad SD3 (Jones & Paulhus
2014) for the triad [UNVERIFIED]. The Short Dark Tetrad **SD4** — Paulhus, Buckels, Trapnell &
Jones (2021), *European Journal of Psychological Assessment*, 37(3), 208–222,
doi:10.1027/1015-5759/a000602 [SD4 VERIFIED, PDF fetched]: 28 items, 4 subscales x 7 items.
Study samples: N = 868 (MTurk), N = 999 (students), N = 660 (students). Study 3 reliabilities
(alpha / omega): Narcissism .80/.80; Machiavellianism .75/.76; Psychopathy .81/.81; Sadism
.81/.81. Big Five correlations in Study 3: Sadism, Psychopathy and Machiavellianism correlate
negatively with Agreeableness (r = -.41, -.39, -.31); Narcissism correlates positively with
Extraversion (r = .49). The authors state the SD4 "extended content coverage of the SD3" and
better separates Machiavellianism from psychopathy.

**Validity issues.** The literature repeatedly finds high overlap between Machiavellianism and
psychopathy, and a strong common "dark core" (D-factor) largely tied to low Agreeableness /
low HEXACO Honesty-Humility [UNVERIFIED background]. Follow-ups: "The Dark Five: A
reconsideration of the SD4" (*Personality and Individual Differences*, 2024), cross-cultural
adaptations (Romanian, Polish, Spanish) and a study of the SD4 as an integrity screening
instrument (PubMed 38172254) exist [existence VERIFIED via search hits; contents UNVERIFIED].

**Standing.** Well-defined research constructs with acceptable short-scale reliability.
They are trait measures of *socially aversive* personality, validated for research on
between-person differences. Using them for employee selection or monitoring is contested, and
their use as a "risk" signal in a live system would be an unvalidated extrapolation.

### 4. Combining two trait inventories — tensor/Kronecker products

**Honest search result.** A web search for Kronecker/tensor products of two personality
inventories (Big Five x DISC) returned only mathematics and graph-theory pages; **no
psychometric literature was found that forms the Kronecker (outer) product of two trait
inventories to define a joint state space** [VERIFIED search, null result]. I am not aware of
any such practice in the psychometric literature.

**Why not.** Psychometrically, the two inventories are not independent measurement bases: DISC
dimensions are largely re-expressions of Big Five Extraversion and Agreeableness (see §2), so a
5 x 4 product would be heavily redundant, and the product of two trait scores has no established
construct meaning. Multiplicative (interaction) terms between two traits are used in regression
(moderated relationships), but that is a different thing from treating the outer product as a
state.

**Standard approaches.** (a) Joint/common factor analysis of the pooled item sets to see whether
the second inventory adds any dimension not in the first (this is how DISC has been shown to
collapse onto the Big Five); (b) circumplex models — the Interpersonal Circumplex (Wiggins;
Leary), the Abridged Big Five Circumplex (AB5C, Hofstee, de Raad & Goldberg 1992) which places
traits on planes defined by pairs of Big Five factors [UNVERIFIED]; (c) hierarchical models
(DeYoung's Big Two/aspects, the General Factor of Personality); (d) the **HEXACO** alternative
(Ashton & Lee), a six-factor lexical model adding Honesty-Humility, which absorbs much of the
Dark Triad/Tetrad variance and is the usual scientific route to modelling "dark" traits inside
a mainstream taxonomy [UNVERIFIED background].

### 5. State versus trait

Spielberger's State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI; Spielberger, Gorsuch & Lushene 1970; Form Y
1983) is the canonical operationalisation of the distinction: the S-scale asks how one feels
"right now, at this moment", the T-scale how one "generally" feels [UNVERIFIED, not fetched].
Trait = stable disposition (density of states over time); state = transient condition. Trait
scores predict the *base rate* or average of states, not the current value; within-person
variability in states is large relative to between-person trait differences (whole-trait /
density-distribution accounts, Fleeson 2001 [UNVERIFIED]). Consequently a Big Five, DISC or SD4
score administered once cannot be read as a real-time state. At most it is a prior.

### 6. Real-time state measurement

**Heart-rate variability.** Task Force of the ESC and NASPE (1996), "Heart rate variability:
Standards of measurement, physiological interpretation and clinical use", *Circulation* 93,
1043–1065 (also *Eur Heart J* 17, 354–381) [citation VERIFIED via PubMed record of the 1997
comment; content UNVERIFIED]. It defines time-domain (SDNN, RMSSD, pNN50) and frequency-domain
(VLF, LF, HF, LF/HF) measures and standard short-term (5-min) and 24-h recording conditions
[UNVERIFIED]. HRV (especially RMSSD/HF, vagally mediated) decreases under acute mental stress
and is widely used as a stress index (e.g. Kim et al. 2018 review in *Psychiatry Investigation*
[UNVERIFIED]). Caveats: LF/HF as a "sympathovagal balance" index is disputed (Billman 2013
[UNVERIFIED]); HRV is confounded by respiration, posture, movement, caffeine, fitness, age and
circadian phase; short windows are noisy; wearable PPG-derived HRV is less accurate than ECG.

**Electrodermal activity.** Skin conductance level and non-specific responses index
sympathetic arousal (Boucsein, *Electrodermal Activity*, 2nd ed. 2012; Society for
Psychophysiological Research publication guidelines, Boucsein et al. 2012 [UNVERIFIED]). Arousal
is not valenced: EDA cannot distinguish stress from excitement or effort.

**Pupillometry.** Kahneman & Beatty (1966, *Science*, "Pupil diameter and load on memory") and
Beatty (1982, *Psychological Bulletin*, "Task-evoked pupillary responses, processing load, and
the structure of processing resources") established task-evoked pupil dilation as a cognitive-load
index [UNVERIFIED, not fetched]. Winn et al. (2018, *Trends in Hearing*, best-practice paper)
covers practical constraints [existence VERIFIED via search; content UNVERIFIED]. Fatal
confound for control rooms: pupil size is dominated by luminance and gaze angle; screens,
alarm flashes and lighting changes swamp the cognitive signal unless luminance is controlled.

**Eye tracking.** Fixation duration, saccade rate, blink rate, scan-path entropy and dwell on
displays are used as workload/attention indices; they are task-sensitive and useful for
interface evaluation, but their mapping to "stress" or "emotion" is weak.

**EEG workload indices.** Frontal theta increase and parietal alpha decrease with workload are the
robust findings (Gevins & Smith; Borghini et al. 2014 review; Hogervorst, Brouwer & van Erp 2014,
*Frontiers in Neuroscience*, "Combining and comparing EEG, peripheral physiology and eye-related
measures for the assessment of mental workload" [existence VERIFIED via search; content
UNVERIFIED]). Practical: dry-electrode headsets, motion artefacts, individual calibration, and
poor cross-session/cross-person generalisation limit field deployment.

**Speech-based stress/emotion recognition.** A 2025 systematic review, "Measuring negative
emotions and stress through acoustic correlates in speech" (PMC12289014) [existence VERIFIED via
search; content not fetched — page blocked] and Frontiers in Computer Science 2021, "An
Evaluation of Speech-Based Recognition of Emotional and Physiological Markers of Stress"
[existence VERIFIED; content UNVERIFIED] are recent entry points. Known concerns (background,
UNVERIFIED): most models are trained on acted or elicited corpora; accuracies reported on those
corpora fall sharply on spontaneous, cross-corpus, cross-language or noisy data; "ground truth"
is usually self-report or actor intent; pitch/intensity/speech-rate changes reflect arousal and
respiration, not discrete emotion; speaker, microphone, headset and channel effects dominate.
There is no accepted accuracy ceiling to quote; any single number would be corpus-specific.

**The affective-computing critique.** Barrett, Adolphs, Marsella, Martinez & Pollak (2019),
"Emotional expressions reconsidered: Challenges to inferring emotion from human facial
movements", *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 20(1), 1–68,
doi:10.1177/1529100619832930 [VERIFIED, APS page fetched]. They evaluate the "common view"
against reliability, specificity, generalizability and validity. Findings quoted from the fetched
page: on reliability, "a smile might signal submission instead of happiness"; on specificity,
"no facial configuration" was found to be distinctive of a particular emotion, even across
cultures; on generalizability, there is "a dearth of systematic and controlled observations of
facial movements and emotion in people from remote cultures". Conclusion: "facial movements
commonly thought to signal particular emotions regardless of context, person, and culture are not
universally diagnostic of emotional states." They explicitly question use of the common view by
"technology companies trying to figure out how to 'read emotions'". The argument transfers to
vocal expression: the same absence of reliable, specific, context-free signatures applies.

**Net.** Physiological channels measure *arousal* and *load* with reasonable validity when
confounds are controlled; they do not measure discrete emotions, "mood", trust or team climate.
Multimodal fusion improves robustness for workload classification within a person/session but
not the construct validity of an "emotion" label.

### 7. Ethics and professional guidance

- **ITC (International Test Commission)** *International Guidelines for Test Use* (2001;
  Bartram) and *Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests* (2nd ed. 2017) — competence of
  users, fitness of the test for purpose, informed consent, feedback, security, fairness
  [existence VERIFIED via search; text UNVERIFIED]. Also ITC guidelines on computer-based and
  internet-delivered testing (2005) and on technology-based assessment (2022 with ATP)
  [UNVERIFIED].
- **EFPA Test Review Model** (Evers et al. 2013 revision, "Assessing the quality of tests:
  Revision of the EFPA review model") — a standard rubric for rating a test's theoretical basis,
  norms, reliability and validity; commercial DISC instruments would be rated on these criteria
  and the absence of independent validity evidence would show [existence VERIFIED via search;
  content UNVERIFIED].
- **AERA/APA/NCME Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (2014)** — validity is a
  property of *interpretations for a use*, not of a test; using a trait test to infer momentary
  state or fitness for duty is a new use requiring its own validity evidence [UNVERIFIED].
- **APA Ethics Code** Standard 9 (Assessment): use instruments whose validity and reliability
  have been established for the population and purpose; informed consent; explain results;
  qualified users [UNVERIFIED].
- **SIOP Principles for the Validation and Use of Personnel Selection Procedures (2018)** —
  applies if any score influences staffing decisions [UNVERIFIED].
- **Regulatory context** — EU AI Act (2024) prohibits emotion-recognition systems in the
  workplace except for medical or safety reasons, and classifies employment-related and
  emotion-inference systems as high-risk; GDPR treats biometric and health-related data as
  special-category [UNVERIFIED, not fetched — must be checked against the final text before
  citation].
- **Core ethical points for continuous operator monitoring:** consent that is meaningful under
  employment power asymmetry; purpose limitation (safety support vs performance management);
  data minimisation; no covert inference of health or personality; operator access to and
  ability to contest inferences; validation before deployment; human-in-the-loop; no use of
  "dark" trait scores as risk flags without evidence and legal review.

## What an engineering treatise can legitimately say

- It may cite the Big Five as the mainstream, replicated, stable trait taxonomy and use it as a
  *prior* or descriptive frame for between-operator differences. It must not present Big Five
  scores as real-time state, and must not quote reliability coefficients without a primary source.
- It may use DISC only as a lay communication vocabulary, and must say that DISC lacks
  independent peer-reviewed validity evidence and that its dimensions collapse largely onto Big
  Five Extraversion/Agreeableness. It must not describe DISC as a validated construct system.
- It may cite the Dark Tetrad and the SD4 (28 items; Study-3 alphas .75–.81) as research
  instruments. It must not use Dark Tetrad scores as operational risk flags for individual
  operators, and must not claim they predict control-room behaviour.
- It must not claim that a Kronecker/tensor product of two trait inventories has any
  psychometric precedent; if it uses such a product it must present it as an engineering
  parameterisation with no measurement-theoretic warrant, and should expect redundancy.
- It may say that HRV, EDA, pupillometry, eye tracking and EEG provide validated indices of
  *arousal* and *cognitive workload* under controlled conditions, with named confounds, and that
  these are the legitimate real-time channels. It must not claim any of them measures emotion,
  mood, morale or trust.
- It must acknowledge Barrett et al. (2019): facial and, by extension, vocal "emotion reading"
  is not reliably, specifically or generalisably diagnostic of emotional state; speech emotion
  recognition accuracies are corpus-specific and should not be quoted as system performance.
- It must frame any operator-state estimate as a probabilistic, uncertain, per-person-calibrated
  signal for *decision support*, subject to consent, purpose limitation, transparency and
  independent validation, consistent with ITC/EFPA/APA/AERA standards and (where applicable)
  the EU AI Act's restrictions on workplace emotion recognition.
- It must not present "team psychological state" as measurable from individual physiology
  aggregated; that is an untested inferential leap.
