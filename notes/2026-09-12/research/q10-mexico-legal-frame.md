## Findings

### 1. Data protection — the new LFPDPPP (DOF 20 March 2025) and its application to operator psychological/physiological data

**Status and authority (VERIFIED).** The diputados.gob.mx consolidated text is headed "LEY FEDERAL DE PROTECCIÓN DE DATOS PERSONALES EN POSESIÓN DE LOS PARTICULARES — Nueva Ley publicada en el Diario Oficial de la Federación el 20 de marzo de 2025 — TEXTO VIGENTE — Última reforma publicada DOF 14-11-2025" [LFPDPPP-2025]. It was issued as "Artículo Tercero" of a decree that also expides the new Ley General de Transparencia and Ley General de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de Sujetos Obligados and reforms Art. 37 of the Ley Orgánica de la Administración Pública Federal. Transitory Segundo abrogates "La Ley Federal de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de los Particulares, publicada en el Diario Oficial de la Federación el 5 de julio de 2010". Entry into force: day after publication, i.e. 21 March 2025 [KPMG-2025, VERIFIED secondary]. The only amendment since is the 14-11-2025 decree on procedural harmonisation with the Código Nacional de Procedimientos Civiles y Familiares, which touched Art. 4 only (VERIFIED from the reform annex).

The regulator is no longer INAI. Art. 2 fr. XV: "Secretaría: Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno" (SABG) [VERIFIED]. Transitory Quinto: INAI's human resources "pasarán a formar parte de la Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno y Transparencia para el Pueblo"; Transitory Décimo: pending data-protection procedures continue "ante la Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno" [VERIFIED]. Transitory Vigésimo orders the federal judiciary to set up specialised district courts and collegiate circuit courts in access-to-information and data protection within 120 days [VERIFIED].

**Reglamento / Lineamientos (secondary, VERIFIED as to what the sources say).** Transitory Décimo Segundo of the decree required the Executive to "expedir las adecuaciones correspondientes a los reglamentos y demás disposiciones aplicables ... dentro de los noventa días naturales siguientes a la entrada en vigor" [VERIFIED]. Chambers' Data Protection & Privacy 2026 (Mexico) states: "As of February 2026, no updated regulations or technical corrections have been published" and that organisations continue to rely on the 2011 Regulations and legacy guidance; SABG opened stakeholder dialogues in January 2026 but had issued no formal implementing guidance [Chambers-2026]. A Mexican practitioner note updated 9 July 2026 repeats: "A julio de 2026, el reglamento actualizado de la LFPDPPP sigue sin publicarse ... Mientras no se publique el nuevo reglamento, el de 2011 sigue aplicando de forma supletoria en todo lo que no contradiga a la nueva ley" [Sharkit-2026]. I found no DOF publication of a new Reglamento through September 2026 (gap: not exhaustively checked against the DOF index). Working assumption for the paper: the 2011 Reglamento (DOF 21-12-2011) and 2013 Lineamientos del Aviso de Privacidad (DOF 17-01-2013) apply supletoriamente where compatible (K, UNVERIFIED dates).

**Definitions (VERIFIED, Art. 2).**
- Fr. V "Datos personales: Cualquier información concerniente a una persona identificada o identificable."
- Fr. VI "Datos personales sensibles: Aquellos datos personales que afecten a la esfera más íntima de la persona titular, o cuya utilización indebida pueda dar origen a discriminación o conlleve un riesgo grave para esta. De manera enunciativa más no limitativa se consideran sensibles los datos personales que puedan revelar aspectos como origen racial o étnico, estado de salud presente o futuro, información genética, creencias religiosas, filosóficas y morales, opiniones políticas y preferencia sexual". Gloss: sensitive data are those touching the most intimate sphere or whose misuse could cause discrimination or grave risk; the list is expressly *non-exhaustive* ("enunciativa más no limitativa") and includes "present or future state of health". Two things to note: (i) the 2010 list item "afiliación sindical" no longer appears in the fetched text (the INAI 2018 guide confirms the 2010 federal list did include it) — a drafting change worth flagging; (ii) the words "biométricos", "emocional", "psicológico" do not appear anywhere in the law (grep of the full PDF: 0 hits for "biométric", 0 for "trabajador").
- Fr. IV "Consentimiento: Manifestación de la voluntad libre, específica e informada de la persona titular de los datos mediante la cual se efectúa el tratamiento de los mismos".
- Fr. XIX "Tratamiento" covers "cualquier operación o conjunto de operaciones efectuadas mediante procedimientos manuales o automatizados" [KPMG-2025 quoting the law; VERIFIED secondary].

**Is inferred stress/arousal/"psychological state" sensitive data?** The law does not say so expressly; but (a) inferred affective or fatigue state from physiological signals (HRV, EDA, eye-tracking, voice) is information that "may reveal ... estado de salud presente o futuro" and whose misuse "pueda dar origen a discriminación", so under the open-ended definition it is very likely sensitive; (b) the INAI 2018 biometric guide (still the only official Mexican guidance) reasons that biometric data, though unlisted, are sensitive when they fall in any of the three tests: "a) Que se refieran a la esfera más íntima de su titular; b) Que su utilización indebida pueda dar origen a discriminación, o c) Que su uso ilegítimo conlleve un grave riesgo para su titular" and gives the example that iris data "podría considerarse sensible en los casos en que permita obtener información sobre el estado de salud" [INAI-Biom, VERIFIED]. The guide's definition of biometric data is broad: "Propiedades físicas, fisiológicas, de comportamiento o rasgos de la personalidad, atribuibles a una sola persona y que son medibles", and its list of physiological biometrics includes "pulsación cardíaca" [INAI-Biom, VERIFIED]. Chambers 2026 states that in practice biometric data are treated as sensitive requiring express written consent [Chambers-2026]. Conclusion: a control-room system that ingests physiological signals and outputs an operator-level state estimate should be designed on the assumption that both the raw signals and the inferred state are *datos personales sensibles*.

**Consent for sensitive data (VERIFIED, Art. 8).** "Tratándose de datos personales sensibles, el responsable deberá obtener el consentimiento expreso y por escrito de la persona titular para su tratamiento, a través de su firma autógrafa, firma electrónica, o cualquier mecanismo de autenticación que al efecto se establezca. No podrán crearse bases de datos que contengan datos personales sensibles, sin que se justifique la creación de las mismas para finalidades legítimas, concretas y acordes con las actividades o fines explícitos que persigue el sujeto regulado." Gloss: express *written* consent (handwritten or electronic signature or an equivalent authentication mechanism) and a justified, legitimate, concrete purpose for any sensitive-data database. Art. 9 lists the exceptions to consent (legal provision; public sources; prior disociación; "los datos personales se requieran para ejercer un derecho o cumplir obligaciones derivadas de una relación jurídica entre la persona titular y el responsable"; emergency; medical care under professional secrecy; judicial order) [VERIFIED]. The employment relationship is a "relación jurídica" under fr. IV, but that exception is generally read as covering data *necessary* for performing the contract, not novel sensitive-data monitoring; and the text of Art. 8 is specific to sensitive data, so the conservative reading is that Art. 8 written consent is still required for physiological/psychological monitoring. Note also the structural problem of consent validity in a subordinate relationship (LFT Art. 3 Bis defines "hostigamiento" in terms of "una relación de subordinación real") — free consent is contestable, so purpose-limitation and proportionality must carry the weight.

**Principles (VERIFIED).** Art. 5: "El responsable deberá observar los principios de licitud, finalidad, lealtad, consentimiento, calidad, proporcionalidad, información y responsabilidad en el tratamiento de datos personales." Art. 11 (finalidad): processing "deberá limitarse al cumplimiento de las finalidades previstas en el aviso de privacidad"; a new purpose needs new consent. Art. 12 (proporcionalidad): "El tratamiento de datos personales será el que resulte necesario, adecuado y relevante en relación con las finalidades previstas en el aviso de privacidad, para los datos personales sensibles, el responsable deberá realizar esfuerzos razonables para limitar el periodo de tratamiento de los mismos a efecto de que sea el mínimo indispensable." Gloss: for sensitive data the retention period must be the minimum indispensable — this argues for on-the-fly (ephemeral) computation of the operator state and no long-term per-operator logs. Art. 13 (responsabilidad) requires measures to ensure the aviso de privacidad is honoured by the controller and by third parties with whom it has a legal relationship (i.e. the Dutch supplier as encargado).

**Aviso de privacidad (VERIFIED).** Art. 15 minimum content: identity/domicile of the responsable; "Los datos personales que serán sometidos a tratamiento, identificando aquéllos que son sensibles"; purposes, "distinguiendo aquéllas que requieren el consentimiento"; means to limit use; ARCO mechanisms; change-notification procedure. Art. 16: when data are collected "por cualquier medio electrónico, óptico, sonoro, visual, o a través de cualquier otra tecnología" a simplified notice must be given with a pointer to the full notice — directly applicable to sensor capture in a control room.

**ARCO rights and automated processing (VERIFIED).** Arts. 21–26. Art. 26 (oposición) fr. II gives a right to object when "Sus datos personales sean objeto de un tratamiento automatizado, el cual le produzca efectos jurídicos no deseados o afecte de manera significativa sus intereses, derechos o libertades, y estén destinados a evaluar, sin intervención humana, determinados aspectos personales de la misma o analizar o predecir, en particular, su rendimiento profesional, situación económica, estado de salud, preferencias sexuales, fiabilidad o comportamiento." Gloss: a right to object to automated evaluation/prediction of professional performance, health, reliability or behaviour without human intervention. This is not a prohibition (unlike GDPR Art. 22's default ban), and it does not apply "en aquellos casos en los que el tratamiento sea necesario para el cumplimiento de una obligación legal impuesta al responsable" — but a display that scores operator "fiabilidad" or "comportamiento" is squarely within the wording, and objection must be honoured absent a legal duty.

**Security, confidentiality, breach (VERIFIED).** Art. 18: "Todo responsable deberá establecer y mantener medidas de seguridad administrativas, técnicas y físicas ... Asimismo, se tomará en cuenta el riesgo existente, las posibles consecuencias para las personas titulares, la sensibilidad de los datos y el desarrollo tecnológico." Art. 19: security breaches that "afecten de forma significativa los derechos patrimoniales o morales" must be notified "de forma inmediata" to the data subject. Art. 20: confidentiality controls on everyone intervening in processing, surviving termination of the relationship.

**Sanctions (VERIFIED).** Art. 58 lists infractions (incl. fr. IV "Dar tratamiento a los datos personales en contravención a los principios", fr. V omissions in the aviso de privacidad). Art. 59: I. apercibimiento; II. "Multa de 100 a 160,000 veces la Unidad de Medida y Actualización" (fr. II–VII infractions); III. "Multa de 200 a 320,000 veces la Unidad de Medida y Actualización" (fr. VIII–XVIII); IV. for repeated infractions an additional fine of 100–320,000 UMA, and "En tratándose de infracciones cometidas en el tratamiento de datos sensibles, las sanciones podrán incrementarse hasta por dos veces, los montos establecidos." Criminal provisions: Art. 62 (3 months–3 years for a for-profit security breach by an authorised processor), Art. 63 (6 months–5 years for deceptive processing for gain), Art. 64 "Tratándose de datos personales sensibles, las penas a que se refiere este Capítulo se duplicarán." (2026 daily UMA value not verified here — (K) approx. MXN 117; 320,000 UMA is on the order of MXN 37 million, doubled for sensitive data.)

**Employees / biometrics in the law.** No article addresses the employment relationship or biometric data expressly (VERIFIED by full-text grep). The employer-employee application rests on the general rules plus the INAI-era guidance. INAI's biometric guide includes an employment example: biometric data collected "para controlar el acceso a las instalaciones o sistemas informáticos del empleador ... deberían eliminarse tan pronto como ... se termine la relación laboral" and recommends privacy-by-design and Evaluaciones de Impacto a la Protección de Datos Personales [INAI-Biom, VERIFIED]. (Its "Recomendaciones para el tratamiento de datos personales en el entorno laboral" — a separate INAI document — could not be fetched within budget; UNVERIFIED.) Chambers 2026: "Workplace monitoring and the use of IT systems should be structured around necessity and proportionality" [Chambers-2026].

**International transfer (secondary).** Chambers 2026: transfers require the foreign recipient to assume obligations equivalent to the controller's, typically by contract; no adequacy concept, no government approval; "Mexico has not received EU adequacy status"; "Mexican private-sector rules do not expressly claim extraterritorial reach" [Chambers-2026].

### 2. Labour law — LFT, Constitution, ILO C190

**Constitution (VERIFIED, CPEUM texto vigente, últimas reformas DOF 02-06-2026).** Art. 16, para. 1: "Nadie puede ser molestado en su persona, familia, domicilio, papeles o posesiones, sino en virtud de mandamiento escrito de la autoridad competente..."; para. 2 (added DOF 01-06-2009): "Toda persona tiene derecho a la protección de sus datos personales, al acceso, rectificación y cancelación de los mismos, así como a manifestar su oposición, en los términos que fije la ley, la cual establecerá los supuestos de excepción a los principios que rijan el tratamiento de datos, por razones de seguridad nacional, disposiciones de orden público, seguridad y salud públicas o para proteger los derechos de terceros." Art. 6, apartado A fr. II: "La información que se refiere a la vida privada y los datos personales será protegida en los términos y con las excepciones que fijen las leyes." Horizontal effect of fundamental rights between private parties (employer/employee) is established SCJN doctrine (K, UNVERIFIED — no tesis fetched).

**LFT (VERIFIED, texto vigente, última reforma DOF 14-05-2026).**
- Art. 2: work norms aim at "propiciar el trabajo digno o decente en todas las relaciones laborales, en un entorno libre de violencias y con respeto pleno a los derechos humanos" (para. reformed DOF 15-01-2026). "Se entiende por trabajo digno o decente aquél en el que se respeta plenamente la dignidad humana del trabajador; no existe discriminación por ... condiciones de salud ... y se cuenta con condiciones óptimas de seguridad e higiene para prevenir riesgos de trabajo."
- Art. 3: work "exige respeto para las libertades y dignidad de quien lo presta ... Debe efectuarse en condiciones que aseguren la vida digna y la salud para las y los trabajadores ... en un entorno laboral libre de discriminación y violencias" (reformed 01-05-2019, 15-01-2026). "No podrán establecerse condiciones que impliquen discriminación entre los trabajadores por motivo de ... condiciones de salud ... o cualquier otro que atente contra la dignidad humana. No se considerarán discriminatorias las distinciones, exclusiones o preferencias que se sustenten en las calificaciones particulares que exija una labor determinada." Gloss: distinctions based on genuine occupational qualifications are allowed — this is the hook for fitness-for-duty in safety-critical roles.
- Art. 3 Bis a): "Hostigamiento, el ejercicio del poder en una relación de subordinación real de la víctima frente al agresor en el ámbito laboral, que se expresa en conductas verbales, físicas o ambas".
- Art. 16 (paras added DOF 15-01-2026): employers and workers "deberán contribuir al mantenimiento de un entorno laboral libre de discriminación y violencias hacia las mujeres"; employers must train staff to prevent violence against women.
- Art. 132 (employer obligations): fr. XVI install and operate workplaces "de acuerdo con las disposiciones establecidas en el reglamento y las normas oficiales mexicanas en materia de seguridad, salud y medio ambiente de trabajo, a efecto de prevenir accidentes y enfermedades laborales"; fr. XVII "Cumplir el reglamento y las normas oficiales mexicanas en materia de seguridad, salud y medio ambiente de trabajo"; fr. XVIII post and disseminate the relevant NOM provisions and "difundir a los trabajadores la información sobre los riesgos y peligros a los que están expuestos"; fr. XXXI "Implementar, en acuerdo con los trabajadores, un protocolo para prevenir la discriminación por razones de género y atención de casos de violencia y acoso u hostigamiento sexual"; fr. XXXIV (added 2026) electronic recording of each worker's working hours (a new, mandatory workplace data flow; STPS to issue general rules).
- Art. 133 (employer prohibitions): fr. I refuse workers on grounds incl. "condiciones de salud"; fr. VII "Ejecutar cualquier acto que restrinja a los trabajadores los derechos que les otorgan las leyes"; fr. XII–XIII commit or tolerate "hostigamiento y/o acoso sexual"; fr. XIV demand non-pregnancy certificates. There is no fraction on electronic surveillance.
- Art. 134 fr. X (worker obligation): "Someterse a los reconocimientos médicos previstos en el reglamento interior y demás normas vigentes en la empresa o establecimiento, para comprobar que no padecen alguna incapacidad o enfermedad de trabajo, contagiosa o incurable". Gloss: medical examinations are lawful only if provided for in the reglamento interior de trabajo (which is negotiated with the workers' representatives, Arts. 422–425 (K)) — continuous physiological monitoring is not a "reconocimiento médico" in this sense.
- Art. 47 (dismissal without employer liability): no ground refers to emotional or psychological state. Relevant grounds are fr. VII "Comprometer el trabajador, por su imprudencia o descuido inexcusable, la seguridad del establecimiento o de las personas que se encuentren en él", fr. XII "Negarse el trabajador a adoptar las medidas preventivas o a seguir los procedimientos indicados para evitar accidentes o enfermedades", fr. XIII intoxication. A sonified "stress level" is not itself a rescission ground; using it as evidence of fr. VII would face the Art. 26 fr. II LFPDPPP objection right and Art. 3 anti-discrimination rule.
- Art. 51 fr. II (worker may rescind for employer "malos tratamientos u otros análogos") and fr. VII (grave danger to safety or health).
- Art. 330-I (teletrabajo, added DOF 11-01-2021) — the only express statutory proportionality rule for technological supervision of workers: "Los mecanismos, sistemas operativos y cualquier tecnología utilizada para supervisar el teletrabajo deberán ser proporcionales a su objetivo, garantizando el derecho a la intimidad de las personas trabajadoras ... y respetando el marco jurídico aplicable en materia de protección de datos personales. Solamente podrán utilizarse cámaras de video y micrófonos para supervisar el teletrabajo de manera extraordinaria, o cuando la naturaleza de las funciones desempeñadas ... lo requiera." Gloss: formally limited to telework, but it is the clearest legislative statement of the Mexican standard (proportionality; cameras/microphones only exceptionally or where the nature of the function requires) and would be cited by analogy for on-site sensor monitoring.
- Art. 291-J (digital platforms, added DOF 24-12-2024) — Mexico's first algorithmic-management rule: assignment rules "a través de algoritmos o mecanismos análogos deberán ser transparentes, claras y conocidas"; "Se entenderá por algoritmo el uso de sistemas de toma de decisiones que permitan ejercer de manera automatizada o análoga el mando y supervisión sobre la persona trabajadora"; a plain-language "política de gestión algorítmica del trabajo" is mandatory; "El algoritmo deberá ser razonable en sus requisitos y elementos, atendiendo a las condiciones objetivas del trabajo y no poner en riesgo la salud e integridad del trabajador, ni ser factor de discriminación en su contra." Art. 291-K fr. IV: platforms must guarantee security of workers' personal data and ARCO + portability rights. Scope is platform work only, but it signals the legislator's template for algorithmic supervision.
- Art. 475 Bis: "El patrón es responsable de la seguridad e higiene y de la prevención de los riesgos en el trabajo, conforme a las disposiciones de esta Ley, sus reglamentos y las normas oficiales mexicanas aplicables."
- Sanctions: Art. 994 fr. V "De 250 a 5000 Unidades de Medida y Actualización, al patrón que no observe en la instalación de sus establecimientos las normas de seguridad e higiene o las medidas que fijen las Leyes para prevenir los riesgos de trabajo"; fr. VI 250–5000 UMA for discriminatory acts; Art. 1002 residual 50–5000 UMA. (Per-worker multiplication under Art. 992 (K, UNVERIFIED).)
- Full-text grep of the LFT: "videovigilancia" 0, "monitoreo" 0, "inteligencia artificial" 0 — there is no general statutory regime for workplace surveillance.

**ILO Convention 190 (VERIFIED, DOF 19-06-2023 promulgation decree).** Senate approval decree published DOF 6 April 2022; instrument of ratification signed 7 April 2022 and "depositado ante el Director General de la Oficina Internacional del Trabajo, el seis de julio del propio año"; "TRANSITORIO ÚNICO.- El presente Decreto entrará en vigor el seis de julio de dos mil veintitrés." C190 Art. 1(1)(a) defines violence and harassment as behaviours "que tengan por objeto, que causen o sean susceptibles de causar, un daño físico, psicológico, sexual o económico"; Art. 9(b) requires employers "tener en cuenta la violencia y el acoso, así como los riesgos psicosociales asociados, en la gestión de la seguridad y salud en el trabajo". As a ratified treaty it ranks above federal statute under CPEUM Art. 1/133 (K). The LFT 15-01-2026 reform ("entorno libre de violencias") is the domestic follow-through.

**Case law on employee monitoring.** Not fetched (budget); (K, UNVERIFIED) SCJN and Tribunales Colegiados have accepted workplace video recordings as evidence when the worker was informed and the purpose was legitimate, and have applied a proportionality test; there is no Mexican decision on physiological or affective monitoring.

### 3. NOM-035-STPS-2018 and related STPS norms

**NOM-035 (VERIFIED, DOF 23-10-2018).** Objective (1): "Establecer los elementos para identificar, analizar y prevenir los factores de riesgo psicosocial, así como para promover un entorno organizacional favorable en los centros de trabajo." Scope (2): applies nationally to all workplaces, tiered: a) up to 15 workers: 5.1, 5.4, 5.5, 5.7, 8.1, 8.2; b) 16–50 workers: 5.1, 5.2, 5.4–5.8, 7.1 a), 7.2, 7.4–7.9 and Chapter 8; c) more than 50 workers: 5.1, 5.3–5.8, 7.1 b), 7.2–7.9 and Chapter 8.

Employer obligations (Ch. 5): 5.1 written policy on psychosocial risk prevention, workplace violence prevention and a favourable organisational environment; 5.2 (16–50) identify and analyse psychosocial risk factors; 5.3 (>50) also evaluate the organisational environment; 5.4 adopt prevention/control measures (Ch. 8); 5.5 identify workers who suffered severe traumatic events and refer them; 5.6 "Practicar exámenes médicos y evaluaciones psicológicas a los trabajadores expuestos a violencia laboral y/o a los factores de riesgo psicosocial, cuando existan signos o síntomas que denoten alguna alteración a su salud y el resultado de la identificación y análisis ... así lo sugiera y/o existan quejas de violencia laboral"; 5.7 disseminate information (incl. e) the results of the identification/analysis); 5.8 keep records of results, control measures, and workers examined.

Method (Ch. 7): 7.1 a) 16–50 workers: identification/analysis "incluyendo a todos los trabajadores (ver Guía de referencia II)"; b) >50: identification/analysis plus organisational-environment evaluation, "se podrán realizar con una muestra representativa conforme a ... la Guía de referencia III". 7.4: workplaces may use "cualquier método que contemple lo siguiente: a) La aplicación de cuestionarios que cumplan con lo dispuesto por los numerales 7.2 y/o 7.3 ... y 7.5; b) La forma como se deberá realizar la aplicación de los cuestionarios; c) La manera de evaluar los cuestionarios, y d) Los niveles de riesgo y la forma de determinarlos conforme a los resultados de los cuestionarios." 7.5: self-developed questionnaires must be validated on workers in Mexican workplaces with documented sample size, reliability and validity. 7.6: results "deberá integrarse al diagnóstico de seguridad y salud en el trabajo a que se refiere la NOM-030-STPS-2009". 7.7 report content (method, results, conclusions, recommendations, evaluator's name and cédula profesional). 7.8 "El resultado ... deberá estar disponible para consulta de los trabajadores." 7.9 "deberá realizarse, al menos, cada dos años."

Confidentiality: the Guías de Referencia II and III require the applier, before administering the questionnaire, to "Enfatizar en la protección de la privacidad y confidencialidad del manejo de los datos, y que el uso de la información proporcionada por el trabajador (ver Guía de referencia V Datos del trabajador) y de sus resultados será exclusivamente para fines de mejora del ambiente de trabajo" [VERIFIED]. Guía V is a worker-data sheet. I did not find, in the fetched text, a sentence literally prohibiting use of results for "discriminación o represalias"; the operative constraints are the exclusive-purpose statement above, Ch. 8's requirement of "Lineamientos para prohibir la discriminación y fomentar la equidad y el respeto" (8.4 a) 2)), and 8.1 b) "mecanismos seguros y confidenciales para la recepción de quejas". The Guías are "un complemento para la mejor comprensión de la presente Norma y no es de cumplimiento obligatorio".

Records (10): documentary evidence "se deberán conservar al menos durante un año". Vigilancia (11): "La vigilancia del cumplimiento de la presente Norma Oficial Mexicana corresponde a la Secretaría del Trabajo y Previsión Social." Concordance (13): "no es equivalente con ninguna norma internacional". Transitorios: in force one year after publication (23-10-2019); numerals 5.2, 5.3, 5.6, 5.8, 8.3–8.5 and Ch. 7 two years after (23-10-2020).

**Would continuous sensor-based monitoring satisfy or conflict with NOM-035?** It cannot *replace* the NOM's method: 7.4 defines any acceptable method in terms of *questionnaires* meeting 7.2/7.3/7.5, and 7.5 demands psychometric validation on Mexican workers. A physiological/affective state estimator is not a questionnaire and measures individual state, not the organisational risk factors enumerated in 7.2 (ambiente, cargas, control, jornadas, work-family interference, leadership, violence). It also does not conflict per se: NOM-035 is a floor. But three NOM-035 features pull against an individual, continuous display: (i) the >50-worker tier is expressly *sample-based* and organisation-level; (ii) the questionnaire process is anonymised/confidential and "exclusively for improvement of the work environment"; (iii) 5.6 makes individual psychological evaluation a *triggered* measure (signs/symptoms + risk results + complaints) performed by medical/psychological professionals, not a standing employer function. A system that flags an individual operator's state to supervisors is therefore outside the NOM's model and must be justified independently under LFPDPPP Arts. 8, 11, 12 and LFT Arts. 3, 132 XVI/XVII, 475 Bis (safety), not as NOM-035 compliance. It can legitimately be positioned as an *additional* control measure under 5.4/Ch. 8 (e.g. workload distribution) if it operates on aggregated, non-identifying signals.

**NOM-030-STPS-2009** (Servicios preventivos de seguridad y salud en el trabajo; the "diagnóstico" into which NOM-035 results are integrated — VERIFIED via the 7.6 cross-reference; content K/UNVERIFIED). **NOM-036-1-STPS-2018** (Factores de riesgo ergonómico — manual handling of loads; DOF 23-11-2018; K/UNVERIFIED) is not relevant to seated control-room work beyond the general ergonomics duty; there is no Mexican NOM specific to control-room ergonomics (ISO 11064 is not adopted as NOM/NMX so far as found). **Reglamento Federal de Seguridad y Salud en el Trabajo (DOF 13-11-2014)** Art. 43 imposes the psychosocial-risk obligations underlying NOM-035 and Art. 55 ergonomic factors (K, UNVERIFIED article numbers). Inspection and sanctions: STPS Inspección del Trabajo under the Reglamento General de Inspección del Trabajo y Aplicación de Sanciones (DOF 17-06-2014, K) with LFT Art. 994 fr. V fines of 250–5000 UMA per breach (VERIFIED).

### 6. Cross-border reach of EU law and trade agreements

**EU AI Act (Reg. (EU) 2024/1689) (K, UNVERIFIED — EUR-Lex fetch blocked).** Art. 2(1)(a) applies to providers placing on the market or putting into service AI systems *in the Union*; Art. 2(1)(c) applies to providers and deployers in third countries "where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union". A system supplied by a Dutch provider but placed into service and whose output is used only at a Mexican site is outside Art. 2 — the Art. 5(1)(f) prohibition on emotion inference in the workplace does not bite. Caveats: (i) if the same product is marketed in the EU it is prohibited there and the provider must not "place on the market" it in the EU; (ii) if any output (e.g. operator-state analytics, model retraining on Mexican operator data, dashboards viewed by Dutch staff) is used in the Union, Art. 2(1)(c) is arguable; (iii) Dutch research-funding or ethics-committee conditions may import AI Act values contractually.

**GDPR (K, UNVERIFIED text).** Art. 3(1) applies to processing "in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or processor in the Union, regardless of whether the processing takes place in the Union or not". If the Dutch company acts as controller or processor of the Mexican operators' physiological/psychological data (e.g. hosts the model, receives telemetry, runs the research analysis), GDPR — including Art. 9 (health/biometric data: prohibition unless an exception such as explicit consent or Art. 9(2)(h)/(j) applies) and Art. 35 DPIA — applies to that Dutch establishment even though the data subjects are in Mexico. The only way to keep GDPR out is for the Dutch company to have no controller/processor role (turn-key delivery, no telemetry, no research data return). Dutch works-council (WOR Art. 27) consent does not apply to a Mexican site; the Mexican counterpart is the reglamento interior de trabajo/collective agreement and the LFT Art. 132 XXXI protocol "en acuerdo con los trabajadores".

**Mexico–EU data transfer.** Mexico has no EU adequacy decision [Chambers-2026]; transfers Mexico→Netherlands are governed by LFPDPPP transfer rules (recipient assumes equivalent obligations, contractual) and, for sensitive data, by consent/aviso content; transfers Netherlands→Mexico of any EU-origin personal data would need SCCs. The modernised EU–Mexico Global Agreement (negotiations concluded 17 January 2025 per EEAS; signature/ratification status in 2026 not verified) contains a digital-trade chapter with a data-flows clause subject to each side's data-protection rules; it does not create adequacy (K, UNVERIFIED).

**USMCA/T-MEC Chapter 19 (K, UNVERIFIED).** Art. 19.11 (cross-border transfer of information by electronic means) and 19.12 (no data-localisation requirement) bind Mexico vis-à-vis the US and Canada only; Art. 19.8 requires a legal framework for personal-data protection. They do not benefit an EU supplier directly, but they explain why Mexican law contains no localisation requirement for control-room data.

## Verdict on the assumption "there are no restrictions in Mexico"

The assumption is **wrong as stated and should be replaced with "there is no prohibition, but there are binding constraints"**. What Mexico does *not* have (September 2026): an AI statute; any rule naming emotion recognition; a general workplace-surveillance statute; a general cybersecurity statute; EU adequacy; a works-council veto. What Mexico *does* have, all in force and enforceable:

1. LFPDPPP 2025 — inferred psychological/affective state and the physiological/biometric inputs are, on the law's own open-ended definition, sensitive data ("estado de salud presente o futuro", discrimination risk); Art. 8 requires **express written (signed) consent** and a justified purpose for any database of them; Art. 12 requires the retention of sensitive data to be "el mínimo indispensable"; Art. 26 fr. II gives operators a **right to object** to automated evaluation of their "rendimiento profesional ... estado de salud ... fiabilidad o comportamiento"; Arts. 15–16 require a specific aviso de privacidad at the point of sensor capture; Arts. 18–20 impose security and confidentiality; fines run to 320,000 UMA, doubled for sensitive data (Art. 59), with criminal doubling under Art. 64. Enforcement authority is the SABG; a Reglamento for the new law was still unpublished as of July 2026.
2. LFT — dignity, health-based non-discrimination and "entorno libre de violencias" (Arts. 2, 3, 16, 56, as reformed 15-01-2026 to implement C190); medical examinations only as provided in the reglamento interior (Art. 134 X); technological supervision must be proportionate and privacy-preserving (Art. 330-I, telework, applied by analogy); algorithmic management must be transparent, reasonable, non-discriminatory and not endanger health (Art. 291-J, platforms, as template); safety obligations and NOM compliance (Arts. 132 XVI–XVII, 475 Bis) with 250–5000 UMA fines (Art. 994 V).
3. NOM-035-STPS-2018 — psychosocial risk must be identified by validated questionnaires at least every two years, results confidential and used "exclusivamente para fines de mejora del ambiente de trabajo", individual psychological evaluation only when triggered; a continuous individual state monitor neither satisfies nor is authorised by the NOM.
4. Constitution Art. 16 (privacy, data protection) with horizontal effect.
5. Sector: in CENACE/CFE/PEMEX control rooms the *public-sector* data-protection law applies, the site is strategic infrastructure, and SASISOPA/Código de Red management-of-change and cybersecurity rules govern any new system.

**Compared with the EU.** EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) makes workplace emotion inference a prohibited practice (safety/medical exception aside); GDPR Art. 9 bans health/biometric processing absent an exception; Dutch WOR Art. 27 gives the works council a consent right over monitoring systems. Mexico has none of these three; its regime is consent-and-proportionality based rather than prohibition based. The practical gap is therefore: in Mexico the system is *lawful if consented, purpose-limited, proportionate, confidential and objectable*; in the EU it is *unlawful regardless of consent* unless it qualifies as a safety or medical use — and even then GDPR Art. 9 and works-council consent stand. The Dutch supplier remains exposed to GDPR (Art. 3(1)) for any processing it does as controller/processor, wherever the operators sit.

## What an engineering treatise can legitimately say

- Mexico permits the deployment; the treatise should state that the legal basis is **LFPDPPP Art. 8 express written consent plus a documented legitimate purpose** (Art. 8 para. 2), not "no restrictions". Consent must be individually signed (autograph or e-signature), revocable, and separate from the employment contract.
- **Purpose must be fixed and safety-oriented** (Arts. 11–12): e.g. "real-time workload/vigilance support of the on-shift operator to prevent operating errors", never performance appraisal, discipline or scheduling; any new purpose requires fresh consent. The purpose framing also aligns with LFT Art. 3's "calificaciones particulares que exija una labor determinada" defence against discrimination claims.
- **Design for data minimisation and ephemerality**: compute state on-line, keep no per-operator time series beyond what the safety purpose needs (Art. 12 "mínimo indispensable"), pseudonymise any retained data, and separate the research dataset (which needs its own consent and, if it reaches the Netherlands, GDPR Art. 9 basis and a DPIA).
- **Display design**: sonify to the operator (self-regulation) or to the team as an aggregate, not as a supervisor-facing individual score; this keeps the system outside Art. 26 fr. II's "evaluar ... sin intervención humana ... su rendimiento profesional ... fiabilidad o comportamiento", and outside NOM-035 5.6 (individual psychological evaluation is a triggered, professional act). Provide a human-in-the-loop path and an opt-out that does not affect employment (right of oposición).
- **Aviso de privacidad at the sensor** (Arts. 15–16): simplified notice in the control room naming the sensitive data captured, the purposes needing consent, retention, ARCO channel, and the SABG as authority; the full notice in the reglamento interior de trabajo.
- **Worker participation**: adopt the system through the reglamento interior / collective agreement and the LFT Art. 132 XXXI-style protocol "en acuerdo con los trabajadores"; document a NOM-035 Ch. 8 control-measure rationale (workload distribution) rather than presenting the system as NOM-035 compliance.
- **Security posture**: Art. 18–20 measures proportional to sensitivity; ICS-zone segregation; no remote telemetry to the supplier by default; incident notification plan (Art. 19).
- **Sector gate**: if the site is CENACE, CFE or PEMEX, switch to the LGPDPPSO public-sector regime (evaluación de impacto, avisos, encargado contract), add SASISOPA/Código de Red management-of-change and cyber approvals; if private industry, LFPDPPP applies.
- **Freedoms Mexico has that the EU lacks**: no per-se prohibition on affective inference at work; no works-council veto; no AI-Act conformity assessment, CE-marking or fundamental-rights impact assessment; no EU-style Art. 22 default ban on automated decisions (only an objection right); no mandatory DPIA under the federal private-sector law (recommended by INAI guidance, required under the public-sector law); no data-localisation rule; consent is a fully valid basis for sensitive data.
- **What the treatise must not say**: that inferred emotional state is "not personal data" or "not sensitive" in Mexico; that operators' consent is unnecessary because of the employment relationship; that NOM-035 authorises or is satisfied by sensor monitoring; that Mexican law has adopted ISA-18.2/IEC 62682 or ISO 11064 as binding; that GDPR/AI Act are irrelevant to the Dutch supplier.
