### 🧠 Music Therapy and Clinical Work

MPN Conductor takes a nine-part description of a character and produces a score, MIDI, audio and parts. It was built for drama, and the parts of it that work are aimed at a composer scoring a scene. It is not validated for clinical use of any kind. Rather than ask you to take that on trust, here is what is wrong with it, in terms you can check.

Three of the checks take about a minute. Render the same input twice and you get two different scores, because the generator is not seeded and nothing fixes it. Import a script through the product and four of the nine state components are not read from the text at all but assigned by calls to the random number generator, and on one path the two called trauma and entropy are assigned that way too. Set the three register values in the interface and follow them: they are discarded before they reach a frame. The system therefore cannot track anything over time, because it does not hold still, and it cannot be a discussion tool about a particular person, because part of what you would be discussing was not about them.

The fourth check is slower and matters more. No quantity in this theory has ever been measured by anything except the system itself. The large library of worked examples shipped with the project is this system's reading of the author's own written commentary on some plays, so the numbers are the instrument's account of its author's prose rather than an independent assignment by anyone. Two listener studies exist. Neither can be cited, because the stimuli came from the unseeded generator and cannot be reproduced, and one of the two is a null result. That is the entire evidential position.

It is worth being equally plain about the surrounding literature, which the project does not get to inherit. The evidence in music therapy is for live, relational work delivered by a credentialed professional, and no review or trial isolates machine-generated music. Using this software would not be music therapy; in the United Kingdom the title is protected in law, and the professional scope documents treat the therapeutic relationship rather than the audio as the thing that does the work.

Before a clinical use could even be proposed, several things would have to be true and none of them is yet. The generator would have to be seeded and the random assignments removed, so that the state a person set is the state that is heard. State values would have to be elicited from raters other than this system, under a published protocol with a published reliability figure. A listening study would have to exist that the mapping could fail. And there would have to be a separate account, which has not been written, of population, supervision, consent, and what a generated cue can do wrong in a session. Claims of that kind also engage medical device regulation in both major jurisdictions, which is one more reason to make none here.

What we would welcome is the opposite of a claim. If you are a music therapist, the questions we have are whether this should exist, which direction it ought to run in, and where the idea first goes wrong. The listening pack has a section written for therapists, and an answer saying the premise is mistaken is worth as much to us as any other. Nothing is promised in return except that what you tell us is published with everything else, including the parts that do not suit the project.
