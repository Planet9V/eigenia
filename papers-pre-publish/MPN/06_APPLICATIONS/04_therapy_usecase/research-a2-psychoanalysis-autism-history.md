## Findings

### 1. The refrigerator-mother theory: origin, claim, refutation, harm

**Origin — Kanner 1943.** Leo Kanner's "Autistic disturbances of affective contact" (*Nervous Child*, 1943) first described the syndrome and included descriptive remarks about the parents of his eleven cases as intellectual and emotionally cool. Kanner's own position was unstable: the 1943 paper also asserts the children's aloneness is present "from the start of life," implying innate origin, while his later public remarks (commonly cited to a 1949 paper and to a 1960 *Time* magazine interview in which parents were described as having been kept "in a refrigerator which did not defrost") supplied the phrase and the blame. **[K43 — UNVERIFIED; I did not fetch Kanner 1943 or the 1949/1960 remarks. Dates and the exact "refrigerator" wording should be re-verified against the primary texts before any citation.]**

**Bettelheim 1967.** Bruno Bettelheim's *The Empty Fortress: Infantile Autism and the Birth of the Self* (Free Press, 1967) is the canonical full statement of the parental-causation thesis: autism as a defensive withdrawal by an infant who has concluded, from a mother's wish (conscious or not) that the child not exist, that the world is annihilating — Bettelheim's explicit analogy being the concentration camp inmate. The prescribed remedy was "parentectomy": removal of the child from the family into residential milieu therapy (Bettelheim's Orthogenic School). **[BET67 — UNVERIFIED as to exact wording; I did not fetch the book or a scholarly edition of it.]**

**Refutation.** Two landmarks are conventionally cited:
- Bernard Rimland, *Infantile Autism: The Syndrome and Its Implications for a Neural Theory of Behavior* (1964) — the first systematic argument, by a psychologist who was himself an autistic child's father, that autism is a neurobiological condition, not a product of parenting. **[RIM64 — UNVERIFIED.]**
- Susan Folstein and Michael Rutter, "Infantile autism: a genetic study of 21 twin pairs" (*Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry*, 1977) — the first twin study, showing markedly higher concordance in monozygotic than dizygotic pairs and establishing a substantial genetic contribution. **[FR77 — UNVERIFIED as to exact concordance figures; do not quote numbers without re-checking.]**

Bettelheim's own record was additionally discredited posthumously (after his death in 1990) by allegations of credential falsification, fabricated outcome claims, and physical abuse of children at the Orthogenic School. **[BET-POST — UNVERIFIED in this session.]**

**Documented harm.** The harm is of four kinds and is the reason this history is not merely of academic interest: (i) mothers were told, by clinicians, that they had caused their child's disability; (ii) children were separated from families under "parentectomy"; (iii) families were diverted from education, communication support and practical services into open-ended psychotherapy; (iv) a generation of mothers carried lasting guilt and social stigma. Bishop & Swendsen (2021) record the French inflection of this specifically: French psychoanalytic accounts held mothers culpable and did so in a double bind — "accusing mothers of being simultaneously too distant and overly possessive." **[BS21 — VERIFIED, fetched.]**

### 2. France: the critical case

**Why France.** Bishop & Swendsen (2021), *BJPsych Bulletin* 45(2):89–93, doi:10.1192/bjb.2020.138, is a peer-reviewed treatment of exactly this question. Their findings as fetched: psychoanalysis remained entrenched in French child psychiatry long after other Western countries abandoned it for autism; Lacan's influence created conditions of unusual institutional protection; and the entrenchment is measurable in training. Their quoted figures: "of the 26 universities charged with training clinical psychologists, half still provide substantial psychoanalytic training," and "in nine of these universities, the training provided in clinical psychology is *exclusively* psychoanalytic in orientation." `[quoted via fetch]` They also record that French psychoanalysts "waged a campaign against those who challenge them, including an attempt to sue director Sophie Robert." Their evidential conclusion is Popperian: psychoanalysis is "able to explain all phenomena but with no possibility of being disproven." **[BS21 — VERIFIED.]**

**"Le Mur" (2011) and the litigation.** Sophie Robert's documentary *Le Mur : la psychanalyse à l'épreuve de l'autisme* (2011) intercut interviews with French psychoanalysts. Three of them — **Esthela Solano-Suarez, Éric Laurent and Alexandre Stevens**, of the École de la Cause freudienne — sued.
- **First instance, Tribunal de grande instance de Lille, 26 January 2012:** the film was banned pending removal of the three plaintiffs' interviews; Robert and her production company were ordered to pay €25,000 damages plus €9,000 toward publication of the judgment.
- **Cour d'appel de Douai, 16 January 2014:** the ban was **overturned**. The court found no deliberate distortion (*dénaturation*) or ridiculing of the psychoanalysts' statements, and emphasised filmmakers' freedom of expression where interviewees freely consent to being recorded without a right of final cut. The psychoanalysts were ordered to pay Robert €5,000 provisional damages plus €5,000 each in procedural costs, and €5,000 in costs to Autistes Sans Frontières.
- **Cassation:** no Cour de cassation step is recorded in the source fetched. **[AFIS — VERIFIED, fetched. Note: this is the Association française pour l'information scientifique, a party-adjacent outlet in the underlying controversy; the dates and dispositif should ideally be confirmed against the judgment itself, which I did not obtain.]**

Note the conflict of interest directly relevant to section 3: **Éric Laurent, author of *La bataille de l'autisme*, was one of the plaintiffs who successfully obtained a first-instance ban on a documentary critical of Lacanian autism theory, later reversed on appeal.** **[VERIFIED that Laurent was a plaintiff; the book authorship link is UNVERIFIED but is not in serious dispute.]**

**HAS/ANESM, March 2012.** The joint recommandation de bonne pratique *"Autisme et autres troubles envahissants du développement : interventions éducatives et thérapeutiques coordonnées chez l'enfant et l'adolescent"* was published **8 March 2012** by the Haute Autorité de Santé with ANESM (Agence nationale de l'évaluation et de la qualité des établissements et services sociaux et médico-sociaux). As reported by Psychomédia (fetched): the bodies found it impossible to conclude as to *"la pertinence"* of interventions founded on psychoanalytic approaches and institutional psychotherapy, which were classified **"non consensuelles."** On packing, HAS and ANESM declared themselves **"formellement opposées à l'utilisation de cette pratique"** outside authorised clinical research meeting specified conditions, on grounds of absent efficacy and safety data. `[quoted via fetch]` **[PSYMED — VERIFIED as a fetched secondary report; the HAS primary PDF is NOT verified, see Gaps.]**

The tripartite structure — *interventions recommandées* / *non consensuelles* / *non recommandées* — and the placement of psychoanalytic approaches and psychothérapie institutionnelle in the middle ("non consensuelles": insufficient data plus divergence of expert opinion, therefore neither endorsed nor formally prohibited) is the crucial 2012 fact. It was a **withholding of endorsement, not a prohibition**, and this is why the 2012 text did not end the practice. **[Structure UNVERIFIED against the primary text; the "non consensuelles" label itself is VERIFIED via Psychomédia.]**

**The 2012 grading system.** HAS recommendations of this period used grade A / B / C plus *accord d'experts* (AE). I could **not** verify which grade, if any, attached to the psychoanalytic or packing statements. **Do not assert a grade letter.** **[UNVERIFIED — see Gaps.]**

**Assemblée nationale, 2012.** Deputy Daniel Fasquelle tabled both a bill to make autism the *grande cause nationale* 2012 and a *proposition de résolution* (n° 4134) inviting the Government to promote autism care based on HAS recommendations — in effect, to defund psychoanalytic approaches. Search-result metadata (Le Quotidien du Médecin, republished) indicates the Fasquelle resolution was **"quasi unanimement rejetée à l'Assemblée."** Autism *was* designated Grande cause nationale 2012. **[AN2012 — UNVERIFIED; the rejection is from a search-result title only and must be confirmed before use.]**

**Packing (*le packing*).** Wrapping a child in cold wet sheets, practised in some French institutions and theorised in psychodynamic terms as restoring body-image boundaries. HAS/ANESM 2012 position as above: formally opposed outside authorised research protocols. **[VERIFIED via Psychomédia.]**

**Current HAS position — this is the decisive, current fact.** HAS has now **superseded** the 2012 text. The new recommandation de bonne pratique, *"Trouble du spectre de l'autisme : interventions et parcours de vie du nourrisson, de l'enfant et de l'adolescent,"* was **adopted by the HAS Collège on 8 January 2026** and publicised in February 2026. It is explicitly an *actualisation* of the 2012 HAS/ANESM RBP. Its verbatim classification is no longer "non consensuelles" but a flat non-recommendation:

> **Interventions recommandées** — "Interventions précoces, développementales, comportementales dans plusieurs domaines fonctionnels comme la communication et le langage, les habiletés sociales, l'autonomie, la sensorialité et la motricité"
>
> **Interventions non recommandées** — "Méthodes sans preuves, preuves insuffisantes ou méthodes inefficaces dans le cadre du TSA : Doman­Delacato, Padovan, SonRise, 3i, **psychanalyse**, Snoezelen, neurofeedback, Tomatis, **Packing**, etc."

`[quoted via fetch]` **[HAS2026 — VERIFIED via santementale.fr, which reproduces the HAS *messages clés*; the HAS originals (Messages clés / Recommandations / Argumentaire) were NOT fetchable, see Gaps.]**

The trajectory therefore runs: 2012 "non consensuelle" (no endorsement, no prohibition) → 2026 **"non recommandée," in the category "méthodes sans preuves, preuves insuffisantes ou méthodes inefficaces."** Psychanalyse is now listed by the French national health authority alongside Doman-Delacato and Tomatis. Any proposal in a French or Francophone context that carries psychoanalytic framing near autism is proposing something the national authority named, by name, as not recommended, eight months before the date of this memo.

### 3. The Lacanian position on autism, as its proponents state it

**The claim.** Lacanian clinicians — principally **Jean-Claude Maleval** (*L'autiste et sa voix*, Seuil, 2009; later *La différence autistique*; *De la structure autistique et des faux autistes*) — hold that autism is a **distinct clinical structure**, not a subtype of psychosis and not a deficit. This is presented as a break from earlier psychoanalytic practice, which assimilated autism to childhood psychosis.

From Maleval's own conference text *"De la structure autistique"* (fetched): the structure is characterised by (i) initial **retention of the pulsional objects** — above all the voice — such that the child "refuses initially to let the pulsional objects enter into exchange" (`"l'enfant autiste refuse initialement de faire entrer les objets pulsionnels dans l'échange"` `[quoted via fetch]`), mutism being read as protective rather than as incomprehension; (ii) alienation operating **without** the function of the master-signifier; (iii) a treatment of jouissance via the **bord** (rim/edge) — `"Le bord opère une coupure dans la jouissance, il met celle-ci à distance"` `[quoted via fetch]`. The *bord* is built from three interlocking elements: the **objet autistique** (an inseparable attachment object), the **double** (identification with a non-living or partial other), and the **îlot de compétence** / specific interest. The clinical aim is explicitly **not cure** but helping the subject build and extend workable *bords* that make jouissance liveable and permit social participation and autonomy — Maleval citing Donald Triplett's independent adult life as the model outcome. **[MAL — VERIFIED, fetched.]**

**On parental blame.** This is the point on which the Lacanians most insist they differ from Bettelheim, and it must be stated accurately. In the text I fetched, Maleval's etiology is located in the *subject's own* defensive organisation — retention of the pulsional objects — and **parents are not assigned causal responsibility**. The fetch reported that the text "makes no mention of parents causing autism" and "entirely bypasses the refrigerator mother thesis." That is an **absence of blame in this text**, which is not identical to an **explicit repudiation** of the thesis, and I did not verify that Maleval anywhere repudiates it in terms. Treat as: *the Lacanian structural account as stated does not require or contain maternal causation, and its proponents present it as having broken with Bettelheim.* **[MAL — VERIFIED as to absence in the fetched text; explicit repudiation UNVERIFIED.]**

**Contemporary Lacanian output.** Taels & Vanheule, "Psychoanalytic work with autistic objects and affinities: A Lacanian approach," *Theory & Psychology* (2025), doi:10.1177/09593543251386369, indicates the framework is still being published in peer-reviewed psychology venues. Éric Laurent, *La bataille de l'autisme : de la clinique à la politique* (Navarin, 2012), is the movement's explicitly political statement, written during the HAS controversy. **[TV25, LAU12 — UNVERIFIED; not fetched.]**

**Current standing.** Three things are true simultaneously and should not be collapsed:
1. The framework continues to be published, taught (see Bishop & Swendsen's training figures) and practised, chiefly in France, Belgium and parts of Latin America. **[VERIFIED in part.]**
2. It has **no accepted efficacy evidence base**. I found no controlled outcome evidence for any psychoanalytic intervention in autism, Lacanian or otherwise, and Bishop & Swendsen's argument is that the framework is constructed so as to be unfalsifiable. **[BS21 — VERIFIED.]**
3. The French national health authority, having previously declined to endorse it, now lists *psychanalyse* under "interventions non recommandées / méthodes sans preuves." The Lacanian claim of innocence on parental blame, even if granted in full, does **not** affect (2) or (3). Being unlike Bettelheim on causation is not evidence of efficacy. **[HAS2026 — VERIFIED.]**

**Autistic self-advocacy view.** Autistic self-advocates in France (and the associations that intervened in the *Le Mur* litigation, e.g. Autistes Sans Frontières) treat the persistence of psychoanalytic framing as an ongoing harm, not a historical one. The specific objections are: that the theory reads autistic communication, special interests and attachment objects as defensive formations against a psychic threat rather than as valid ways of being and knowing; that it interposes an interpreter between the autistic person and their own account of themselves; and that its non-falsifiability makes it unaccountable to the people it describes. **[Characterisation — UNVERIFIED as to any single cited source; I did not fetch a self-advocacy position statement on Lacanian theory specifically. See Gaps.]**

### 4. The neurodiversity critique

**Double empathy.** Damian Milton, "On the ontological status of autism: the 'double empathy problem'," *Disability & Society* 27(6) (2012): 883–887, doi:10.1080/09687599.2012.710008. The claim: breakdowns in understanding between autistic and non-autistic people are **mutual and bidirectional**, a mismatch of salience and experience between two parties, not a one-sided deficit of "theory of mind" located in the autistic person. The implication for any interpretive or theory-laden clinical framework is direct: a clinician's failure to understand an autistic person is evidence of a mismatch, not evidence of the autistic person's impairment — and certainly not licence to substitute the clinician's interpretation for the person's own report. **[MIL12 — UNVERIFIED; not fetched. Volume/page/DOI should be re-checked.]**

**Language.** Kenny, L., Hattersley, C., Molins, B., Buckley, C., Povey, C., & Pellicano, E. (2016), "Which terms should be used to describe autism? Perspectives from the UK autism community," *Autism* 20(4), doi:10.1177/1362361315588200 (first published online 2015). **n = 3,470** UK respondents after exclusions (from 4,622). Endorsement of "autistic": **autistic adults 61%** vs "person with autism" 28%; **parents 51%** vs 22%; **professionals 38%** vs **49%** for "person with autism"; family/friends 52%. The authors' own conclusion is *not* a mandate for identity-first language: "there is no universally accepted way to describe autism," preferences varied with beliefs and context, and they recommend a **person-centred approach** — asking the people you work with. **[KEN16 — VERIFIED, fetched.]** The honest reading: autistic adults themselves clearly prefer identity-first; professionals are the group out of step; and the paper's own recommendation is to ask rather than to impose.

**ABA and normalisation goals.** The self-advocacy critique of ABA and of intervention research generally is that trials measure **reduction of autistic behaviour** — eye contact, stimming, "compliance," scores on clinician-rated symptom scales — and treat that reduction as benefit. Autistic people report wanting different outcomes measured: **wellbeing, anxiety and mental health, sensory comfort, autonomy and self-determination, communication that works (including AAC, not only speech), quality of relationships, participation and access, and the absence of burnout and masking-related harm.** The objection to normalisation goals is that suppressing autistic behaviour can raise distress while improving the measured score. **[Characterisation — UNVERIFIED as to a specific cited source; I did not fetch an outcome-priorities study. This is a well-documented position but the citation needs supplying, see Gaps.]** Note that the current HAS text does recommend developmental and behavioural interventions, so the French guideline position and the neurodiversity critique of behavioural intervention are **not** aligned; they are two separate critiques of psychoanalytic practice arriving from different directions. Do not conflate them.

### 5. Participatory norms

**AASPIRE.** Nicolaidis, C., Raymaker, D. M., Kapp, S. K., Baggs, A. E., Ashkenazy, E., McDonald, K. E., Weiner, M., Maslak, J., Hunter, M., & Joyce, A. (2019), "The AASPIRE practice-based guidelines for the inclusion of autistic adults in research as co-researchers and study participants," *Autism* 23(8): 2007–2019, doi:10.1177/1362361319830523. **[VERIFIED, fetched.]** Operational content:

*As co-researchers:* state explicitly which engagement level is on offer (equal partnership / authentic collaboration / consultation) and "explicitly communicate what type of engagement community partners can expect"; written role descriptions; recruit for diverse lived experience, not only community leaders; **jointly create the collaboration processes and decision rules**; individualised accommodations (text chat, multiple meeting formats, materials circulated in advance, strong facilitation, plain language); **name and address power imbalances rather than pathologising disagreement**; build trust through visioning and demonstrable follow-through on feedback; "keep/change" evaluation exercises; **autistic partners as co-authors**; lay-language summaries; community capacitation; and **fair compensation** — "be transparent about project funding" and pay community partners whenever academic staff or participants are paid. `[quoted via fetch]`

*As participants:* do not impose decisional-capacity assessment by reason of diagnosis alone; co-produce accessible consent materials (simplified language, images, text-to-speech); offer multiple participation modes, synchronous and asynchronous; **do not assume instruments validated in general populations are valid for autistic adults** — adapt through a participatory process and **re-test psychometric properties**; accessible interview guides with concrete rather than abstract questions and advance sight of materials; and use proxies only where direct participation is impossible despite accommodations, distinguishing "supported participation" from proxy report.

The instrument clause is the one that bites hardest on a new tool: **an outcome measure not validated with autistic people is not evidence about autistic people.**

**Autistica** (UK) operates research priority-setting and involvement frameworks derived from a James Lind Alliance-style priority-setting partnership; **AIMS-2-TRIALS** (EU IMI2 autism research programme) operates an **Autism Representatives (A-Reps)** structure embedding autistic people and family members in governance and study design. **[AUTISTICA, A2T — UNVERIFIED; not fetched. Confirm structures and cite properly before relying on them.]**

**"Nothing about us without us," operationally,** for a tool-development programme means, minimally: autistic people on the team with decision rights and pay from before the research question is fixed, not consulted on a finished design; power to veto or redirect; co-authorship; the outcome measures chosen by the affected community rather than by the developers; accessible materials as standard; and published reporting of what the partners changed. Consultation after the tool exists is not participation.

### 6. Does any current reputable source support a psychoanalytically framed intervention for autistic people?

**No — not on my searching, and the trend is against it.** Stated plainly:

- I found **no** systematic review, controlled trial, national guideline or health-technology assessment recommending a psychoanalytic (Freudian, Kleinian, Lacanian, or institutional-psychotherapy) intervention for autistic people.
- The **Haute Autorité de Santé**, the authority in the country where the approach is most entrenched, moved from withholding endorsement in 2012 ("non consensuelles") to listing **"psychanalyse"** by name among **"Interventions non recommandées — méthodes sans preuves, preuves insuffisantes ou méthodes inefficaces dans le cadre du TSA"** in the recommendation adopted **8 January 2026**. **Packing** is in the same list. **[VERIFIED.]**
- What *does* exist is a continuing **theoretical and case-study literature** in psychoanalytic and theoretical-psychology venues (e.g. Taels & Vanheule 2025 in *Theory & Psychology*; Maleval's books; the Palgrave Lacan series volume *The Autistic Subject: On the Threshold of Language*). This is publication, not efficacy evidence, and none of it is an outcome trial. Peer-reviewed publication of a theory is not reputable support for an intervention.
- The strongest genuinely academic defence of the framework available to me is that it is **a hermeneutic, not a treatment claim**, and that it is not the refrigerator-mother thesis. Both may be granted without producing any support for intervening on autistic people with it.

## What any Lacanian-derived tool must say, concede or avoid if it is proposed near autism

- **Lead with the harm, not with the theory.** Any document proposing this must open by stating that psychoanalytic theorising about autism produced the refrigerator-mother thesis, parentectomy, decades of maternal blame, and *le packing* — and that this is the reason the burden of proof sits where it does. Burying the history in a limitations section will read, correctly, as concealment.
- **State the current regulatory fact verbatim and up front.** The HAS recommendation adopted 8 January 2026 lists *psychanalyse* and *Packing* under "Interventions non recommandées : méthodes sans preuves, preuves insuffisantes ou méthodes inefficaces dans le cadre du TSA." A tool whose vocabulary comes from Lacan cannot be proposed into French or Francophone music-therapy settings without confronting that sentence directly. Do not paraphrase it into something softer.
- **Concede that the tool has no efficacy evidence and do not imply otherwise by association.** Lacanian autism theory has a publication record, not an outcome record. Citing Maleval, Laurent or *Theory & Psychology* as "the literature" without saying that none of it is a trial would be a material misrepresentation.
- **Do not claim the Lacanian account is exonerated by not blaming parents.** The accurate statement is narrower: the structural account as stated locates autism in the subject's own economy and does not require maternal causation. That is a real difference from Bettelheim. It is not evidence of benefit, and it does not answer the objections about interpretive authority.
- **Disclose the *Le Mur* conflict of interest if Éric Laurent is cited.** Laurent was one of three plaintiffs who obtained a first-instance ban (Lille, 26 January 2012) on a documentary critical of this position — a ban overturned on appeal (Douai, 16 January 2014), with costs and provisional damages awarded against the plaintiffs. Citing *La bataille de l'autisme* as neutral scholarship without that context is not defensible.
- **Never let the framework override an autistic person's own account of themselves.** The specific hazard of *bord*, *objet autistique*, *double* and voice-retention is that they license reinterpreting a person's interests, attachments, stimming and silence as defences against a psychic threat. Milton's double empathy problem says the opposite: a failure of the clinician to understand is a mutual mismatch, not a datum about the autistic person's interior. Build in an explicit rule that the person's self-report outranks the interpretation.
- **Do not use the vocabulary with autistic users or their families without translation and consent.** Terms like *jouissance*, *foreclosure*, *the Other*, *retention of the object* are unfalsifiable to the person they are applied to and cannot be contested by them. If the theory is scaffolding for developers, say so and keep it out of the user-facing layer entirely.
- **Do not describe the tool as therapeutic, as treating autism, or as pursuing any normalisation outcome.** No reduction of stimming, eye-contact targets, "compliance," or clinician-rated symptom scores. If the tool measures anything, measure what autistic people say they want measured: wellbeing, anxiety, sensory comfort, autonomy, communication that works, participation — and measure them with instruments validated *in autistic populations*, per AASPIRE. An unvalidated measure yields no evidence.
- **Meet AASPIRE before building, not after.** Autistic co-researchers with decision rights and pay, named engagement level, jointly written collaboration rules, co-authorship, accessible consent and materials, and published account of what the autistic partners changed. Retrospective consultation on a finished tool is not participation and should not be described as such.
- **Distinguish three separate critiques and do not use one to deflect another.** (i) Psychoanalysis lacks evidence and is now formally not recommended in France; (ii) ABA and behavioural normalisation goals are criticised by autistic advocates; (iii) the neurodiversity critique of interpretive authority. HAS recommends behavioural/developmental interventions, so (i) and (ii) are *not* allies. "Behaviourism is also criticised" is not a defence of a psychoanalytic framing.
- **Follow Kenny et al. (2016) honestly.** 61% of autistic adults endorsed "autistic"; professionals were the outlying group, preferring "person with autism" (49% vs 38%). The paper's own recommendation is to **ask** rather than impose. Use identity-first as default and ask individuals; do not cite the paper as though it mandated either convention.
- **Have a stated position on what would falsify the tool's claims.** The central scholarly charge against this framework (Bishop & Swendsen 2021) is unfalsifiability. A tool inheriting the vocabulary inherits the charge unless it specifies, in advance, what observation would count as the tool not working.
