PART 3. Four descriptions of a state a person can be in

  A: A state in which something the person cannot put into words keeps forcing itself upon them. It fits no account they can give of their life. It returns unchanged, cannot be negotiated with, argued away or absorbed. To meet it is to meet a brute fact that does not yield.

  B: A state organised by rule, language, obligation and position. The person is placed by names, duties and law. What anything means comes from its difference from other things inside that order, and what is at stake is one's place in it and whether the order holds.

  C: A state organised by image and identification. The person sees themselves through a picture of themselves, measures themselves against others, and is invested in how they appear. It runs on rivalry, admiration, and an ideal self that is never quite reached.

  D: A state organised by the periodicity of attention. The person's engagement rises and falls on a fixed cycle independent of circumstance. What matters is where in the cycle they currently stand, and events are experienced as arriving early or late with respect to it.

**3a.** For each of A, B, C and D, choose the ONE scale from Part 1 you would use to score a character in that state, and give a confidence from 0 to 100 and a one-sentence reason grounded in the interval content rather than in any name.

**3b.** For each of A, B, C and D, if the state is at its most extreme and most painful, does your choice change? If so, to which scale, and why.

**3c.** Is any of the four descriptions one you cannot match to a scale in a principled way? Say which and why, or say none.
