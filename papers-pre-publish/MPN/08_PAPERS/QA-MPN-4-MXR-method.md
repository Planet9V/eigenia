## 1. Study-by-study critique

A general finding governs every subsection and is stated once here rather than six times. **DM-14 (152) requires every experiment to register its "sample size and stopping rule", and DM-15 (153) requires a "pre-specified analysis plan naming the statistical model, the primary outcome and the handling of exclusions and missing data". Not one of the six studies in section 5 supplies any of those things.** Five of the six explicitly defer the sample to the registration (240, 248, 295, and by silence 256 and 303); none states an effect size, a power target, an alpha, a stopping rule, an exclusion rule, a missing-data rule, or a multiple-comparison policy; and only 5.2 names a primary outcome, and then only by saying that the plan "says which of the three is primary" (248) without saying which. Section 5 is therefore not a set of designs but a set of design *sketches* that defer the load-bearing content to documents that do not exist. A paper whose own requirement DM-14 demands pre-registration cannot exhibit six studies that would each fail DM-14 as written.

The second general finding: **section 5 lists six studies (234) and section 9 lists six milestones (349–370), but they are not the same six.** M2↔5.1, M3↔5.2, M4↔5.5, M5↔5.4, M6↔5.3. Study 5.6, the adversary-discourse study, appears in no milestone, has no gate, no predecessor and no successor in the roadmap, and is therefore unscheduled and ungated. Either it is not part of the programme or the roadmap is incomplete; the paper does not say which.

### 1.1 Study 5.1 — Listener studies of the mappings (238–242)

**Question posed.** For each of tempo, dynamics, roughness and harmonic distance, does the target population read the polarity the notation assumes?

**Capability.** Partially. This is the strongest study in the paper, because the participants are real, the stimuli are real audio, and the dependent variable (a human judgement) is not generated by the environment. But as specified it cannot answer the question it poses, for four reasons.

**Independent variable not operationalised (238–240).** The paper never says whether the magnitude-estimation stimuli are *single-parameter sweeps* (tempo varied with mode, roughness, harmony and dynamics held fixed) or *full renderings* of frame tables. This is not a detail; it is the whole design. Single-parameter sweeps yield clean polarity estimates that do not transfer to the operational case, because in any realistic scenario the four indices covary — an upset raises activity, deviation and load together (MPN-2 §3.2 lines 113–129), so tempo, harmonic distance and velocity all move in the same direction at once and the listener has no way to attribute a percept to a parameter. Full renderings yield a result about the ensemble and cannot license a per-mapping keep/reverse/drop decision, which is exactly what 242 claims the study licenses. The paper needs a factorial or orthogonal-array stimulus design and does not have one.

**Dependent variable not operationalised.** "Which direction of change means *more* of the underlying quantity, activity, load, instability or deviation" (238). The underlying quantities are the working group's own composite constructions (MPN-2 §3.2 line 105: "the working group's own constructions"), with no external referent and no everyday meaning. The listener's answer will therefore be determined by the verbal gloss the experimenter supplies for "activity" or "instability" in the instructions — the instruction text becomes an uncontrolled experimental factor that the paper never mentions. Two labs glossing "instability" differently would get different polarities from the same audio.

**Scaling collected and never analysed.** MPN-2 §9 (line 413) specifies "magnitude estimation for polarity **and scaling**… so that the defaults of section 3 can be kept or reversed". Line 238 preserves "and by how much", but the measures (240) are "the magnitude-estimation slopes and their polarity agreement", and the analysis is "a mixed model with participant as a random effect and **mapping direction** as the fixed effect of interest, with the polarity criterion… stated in advance". Scaling has a measure and no analysis, no criterion and no consequence. This matters because every function in MPN-2's mapping table is *linear* in its index (`tempo = t_min + u(t_max − t_min)`, `v = round(30 + 90l)`, `k = round(12 x_p)`), and Walker's paradigm characteristically returns power-law exponents that are not 1. A confirmed polarity plus an exponent of 0.4 would falsify the declared function while passing the declared criterion.

**No baseline and no control condition.** There is no visual-only comparison, no scrambled-audio control, no catch condition on a mapping the working group believes is *not* readable. The "false-alarm rate on catch trials" (240) is a within-task attention check, not a control condition. Crucially, **MPN-2 §9's own retention criterion for the two-stream task — "the notation is kept only if detection latency in the two-stream task does not exceed the visual baseline" (MPN-2 line 413) — is absent from 238–242**, and with it the visual baseline condition that criterion requires. The paper has dropped a pre-existing acceptance criterion from a sibling paper without saying it did.

**Ceiling and floor.** Tempo carries the STRONG tier (MPN-2 line 137; restated at 202) and will almost certainly ceiling: essentially every listener will read faster as more. Harmonic distance is LR-chain position over twelve steps (MPN-2 line 139), a quantity whose perceptual availability to untrained listeners is unestablished, and will probably floor. Roughness (COMPONENT tier, "one fitted predictor among several") sits between. A single "declared majority" criterion (240) applied across four mappings of radically different prior strength is miscalibrated by construction: it will be overpowered for tempo and underpowered for harmonic distance, and the study will "confirm" the mapping the programme least needed confirming.

**Order and learning effects.** Not mentioned anywhere. Magnitude estimation is notoriously anchor-dependent and subject to regression effects; the order of the four mappings, the order of directions within a mapping, and the first-presented anchor are all uncontrolled in the text. The two-stream task has obvious practice effects across trials and event types.

**Multiple comparisons.** Four mappings × two directions, plus detection latency and identification accuracy per scripted event type, plus a false-alarm rate. No correction, no family definition, no primary outcome. DM-15 is not met.

**Sample.** "Drawn from operators of the target population or people of comparable training" (240). MPN-2 §9 says "operators of the **target site**". There is no site: the environment is closed and the plant is synthetic (19, 54). "Target population" is therefore undefined, and the justification offered — "the environment's cost structure favours more participants over more conditions" (240) — is a budgeting heuristic, not a sample-size justification. No N, no power, no stopping rule.

**What a positive result licenses.** The paper claims (242) it would license "for each mapping in Paper 2's table, that the intended polarity is or is not the one the target population reads". Under the single-parameter reading that claim is over-broad (it holds only for isolated parameters, not for the rendering the system actually produces); under the full-rendering reading it is unsupportable per mapping. And in neither case does it license anything about scaling, which the analysis does not test but which the mapping table asserts.

**VERDICT: capable in principle, under-specified in practice. The strongest study in the paper and still not registrable as written: stimulus construction undefined, scaling criterion dropped, MPN-2's own two-stream retention criterion and its visual baseline silently removed, no sample-size justification, no multiplicity control.**

### 1.2 Study 5.2 — Situation awareness and workload in the simulator (246–252)

**Question posed.** Does the display raise, lower or leave unchanged situation awareness at each of Endsley's three levels, and subjective workload, in a simulated control room?

**Capability.** Yes in form — this is a standard, defensible human-factors design (within-participant, counterbalanced, SAGAT freeze-and-probe, NASA-TLX, multiple-resource theory as the prediction under test). It is the second-strongest study. Its defects are of specification and of control conditions.

**Control condition is the wrong one.** The comparison is "scenario blocks with and without the display" (246). With/without confounds *information* with *sound*. Any continuous auditory stimulus changes arousal, alerting and time-sharing behaviour whether or not it carries information. The design needs a third arm — a rendering of a shuffled, time-reversed or decorrelated frame table, loudness- and spectrum-matched — so that the informational contribution is separable from the acoustic one. Without it, a positive SA result is attributable to "there was sound" and the paper will not be able to tell the difference. This is the single most consequential missing control in section 5.

**The SAGAT/sonification interaction is unaddressed.** "The displays blank and the participants answer situation-awareness probes" (98). What happens to the *audio* at a freeze? If it continues, participants receive live state information while answering the probe, and the probe measures the display rather than the participant's awareness. If it stops, the cessation is itself a salient event (the notation makes cessation meaningful — the liveness figure, 204), and the freeze becomes a cue. Freeze-probe was designed for visual displays; applying it to a continuous auditory display requires a decision the paper never makes.

**Learning, order and scenario confound.** "Counterbalanced" (246) is asserted without saying what is counterbalanced against what. With/without display must be crossed with scenario and both counterbalanced (a Latin square at minimum), because scenarios are not interchangeable: the plant twin's scripted disturbances differ in difficulty and in how much they reward an overview. Without that, condition and scenario are confounded and the analysis cannot separate them. Repeated exposure to the same plant twin also produces strong practice effects on SA — participants learn the generator, not the display.

**Missing dependent variable.** The measures are probe-level SA, six TLX subscales and total, and "acknowledge latency and command rate as behavioural covariates" (248). There is no *performance* outcome: time to detect a process excursion, time to correct intervention, number of missed excursions. Acknowledge latency and command rate are demoted to covariates. The decision-relevant question — does the crew catch the upset sooner — is not measured. This is a strange omission given that the programme's stated benefit hypothesis elsewhere is "detection and orientation" (MPN-4 field edition line 15) and that its transferred precedent (Loeb and Fitch) is a *detection-latency* study.

**Multiplicity.** Three SA levels × (with/without) × (with/without personal stream, 250), plus six TLX subscales plus a weighted total, plus two covariates. That is upwards of twenty tests with no correction and no named primary outcome. The paper says the plan "says which of the three is primary" (248) — it does not say which, so the paper as filed does not satisfy its own DM-15.

**The operator-state factor is confounded (250).** "The comparison runs with and without the personal stream as a separate factor." A participant hearing a rendering of their own physiology is in a biofeedback loop: the rendered arousal index changes the arousal it renders. The measured quantity is then a closed-loop equilibrium, not the participant's state under the display, and SA/TLX differences between the arms confound the cost of an extra stream with the effect of the feedback loop. The paper identifies the attention-cost hypothesis (250) and misses the feedback-loop confound entirely. This is also the one place where a continuous physiological rendering could plausibly produce a self-amplifying arousal spiral in a participant, which is a *measurement* problem (the DV becomes unstable) quite apart from anything else.

**Sample.** "Again fixed in the registration; the design's within-participant structure is what makes a modest sample informative" (248). That is an assertion, not a justification; and for probe-level SA the binding quantity is the number of *probes* per participant per level, not the number of participants, which the paper never mentions at all.

**What a positive result licenses.** The claim at 252 is appropriately hedged ("in a simulated control room with a synthetic plant"; "would not allow a claim about a real plant"). Good. But it is still too strong in one respect: without the decorrelated-audio arm it would license "adding this sound changes SA", not "the display raises SA".

**VERDICT: the right instruments, the wrong control. Add a decorrelated-rendering arm, settle the audio-at-freeze question, cross condition with scenario, name the primary level, and add an objective detection outcome; as written it cannot distinguish information from noise.**

### 1.3 Study 5.3 — Early-warning-signal analysis (254–256)

Analysed in full at §4 below. In brief, as a *study*:

**The study is four lines long and consists almost entirely of a forward reference (256).** No independent variable, no dependent variable, no sample, no analysis plan, no criterion, no claim statement. It is the only study in section 5 with no "this study would allow the working group to claim…" sentence — a telling omission in a paper that supplies one for every other study.

**It proposes a data collection the same paper declares infeasible.** Line 256 requires "matched series of participant operator-state indices from extended simulator sessions". Line 319 states that the environment "cannot supply that from participants without sessions far longer than any run so far contemplated, which is itself a finding about feasibility." The paper therefore designs an arm and then, sixty lines later, says the arm cannot be run. The honest statement is that the participant arm of 5.3 does not exist, and the study reduces to an analysis of generator output.

**"Scripted regime changes with the transition times known to the generator" (256) is not the right stimulus.** Early-warning indicators are precursors of a *fold* — a saddle-node bifurcation approached by a slowly drifting control parameter. A scripted step change, a scheduled disturbance, or a triggered mode switch produces no critical slowing down whatever, because nothing is approaching a bifurcation; and a scripted *ramp* produces rising variance whether or not a fold lies at the end of it. The design does not distinguish the two, so a positive result is uninterpretable and a negative result is uninformative.

**VERDICT: not a study. A forward reference to an analysis whose human arm the paper itself declares infeasible and whose synthetic arm has no stated generative model to test against. Weakest-but-one in the paper; see §7.**

### 1.4 Study 5.4 — Training a state-conditioned renderer on synthetic vectors (258–289)

Analysed in full at §5 below. As a study:

**The question is well posed and the comparison structure is right.** DM-13 (151) requires the fixed renderer, a text-prompted baseline and an objective metric independent of the training signal, all on the same frame tables under one protocol. The mermaid at 266–287 draws it correctly. This is the most rigorous evaluation design in the paper.

**The training target is never stated, and every reading of it is fatal.** Line 260: "the environment's advantage is that it can generate the corpus rather than collect one: a labelled set on the order of a thousand clips and ten hours, with vector labels that are exact by construction because the vector generated the clip." What audio is in those clips? The paper never says. There are only two possibilities in a closed environment.
- If the audio is the **fixed renderer's output**, then the learned renderer is trained to imitate the fixed renderer, its performance ceiling is the fixed renderer, and DM-13's central comparison ("does the learned renderer beat the fixed renderer") is guaranteed to return "no better, probably worse". The study is then a distillation-fidelity experiment mislabelled as a renderer comparison.
- If the audio is **anything else** — real music, pretrained-model samples — then the vector labels are *not* "exact by construction", because the vector did not generate the clip; they would have to be assigned, which reinstates MPN-3's dropped requirement of "more than one annotator and a reliability figure" (MPN-3 line 257) and destroys the paper's claimed advantage.

The paper takes the first horn's benefit (free exact labels) and the second horn's claim (a renderer that could beat the fixed one) without noticing they are incompatible.

**"Held-out vectors, not merely held-out clips" is correct and near-vacuous at the stated dimensionality.** The first milestone is "a two-dimensional conditioner over the plant-health flag and the activity index" (260). The plant-health flag is *binary* (MPN-2 line 138: `x_p < 0.5 and a_hp = 0`). So the held-out vector set is a held-out set of activity-index values in [0,1] — one continuous dimension. Holding out an interior interval is trivial interpolation; holding out an end interval is extrapolation and is a different and much harder test. The paper does not say which, so the generalisation requirement it inherits from MPN-3 is not operationalised.

**The objective metric is not independent of the training signal in this environment.** "A Fréchet audio distance or a divergence against a reference set" (260). Against *which* reference set? In a closed environment the only reference audio is the fixed renderer's, which is (on horn one) the training target. FAD against the training distribution is not an independent metric; it is a training-loss proxy. The alternative offered, "a separately trained probe that recovers the vector from audio", is better but the paper does not say what the probe is trained on: a probe trained on fixed-renderer audio and applied to learned-renderer audio is a domain-shift measurement, and a probe trained on learned-renderer audio is circular. Neither variant is specified.

**The preference-alignment stage makes the listener study non-independent (262).** Pairs are constructed as "two renderings of the same frame table, one conforming to the declared mapping and one with a mapping deliberately reversed, so that the preference signal is which rendering expresses the declared state." The evaluation is then a listener study in which listeners "recover the declared state from the audio" (280). The training label and the evaluation target are the same construct. The paper anticipates the objection and answers it with "the measure of whether that worked is not the reward; it is the held-out probe and the listener study" (262), which does not meet the point: the reward and the listener task are the same function of the same variable, applied to different populations of judges. That is a weaker form of independence than DM-13 asserts.

**Missing baselines.** Three arms are specified (fixed, learned, text-prompted). Two are missing. There is no **floor**: a mismatched or randomly paired rendering, which is what establishes the listener task's actual chance level and the possibility that all three arms are at chance. And there is no **competitor**: a plain parameter-mapping auditory graph (pitch ∝ deviation, rate ∝ activity), which is the display MPN would have to beat to justify its complexity. Without the floor, a three-way tie is uninterpretable; without the competitor, the comparison is internal.

**Listener rating instrument unspecified.** "A listening study with at least twenty-five listeners and preferably at least five ratings per sample" (260) gives Ns but no instrument. The working group's own synthesis (`RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS-2026-09-11.md` line 77) notes that MUSHRA presupposes a hidden reference that vector-conditioned generation lacks, so absolute or pairwise rating is the appropriate form. Neither MPN-3 §5 nor this paper picks one.

**Sample provenance is misapplied.** The 25-listener figure derives from EMOPIA's *descriptive quality survey* (q7 line 76: "25 participants, each rating 12 random samples on humanness, richness and overall quality"). Here it is used for a three-arm comparison with confidence intervals. "What EMOPIA did" is not "what suffices to detect a difference between three renderers"; a power analysis is needed and is absent.

**What a positive result licenses.** The claim at 289 is careful and correct as far as it goes ("better, worse or no differently… under one protocol, on synthetic data"), and the closing sentence restating MPN-3's "rule-based mapping from a state vector to a text prompt" gate is good practice. The overstatement is upstream, at 333, where the *outcome* is described as "a trained renderer, or the finding that the fixed renderer is not beaten" — on horn one, "the fixed renderer is not beaten" is a foregone conclusion, not a finding.

**VERDICT: the best-specified evaluation in the paper attached to an unspecified and probably self-defeating training corpus. Fix the corpus question first; everything downstream is contingent on it.**

### 1.5 Study 5.5 — Calibrating the psychometric-to-music mapping (291–299)

This is the study the series is named after (293) and it is the weakest in the paper. Five independent defects, any one of which is disqualifying.

**(i) The independent variable is undefined, and the paper contradicts itself about what it is.** Line 293: "A profile generator samples synthetic operator vectors over **the full feature space of section 2.2**… the renderer renders each **by a candidate mapping**." Section 2.2 (64–72) enumerates roughly twenty components: D, I, S, C; O, C, E, A, N; four Dark Tetrad scores; τ; H; r, s, i; and δ. But section 4.3 (216) states the *only* declared translation from the profile space to the renderer: "plant deviation from the trauma scalar, activity from the entropy scalar, instability from the frame-to-frame change of the trauma scalar, and load from one minus the register-triple index… The four DISC values name the actor stream's instrument." That is five components reaching the audio (τ, H, Δτ, an RSI index, and DISC as an instrument label), through four scalar indices. Fourteen of the twenty components of section 2.2 have **no declared path to the rendering at all**, and E-4 (44) requires the declared translation of MPN-2 §8.2 precisely so that no undeclared path exists. The "candidate mapping" of line 293 is therefore either (a) the declared translation, in which case most components cannot be recovered because they were never rendered, and the study's headline question is answered trivially and vacuously, or (b) some new undeclared mapping, in which case the study's independent variable is unspecified and E-4 is breached. The paper never notices the contradiction between 216 and 293.

**(ii) The recovery problem is not identifiable, provably, before any data are collected.** Even on the most generous reading, the renderer exposes at most a handful of simultaneously-varying perceptual degrees of freedom — tempo, mode, harmonic-chain position, metre, roughness, dynamics, and per-stream timbre (MPN-2 §3.3) — and under §4.3's translation these are driven by exactly four scalars. Twenty components cannot be recovered from four. The information-theoretic ceiling on "per-component recovery accuracy" (295) is set by the rank of the translation, not by listeners, and it is four. No listener sample size changes this. A design whose ceiling is computable in advance from its own specification, and which does not compute it, is not a design.

**(iii) The circularity is total and the paper's disclaimer does not reach it.** The generator samples vectors from a declared covariance structure (74, 293); the renderer renders them; listeners are asked to recover them; the answer is scored against the generator's own draw. Whatever comes out is a fact about an encoding the working group designed, evaluated against a distribution the working group chose. It contains no information about human personality, about control-room operators, or about whether any psychometric construct has a musical signature. The paper's disclaimer — "what no outcome licenses is a claim that the recovered components are properties of a real person" (297) — blocks only the crudest misreading. It does not block the misreading the rest of the paper actually commits (see §2 below): that a positive result "calibrates the psychometric-to-music mapping" (299, 331) and that a negative result shows "the psychometric layer carries nothing" (381). Neither follows. A negative result shows the *encoding* is not audible; a positive result shows it is. Psychometrics is not under test in either case, because the vectors are draws from an assumption.

**(iv) The stated primary outcome is incoherent.** "The analysis is stated in advance and is deliberately unkind to the hypothesis: the primary outcome is **the recovery of the components that the psychometric literature says are redundant**, because if the rendering separates DISC dominance from Big Five Extraversion when the generator made them correlated, the separation is an artefact of the mapping" (295). As a falsification check this is clever. As a *primary outcome* it is backwards: high recovery on redundant components means artefact (bad), and low recovery on redundant components means… what? It is consistent with a good mapping and equally consistent with a mapping that recovers nothing at all. The measure has no interpretable direction without being conditioned on recovery of the *non*-redundant components, which the paper does not name as an outcome. And there is a subtler trap: if the generator imposes the correlation via a copula (74), then recovering D and recovering E are the same measurement, and the "confusion structure between components" (295) is a property of the sampler's correlation matrix, not of the rendering.

**(v) The response format collapses the study into a stereotype-agreement task.** Listeners are "given the rendering and a forced-choice or rating response on the underlying components" (293). To answer, the listener must be told what "Dominance" or "Machiavellianism" means. The judgement is then "does this music sound like the sort of person the word describes" — a measure of shared cultural stereotype about music and personality, filtered through the experimenter's gloss, not a measure of vector recovery. Above-chance performance is predictable on stereotype grounds alone (loud, fast, major → dominant; sparse, minor → introverted) and would be produced by *any* mapping that happened to align with the stereotype, including a mapping with no relation to the intended one.

**Other defects.** One-at-a-time ablation (295) cannot estimate marginal contributions of musical parameters, which interact perceptually (tempo × velocity on arousal; roughness × harmonic distance on tension, per MPN-2 line 141 and the COMPONENT tier) — a factorial or at least a fractional-factorial ablation is needed. No learning-effect control: listeners doing hundreds of trials on a fixed code will learn it, and without a train/test split the recovery estimate is contaminated by within-experiment learning. No multiplicity control across ~20 components. No sample-size figure: "the sample needs enough listeners for per-component confidence intervals rather than a single aggregate figure" (295) is a statement of desire.

**What a positive result licenses.** The paper says it would license "that a given psychometric-to-music mapping does or does not carry information about the vector that produced the rendering" (299). Strictly, with (ii) unaddressed, it licenses only: *for the four scalars that actually reach the renderer*, listeners can or cannot discriminate levels of a synthetic parameter under the experimenter's own verbal gloss. That is a sonification-discriminability result. It is not calibration of a psychometric mapping, and it is certainly not "the gate on every use of the psychometric layer elsewhere in the programme" (299).

**VERDICT: the weakest study in the paper. Its independent variable contradicts §4.3, its recovery ceiling is four degrees of freedom against twenty components, its primary outcome has no interpretable direction, and the construct it purports to calibrate is not present in the design at all. Reframe or drop.**

### 1.6 Study 5.6 — An adversary-discourse rendering study (301–307)

**Question posed.** Can listeners recover an authored discourse assignment from its rendering?

**Capability.** Yes, for a narrow and honestly-stated question — and the paper states the boundary well at 307 ("It would say nothing about whether real threat actors fall into these four positions, which is a question the environment cannot ask because it has no real threat actors in it"). That sentence is the best piece of self-limitation in section 5.

**But the training condition destroys the measurement (303).** "Listeners, trained or untrained according to the condition, classify the discourse from the audio alone." Training a listener on a codebook the working group invented (root-position parallel triads = master, stepwise sequences = university, syncopated suspensions with unresolved entries = hysteric, sparse off-beat tones = analyst; 210) and then testing whether they can apply it is a **paired-associate learning task with four items**. It will succeed. Above-chance classification in the trained condition measures whether four musical textures are discriminable and memorable, which is a fact about auditory memory and not about the notation's content. The paper does not say what the training contains, and the difference between "training on the codebook" and "training on the discourse concepts" is the difference between a trivial result and an informative one.

**The written-description control is the right control and is under-used (303).** Comparing audio classification against classification from a written case description tests whether the *taxonomy* is codable at all — exactly the standard MPN-1 F-13 imports from Gadalla and colleagues. But the paper compares "classification accuracy against chance and against the written-description control" without saying what the criterion is: must audio match the written control, exceed chance by a stated margin, or what? And the written control should carry inter-rater reliability (F-13: "SHOULD report inter-rater reliability on the same basis before reporting anything else"), which 305 acknowledges in the abstract and does not build into the design.

**Chance level and confusion matrix without an N.** Four classes, chance 25%, plus a fifth "capitalist" label outside the orbit (210) which may or may not be a response option — the paper does not say. The interesting output is declared to be the confusion matrix (303), which requires a per-cell sample the paper never specifies; a 4×4 or 5×5 confusion matrix needs an order of magnitude more trials than an overall accuracy figure.

**Circularity.** The author writes the discourse; the working group designed the code; the listener recovers it. This is author-to-listener transmission through a channel both ends of which the working group controls. It is a legitimate and useful engineering measurement (does the code transmit?), and it is not evidence about Lacanian discourse, threat actors, or anything outside the environment. The paper says the last part and not the first.

**Design confound not mentioned.** Condition 2 is "the audio with the operational streams present" (303). Adding the three operational streams changes both the informational context *and* the masking/segregation environment (Bregman; 204). A drop in accuracy in condition 2 would be attributable to masking, not to distraction or to interference of meaning, and the design cannot separate them without a spectrally-matched distractor arm.

**What a positive result licenses.** The paper's claim (307) is nearly right: "that listeners can or cannot recover an authored discourse from its rendering at a stated rate, which is the precondition for the adversary stream carrying any information at all." The word "authored" is doing the necessary work. The one overstatement is "precondition": recovery by *trained* listeners is a weak precondition, since operational use would not come with a codebook drill.

**VERDICT: honest about its external boundary and confounded at its internal one. The trained condition makes a trivial result inevitable; specify what training contains, add a masking-matched distractor arm, and set the confusion-matrix sample.**

## 2. The synthetic-data problem

The environment generates its own plant, its own operators, their psychometric vectors, their intended discourses and the ground truth against which everything is scored. The consequence has to be stated plainly, because the paper states it in some places and then contradicts it in others.

### 2.1 Which studies can yield evidence about the world

| Study | Yields evidence about | Why |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **5.1 Listener mappings** | **The world** (human auditory-semantic mapping) | Participants are real, stimuli are audio, the DV is a human judgement. The synthetic frame table only fixes the stimulus, which is exactly what a stimulus generator is for. External validity limited to the listener population actually recruited. |
| **5.2 SA and workload** | **The world, conditionally** | Participants real, DVs real. Evidence about humans performing a synthetic task. |

| **5.5 Psychometric calibration** | **The generator only** | The vectors are draws from an assumed distribution; the covariance is imposed; the ground truth is the draw. Nothing enters or leaves that is not the working group's own. |
| **5.6 Adversary discourse** | **The world, very narrowly** | Human listeners learning and applying a four-item code. A fact about auditory learning. Nothing about adversaries. |

Restated as one sentence: **the environment can produce real evidence about how people hear sounds, and it cannot produce evidence about anything the sounds are supposed to be about.**

### 2.2 Every place the paper conflates the two

1. **Line 17 → 76 → 299 → 331 → 381 (the load-bearing chain).** Line 17 identifies the programme's central gap: "no study has shown that a listener recovers anything about a psychometric vector from a rendering of it". Line 76 says "Validating the mapping is the point of the environment, and section 5.5 is the experiment that would do it." Line 299 calls 5.5 "the gate on every use of the psychometric layer elsewhere in the programme." Line 331 calls the outcome "a calibrated psychometric-to-music mapping". Line 381 says there is "no point training a conditioner on a psychometric layer that M4 has shown to carry nothing." Every step after the first substitutes *evidence about the encoder* for *evidence about the construct*. 5.5 cannot validate a psychometric-to-music mapping because no psychometrics enter it; it can only establish whether a code the working group wrote is audible. Line 297's disclaimer covers only the "properties of a real person" reading and does not cover this one.

2. **Line 82.** "An environment in which the intended discourse is written down before the audio is rendered can ask the only question that matters at this stage: given the rendering, does a listener recover the discourse the author wrote?" The phrase "the only question that matters" elevates an encoder-transmission question to the status of the research question. It matters, but it is not the only one and not the first; whether the taxonomy codes anything (F-13, inter-rater reliability on real material) comes first.

3. **Line 80.** "the environment has something a field deployment never has, which is the ground truth of what the estimate should have been." This is precisely backwards as an epistemic claim. A field deployment lacks that ground truth because the ground truth is a matter of fact that has to be established; the environment has it because it is stipulated. Stipulated ground truth is not ground truth, it is the experimenter's intention, and calling it "ground truth" is where the conflation is lexicalised.

4. **Line 218.** "The story is written down before the rendering, so that whether a listener recovers the story is a question with an answer." True and well put — and immediately preceded by the honest statement "the input is a story, the render is a rendering of the story, and nothing about a person has been measured." The paper states the limit correctly here and then does not carry it into 5.5's claim sentence.

5. **Line 260.** "vector labels that are exact by construction because the vector generated the clip." This treats generator-parameter identity as equivalent to a validated label. In EMOPIA — the source of the corpus figure — the labels are human judgements of perceived affect with four annotators and cross-checks (MPN-3 line 239; q7 line 76). Exactness-by-construction and annotator-reliability are not the same property and are not substitutable; the substitution converts a perceptual-label corpus into a parameter-recovery corpus, which is a different training problem.

6. **Line 335.** "What this environment can hand [a field edition] is a listener-study result per mapping with its polarity criterion, a probe-level situation-awareness result and a workload result from a simulator, a flood-behaviour conformance record, a renderer comparison with confidence intervals, and a statement about early-warning indicators on synthetic series." Three of those six are generator-internal (flood conformance, renderer comparison's objective half, early-warning-on-synthetic-series) and are listed alongside three that are not, with no distinction drawn. A field edition cannot "carry" a generator-internal result as evidence for anything about a plant.

7. **Line 206.** "the generator knows the true plant state, so the notation table can be checked against the state that produced it rather than against another rendering." This one is *correct* and should be kept: it is a conformance claim (does the implementation compute what the specification says), not an empirical claim. It is the model for how the rest of the paper should talk about generator ground truth. Cited here as a positive instance, not a defect.

### 4.2 Stationarity, detrending, and the surrogate specification

**Stationarity.** The estimators at 313 are the standard rolling AC(1) and variance. The paper says detrending is "declared in the header" (315) and never names a method, a bandwidth, or a selection rule. This is the single most exploitable degree of freedom in the whole analysis: the Gaussian-kernel bandwidth in the Dakos toolchain directly determines how much low-frequency power survives into the residual and therefore directly determines the rolling AC(1) trend. Choosing it after seeing the data guarantees a positive result on demand. Nothing in DM-15 (153) prevents this, because "the statistical model" does not obviously include a smoothing bandwidth. Neither does the paper require stationarity *testing* of the detrended residual before the indicator is computed.

**Which series.** The channels at 317 are *derived indices*, not raw observables. Every MPN index is clipped (`min(1, ·)` appears in all four of MPN-2's index definitions, lines 109–127) and the instability index is a `max` of two normalised quantities (MPN-2 line 121). Clipping destroys Gaussianity, compresses variance near the bounds, and changes AC(1) in a direction that depends on where in its range the series sits. A series drifting *into* saturation shows falling variance and rising AC(1) for reasons that have nothing to do with a fold. The paper flags pupil luminance and HRV respiration as "hard cases" (317) and never mentions the clipping problem in its own indices, which is the more certain artefact of the two.

**Surrogates.** Line 321 is the best paragraph in section 6 and is still not a specification. "The surrogates therefore have to be generated from the same detrended series under the null of no approaching fold" names a null in words and no algorithm. The standard toolchain the paper cites (Dakos et al.) uses phase-randomised or AAFT/IAAFT surrogates preserving the power spectrum and (for AAFT) the amplitude distribution, tested against the **Kendall τ of the rolling indicator trend** as the statistic, with a declared surrogate count (typically 1,000). The paper specifies **none of the four**: not the surrogate algorithm, not the preserved statistic, not the number of surrogates, not the test statistic. It also does not say whether the null is "no fold" or "no trend in the indicator", which are different nulls with different surrogate constructions.

**Multiplicity.** Line 317 admits at least six channel families (arousal, load, pupil, keystroke, speech, plus link metrics and the synthetic plant state), and each carries two indicators (AC(1) and variance), and each indicator is computed over an unspecified set of window lengths. That is a minimum of twelve tests and realistically many more, with no family-wise correction, no false-discovery control and no pre-declared channel. Given a free choice of channel, window and bandwidth, a positive early-warning result is available by construction.

### 4.3 Is bimodality demonstrated or assumed?

Assumed. Neither §5 nor §6 contains a procedure for demonstrating bimodality (a mixture model, a dip test, a bimodality coefficient on the stationary distribution) or hysteresis (an up-sweep and a down-sweep of the control parameter showing path dependence). Requirement (c) is transcribed at 319 and dropped. Without it the analysis cannot distinguish a fold from any of the many non-catastrophe mechanisms Zahler and Sussmann enumerate — which the paper itself cites at 319 and then does not act on.

### 4.4 How a positive result could be an artefact of the generator, and what the paper guards against

The paper guards against **one** artefact and misses at least six.

**Guarded (321).** "A generator that produces a regime change also produces, incidentally, a slow drift and a changing noise level, and both raise a rolling variance without any critical transition underneath." Correct, well stated, and the proposed remedy — run the test on the synthetic series first where the answer is known, and treat an indicator that fires on a fold-free generator as falsified — is the right instinct and the best methodological content in the paper.

**Unguarded:**

1. **Engineered hysteresis mistaken for dynamical hysteresis.** The alert rule has hysteresis (MPN-2 §4.2) and the flood machine enters at ten and exits below five (159, 343; MPN-3 §8.4). Any series passing through either machine exhibits **bimodality and hysteresis by construction**. If van der Maas's flags are evaluated on the notation table, the rendering state, or any index downstream of these machines, requirement (c) is satisfied by an `if` statement and a cusp will appear to be "demonstrated". Nothing in §6 excludes machine-downstream series from the analysis, and 317 explicitly admits "the link metrics and the synthetic plant state".
2. **Clipping and saturation in the indices** (see §4.2) producing indicator trends with no dynamical content.
3. **Counting-process variance–mean coupling.** The alarm generator produces counts with a deadband and a "chatter tendency" (56). For any counting process the variance scales with the mean, so a rising alarm rate mechanically raises the rolling variance of the alarm-derived channels, and binning changes AC(1) with bin occupancy. The activity index is driven by `a_10` (MPN-2 line 115), so this reaches the tension index and everything downstream.
4. **Smoothing in the index definitions.** The instability index uses a frame-to-frame difference and the load index uses trailing counts and latencies (MPN-2 lines 119–127). Differencing and trailing-window aggregation each impose their own autocorrelation structure on the output regardless of the input. Estimating AC(1) on a trailing-window statistic measures the window, not the process.
5. **Seed sharing.** DM-3 (141) requires everything seeded from one recorded seed. If the scenario schedule and the noise realisation draw from the same stream, transition times and noise excursions can be correlated across runs, and repeated runs are not independent replicates. The manifest records the seed; nothing requires *independent* streams per component.
6. **Analyst degrees of freedom**: window length, detrending bandwidth, channel choice, indicator choice, all unspecified (see §4.2).
7. **The unstated generative model.** The paper never says whether the plant twin's dynamics contain a fold. This matters both ways. If the generator has no fold, a positive indicator is by definition an artefact — which is the useful null experiment the paper describes at 321. If the generator *is* built with a fold (which it would have to be, to test detection at all), then detection is guaranteed by construction and tells you about the estimator's sensitivity, not about control rooms. The paper needs both generators, pre-registered, and needs to say which result comes from which. It currently describes only the first and then proposes to report "a comparison of predicted and observed times to transition where a potential has been fitted" (256), which presupposes the second.

**Net.** The one honest and genuinely valuable result available here is a **false-positive-rate characterisation of the EWS estimator on fold-free generators of declared spectral properties**. The paper identifies this (321, 335, 381) and deserves credit for it. But it overstates the reach: line 335 offers it as "evidence a later field edition would carry", when in fact a false-positive rate measured on one synthetic generator transfers to real plant data only insofar as the generator's spectral and distributional properties match a real historian's, which is unknown and untested.

## 5. The learned-renderer plan (5.4 and §7's third outcome)

Collected verdicts, with the detail in §1.4.

**Is the data budget sufficient for the claim?** No, and the question is currently unanswerable because the training target is unstated (260). At a thousand clips and ten hours the budget *matches* MPN-3 §5.5 row 1 in magnitude and fails it in kind: MPN-3 requires labels from "a documented protocol with more than one annotator and a reliability figure", and MX substitutes "exact by construction". The substituted property makes the corpus suitable for learning a deterministic inverse of the fixed renderer and unsuitable for learning a perceptual axis, which is what "conditioned generation" means in the literature the budget is drawn from (q7 line 112).

**Is the held-out design right?** In form yes ("a held-out set of vectors and not merely of clips", 260, correctly inherited from MPN-3 §5.5 row 3). In substance it is nearly empty at the declared first-milestone dimensionality: a binary flag × one continuous index means "held-out vectors" is a held-out interval of a single scalar, and the paper does not say whether the held-out region is interior (interpolation, trivial) or terminal (extrapolation, the real test).

**Is the evaluation metric independent of the training signal?** No. FAD "against a reference set" (260) has no reference set in a closed environment other than the training distribution. The probe alternative is under-specified in exactly the way that decides whether it is independent (what corpus is the probe trained on?). And the preference-alignment stage (262) makes the *listener* evaluation share its target construct with the training label, which is a second and separate independence failure that the paper's rebuttal at 262 does not address.

**Is the baseline the right one?** The two baselines named — the fixed renderer and the unmodified pretrained model with a text prompt (151, 260, 275) — are the right two for the internal comparison, and DM-13 binding them under one protocol on the same frame tables is good design. Two are missing: a **floor** (mismatched/random rendering, to establish the listener task's chance level and rule out an all-at-chance three-way tie) and an **external competitor** (a plain parameter-mapping auditory graph), without which the comparison cannot say whether MPN's musical apparatus earns its complexity.

**Does anything risk training and evaluating on the same generator?** Yes, at three points. (i) The corpus and the reference set for FAD are both generated by the environment. (ii) The probe, if trained in-environment, learns the environment's audio statistics. (iii) The DPO preference label and the listener-study target are the same construct ("expresses the declared state"). The paper's protections — DM-12's packaging separation (150) and DM-13's protocol requirement (151) — address *code* separation and protocol uniformity, and neither addresses distributional independence, which is the property at issue.

**One further point.** The honest expectation stated at 333 — that a two-dimensional conditioner on a synthetic corpus "will be close to the fixed renderer and will need the held-out probe rather than a preference score to separate them" — is well calibrated against the literature. But under the first horn of the corpus dilemma it is not an expectation, it is a theorem, and the study would be spending its listener budget to confirm something derivable from the design.

**One global point about the chain.** Every study that asks a participant to recover something traverses: assumed distribution → sampled vector → declared translation (four scalars) → six musical parameters → synthesised audio → participant judgement under an experimenter-supplied verbal gloss. The translation step is many-to-one and not invertible, and the verbal gloss is an uncontrolled factor at the far end. The chain is interpretable in the forward direction (you can always say what produced a given sound) and not in the reverse direction (you cannot say what a given response implies about the input), which is precisely the direction every listener study in section 5 runs.

## 7. Three weakest studies, ranked, and what would fix each

### Weakest — 5.5, Calibrating the psychometric-to-music mapping (291–299)

**Why.** Four independent disqualifiers: the independent variable contradicts §4.3's declared translation (216 vs 293) and would breach E-4 on the only reading that saves the study; the recovery ceiling is four degrees of freedom against twenty components and is computable in advance from the paper's own specification; the primary outcome as stated (recovery of redundant components) has no interpretable direction; and the response format makes it a stereotype-agreement task. Worst of all, it is positioned as the study the series is named after (293), the gate on the psychometric layer (299), and milestone M4 on which M5 depends (381) — so its weakness propagates.

**Fixes, in order of preference.**
1. **Split it in two and rename both.** (a) A *sonification-discriminability* study: take the four scalars that actually reach the renderer under §8.2's declared translation, and measure per-scalar discriminability and cross-scalar confusion with a proper factorial stimulus design. This is a clean, answerable, useful experiment and needs no psychometrics. (b) A separate *psychometric-signature* study, which cannot be run in this environment at all and requires real inventory data with observed covariance and an external criterion. Say so, and put (b) outside the roadmap.
2. If the study is kept as one: state the candidate mapping explicitly as a function from the section 2.2 feature space to the musical parameters, compute and publish its rank *before* collecting data, and declare the identifiable subspace as the study's scope.
3. Replace the primary outcome with a two-part criterion: recovery of non-redundant components above a declared threshold **and** recovery of redundant components below one, with the direction of each stated in advance.
4. Add a train/test split with feedback withheld at test, to separate code learning from code reading.
5. Pre-register the per-component family and its multiplicity control.

### Second weakest — 5.3 / §6, Early-warning-signal analysis (254–256, 309–323)

**Why.** It is four lines of design plus a section of transcription. Its human arm is declared infeasible by the same paper (319). Four of MPN-1's five requirements are unmet, including (c) and (e) which are unmeetable under the stated design. The surrogate procedure — the one thing the paper correctly identifies as where a synthetic environment fools itself (321) — is specified in words and not in algorithm, count or statistic. And the environment contains engineered hysteresis in the alert and flood machines that would satisfy van der Maas's flags without any dynamics at all.

**Fixes.**
1. **Declare the generative model.** Build two plant twins, pre-registered: one with an explicit saddle-node in a declared state variable, one provably fold-free with matched spectral and distributional properties. Report the indicator on both. The fold-free arm is the false-positive rate; the fold arm is the sensitivity. Neither alone is a result.
2. **Specify the surrogate test completely**: AAFT or IAAFT surrogates preserving the power spectrum and amplitude distribution; N = 1,000; test statistic = Kendall τ of the rolling indicator over the pre-transition window; null = no monotone trend in the indicator; one-sided; alpha declared.
3. **Pre-register window length and detrending bandwidth**, and publish a sensitivity surface over both rather than a single choice.
4. **Exclude every series downstream of a hysteretic state machine** (alert rule, flood machine) from bimodality and hysteresis tests, and say so in the plan. Compute indicators on *unclipped, un-differenced* channels wherever a clipped or differenced index exists.
5. **Declare one primary channel** before looking, with the remaining channels reported as exploratory under an explicit FDR control.
6. **Delete the participant arm from 5.3** or state its required session length explicitly and schedule it as its own milestone. Drop requirement (e) from the programme, or add a bistable long-run generator experiment that can actually supply spontaneous escapes.
7. Correct line 319: §5.6 is not "adopted whole" while (a)'s exclusion of staff physiology is reversed at 317.

### Third weakest — 5.4, Training a state-conditioned renderer (258–289)

**Why.** Its evaluation architecture is the best in the paper and it rests on an unstated training corpus, every reading of which defeats the study: fixed-renderer audio caps the learned renderer at the fixed renderer and makes the headline comparison a foregone conclusion; anything else destroys the "exact by construction" labelling that is the environment's claimed advantage. Compounding this, the objective metric is not independent of the training distribution in a closed environment, the preference stage shares its construct with the listener evaluation, and the 25-listener figure is imported from a descriptive survey into a powered three-arm comparison.

**Fixes.**
1. **State the training target audio explicitly** in one sentence. If it is the fixed renderer's output, relabel the study as a distillation/compression study and drop the "beats the fixed renderer" framing from 289 and 333.
2. **Source a corpus outside the environment** if the comparison claim is to be kept: real clips scored on the two axes by ≥2 annotators with a reported reliability figure, i.e. MPN-3 §5.5 row 1 as written.
3. **Define the reference set for FAD** and show it is disjoint from the training distribution; failing that, drop FAD and rely on the probe, specifying the probe's training corpus and the domain-shift check.
4. **Add two arms**: a mismatched-rendering floor and a plain parameter-mapping auditory-graph competitor.
5. **Declare interpolation vs extrapolation** for the held-out vector set.
6. **Power the listener study** for the three-way (now five-way) comparison rather than importing EMOPIA's N, and name the rating instrument (absolute rating or pairwise; not MUSHRA, per the working group's own note in `RESEARCH-SYNTHESIS-2026-09-11.md` line 77).
7. Declare the preference-pair count (MPN-3 §5.5 row 7).

*Close fourth:* **5.6**, whose trained condition makes a trivial result inevitable and whose only fix is to specify what training contains and add a masking-matched distractor arm — and which additionally has no milestone or gate anywhere in section 9.

## 8. Verdict, with a severity-ordered fix list

**Verdict.** MPN-4-MX-R is an unusually honest paper about what has not been measured and an unusually weak one about how to measure it. Its normative scaffolding is excellent: DM-2 through DM-6 on provenance and determinism, DM-11's conformance gate, DM-12/DM-13's renderer separation and one-protocol comparison, DM-17's tier-carrying and DM-23's claim-to-dataset binding are the right requirements, and the milestone ordering at 379–381 (conformance before listeners, polarity before calibration, calibration before training) is correctly reasoned. The paragraph at 321 on how a synthetic environment fools itself is the best methodological writing in the series.

But sections 5 and 6 do not meet the standard sections 2 and 3 set. Not one of the six studies would pass the paper's own DM-14 and DM-15 as filed: there is no sample size, no power calculation, no stopping rule, no exclusion rule, no multiplicity policy, and only one gesture at a primary outcome. Two studies (5.1, 5.2) are sound in form and lack the control conditions that would make their results interpretable. One (5.5) is not salvageable in its present framing because the construct it names is not present in its design. One (5.3) is a forward reference to an analysis whose human arm the paper declares infeasible and whose synthetic arm has no declared generative model to test against. One (5.4) has the best evaluation design in the paper attached to an unstated corpus that probably defeats it. One (5.6) is honest about its external limits and confounded at its internal ones, and is absent from the roadmap entirely.

The central intellectual defect is the one the paper is most careful about in the abstract and least careful about in the claim sentences: **stipulated ground truth is treated as ground truth** (80), and evidence about the encoder is repeatedly promoted to evidence about the construct (17→76→299→331→381). The environment can measure how people hear sounds. It cannot measure what the sounds are about. Half the programme is designed as though it could.

**Fix list, severity-ordered.**

1. **[Blocking] Resolve the 216-vs-293 contradiction and state the candidate psychometric mapping as an explicit function with a computed rank.** Until this is done, study 5.5 has no independent variable and milestone M4 has no content. If the rank is four, say so and rescope.
2. **[Blocking] State what audio the learned renderer is trained on** (260). One sentence. Everything in 5.4, in M5 and in outcome three (333) is contingent on it.
3. **[Blocking] Add sample sizes, power analyses, stopping rules, exclusion and missing-data rules, primary outcomes and multiplicity policies to all six studies**, or state explicitly that section 5 contains design outlines and that the registrable protocols are separate documents. As written the paper exhibits six studies that violate its own DM-14 and DM-15.
4. **[Severe] Add the decorrelated-rendering control arm to 5.2** and settle whether audio continues during SAGAT freezes. Without both, 5.2 cannot distinguish information from sound and cannot be interpreted.
5. **[Severe] Specify the surrogate test completely** (algorithm, preserved statistic, count, test statistic, null, alpha), pre-register window and detrending bandwidth, and declare one primary EWS channel. Currently a positive early-warning result is available on demand through analyst degrees of freedom.
6. **[Severe] Exclude hysteretic-machine-downstream series from bimodality and hysteresis tests**, and compute indicators on unclipped, un-differenced channels. The alert rule and flood machine would otherwise satisfy MPN-1 requirement (c) with an `if` statement.
7. **[Severe] Declare the plant twin's generative model** and run the EWS analysis on both a fold-bearing and a provably fold-free twin with matched spectra. Report sensitivity and false-positive rate separately.
8. **[Severe] Restore MPN-2 §9's two-stream retention criterion and its visual baseline condition to 5.1** (currently dropped, see table row 14), and give the scaling half of the magnitude estimation an analysis and a criterion (row 15).
9. **[Major] Fix the "load index" name collision** (MPN-2 line 127 vs MX line 224). Two different quantities carry the same name in the same schema and the same EWS channel list.
10. **[Major] Resolve the DM-17 / φ contradiction**: N-25's relaxation makes the fatigue index a mapping, and MPN-2 gives it no tier for DM-17 to print. Either tier it or leave it as an annotation.
11. **[Major] Correct line 319.** MPN-1 §5.6 is not "adopted whole" while requirement (a)'s exclusion of staff physiology is reversed at 317. Say which requirements are adopted, which are modified and why.
12. **[Major] Add the missing arms to 5.4**: a mismatched-rendering floor and a plain parameter-mapping competitor; define the FAD reference set or drop FAD; specify the probe's training corpus; declare interpolation vs extrapolation for held-out vectors; name the rating instrument; declare the preference-pair count.
13. **[Major] Specify what training contains in 5.6** and add a masking-matched distractor arm for the with-operational-streams condition. Give 5.6 a milestone and a gate in section 9, or remove it from section 5.
14. **[Moderate] Fix the four reference-implementation figures** in table rows 1–4: three vs five served scalars; a 55-term enumeration labelled 57; and the two misattributions of training to "the projector".
15. **[Moderate] Qualify line 106.** Sufficiency of one accelerator for a ten-hour corpus is extrapolated ~22× from a 27-minute demonstration that reports no metric.
16. **[Moderate] Rewrite the claim sentences at 299, 331, 335 and 381** so that "the encoding is audible" is never written as "the mapping is calibrated" or "the psychometric layer carries something", and so that generator-internal results (flood conformance, the objective half of the renderer comparison, EWS on synthetic series) are listed separately from participant results in the outcome list at 335.
17. **[Moderate] Define the listener population.** "Operators of the target population" (240) has no referent in a closed environment with a synthetic plant; MPN-2 §9's "target site" does not exist here.
18. **[Minor] Add a scenario-validity measure to 5.2** — expert plausibility ratings of the plant twin's scripted scenarios — and report it alongside the SA result, since the study's external reach is bounded by it.
19. **[Minor] Require independent random streams per stochastic component** in DM-3, not merely one recorded seed, so that repeated runs are genuine replicates.
20. **[Minor] Note the F-requirement question.** The relaxation table (25–42) covers N- and E-requirements only, and line 13 says F-1 through F-14 bind except where MX-R relaxes them. Operator-state mode (220–230) renders per-participant physiological inference, which a reader will compare against F-10 and F-11. This is a normative-documentation gap, not an ethics finding: the paper should say in one line whether the F-requirements' deployment clauses are considered inapplicable to a study condition, so that no reader has to infer it.
