## 1. Disposition

The paper survives review as a paper. Its argument holds, its central finding holds and is stronger than when it was drafted, and its method of reporting against itself is intact. What does not hold is the accuracy of two sections, section 1 and section 11, and the accuracy of those two is the whole of the paper's claim to be trusted. A paper whose case is that it reports the evidence against itself cannot have its summary understate its own disclosure and its implementation section understate what the implementation does.

Two structural findings are accepted and they govern everything else.

**The executive summary is not true to the paper.** Section 1 calls the central correction a mislabelled file. Sections 8.2 and 11 say something categorically larger: no quantity in the theory has ever been observed by anything except the system itself. Most readers stop at section 1. One sentence fixes it and it is the highest-value edit in the review.

**The ingest path fabricates state.** The paper's section 11 discusses what is fixed and what moves and does not reach the thing a user would hit first. For a script a user imports, four of the nine components are generated by calls to the platform random number generator, and on one path trauma and entropy are too. Verified independently: `src/lib/play_parser.ts:254-257` assigns all four DISC components with `Math.random()`, `:264-266` does the same for the Dark Triad, `src/app/api/process-play/route.ts:48` sets `trauma = Math.random() * 0.8 + 0.1`, and `src/app/play-library/import/page.tsx:145-146` sets both trauma and entropy to `Math.random()`. The wizard exposes seventeen sliders per character including the three registers, and `src/app/play-library/page.tsx:634-637` writes only trauma, entropy and `focusLayer: lineIdx % 7` into each frame, discarding the rest, so the composer path falls back to its hard-coded triple for every user script and the state does not move within a scene at all.

That second finding also revises the cost of seeding. "Seed the generator, which is days" was counted as one job. It is two: making the existing calls reproducible, and deciding what the ingest path should do instead of inventing four components. The second is a policy decision, not an engineering task, and S4 cannot be written without it.

### Accepted in part

**U2, the therapist audience.** The User Advocate is right that a disclaimer at the bottom of section 11 does not reach the surface where the claim is made, and right that the README's "Therapeutic Applications" section makes the claim in the product. The Advocate's preferred remedy, carrying the clinical content into S1, is declined: population, supervision, consent and failure modes are a paper's worth of material and the series has a paper for them. What S1 does instead is state the position where a reader meets the claim, in section 1 and in section 3, name the README as something that requires correction, and say which paper carries the content. The author's own `06_APPLICATIONS/04_therapy_usecase/` material is the input to that paper and is not summarised here.

**CG's correlation-matrix point.** The Guardian observes that three magnitudes summing to a constant have a singular correlation matrix and that equicorrelation at $-0.5$ is the boundary of the positive semi-definite cone, so a test aimed at strong mutual negative correlation is aimed at an extreme point. The observation is correct and it does not defeat the test as section 10 states it, because that test rates the three registers *without* the sum constraint, so the matrix is not singular. It does sharpen what the test can find: the question is not whether the three are mutually strongly negatively correlated, which is close to arithmetically impossible, but whether they are negatively coupled at all against a null of independence. Section 10 is amended to say that.

### Declined

**The Skeptic's suggestion that section 7 become two sentences.** Revision 3 already cut section 7 to the one item the paper uses plus two cautions. Cutting further would remove the cautions, which are the section's best content.

**The Advocate's suggestion that the assertions be restated in the paper with their full formal content.** That duplicates the register, which is the thing being cited. The fix is to cite it properly, which is accepted above.

**Reopening the Atlas as the classification of record.** The Skeptic showed that name-matching moves the counts by three and that the Atlas covers only sixteen of thirty entries with domains. Both are now in the paper. Neither is a reason to prefer the implementation's set, which merges a persuasion taxonomy into a bias taxonomy. The Atlas stands.

The theory is unchanged by this review. What changed is the paper's account of its own evidence, which is now: none. Every number the programme has was produced by the system or by a heuristic, and the annotation instrument is not one item on a list of four but the only route to a number the theory did not generate itself.

That is a harder position than the paper started in and it is a more defensible one, because it is true and it is now stated where a reader will see it.
