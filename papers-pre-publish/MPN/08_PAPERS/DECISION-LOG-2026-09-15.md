## Decision A. Assertion B4 is amended

**The author's words.** "Amend B4."

**What was struck.** The third clause of the claim, "and bias, which lives between speakers, takes the harmonic relation between staves rather than any channel on a single stave"; the formal-content sentence "The bias channel is a between-stave quantity and therefore does not consume a within-stave channel"; and the falsifier's third disjunct, "Or the between-stave channel turns out to be a within-stave channel in disguise, in which case the budget is exceeded after all".

**Why.** Decision A of 14 September took BL-8 as option (e), under which the bias layer occupies no musical channel and renders nothing on any surface. B4 and that decision contradicted each other. The arbitration recorded the contradiction as decision 9.1 and declined to resolve it, restating an assertion being an amendment to the calculus and the calculus being the author's.

**What it does to the theory.** It makes B4 **easier to falsify**, which is the point of making it. The struck disjunct was an escape: a layer could exceed the per-stave budget and the assertion would survive by relocating that layer between staves. With the escape gone, nothing in B4 is exempt from the budget, so a measured channel budget that differs from two to three falsifies the assertion directly. MPN-S5 already shows every surface oversubscribed against it on either count.

**What it does not touch.** $f_{\text{harmony}}$ in MPN-S1 section 6's definition of $\Phi$. That is a coordinate of the state mapping and a different object from the between-stave harmonic relation. It stays, and naming $f$ remains arbitration decision 9.3, still open.

**Applied in.** `ASSERTIONS-REGISTER.md` entry B4, with the struck text preserved at the end of the entry for comparison. `S1-mckenney-lacan-theory.md` section 9.3.

## Decision B. A named per-person comparison is permitted on a stave, and a score carries several personas

**The author's words.** "you can use a per-person comparison on a stave. this could be an actor, or character from a play or any person or character or persona in a book, podcast etc. We will have several personas, people, etc in a score, so this is important that we can scale properly and add and remove as need."

**What it answers.** Arbitration decision 9.2, which offered three options. The author has taken neither option one, the channel line, nor option three, refusal everywhere. He has taken the substance of **option two**: the comparison is permitted on a stave, which under D42's current scope means it is permitted **untested**, and extending D42's scope to cover it becomes a test to run rather than a choice to make. MPN-S6 item EX-4 is thereby decided in its first branch.

**What it reverses.** The arbitration's rulings 8.4 and 8.5 were written against option one, and the repairs applied to both papers on 15 September say the line is the channel. Those repairs are superseded by this decision and are listed at section 4 below.

**What it adds that no decision before it had.** A score carries several personas, added and removed as the material requires. No document in this programme had asked what that costs. `MPN-NOTE-06-persona-scaling.md` computes it. In brief, and every figure there is computed rather than estimated:

- **The stave budget does not get worse as the cast grows.** Oversubscription runs 2.7x at two personas to 3.7x at fourteen and then flattens. Scaling makes the legibility problem wider, not worse.
- **The relation scales quadratically.** Pairs go from 1 at two personas to 91 at fourteen. A moderated structure collapses that to $N-1$. This makes MPN-S5 item S5-16 load-bearing rather than a convenience.
- **Adding a persona never re-voices anyone already on the page, and the guarantee is cheap.** The append-only timbre curve costs at most **13.4 per cent** of separation across the whole range two to fourteen, and nothing at all at $N = 2, 5, 6$ and $14$. Removal is free and repacks nothing.
- **The seventh persona is the flat DISC profile** at the centre of the reachable set, at separation exactly 1.0. Every other position is a cube corner. That is geometry, not choice.
- **The cast ceiling is the one unmeasured number in the channel.** At $\epsilon = \sqrt{3}/2$ a growing score holds 14 personas; at 1.0 it holds 7; at $\sqrt{2}$ it holds 6; at 2.0 it holds 2. $\epsilon$ is the discrimination experiment of design section 5b.5 and it has never been run.

**What it leaves open, and it is the author's.** MPN-S6's EX-R1 rules Expression a **one-stave** surface. A score of several personas is not one stave. Either Expression is scoped to the interaction stave of an $N$-persona score, or EX-R1 is restated. Filed as SC-5 in `MPN-NOTE-06-persona-scaling.md`.

## Decision C. Backchannel is a sixth Layer 1 measure

**The author's words.** "backchannel is a sixth measure."

**What it answers.** Arbitration decision 9.6, option two. D64 settled the count at five and reserved the naming of a sixth to the author in its own words: "Naming a sixth is the author's; counting an undefined one is available to nobody." The author has named it. This is the exercise of a reserved authority and not an override of a decision.

**What it makes right that was called wrong.** Two things, and both are corrections to this programme's own rulings rather than to the corpus.

`s6_three_clef.py` prints six $\Psi$ quantities, listing backchannel among them, and totals eighteen. D65 settled at seventeen, and the script was therefore recorded as defective in the verification file, in MPN-S5 as item **S5-8**, in MPN-S6 as item **EX-8**, and in the arbitration at rulings 4.6 and 6.14. **Under this decision the script was right and the ruling was wrong.** S5-8 and EX-8 are withdrawn, not completed.

Design section 6.1's class A header states **five settings**. The arbitration's ruling 7.6 found the design's own table gives five measures and four settings, adjacency having no decision to make, and opened MPN-S5 item **S5-21** to correct the header from five to four. With backchannel a measure in its own right it carries its own setting, the lexicon, so the count is six measures and five settings and **the header was right all along**, by a route it did not state. S5-21 is withdrawn.

**What it changes, and it is larger than it looks.** Sixty-five sites across four documents state a count that this decision moves: 33 in MPN-S5, 12 in MPN-S6, 17 in the design and 3 in the PRD. Most are not the bare total. They are derived counts of the form "two of the five are unavailable outright on a script", "three of the five carry no information at two speakers", "four of the five are timing quantities". Each has to be re-derived rather than incremented, and a global substitution of six for five would produce exactly the class of count error this programme keeps finding. Filed as **SC-7**, and it is a pass rather than an edit.

**One question has to be answered before that pass can run, and it is the author's.** Is a backchannel measurable on a script, which is class A text with no timings?

- If **yes**, being a lexical count of short supportive utterances, then it is available where a lexicon exists and the script's available set goes from three of six to four of six, which strengthens the weakest surface in the programme.
- If **no**, being defined by its timing relative to another speaker's turn, then it patterns with overlap and latency, the script's available set stays at three of six, and the unavailable set goes from two to three.

The design's own table defined backchannel inside the overlap row, whose decision is "the threshold, and whether backchannels count", which points to the second answer. Naming it a measure does not by itself settle it. Until it is settled, MPN-NOTE-06 and both papers state the count as six and leave the per-class availability where it was.

**A consequence worth seeing whichever way that lands.** At two speakers the design's degeneracies are adjacency deterministic, reciprocity 1 by construction and floor share one number and its complement, which is three of five. Backchannel given and received are **not** degenerate at two speakers. So a sixth measure makes the two-speaker case less empty than the arbitration found it, and that bears directly on decision 9.4, the default measure set, which is still open. It does not rescue Expression, where EX-R3 withholds the overlap-derived measures and backchannel goes with them.

## Decision D. Backchannel is measured differently on different ingest classes

**The author's words.** Asked whether a backchannel is measurable on a play script, which is class A text with no timings, the author chose: **it depends on the ingest class.** Lexical on class A, where the text is exact and the labels are given; timing-derived on classes B and C, where a detector is running anyway.

**What it settles.** The recount that decision C opened. Each of the sixty-five derived counts is now re-derivable, because the rule that decides them is stated. On class A a backchannel is a lexical object and is available wherever a lexicon exists, so the script's available set goes from three of six to **four of six**, which strengthens the weakest surface in the programme. On classes B and C it patterns with overlap and latency.

**What it costs, and the cost is real.** One measure with two definitions is two measures wearing one name unless the output says which one produced the number. Three consequences follow and none is optional.

The measure carries **two settings rather than one**: a lexicon for the lexical definition and a timing rule for the timing-derived one. That takes Layer 1 from six measures and five settings to six measures and **six settings**, which moves the same sixty-five sites a second time and moves design section 6.1's class A header again.

A backchannel count from class A and a backchannel count from class C are **not comparable**, and nothing in the design currently stops a surface putting them side by side. The output has to declare which definition produced the number, in the same way D9 and D30 make the floor-share unit a declared setting shown on every output, and for the same reason: a reader comparing two numbers produced by two definitions is comparing the definitions.

And the lexical definition inherits S4's keyword-counter precedent **on class A specifically**, which is the class the scope ruling makes primary. MPN-S6 section 4 withholds the overlap-derived measures precisely because a backchannel lexicon is a keyword counter and S4 found one read as a measurement through three revisions of a paper. Decision D puts a keyword counter on the primary material as a measure in its own right. That is defensible and it is not free, and the paper that argues hardest against it is the one that now has to carry it.

**Filed as EX-18** in MPN-S6 and as **SC-7** in `MPN-NOTE-06-persona-scaling.md`. The recount is a pass rather than an edit, and a global substitution of six for five would produce exactly the class of count error this programme keeps finding.

## Decision E. Expression is an N-persona surface, and EX-R1 is restated

**The author's words.** Asked whether to scope Expression to a single interaction stave or restate EX-R1 for N personas, the author chose: **restate EX-R1 for N personas.**

**What was wrong before, and it was a reading rather than a decision.** Draft 1 and revision 2 of MPN-S6 read D70, "Expression renders the Ψ stave alone", as making Expression a **one-stave** surface. That count was an artefact of the design's reference score, which is two Φ staves and one Ψ stave and is therefore a two-speaker layout; removing the two Φ staves from it leaves one. **D70 rules out the state stave. It never ruled on how many interaction staves there are.** Per-person Layer 1 measures are indexed by speaker, so with several personas the layout is one interaction stave per persona and still no state staves, because Φ still does not run here.

**What the restatement does to the arithmetic, and it reverses a finding.** MPN-S6's falsifier one held that at two speakers the surface has almost nothing: adjacency deterministic, reciprocity 1 by construction, floor share one number and its complement, leaving latency and one scalar on a single stave. That is true of **one** Ψ stave carrying the relation. It is false of per-persona staves, because persona A's floor share is a number on A's stave and persona B's is a number on B's, and the fact that they sum to one is a relation between two staves rather than an emptiness in either.

Recomputed at two personas with EX-R3's withholding in force, each persona's stave carries **floor share and latency**: degree and reciprocity are the constants, overlap and backchannel are withheld, and what remains is two quantities inside a budget of two to three. **It fits exactly, with nothing displaced and nothing left over.**

That pair is precisely what the arbitration put forward as the candidate answer to its decision 9.4 and left open. The restatement does not make that decision harder; at two personas it makes the candidate the only answer the arithmetic admits. **Above two personas decision 9.4 is still open**, because four measures then compete for two or three places and nothing in this programme says which two a reader can hold.

**What it costs.** Above two personas the surface is oversubscribed 1.3x to 2x per stave with EX-R3 in force and 2x to 3x without it, so D53's rule that a surface must choose is load-bearing rather than a formality. And the one-stave framing that MPN-S6 was built around is gone from section 9, EX-R1, EX-11 and falsifier one, all four restated.

**What it does not cost.** Nothing about cast change. A persona is appended at the point farthest from every persona already placed, so **no persona already on the page ever changes voice**, at a cost of at most 13.4 per cent of timbre separation across the range two to fourteen and nothing at all at 2, 5, 6 and 14 [`MPN-NOTE-06`]. Removal is free and repacks nothing. So a cast change is a D53 speaker-set discontinuity taking its marked seam, and it is never a D36 relabel. That is computed, not asserted.

**Applied in.** MPN-S6 section 9 (the ruling, consequence one and consequence three, with the superseded paragraph preserved rather than deleted), section 10 falsifier one, section 11 rulings EX-R1 and EX-11, and two new queue items EX-18 and EX-19. Design decision D70, clarified rather than amended, because the clarification says what D70 always ruled and what it never ruled.

**SC-5 of `MPN-NOTE-06-persona-scaling.md` is closed by this decision.**
