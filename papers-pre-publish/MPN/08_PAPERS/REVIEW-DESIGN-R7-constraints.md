## Findings

### 1. The texture channel is budgetable today and section 5a.4 defers it. Source (c). NOT BLOCKING

Section 5a.4 says the budget can be computed for two channels of eight and that the other six wait on the interpolation margin, the rounding rule and the maximum chain length. Texture is the largest of those six, carrying eight of the thirty biases, and it does not wait on any of the three. S3 section 2.4 states both texture ladders in full: density is $0.3H + 0.7\tau$, fragmentation is $\max(0, 0.7H - 0.3\tau)/0.7$, and "both ladders are at even fifths, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8, declared in advance rather than fitted, giving five stages each". That is a stated band width of 0.2 in the derived coordinate, the same kind of statement as the dynamics table 5a.4 does use. Applying 5a.4's own arithmetic gives 0.2 divided by eight, or 0.025 per bias in the derived coordinate, which is computable this afternoon against the same source the section already cites. The reconciliation confirms that the texture entries ride on coordinates that are in S3's ladders rather than on anything unfixed: survivorship rides on fragmentation, information bias and the ostrich effect on entropy and on the Real. The claim that texture is blocked is not a real constraint, and a work item that is wrongly deferred costs the programme the eight largest entries of the layer. Corroborating evidence that the count was never computed: the script the section cites prints "the other five channels have no stated band widths" at line 262 and "the other six wait" at line 265, two adjacent hardcoded strings that disagree, neither derived from the data the script loads.

### 2. MPN-NOTE-05 unblocks one of the six, and three of them have no band width in any cited source. Sources (b) and (c). BLOCKING

The note recommends two of the three numbers, and only one of the two reaches a channel budget. The maximum chain length genuinely unblocks harmony. At $k_{\max} = 23$ with the absolute form, chord position is $\operatorname{round}(23 f(\text{state}))$, so one chain position corresponds to a state change of $1/23 = 0.0435$, seven biases write to the channel, and 5a.4's rule gives 0.0062 per bias in trauma. That figure can be written into D48 now. The rounding rule does the opposite of unblocking. Its recommendation is that $\Phi$ emit the blended degree as a continuous value in cents and that the notated pitch become the rounded pre-image, a property of the notation renderer. D48 defines a device's budget as "the width of the band the parameter is discretised into", and under the recommendation $\Phi$ no longer discretises mode at all, so the quantity D48 measures ceases to exist on the mode channel and the bound becomes a property of whichever renderer is attached, with the audio path carrying the exact value and therefore imposing no bound whatever. Melody and intervals are worse placed still, because the rounding rule never touched them: section 5a.3 sources both from the leitmotif transformation, and S3 section 3 records that the transformation is selected by threshold and rewrites every pitch, with no ladder, no stated jump set and no discretisation anywhere in S3. So of the six, texture was never blocked, harmony is unblocked by $k_{\max} = 23$, and melody with six entries, intervals with one and mode with one have no stated band width in S3, in the note, or in this design, and no work item anywhere names the job of producing one. D48 requires every bias device to carry a budget and item 5 of section 10a is scheduled to build now, so seven of the thirty devices are unbuildable to the design's own rule. This is blocking for item 5. It is worth adding that the note is headed "Estimate for the author's decision. Not a ruling", so even the harmony figure is not yet fixed and revision 7 carries neither recommendation in D48.

### 3. The budget formula hands a full band to every single-bias channel, which section 5a.3 calls assignment. Source (c). NOT BLOCKING

Section 5a.3 states the discipline as "the layer modulates and never assigns. It perturbs the parameter $\Phi$ computed; it does not replace it." Section 5a.4 then derives the per-bias budget by dividing one band step by the number of biases writing to the channel, which is why dynamics gives 0.10 divided by three. Three channels carry exactly one bias each: timbre, mode and intervals. On each of those the formula returns the whole band, and 5a.4's own sentence for why a bound is needed is that "a perturbation wider than a band moves the marking by itself, which is assignment wearing a hat". A perturbation exactly one band wide does the same thing. So on the three single-entry channels the formula licenses precisely what 5a.3 forbids, and the boundary between modulation and assignment collapses. Mode is already handled by D47 and intervals is inside finding 2, so the live case is timbre. The repair is a rule refinement rather than new work, which is why this is not blocking, but D48 as worded cannot be implemented on a one-entry channel.

### 4. Item 5a's claim that the timbre channel depends on nothing else in the table is false on three counts. Source (c). BLOCKING

Section 10a lists item 5a as "Build now. It depends on nothing else in this table." The document states three dependencies elsewhere. First, section 4.4 rules that the DISC profile "is not a Layer 3 output at all: under the scope ruling it is assigned by whoever writes the character, which makes it Layer 2 material, a human's judgement recorded as such". Layer 2's rating store is built in item 2 of the same table, so the channel that is live because a profile is assigned depends on the store that holds the assignment. Second, the discrimination experiment of 5b.5, which item 5a itself names as the thing that unblocks the channel, requires the six canonical timbres to be synthesised and presented in pairs. Item 2 builds "the graph renderer" and nothing else; the score and audio renderers arrive with item 4, which is gated behind item 3. An experiment that needs synthesised audio depends on an audio path the table schedules two items later. Third, item 5a says "the six canonical profiles of section 5b.3 need no further work", while section 5b.4 establishes that "every one of the six canonical profiles sits exactly on a tie" in the shipped family selector, which the run of `s6_timbre_capacity.py` confirms at all of N equals four, five and six, with six of six profiles within 0.05 of a tie at N equals six. A profile whose family label is undefined is not a profile that needs no further work; TC-1 has to land before any of the six can be used, and TC-1 is inside item 5a rather than outside it. The ordering as written tells an implementer to start with the one item that cannot start.

### 5. Nothing in the reference implementation carries an assigned profile to a rendered sound, and three separate things have to change. Sources (b) and (c). BLOCKING

Section 5b says the channel is live because the profile is assigned. In the repository the assignment has nowhere to enter, nothing to compute and nothing to render.

There is no input path. `inferDISC` at `/home/claude/mpn-conductor-standalone/src/lib/play_parser.ts:254` returns `null` by design under decision 9, and its call site is line 222; the comment there records the open task as building an instrument that "either derives DISC from the text under a stated, published protocol, or takes it from the user", and neither exists. The running application does not leave the profile absent, however: `createActorProfile` at `/home/claude/mpn-conductor-standalone/src/app/mpn-conductor/page.tsx:96` substitutes a hardcoded table of fourteen character names keyed on the lowercased speaker string, and at lines 115 to 118 every name not in that table receives the flat profile `{ D: 0.5, I: 0.5, S: 0.5, C: 0.5 }`. That default is exactly the null direction section 5b.2 identifies, so every character the author writes renders at the dead centre of the timbre space, and the fourteen that are in the table are fabricated values in the second file rather than assigned ones. The only user-facing surfaces that name a profile, `TimbreInstrumentPicker.tsx:27` and `ActorInstrumentPicker.tsx:49`, type it as a single letter of `'D' | 'I' | 'S' | 'C' | 'mixed'`, which cannot express the four coordinates the map requires.

There is no map. S3 section 2.6 specifies the orthonormal contrast basis and sends $h_1$ to inverted log attack time, $h_3$ to spectral centroid and $h_2$ to the third dimension. None of that is implemented. A search of the whole `mpn-lab` tree for a Hadamard basis, a spectral centroid or an attack-time term from DISC returns nothing. The `timbre` object is a constant literal at `psychometric_calculus.ts:326` to `334` and its only modulation is the Dark Triad block at lines 336 to 340, so the DISC coordinates never reach a timbral parameter at all.

There is no correct selector. The single consumer of the profile is `discToInstrument` at `psychometric_calculus.ts:204`, called once at line 316, and it returns an instrument name by taking the argmax of the four coordinates. D51 replaces that selector, and finding 4 records that the canonical six all sit on its ties.

The shortest honest statement of what must change before one assigned profile reaches one rendered sound is therefore: the contrast map of S3 section 2.6 has to be written, because it does not exist in the code in any form. The input path and the selector are the other two, and the flat default at `page.tsx:115` has to go, because it is the one point of the space the channel cannot carry.

### 6. Section 6.2 claims the score is synchronous on class B, which sections 4.1 and 7.6 define as live capture. Source (c). BLOCKING

D52 and section 6.2 restore the author's framing as "a running score rendered against the dialogue, synchronous on class A and class B, trailing by seconds on class C", and the justification given is that "generated material is known in full before a single note is rendered" and "nothing has to be recognised in real time". That justification holds for class A and for pre-recorded material. It does not hold for class B as this document defines it. Section 7.6 states that "Class B, a microphone per speaker, has no diarisation step at all and is the first class on which the full measure set is trustworthy live", and section 10a item 6a schedules class B as live capture. Removing the diarisation step does not remove the recognition step: a live multi-track capture still has to be transcribed as it arrives, and section 4.1 cites meeting-benchmark word error running from 35 to 46 per cent across vendors, a figure that is about recognition rather than attribution and therefore survives the move to one channel per speaker. So the same class is synchronous in section 6.2 and a live recognition problem in section 7.6. One of the two has to change. The narrow repair is to scope D52 by whether the material is known in advance rather than by ingest class, which is what section 6.2's own argument actually supports.

### 7. Participant mode is gated on nothing in section 7.4 and on class B in D18, and section 10a builds it before class B exists. Source (c). BLOCKING

Section 7.4 closes with "Participant mode consumes Ψ like every other surface, so it is gated on Ψ existing and on nothing else." D18 says "Multi-party live participant mode requires class B, a microphone per speaker", and section 7.6 keeps D18 standing explicitly, on the ground that it is a measurement rather than a permission. Section 10a then builds "Live, both modes" at item 6, after item 3, and class B live capture at item 6a, after item 6. An implementer following the table builds participant mode one item before the ingest class D18 says it requires. The three statements cannot all be kept. Either D18 is withdrawn, which section 7.6 forbids, or 7.4's gating sentence is wrong, or item 6 is split so that the participant mode arrives with 6a.

### 8. PM-1, the observer-effect experiment, cannot be run on the material section 7.4 says it uses. Source (c). BLOCKING

Section 7.4 calls this "the most interesting thing the restoration makes possible" and specifies it as running "the same generated dialogue through the same engine with and without the display in the loop", with the difference being "the effect with everything else held fixed". The observer effect is a change in what the participants of an exchange do when a display of that exchange is in front of them. The same section states, four paragraphs earlier, that "on generated material there is nobody in the room". A generated dialogue that is fixed in advance cannot be altered by a display, so running it twice returns an identical transcript and a difference of zero by construction, which measures nothing. The alternative reading, that the dialogue is generated turn by turn with the display in the loop, defeats the other half of the specification, because two runs then produce two different dialogues and nothing is held fixed. Both horns remove the experiment. What the arrangement can measure is whether a generator conditioned on the display produces different structure from one that is not, which is a property of the generator and not an observer effect, and it should be described as that or PM-1 should be withdrawn.

### 9. The generator of item 1 is not specified to emit timings, and four of the six Layer 1 measures need them. Source (b). BLOCKING

Item 1 of section 10a is the synthetic dialogue generator and its specification is relational: "who leads, who resists, where the coalition sits, which rhetorical move lands on which turn". Nothing there is a time. Section 4.1 defines class A as "labelled text" and section 7.2 states that a script "yields no latency, no overlap and no floor share in time. Four of six measures are unavailable and floor share degrades to a line or word count." Section 6.1 repeats the consequence, that class A "yields two of six measures, one of them a line count". Item 3 then specifies Ψ and validates it against the generator, and section 10 records that closing the ground-truth gap is "the largest single change the scope ruling makes". On a generator that emits no timings, Ψ's domain is two of its six measures and the validation covers floor share by line count and adjacency, with latency, overlap, reciprocity and floor share in seconds untestable. The design never says whether the generator emits turn onsets and offsets. It is one sentence in item 1 and it decides whether item 3 is worth running, so it belongs in item 1 rather than being discovered there. The related unfixed quantity is the timing model the generator would use, since section 4.2 records that gap length and overlap tolerance vary by speech community and that a fixed threshold scores some of them as more interruptive; a generator that invents timings is choosing that distribution.

Section 6.2 requires three things of the live surface and they are not in the same state. The coverage figure of LS-2 is specified: it is computed per work, the corpus run gives the table, and the requirement is that it appear on the face of the output. It is named twice, at section 6.2 and at D54, with no value, no range, no unit, no method for choosing one and no experiment that would fix it, and it appears in neither of section 10's two inventories: not in "the numeric parameters this design leaves unfixed", which lists the parse-confidence threshold, the resolution floor, the staleness horizon, the five Layer 1 defaults, the retention period and the below-floor trigger; and not in "three things the restored capabilities need and do not have", which lists the timbre resolution, the fourteen bias coordinates and B4. Section 10's stated purpose is that the gaps be named so reviewers attack them rather than discover them, and this one is undeclared. It is not blocking because a surface can be built with the constant exposed as a setting, which is what D54 asks for, but it has to join the list.

It is not beside the script; `/home/claude/gen6` holds three Python files and a sections directory. The run succeeded only because line 36 of the resolver carries the literal absolute path `/home/claude/s3_bias_reconciliation.json`, which is a property of this machine rather than of the repository, and the error message the script would otherwise print states the real dependency plainly: the JSON "is produced by `s3_bias_reconcile.py`, which needs the reference implementation checked out". So the fourteen mappings naming no coordinate, the channel occupancy table, the four collisions and the twenty-two by eight partition all rest on an artefact that the corpus does not contain and cannot regenerate. Separately, references [9], [10] and [11] all cite `05_DATA/03_generators/`, and that directory holds one file, `a8_form_analysis.py`. A figure that cannot be re-derived from the repository is a figure an implementer has to take on trust, which is the failure S4 records and section 4.4 cites as the reason for the Layer 3 marking rule.

## Disposition

**REVISE.** Eight blocking findings.

Nothing here says the design cannot be built. Every blocking finding is a dependency the document states in one place and contradicts in another, or a number that something scheduled to build now requires and that no section supplies. Four of the eight sit on the two restorations the revision is built around: the bias layer cannot meet D48 on seven of its thirty devices (finding 2), and the timbre channel is ordered first while depending on three things later in the same table (finding 4) and on a map that does not exist in the reference implementation in any form (finding 5). Two more sit on the live surface, where a class is synchronous in one section and a live recognition problem in another (finding 6) and participant mode is gated on nothing and on class B in the same document (finding 7). One sits at the head of the implementation order, where the generator every downstream item validates against is not specified to emit the timings four of the six measures need (finding 9). One sits under the figures themselves (finding 12).

The two findings that reduce work rather than adding it are 1 and the harmony half of 2: the texture channel's budget is computable against S3 section 2.4 today, and the harmonic channel's follows immediately from $k_{\max} = 23$ if the author takes the note's recommendation. Taking those two together, five of the eight unbudgeted entries of the layer close at once, and the residue is melody, intervals and mode, which need a band width nobody has yet been asked to supply.
