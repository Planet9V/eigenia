# Design: three application papers, and a publication rule

## Understanding Lock, confirmed by the author 16 September 2026

**What is being built.** Three new papers, S7, S8 and S9. Each is a standalone application paper: a reader picks one up without having read S5 and gets what they came for. Each is more detailed and more usefulness-oriented than S5's four sketches, and each develops the McKenney-Lacan theory and the MPN notation further rather than only applying them.

| | Paper | For |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **S7** | The music therapy helper | Music therapists |
| **S8** | Autism engagement | Practitioners and autistic readers |
| **S9** | The team score, and the machine in the room | Teams, their audiences, and alignment researchers |

**S9 has two halves, both confirmed.** Part one is the near-realtime group score: the dialogue, the biases and the meters rendered on a screen as the group is talking, for the group itself and for an audience watching. Part two turns the same instrument on a group that includes an AI, and argues what that buys research into how a machine stands in a social bond.

**A publication rule now governs every public paper.** A publication states what the theory does and proposes. It does not state what the system does not do or does not contain. Capability absences go: stubs, not implemented, does not exist, has not been built, withheld, unspecified, dead, decorative. A forward-looking Open Questions or Further Work section stays, because that is what invites the review the programme exists to get.

**S4 is withdrawn from publication** and lives as an internal record. Its subject was capability absence end to end.

**Standing constraints.** Theory, internal private use, synthetic material, no people, no regulation. No word cap and no length target; truncation is a stated failure of this programme. Zero em dashes. House style: title, byline, Executive Abstract, Abstract, numbered introduction, numbered body, references.

## The design

### 1. What makes these different from S5

S5 gives four use cases about four pages each: the user, the question, the surface, the ingest class, what arrives, what the output is. That is a survey. These three are monographs. Each one carries:

- **A worked session end to end.** Not a description of a surface but a transcript going in and a score coming out, with the reader shown what each parameter did and why.
- **A practitioner's vocabulary.** A music therapist reads Bruscia; the paper meets them there. An alignment researcher reads about social bonds; the paper meets them there.
- **A theory contribution of its own.** Each paper extends the calculus rather than consuming it. S7 proposes the mapping between the Improvisation Assessment Profiles and the state vector. S8 proposes what an engagement surface owes a reader who is the subject rather than the observer. S9 proposes how a non-human participant occupies a position in the notation.
- **Its own evidence.** Each runs the generators on material relevant to its case and reports figures nobody in the programme has computed before.

### 2. S7, the music therapy helper

**Built on** the 26,647 words of therapy research already in the corpus: the music therapy evidence review, the psychoanalysis and autism history, the autism music cognition review, and the tools review. Plus the PRD's quantity model and Bruscia's Improvisation Assessment Profiles, six profiles at five gradients each.

**The theory contribution.** The Autonomy profile runs Dependent, Follower, Partner, Leader, Resister. That is an ordered five-point gradient over a relation between two players. The interaction mapping produces exactly that kind of object from Layer 1. The paper proposes the correspondence: which Layer 1 measures carry which IAP gradient, which gradients the notation can already express, and what the remaining profiles would need. **This is new work and it is the reason a therapist would read the paper.**

**The worked session.** A clinical improvisation rendered as a score, showing the Autonomy gradient moving across the session.

**The usefulness claim.** A therapist writes session notes from memory. The instrument gives them a second record that is not memory, in a medium they already read.

### 3. S8, autism engagement

**Built on** the autism music cognition review and the expression surface of S6, which it supersedes in detail for this reader.

**The theory contribution.** S6 renders the shape of an exchange for a reader who finds sound or image easier than inference. S8 asks the harder question: what does an **engagement** surface owe a reader who is a participant rather than an observer? It proposes the participant's stave, what it renders live, and how a reader acts on it during the exchange rather than after.

**The worked session.** A two-party exchange with the participant's own stave beside the other's, and what a reader does with it in the moment.

**The usefulness claim.** The shape of a conversation becomes readable while it is happening, in a medium the reader prefers.

### 4. S9, the team score and the machine in the room

**Part one, the group and the audience.** The near-realtime score: dialogue, biases and meters on a screen while the group talks. Two audiences with different needs, the participants and the watchers, and the paper specifies both. Draws on the Cognitive Bias Atlas and the bias catalogue in the corpus.

**Part two, the machine in the room.** The same instrument on a group that includes an AI. The theory contribution is the sharpest of the three: the McKenney-Lacan calculus is built on Lacanian registers and the four discourses, which are positions in a social bond rather than properties of a mind. **A machine can occupy a position in a bond without anyone claiming it has a mind.** The paper argues that this is precisely what alignment research lacks: a notation for what a machine is doing in a relation, rather than a metaphor for it. The corpus already applies the four discourses to threat actors in WG-03; this extends the same move.

**The usefulness claim.** A team sees its own dynamics as they happen. An alignment researcher gets a notation for a relation.

### 5. Sequencing

1. The no-negatives pass on the five published papers. It is small, it is mechanical against a word list, and it sets the rule the three new papers are written under.
2. S7, S8, S9, written in parallel by three agents, each with its own source set.
3. One consistency pass across all eight papers.
4. Gate: Skeptic, Constraint Guardian, User Advocate, Arbiter.
5. Publish and register.
