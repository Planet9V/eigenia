## 1. Purpose and method

This report records, for the author, everything that differs between the two surviving editions of Paper 4 and everything that does not, so that the pair can be maintained as one design with two profiles rather than as two unrelated papers.

The two documents are for different things, and the difference in purpose explains almost every difference in text. The field edition deploys the system into a working European control room under the law of that jurisdiction: the plant is real, the alarms are annunciated to people who must act on them, the consoles are staffed by employees, and the paper's central work is to say what may enter the signal chain, who may hear it, what may be logged and what consent and change control must be in place first. The research edition runs the same system at full capability as an instrument in a closed development environment on synthetic data: the plant is a generated twin, the operators in the datasets are generated, the threat actors are written, the only real people are volunteers in a simulator mock-up, and the paper's central work is to say how a run is made reproducible, how a dataset is versioned, how an experiment is registered and what a claim leaving the environment must name. The field edition spends its discipline on gating. The research edition spends its discipline on provenance, determinism, logging and pre-registration. The research edition states this itself at MX 19.

On section numbering, the position is this. Both editions carry eleven top-level headings, ten body sections and a reference list at section 11, and the numbers therefore line up one to one. What no longer lines up is the subject matter behind three of those numbers. Section 2 is the legal frame in the field edition (EU 25 to 97) and the research environment in the research edition (MX 50 to 133). Section 5 is the validation stack in the field edition (EU 188 to 239) and the experimental programme in the research edition (MX 232 to 307). Section 7 is outcome and the underwriting link in the field edition (EU 253 to 265) and outcome alone in the research edition (MX 325 to 337). The research edition also carries more subsections at section 5, six studies (MX 236 to 307) against the field edition's four subsections (EU 190 to 239), and it carries a section 1.2 (MX 46 to 48) where the field edition carries a section 1.1 only (EU 21 to 23). The alignment used below is therefore by slot, each numbered section of one edition against the section of the same number in the other, from section 1 through section 10, with a separate subsection for each section whose subject matter has no counterpart at all in the other edition. Section 3 of this report ends with those.

Nothing is summarised away. Where a clause is operative it is quoted rather than paraphrased, and where a list is long it is given in full. No legal research has been done for this report and no legal analysis of the working group's own is added: where the field edition's content is legal, this report records what it contains and records that the research edition has no counterpart because it is a closed environment on synthetic data.

A previous edition of this report compared the field edition against a Mexican field edition of Paper 4. That Mexican field edition has been superseded by the closed research environment edition read here, which carries a different designation (MX 3, "MPN-4-MX-R, the closed research environment edition"), a different requirement set (DM-1 to DM-23 in place of the superseded edition's longer list), a different reference list (87 in place of 79) and no legal content at all. The previous report is replaced by this one in its entirety, and its legal-instrument inventory, which compared Mexican statutes and norms against European ones, has no subject matter in the current pair and is not carried forward.

## 2. What does not change

The jurisdiction-independent core is carried by both editions, in most cases in the same words. Each item below names the carrying invariant or requirement in each edition and the lines that carry it.

**The scientific position on what physiology indexes.** The field edition states it at EU 17: "Physiology indexes arousal and load, not emotion [15]", with the same reference used at EU 41 to say that an inference admitted under the safety exception "would deliver a signal that, on the review the guidelines point to, measures arousal and load rather than the emotion it would be labelled with". D-2 (EU 104) turns it into an obligation. The research edition states it at MX 17, "Physiology indexes arousal and cognitive load; inferring a discrete emotion from a face is not established, and the working group extends that finding to the voice [6]", carries it into DM-18 (MX 156), "no such label MUST be described as a measurement of an emotion, since inferring a discrete emotion from a face or a voice is not established [6]", restates it at MX 224 and MX 226, and closes on it at MX 389. Same reference in both: Barrett and colleagues, EU [15] and MX [6].

**The position on affective inference from the voice.** Both editions treat the extension of the facial-movement finding to the voice as the working group's own. The field edition does so through the design fact that no voice channel is admitted at all (D-1, EU 103). The research edition does so explicitly at MX 226, where speech-based stress recognition is described as trained on acted or elicited corpora with accuracies that fall on spontaneous, cross-corpus and noisy data, and the ledger for that edition records at `CITATION-LEDGER-MPN-4-MX.md` line 108 that the corpus-specificity attribution is the working group's own reading and not a cited review.

**The evidence tiers of the musical mappings.** The field edition carries the tier discipline through its acceptance criteria: EU 194 says the listener study "is preceded by magnitude estimation of each mapping's polarity and scaling with the target population, since polarity is an empirical property of the data concept and the listeners rather than a convention", and EU 205 makes the keep-or-reverse rule an acceptance criterion. The research edition carries it as DM-17 (MX 155): "Every musical mapping in use MUST carry its Paper 2 evidence tier in the run configuration and in any report of the experiment, so that a result obtained under a CONVENTION-tier mapping is not read as a result about a STRONG-tier one." It applies the tiers by name at MX 202 (tempo, STRONG), MX 210 (the adversary voice-leading styles, CONVENTION), and MX 228 (mode CONVENTIONAL, irregular metre THEORY, roughness COMPONENT). Neither edition claims a mapping is validated.

**The honesty about the reference implementation.** The field edition says at EU 23 that it "does not describe the reference implementation as deployable; Paper 3 lists what must change before it can be cited at all", says at EU 172 that "no such scoring exists in the reference implementation [5]", and makes M1 the conformance and remediation milestone at EU 298, "of which the hard-coded credential, the fabricated identifier and the missing weights come first". The research edition says at MX 17 that the Conductor "is a rule-based sonification prototype whose machine-learning scaffolding is untrained: its first projector was fitted to random targets, its second to ten error strings, and its own reviewer file records zero empirical validation", says at MX 48 that it "does not describe the reference implementation as ready for the work", identifies the two-calculi defect as a confound at MX 86, and makes M1 the same remediation milestone at MX 379. Both cite the same paper for it, EU [5] and MX [5].

**The Milford Haven rationale and the flood behaviour.** The field edition gives the case at EU 267 to 271 with the 275 alarms in eleven minutes, the 26 injured, the damage of about £48 million, the four case-study causes and the two benchmark endpoints, and derives three things from it: the flood rule, the overview finding and management of change. D-14 (EU 116) requires the flood behaviour of N-22 and E-8 to be implemented and the annunciated alarm rate not to rise. The research edition gives the same case at MX 341 with the same figures in words, derives two things from it, the flood rationale at MX 343 and the requirement that the environment reproduce floods at MX 345, and carries DM-21 (MX 159): "The plant twin and the alarm generator MUST be able to produce alarm floods at declared rates and durations, and the engine's flood machine MUST be exercised in every conformance run, because the flood behaviour is the one part of the design with a documented industrial basis." Both editions call it the one rule with a documented industrial basis; the field edition at EU 271 and the research edition at MX 343.

**The validation instruments.** SAGAT: EU 192 and EU 202 against MX 98, MX 238 and MX 246, on Endsley 1995 and Endsley 1988, EU [39] [40] and MX [22] [23]. NASA-TLX: EU 192 and EU 203 against MX 98, MX 238 and MX 246, on Hart and Staveland, EU [41] and MX [24]. The anaesthesia two-stream evidence: EU 194 gives the Loeb and Fitch figures in full, fourteen residents, six variables in two streams, 21 trials, every subject detecting every scripted event, 10.4 seconds combined against 12.8 visual and 13.0 auditory, identification lowest with sound alone at 60 per cent against 88 and 80, with Watson and Sanderson on eyes-free monitoring; the research edition cites the same two studies at MX 98, MX 204 and MX 238, EU [6] [7] and MX [25] [56], and carries the one figure, sixty per cent identification from sound alone, at MX 204. ISO 11064-7: EU 192 as the evaluation principles for control centres, read with ISO 11064-1 and ISO 9241-210, against MX 246, the same three standards, EU [36] [37] [38] and MX [58] [59] [60]. Multiple-resource theory as the prediction under test rather than as evidence of benefit: EU 192 against MX 246, EU [42] and MX [57].

**The absence of any measured lead time or accuracy.** The field edition: D-17 (EU 119) forbids describing an early-warning observable as a lead time until the data requirements of Paper 1 section 5.6 are met and reported; D-20 (EU 122) forbids describing a deployment as delivering a measured lead time, a measured reduction in incident or alarm rate beyond what D-14 records, or an insurance premium delta; EU 251 records that the earlier fifteen to thirty minutes of warning was retracted and that the repository's reviewer file records zero empirical validation; EU 302 records that no milestone licenses a lead time. The research edition: DM-20 (MX 158) requires that "A lead time, a detection accuracy or a classification performance figure MUST be reported only as the outcome of a registered experiment whose analysis plan specified it, and a lead-time claim MUST additionally satisfy the data requirements of Paper 1 section 5.6 in full and report them (F-8)"; MX 17 records that no lead time and no classification accuracy have been measured anywhere in the programme; MX 323 records that none will be stated except as the reported outcome of a registered experiment meeting all five requirements; MX 383 records that no milestone licenses one. Both editions also decline the premium delta on the same ground: EU 261 and MX 383, both citing the ALE/ROSI framework, EU [53] and MX [87], as a sensitivity result rather than a measurement, and both recording that the earlier insider-monitoring claim was retracted in Paper 1.

**The retractions and the state-trait discipline.** Both editions rest on Paper 1's corrections. The field edition refers to them at EU 251 and EU 261; the research edition at MX 17, MX 297 and MX 329. Both decline to read a trait score as a state: the field edition by admitting no trait channel at all, the research edition explicitly at MX 76, "a trait score is not a state", with the state-trait distinction cited at MX [17] and within-person variability at MX [18].

**The series binding.** Both editions state that F-1 to F-14, N-1 to N-27 and E-1 to E-24 bind them: EU 13 without qualification, MX 13 with the qualification "except where profile MX-R relaxes them". Both carry the same RFC 2119 line and the same CC BY 4.0 licence line, EU 13 and MX 13, on the same two references, EU [1] [2] and MX [1] [2]. Both name the same reference implementation in the metadata table, EU 9 and MX 6, with the same words "public repository; audited in MPN-3, not executed".

## 3. Section-by-section delta

Each subsection below takes one section of the field edition, states what that edition says, states what the research edition says or where the corresponding material now lives, and gives the difference with its reason. Reasons are classified as **purpose** (the two documents are for different things), **scientific** (the change follows from what is or is not established) or **editorial** (wording, placement or length).

### 3.1 Section 1, Executive Summary and Scope

**Field edition (EU 11 to 23).** The section says the paper closes the series and states how an MPN system is put into a European industrial control room lawfully and safely, how it is validated, what it can be said to deliver once validated, and where the person-level psychological layer lives "so that it never touches a member of staff" (EU 13). It binds F-1 to F-14, N-1 to N-27 and E-1 to E-24 and numbers its own invariants D-1 through D-20 (EU 13). It states the outcome claimed as narrow (EU 15): a regulated auditory channel carrying the plant's distance from nominal, the alarm system's load and its bunching, the tempo of work at the console and, in incident conditions, an analyst's coded discourse estimate for an adversary; the benefit is hypothesised, not claimed, and the evidence for the hypothesis is transferred from anaesthesia; "No lead time is claimed, no reduction in incident rate, no insurance premium delta, and no result from an industrial control room, because none exists". It states the legal frame in summary (EU 17) with Article 5(1)(f), the Commission's wide reading of emotions, physiological data as health data on Recital 35 and Dutch practice, and works-council consent, and derives the design position: MPN sonifies plant state, task load, interaction tempo and adversary discourse, never an inferred emotion, mood, stress or arousal of an operator; no biometric of staff enters the deployed signal chain; person-level modelling is confined to adversary modelling from threat intelligence, to simulation and training including the crisis exercise, and to one advisory crew-level fatigue annotation on the SAFTE-FAST pattern. It recommends a change of subtitle (EU 19), from "operator state made audible" to "the state of the socio-technical link made audible", because operator state is what the series declines to infer. Section 1.1 (EU 21 to 23) says the paper gives no legal advice for a particular site, reports no validation result, and does not describe the reference implementation as deployable.

**Research edition (MX 11 to 48).** The section says this is the research edition, contrasts it with the field edition in one sentence each, and describes "a closed development environment in which the whole of the MPN programme runs at full capability on synthetic data, so that the measurements the first three papers could not report can be taken" (MX 13). It binds the same three requirement sets "except where profile MX-R relaxes them" and numbers its own DM-1 through DM-23, calling them "research-protocol requirements, not deployment invariants" (MX 13). One paragraph stands in the place of the field edition's legal summary (MX 15): "The programme described here is a private research project run in a closed development environment on synthetic plant data and synthetic operator profiles; the operator complies with all applicable Mexican legal requirements, including consent, and no further legal analysis is in scope for this paper." It states what the environment is for (MX 17) by listing the five measurements the series has never taken: the unvalidated psychometric-to-music mapping and the unwarranted DISC-by-Big-Five tensor product; the evidence tiers running from tempo to the adversary voice-leading styles; physiology indexing arousal and load; no lead time and no classification accuracy anywhere; and the untrained scaffolding of the Conductor. It states the design principle (MX 19): nothing connected to an operating plant, nothing drawn from a production alarm system, every person in its datasets generated, and the discipline spent on provenance, determinism, logging and pre-registration rather than on gating. Section 1.1 (MX 21 to 44) defines profile MX-R by the sixteen requirements it relaxes, in a table, and names the two a reader might expect and will not find, N-27 and E-4. Section 1.2 (MX 46 to 48) says the paper reports no result, does not describe the reference implementation as ready, and does not claim the environment will produce a positive result, adding that a calibration experiment finding no agreement "is a finding, and the roadmap of section 9 is written so that such a finding closes a branch rather than being absorbed".

**Difference and reason.** The scope statement changes from deployment to instrument, which is **purpose**. The legal summary of EU 17, eight sentences of quoted and cited law, is replaced by the single sentence of MX 15, which asserts compliance and declares legal analysis out of scope; the research edition has no counterpart to the field edition's legal content because it is a closed environment on synthetic data. The invariant list changes name and character, from deployment invariants binding a site to research-protocol requirements binding a run, which is **purpose**. The subtitle recommendation of EU 19 has no counterpart: the research edition does not carry the programme's naming question at all, which is **editorial**. The "what this paper does not do" subsection is carried in substance and extended by the sentence about a negative calibration result closing a branch, which is **scientific**. The relaxation table of MX 21 to 44 has no counterpart in the field edition, where nothing is relaxed; it is treated in full in section 5 of this report.

### 3.2 Section 2, The legal frame

**Field edition (EU 25 to 97).** Seven subsections and a decision diagram. EU 27 states the method: the primary texts are quoted as verified and where a reading is the working group's own the text says so. Section 2.1 (EU 29 to 33) quotes Article 5(1) and point (f), Article 3(39) on emotion recognition systems, Article 3(34) on biometric data, Recital 18 on the boundary with physical states including the pilot and driver fatigue example, Recital 44 on the scientific basis, the limited reliability, the lack of specificity, the limited generalisability and the imbalance of power, and Article 113 on the 2 February 2025 application date, with Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 leaving point (f) unaltered. Section 2.2 (EU 35 to 41) gives the Commission's reading in five numbered points and then, in a separated paragraph, the working group's own reasoning on why the safety exception does not open a usable door; it quotes "understood in a wide sense and not interpreted restrictively", the anti-circumvention sentence on attitudes, the education example of emotional arousal and anxiousness, the hybrid-work-teams example, the pilot and driver fatigue sentence, "Concluding that a person is happy is emotion recognition", "should be narrowly interpreted", "The general monitoring of stress levels at the workplace is not permitted under health or safety aspects", the burnout and depression sentence, "limited to what is strictly necessary and proportionate, including limits in time, personal application and scale", the dangerous machines and chemicals example, the purpose-limitation sentence, and the wearables sentence "may increase work-stress while affecting productivity". Section 2.3 (EU 43 to 47) quotes Annex III points 1(c), 2 and 4(b) and Article 26(7), records the deferral of Sections 1, 2 and 3 of Chapter III to 2 December 2027 and of Annex I systems to 2 August 2028 by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, and answers each Annex entry by intended purpose. Section 2.4 (EU 49 to 51) quotes the AI-system definition guidelines on rules defined solely by natural persons, on basic data processing and on descriptive analysis and visualisation, and records three caveats. Section 2.6 (EU 61 to 65) quotes Article 27(1)(l) of the Works Councils Act in Dutch, paraphrases (k), (3), (4) and (5), and sets out the impact-assessment duty on Article 35(1) read with 35(4), the Dutch supervisor's list item 11 "Controle werknemers" and the Article 29 Working Party's criteria, with EU-OSHA and ISO 45003 as occupational-safety context. Section 2.7 (EU 67 to 97) is the admissibility gate, a decision tree applied to every candidate input before the engine's schema boundary, with six decision nodes and seven terminal nodes.

**Research edition.** There is no counterpart. The research edition's section 2 is the research environment (MX 50 to 133), treated at section 3.11 of this report. The whole of the field edition's legal content, the prohibition and its definitions, the Commission's readings, the Annex III entries and the worker-information duty, the AI-system definition question, the GDPR and the occupational-medicine route, the works council and the impact assessment, and the admissibility gate, has no counterpart in the research edition because that edition is a closed environment on synthetic data: nothing in its datasets is a natural person, so there is no data subject, no worker, no works council and no candidate input to gate. The research edition states its own position on the point once, at MX 15, and declares further legal analysis out of scope. Its ledger records the consequence at `CITATION-LEDGER-MPN-4-MX.md` lines 99 to 101: every Mexican legal source of the previous edition and every European legal source of the field edition is dropped.

**Difference and reason.** Whole-section removal, for **purpose**. This report does not evaluate the removal and adds no legal analysis of its own; it records that the field edition contains the material listed above and that the research edition has no counterpart to it.

### 3.3 Section 3, Design invariants

**Field edition (EU 99 to 124).** Twenty invariants, D-1 to D-20, introduced at EU 101 as restating, "as obligations on a deployment, what the three earlier papers established as properties of the foundations, the notation and the engine", binding "every MPN deployment in every mode". Two are given a paragraph of motivation at EU 124: D-2, because the anti-circumvention paragraph reaches labels and a rolling autocorrelation of acknowledge latency annotated "operator approaching overload" becomes a statement about a person; and D-5 with the single-operator clauses of D-3 and D-6, because aggregation does not anonymise a group of one.

**Research edition (MX 135 to 161).** Twenty-three requirements, DM-1 to DM-23, introduced at MX 137: "The following requirements, DM-1 through DM-23, are normative for the closed research environment. They replace the field edition's deployment invariants entirely; nothing in this list is carried from it." There is no motivation paragraph.

**Difference and reason.** The two lists are disjoint in intent: the field list binds a site and its consent, change control and logging posture; the research list binds a run and its manifest, seed, registration and retention. Ten of the twenty D-numbers have a DM counterpart, in every case modified and in four cases inverted; ten have none. Section 4 of this report maps every row of both lists. The reason is **purpose** throughout, with one **scientific** element: DM-17, DM-18 and DM-19 add obligations the field edition has no need of, because the field edition never renders an affective label or an operator-state index and the research edition does both under test.

### 3.4 Section 4, Modes

**Field edition (EU 126 to 186).** Three lawful modes, with a source diagram at EU 130 to 156 and four subsections. Section 4.1, operational mode (EU 158 to 164): inputs are the channels of Paper 2 section 2 marked admissible in operational mode and nothing else; the engine makes four indices and from them tempo, mode, harmonic distance, metre, roughness and dynamics on at most three streams, plant at the centre, alarm rate on the left rendering density, interaction on the right; what the room hears in normal operation, in an upset, in a declared incident and in a flood is described; the paragraph at EU 164 states the limitation that an audible plant stream is telemetry too and that MPN is no defence against forged process variables, offering juxtaposition rather than resolution. Section 4.2, adversary mode (EU 166 to 172): a fourth stream carrying a probability vector over five discourse labels coded by a threat-intelligence analyst from attribution, tradecraft and campaign context, never a member of staff; the master's-discourse hypothesis is voiced, not detected; the standing of the mode is stated with the annotation-and-reliability study as the only empirical precedent. Section 4.3, simulation and training mode (EU 174 to 180): hand-authored vectors in the nine-component form, translated by the declared rule of Paper 2 section 8.2, three intended uses, tabletop, crisis game and debriefing, and the lawfulness resting on the design fact that the vectors are authored and the crew synthetic. Section 4.4, the fatigue annotation (EU 182 to 186): the SAFTE precedent in full with its inputs, output and field-validation figures, the Australian regulator's four cautions, the United States rail rule and Recital 18, then the narrowing to a crew-level text annotation computed from schedule-predicted sleep opportunity.

**Research edition (MX 163 to 230).** Four modes at full capability, with a source diagram at MX 167 to 196 and four subsections. Section 4.1, operational mode (MX 198 to 206): the same computation, the same three streams with their declared timbre family, register, figure and pan, the same liveness figure, with two additions, that the stream budget is a variable under test under DM-7 and that "Who hears it is unrestricted" (MX 206). Section 4.2, adversary mode (MX 208 to 212): the same four voice-leading styles plus the capitalist label, with the discourse written by the scenario author before the run rather than coded by an analyst, the CONVENTION tier printed under DM-17, and the rendering unrestricted. Section 4.3, simulation and training mode (MX 214 to 218): the same nine-component translation of Paper 2 section 8.2, the same three uses, with the addition that the story is written down before the rendering so that whether a listener recovers it is a question with an answer. Section 4.4, operator-state mode (MX 220 to 230): a fourth mode with no counterpart in the field edition, taking participant physiology and behaviour as input and rendering it, in three kinds of derived quantity, arousal and load indices, voice, speech, keystroke and interaction features, and the affective labels the notation itself defines, with rendering unrestricted and everything logged per participant.

**Difference and reason.** Three modes become four, which is **purpose**: the fourth is the object of study rather than a deployment capability, and the research edition says so at MX 224, "Rendering such an index to the person it came from is an extension beyond published practice, which is why it is an experiment here and not a product". The fatigue annotation of EU 182 to 186 loses its own subsection and its precedent apparatus: the research edition carries the fatigue channel as a generated channel under DM-8 and sonifiable under the N-25 relaxation, and its ledger records at line 106 that no advisory-model precedent is relied on and that none of the SAFTE-FAST sources is cited. The telemetry-severance limitation of EU 164 has no counterpart, which is **purpose**: there is no real plant to forge. The analyst's coding of EU 168 becomes the scenario author's written assignment at MX 80, which is **purpose** and is what makes the recovery study of MX 301 to 307 possible. Section 6 of this report gives the full mode-by-mode comparison.

### 3.5 Section 5, Validation and experiment

**Field edition (EU 188 to 239).** Four subsections. Section 5.1, the instruments (EU 190 to 194): ISO 11064-7, ISO 11064-1 and ISO 9241-210; Endsley's three-level model and SAGAT; NASA-TLX; multiple-resource theory as the prediction under test; the two-stream detection task of the anaesthesia literature with the Loeb and Fitch figures and the Watson and Sanderson result; the MPN version with magnitude estimation first; and the statement that physiological workload measures are not used at any stage because they would need the Article 9 route for every participant and the acceptance criteria do not need them. Section 5.2, acceptance criteria (EU 196 to 205): six criteria, each naming its instrument. Section 5.3, staged rollout (EU 207 to 235): four stages with a gate, a diagram and a paragraph of commentary. Section 5.4, what the evidence base is (EU 237 to 239): no controlled study of continuous sonification of plant state in an industrial control room, no standard covering continuous non-alarm sonification, every performance claim a transfer, the pulse oximeter as the one field-proven continuous sonification.

**Research edition (MX 232 to 307).** Six studies, introduced at MX 234: "Each is stated with its design, its measures, its sample, its analysis and, in one sentence, what it would allow the working group to claim. None has been run, and under DM-14 none may start before its registration is filed." Section 5.1 is the listener studies of the mappings; 5.2 situation awareness and workload in the simulator; 5.3 early-warning-signal analysis; 5.4 training a state-conditioned renderer on synthetic vectors, with a comparison diagram; 5.5 calibrating the psychometric-to-music mapping; 5.6 an adversary-discourse rendering study.

**Difference and reason.** Acceptance criteria for a deployment become claims a study would license, which is **purpose**. Three of the six studies have no counterpart in the field edition at all: 5.4 the renderer training, 5.5 the psychometric calibration and 5.6 the adversary-discourse recovery, all three being measurements rather than acceptance gates. The field edition's staged rollout has no counterpart because there is nothing to roll out. The field edition's statement at EU 194 that physiological workload measures are not used at any stage is reversed: the research edition uses them as inputs and as objects of study, which is **purpose**, and adds DM-19 to carry their confounds, which is **scientific**. Section 7 of this report gives both in full.

### 3.6 Section 6, Early-warning signals

**Field edition (EU 241 to 251).** The generic indicators with their authors' caveats, the estimation method, the tie to the fold lines of the cusp, and the application to human mood in two cohorts and a single case. The two formulas for lag-one autocorrelation and variance of the detrended residuals, with the window, the detrending method and the surrogate test declared in the header. The observables are computed on link metrics and nothing else; they are margin annotations under N-19, not sonified, not in the tension index, and by D-17 never computed on a physiological signal. Team physiological synchrony is heterogeneous and inconsistent; sonified EEG yielded only gross events after training. The annotations exist so that the data requirements of Paper 1 section 5.6 items (a) to (d) can one day be met from archived frame tables; until then the annotation is a number in a margin, never a lead time.

**Research edition (MX 309 to 323).** The same indicators, the same estimation method, the same tie to the cusp and the Kramers rate, and the same two formulas with the same header declarations. What changes is the set of channels: "The field edition restricts the annotation to link metrics such as plant deviation and acknowledge latency; here the full operator-state set is admissible, so the analysis may run on an arousal index, a load index, a pupil series, a keystroke-interval series or a speech feature, alongside the link metrics and the synthetic plant state whose transitions the generator knows" (MX 317). Two hard cases are named, the pupil series inheriting every luminance change and the heart-rate variability series inheriting respiration, posture and movement. The analysis plan is Paper 1 section 5.6 adopted whole, with all five requirements listed and the first annotated as feasible on synthetic series and infeasible from participants without sessions longer than any contemplated, "which is itself a finding about feasibility". The surrogate-data step gets its own paragraph at MX 321: a generator that produces a regime change also produces drift and a changing noise level, so the test must be run on the synthetic series first, and "An indicator that fires on a synthetic series whose generator contains no fold is an indicator the environment has just falsified, and that is a result worth having". The sonified-EEG caution is carried at MX 323 with the recommendation to design the training condition into the study.

**Difference and reason.** The channel restriction of D-17 is lifted and the rendering prohibition of N-19 is relaxed, which is **purpose**. The field edition's items (a) to (d) become all five requirements of Paper 1 section 5.6 with the fifth, the Kramers comparison, named, which is **editorial** and **scientific**: the environment can fit a potential where a deployment cannot. The surrogate-falsification paragraph and the feasibility finding have no counterpart in the field edition and are **scientific** additions. The team-physiological-synchrony review cited at EU 249 is not cited in the research edition; its ledger does not list it.

### 3.7 Section 7, Outcome

**Field edition (EU 253 to 265).** Three subsections. Section 7.1 (EU 255 to 257) lists what an auditor or an underwriter can be shown: the works-council consent and its regulation; the impact assessment; the classification reasoning and worker-information record of D-10; the alarm-philosophy entry, its annexed configuration and the management-of-change records; the security-programme coverage of D-12; the alarm-rate metrics before and after commissioning showing no rise; the ISO 7731 verification; the listener-study and simulator-trial results with SAGAT probe-level breakdown, TLX and detection figures against criteria stated in advance; the staged rollout record; and the identity-free frame-table archive with its retention. "Every one of those is evidence about the plant, the alarm system, the process and the display. None is evidence about a person." Section 7.2 (EU 259 to 261) declines to attach a premium delta and says why. Section 7.3 (EU 263 to 265) connects to the 20-second-deficit paper and to the Lacan paper, with the Imaginary channel read as the display.

**Research edition (MX 325 to 337).** Four products, "and none of them is a product". The first is datasets, versioned, manifested, schema-carrying, re-analysable by someone who did not run them. The second is a calibrated psychometric-to-music mapping or the finding that no such mapping carries information, with the three outcomes of section 5.5 and a public commitment to accepting any of them. The third is a trained renderer or the finding that the fixed renderer is not beaten, with the honest expectation stated. The fourth is the evidence a later field edition would carry, listed item by item: a listener-study result per mapping with its polarity criterion, a probe-level situation-awareness result and a workload result from a simulator, a flood-behaviour conformance record, a renderer comparison with confidence intervals, and a statement about early-warning indicators on synthetic series. MX 337 states the boundary: "What the environment cannot produce is a claim about a real control room."

**Difference and reason.** Artefacts for an auditor become datasets and findings for a later paper, which is **purpose**. The underwriting subsection of EU 259 to 261 has no subsection counterpart; the research edition carries the same refusal in one sentence at MX 383 with the same two references. The connection to the operator-decision papers at EU 263 to 265 has no counterpart at all: the research edition cites neither the 20-second-deficit paper nor the Lacan in the control room paper, which is **purpose**, since both are about a real control room. The research edition's fourth product is the explicit handover to a later field edition and is new.

### 3.8 Section 8, The Milford Haven lesson

**Field edition (EU 267 to 271).** The case with the date, 26 people injured, damage of about £48 million, the quoted sentence on 275 alarms in eleven minutes, the four case-study causes quoted or paraphrased, and the benchmark endpoints. Three things follow: the flood rule, with the display going silent when it would be most tempting to speak and the written score continuing as the overview; the overview finding as the one item a continuous display addresses; and management of change, which is why D-11 exists and why every stage of section 5.3 is a change record.

**Research edition (MX 339 to 345).** The same case with the same figures written in words, the same two quoted causes in reported form, and the same benchmark endpoints. Two things follow: the design rationale for the flood behaviour, with the added observation that it "is the rule most likely to be quietly dropped by a researcher who finds that the display is more interesting when it keeps playing"; and the requirement that the environment reproduce floods, DM-21, with every conformance run exercising the flood machine and every listener study including flood scenarios among its stimuli "so that the participants hear what the silence sounds like and the analysis can ask whether they understood it". The overview finding is carried at MX 345 as the claim the environment is best placed to test.

**Difference and reason.** Three lessons become two, which is **purpose**: management of change has no referent in a closed environment, and its place is taken by the conformance-run obligation. The refinery is named in the field edition, "the Texaco refinery at Milford Haven" (EU 269), and not named in the research edition, "a refinery at Milford Haven" (MX 341), which is **editorial**; both cite the same HSE sources. The research edition adds the flood scenarios to the listener studies, which is **scientific**.

### 3.9 Section 9, Programme roadmap

**Field edition (EU 273 to 302).** The four papers mapped onto the working group's phases, then five milestones in a diagram and three paragraphs, each with the claim it licenses, the claims accumulating and none available before its milestone. M1 conformance and remediation; M2 the listener study; M3 the simulator trial with SAGAT and TLX, the first milestone at which the word benefit may be used, qualified by "in a simulator"; M4 the shadow-mode field trial after consent and impact assessment; M5 the two-dimensional learned renderer in simulation mode. A final node and a closing paragraph list what no milestone licenses.

**Research edition (MX 347 to 383).** Six milestones, all internal to the environment, each preceded by a gate that the previous milestone's recorded result opens. M1 conformance and determinism; M2 the listener studies of the mappings; M3 the simulator studies; M4 the psychometric calibration; M5 the trained renderer compared with fixed and baseline; M6 the early-warning analysis. Three paragraphs of commentary explain the ordering, and a final node and closing paragraph list what no milestone licenses.

**Difference and reason.** Five milestones become six and the order changes, which is **purpose** and **scientific**. The field edition's M4, the shadow-mode field trial, has no counterpart and cannot have one; in its place the research edition inserts M4 psychometric calibration and M6 early-warning analysis, and moves the renderer comparison from last to fifth. The gates change from acceptance criteria met and recorded to registration filed, polarity settled, results recorded, calibration or negative finding, and comparison with confidence intervals. The prohibition list changes from four items to four items with different content, compared in section 7 of this report.

### 3.10 Section 10, Conclusion

**Field edition (EU 304 to 308).** Two paragraphs. The first recapitulates: the legal frame quoted rather than summarised, twenty invariants turning readings into obligations a checker can test, three modes keeping the person-level layer where it is lawful, the validation stack as ordinary control-centre practice with criteria stated in advance and rollout staged behind consent and change control, the early-warning observables as numbers in a margin, and the outcome as a list of artefacts none of which the working group prices. The second states what the audible control room delivers if the milestones are reached and what it does not deliver, "any reading of the people in the room".

**Research edition (MX 385 to 391).** Three paragraphs. The first states the design principle: "a closed environment on synthetic data removes the reason for most of the field edition's rules and supplies no reason at all to remove the science", with the counts, "sixteen notation and engine requirements are relaxed, each named with its subject and each for the same reason, and twenty-three research-protocol requirements take their place". The second states that the honesty of the series is unchanged and restates it as research design rather than prohibition, item by item: evidence tiers into the run configuration, affective labels as hypotheses with experiment identifiers, the psychometric mapping unvalidated and its validation the point, no lead time and no accuracy measured and both outcomes the environment exists to measure, and the renderer a prototype whose first milestone is making it repeat itself. The third states what the environment can and cannot deliver.

**Difference and reason.** The conclusion mirrors its own paper, so the differences follow those already recorded. One sentence is worth isolating as the hinge of the whole delta, MX 387: the environment "removes the reason for most of the field edition's rules and supplies no reason at all to remove the science". That is **purpose** for the relaxations of section 5 of this report and **scientific** for everything the two editions share, listed in section 2 of this report.

### 3.11 Research edition section 2, The research environment, with no field counterpart

MX 50 to 133, seven subsections and a diagram. There is no counterpart in the field edition, whose section 2 is the legal frame; a deployment has a real world and does not describe it.

**2.1 The synthetic plant twin and its generators (MX 52 to 58).** A process model with declared variables, each with a nominal value, a normal-band half-width and an alarm limit, which is the structure the plant-deviation index of Paper 2 section 3.2 requires, so the index has a defined value at every frame known to the generator as well as to the renderer. A scenario file drives it: disturbances with start times, magnitudes and decay constants, plus trips and interlock actions. "A scenario is the unit of generation, and a run of a scenario under a seed is the unit of data." Four generators sit on top. The alarm generator turns excursions into annunciations under a rationalisation table assigning each alarm a priority, a deadband and a chatter tendency, and produces the trailing ten-minute rate, the standing, stale and chattering counts, the standing count at medium or high priority, and the one-minute bins from which the dispersion index is computed, with a flood mode at a declared number of alarms inside a declared window. The security-event generator produces a correlated event rate on a declared arrival process. The work generator produces queue depth and acknowledge latency, from a fitted distribution or from participants. The interaction generator produces the HMI event stream, acknowledgements, commands and setpoint changes, synthetically or from participants. Every generator is versioned and every dataset carries a manifest (DM-2, DM-3, DM-6), because "A synthetic dataset with no manifest is not evidence of anything".

**2.2 The synthetic operator population (MX 60 to 76).** Seven profile components, each labelled by its standing: DISC quadrant scores D, I, S, C, a commercial typology with weak non-independent validity evidence collapsing largely onto two Big Five factors; Big Five domain scores O, C, E, A, N, the mainstream replicated between-person taxonomy and not a state model; Dark Tetrad scores, narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy and sadism, validated for between-person research and never as an operational flag; the trauma scalar tau, an engineering quantity with no external referent; entropy H, a hand-set scalar; the register triple r, s, i, an engineering parameterisation; and the discourse estimate delta, a probability vector over the four discourses and the capitalist label. Profiles are hand-authored or generated from a declared sampler with marginals, a copula or factor-model correlation structure and a seed, and the generator may reproduce the redundancy the psychometric literature predicts on purpose. Two statements are load-bearing: the mapping from any component to a musical parameter is unvalidated, and the fifty-seven-dimensional input of the reference implementation is a proposal only whose projection is computed and discarded at inference; and a trait score is not a state, so a profile vector is a generator parameter and any rendering of it is a rendering of a parameter.

**2.3 Synthetic threat actors and their discourse assignments (MX 78 to 82).** A campaign script, a set of observable security events injected into the event generator, and a discourse assignment written by the scenario author before the run, "so that the environment has something a field deployment never has, which is the ground truth of what the estimate should have been". That ground truth is what makes the study of MX 301 to 307 possible.

**2.4 The MPN Conductor as renderer (MX 84 to 88).** One calculus as the source of truth with the browser twin generated from its frame-table fixtures and conformance-tested against it, because "a comparison between two renderings is worthless if the two were computed by calculi that disagree" and the two-calculi defect "would silently become a confound in every listener study the environment runs". Two rendering paths side by side, the fixed path with declared constants and no random-number generator, and the learned path as a conditioner over the frame table driving a pretrained music model. The separation is kept for a different reason than in the field edition: "the two paths must be separable so that they can be run on the same frame table and compared under one protocol (DM-12, DM-13, section 5.4)".

**2.5 Logging and the dataset layout (MX 90 to 94).** A run produces a directory fixed by DM-5 and DM-6 containing the manifest; the scenario file and the generator parameter files; the raw input frames as received at the schema boundary, one row per channel per frame with source timestamps; the intermediate index table with the plant-deviation, activity, instability and load indices, the tension index and the operator-state indices with their quality flags; the notation table with the realised chord, transition word, metre, velocity, marking and rendering state per bar; the rendered-event log, one row per note-on, stream change, alert entry, flood entry and mute with wall-clock time; the audio; and participant responses, sensor streams and session timeline. Each file carries a schema identifier and version, published with the dataset. MX 94 states the relation to the field edition: "This is the exact inverse of the field edition's logging rule ... The inversion is deliberate and is the point of the environment."

**2.6 The simulator (MX 96 to 100).** A control-room mock-up with a small number of positions, each with a process display, an alarm list and an interaction log, driven by the plant twin; freeze points at which the displays blank and participants answer situation-awareness probes; block boundaries at which participants complete a workload instrument; and the scripted-event structure the two-stream detection task needs. Participants are volunteers from the target population or of comparable training, treated as study participants and not as operators, briefed and debriefed under DM-16. "Debriefing from the score is itself a claim the environment can test, since the symbolic score is the artefact the series proposes as the audit trail and nobody has yet shown that a person can read one usefully."

**2.7 The hardware (MX 102 to 106).** The sensing side carries heart-rate variability by chest-strap or finger electrocardiography; electrodermal activity at the palm or wrist; pupillometry and eye tracking from a desk-mounted tracker with a luminance sensor beside it; voice and speech features from a near-field microphone per position with audio retained and feature extraction versioned; and keystroke and interaction dynamics from the position's own input devices, all timestamped against one clock. The rendering side carries a near-field channel at each position and a room channel, "which is the capability the field edition's rules on who hears what removed". Compute for the learned renderer is a single accelerator.

**Diagram (MX 108 to 133).** Generators through the schema boundary to one calculus, to two rendering paths, to per-position and room audio, to the versioned dataset, to the analysis stage, to a claim naming its experiment and dataset under DM-23.

## 5. What the research edition admits that the field edition does not

The field edition admits thirteen channels and no more, gates each one before the schema boundary, renders at most three streams in operational mode, keeps no per-person record and names no affective label. The research edition admits every channel the programme has ever envisaged, renders without restriction on count or audience, logs everything per participant, and renders the affective labels the notation defines as hypotheses under test. This section inventories the difference completely: the channels, the outputs and labels, the rendering and audibility rules, the logging rules, the sixteen relaxed requirements with the exact wording of each requirement and of each relaxation, and the requirements that stand unrelaxed with where each is honoured.

### 7.6 The delta itself

Four stages become six milestones with six gates. The gating substance survives: a stage is entered only when the previous stage's criteria are met and recorded (D-19), and a milestone is entered only when the previous milestone's recorded result opens its gate (MX 349). What changes is what is gated. The field edition gates exposure, from simulator to shadow mode to one console to the room, each step putting the display in front of more people; the research edition gates inference, from an instrument that repeats itself to a mapping whose polarity is known to a psychometric layer that does or does not carry information. The field edition's M4, the only field trial in either document, has no counterpart and cannot have one. Three studies of the research edition, 5.4, 5.5 and 5.6, have no counterpart in the field edition at all. Four of the field edition's six acceptance criteria survive as measures rather than as gates: the SAGAT criterion in study 5.2, the NASA-TLX criterion in study 5.2, the two-stream criterion in study 5.1 and the polarity criterion in study 5.1. Two do not survive at all: the alarm-rate criterion, which needs a real alarm system, and the ISO 7731 criterion, which needs a real console with real danger signals.

### 10.4 Registry implication

The question is whether the registry carries two slugs or one paper with two editions. `MPN-SERIES-PLAN.md` line 61 currently proposes one entry for Paper 4: id `WG-09-MPN-Deployment`, slug `audible-control-room`, badge "Deployment".

**Recommendation: two slugs, two registry entries, one badge each.** Suggested values, in the form the plan already uses:

| Paper | id | slug | badge |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| MPN-4 | `WG-09-MPN-Deployment` | `audible-control-room` | Deployment |
| MPN-4-MX-R | `WG-09-MPN-Research-Environment` | `mpn-closed-research-environment` | Research environment |

The reasons are five.

First, the two documents no longer share a subject. Section 3 of this report shows that three of the ten body sections differ in subject matter rather than in wording, that the whole of the field edition's legal frame has no counterpart, and that the research edition's section 2 and three of its six studies have no counterpart in the other direction. An edition is a variant of one text; these are two texts with a shared core, and the shared core is the three earlier papers rather than each other.

Second, the requirement lists are disjoint by declaration. MX 137 states it: the research requirements "replace the field edition's deployment invariants entirely; nothing in this list is carried from it". A reader who arrives at one entry expecting D-numbers and finds DM-numbers has arrived at the wrong document, and a single entry cannot signal which.

Third, the reference lists diverge by more than half. Fifty-four of the research edition's 87 references do not appear in the field edition and twenty-four of the field edition's 57 do not appear in the research edition, as section 8 records. A single registry entry with one reference count would be wrong for both.

Fourth, the two carry different risks of being read for the wrong purpose, and that asymmetry is the strongest reason of the five. The field edition is a document a site can be shown; the research edition renders affective labels, logs per participant and admits every sensor the programme envisages, and every one of those is lawful only because the environment is closed and the people in the datasets do not exist. A registry that presents the research edition as an edition of the deployment paper invites exactly the reading that both papers spend their length refusing. Two entries, with the research entry's badge naming the environment, make the distinction visible before the document is opened.

Fifth, the plan's own conventions favour it. `MPN-SERIES-PLAN.md` line 52 works branch per paper with one pull request per paper and one new file under `references/WG-09-MPN/` per entry, and line 48 records that the Phase 3 arithmetic and the WG-09 count are maintained by counting documents. A second document is easier to account for than a second edition of one document, and the folder rule at line 52, "new folder, never renamed afterwards", makes a later split expensive.

The cost of the recommendation is that the series is no longer four papers. The plan's series line, "Paper 4 of 4 (Foundations, Notation, Engine, Deployment)", appears in the metadata table of all four papers; the research edition already writes its own as "Paper 4 of 4 (research edition; the European field edition is MPN-4)" at MX 4, which is the right form and should be kept. The WG-09 count rises to ten rather than nine in the later pull requests, and the census re-baseline in each pull request's allowlist absorbs the extra document, the extra characters and the extra mermaid diagrams, of which the research edition carries three.
