## 2. Computation results

All group-theoretic claims hold under the paper's own definitions (section 3.6, lines 160-170):

- P, L, R are involutions on all 24 triads; each preserves exactly two common tones; the moved voice moves 1 semitone (P, L) or 2 (R). Line 166: correct.
- L(C major) = E minor, R(C major) = A minor, P(C major) = C minor. Line 166: correct.
- PLP(C major) = G-sharp/A-flat minor, zero common tones with C major (hexatonic pole). Line 168: correct.
- RLRLRLR = P on all 24 triads. Line 166: correct.
- Group generated by P, L, R has order 24; L and R alone generate the same 24 elements; action is simply transitive. Line 166: correct.
- LR chain from C: C, Em, G, Bm, D, F#m, A, C#m, E, G#m, B, D#m, F# (k = 0..12), returns to C after 24 steps; each LR pair is a transposition by 7 semitones. Line 170: correct, except "and back" (the chain does not return from F-sharp; it continues through 11 more triads).
- PLR Cayley graph diameter is 5 (eccentricity of C major is 5, antipode A-sharp minor). Line 170: correct.
- C major to G-sharp minor: 3 generators (PLP or LPL), versus 9 chain steps. Diagram line 187 and accDescr line 176: correct.

## 3. Errors, severity-ordered, with fixes

1. HIGH. N-26 (line 344) demands bit-for-bit reproduction but the spec leaves four things undefined that the tables depend on: (i) the rounding rule for `round()` (the tables silently use round-half-to-even; three cells change under half-up); (ii) the tie-break among equally short PLR words (N-10, line 328; two ties in 8.1, one three-way tie in 8.2); (iii) the value of s in the first frame (no x_p(t - Δ)); (iv) the variance estimator and bin anchoring of the dispersion index (line 34, line 154). Fix: add to section 3.1 or N-4: "round() is round-half-to-even on exact rational arithmetic; s = 0 in the first frame; the dispersion index uses the population variance over ten one-minute bins ending at the frame end; among equally short PLR words the one that advances furthest along the LR chain is chosen, then lexicographic P < L < R."
2. HIGH. The valence proxy (line 135, N-7 line 325) requires "no standing medium- or high-priority alarm", but section 2 has no priority-resolved channel (a_st is a plain count), and the 8.1 input table adds a column "Medium or high standing" not in the state vector. This contradicts N-1 ("MUST draw only on the channels of section 2"). Fix: add a channel `a_hp` (standing alarms of medium or high priority, count) to the table at line 33.
3. HIGH. N-3 (line 321, "In operational mode a score MUST carry no per-person data") contradicts the state-vector row for phi (line 40): mode "All", personal data "Yes (roster)". For a single-operator console a per-shift roster fatigue index is per-person data. Fix: either exclude phi from operational mode, or reword N-3 to "no per-person data other than the roster fatigue annotation of N-25, which MUST be computed for the crew, never for a named individual".
4. MEDIUM. N-14 (line 332) requires "a distinct ... register" per stream, but the table at lines 206-211 overlaps: Plant 36-60 and Adversary 48-72; Alarm rate 60-72 and Adversary 48-72; Plant and Alarm rate meet at 60. Pan "Left" and "Left-centre" are also barely distinct. Fix: make Adversary 24-36 or 84-96, and set Plant 36-59.
5. MEDIUM. Line 14 attributes to Barrett et al. 2019 [11] the finding that "physiology indexes arousal and load rather than emotion". That paper is about facial movements, not physiology (its title says so). Fix: cite a physiology source (e.g. Cacioppo et al. on autonomic specificity, or Siegel et al. 2018 Psychol Bull meta-analysis) or drop the sentence; keep [11] only for the general unreliability of emotion inference.
6. MEDIUM. Line 14 states the Art. 5(1)(f) prohibition without its exception "for medical or safety reasons". A control-room safety display is exactly where a reader will ask about that exception; omitting it looks like an oversight and weakens the "lawful by design" argument (which does not need the exception, and should say so). Fix: add one sentence: "The safety exception is not relied on; the notation infers no emotion at all."
7. MEDIUM. Line 26 says heart rate, EEG, voice, gaze and keystroke dynamics "are biometric data for its purposes even where they identify nobody". Art. 3(34) still begins "personal data", so data that relate to no identifiable person are not biometric data. Fix: "even where they do not permit unique identification".
8. MEDIUM. Line 43 states as fact that a fixed human-authored mapping "is, on the Commission's own reading ... a visualisation system rather than an AI system [8]". The ledger (row 8) says the paper says "arguably"; it does not. Paragraphs 40-47 of C(2025) 5053 list categories of systems outside the definition; a sonification mapping is not named. Fix: restore "arguably" and cite the specific exclusion category (basic data processing) claimed.
9. MEDIUM. Line 350 "nine alarms" versus the table's eight (see section 2). Fix: "eight alarms".
10. LOW. N-2 (line 320, also line 45) and N-7 (line 325) use "No channel MAY be" and "no rule MAY depend". RFC 2119 [1] defines no negated MAY. Fix: "A channel MUST NOT be", "no rule MUST depend" or "rules MUST NOT depend".
11. LOW. N-8 and line 170 call k the "fifths distance". For major triads the number of fifths from the tonic is k/2; for k odd the chord is a minor triad, not a key. Fix: call k "chain distance" or "LR-chain position" and say once that fifths distance is floor(k/2).
12. LOW. Line 170 "F-sharp, and back": the chain does not turn back; positions 13 to 23 are A-sharp minor to A minor and are never targets because k ≤ 12. Say so, since it explains why C minor at k = 0 is reached only through P.
13. LOW. Line 376: the 1:30 row should read "R P (suspended: direct, sforzando)" to match line 243 and row B of 8.2.
14. LOW. Line 130: the tier list names CONVENTIONAL and reserves lowercase "convention" for the working group's own codings; the table then uses "STRONG as an arousal cue; CONVENTIONAL as a load code", "Not a mapping; advisory" and "THEORY, conditional", none of which are in the list. Fix: define each tier in one line and use only listed labels.
15. LOW. Line 28 ledger row 12 says SAFTE-FAST takes "schedule and sleep inputs"; the paper (line 40) says "Schedule and roster only ... no health or biometric input". If any actual sleep record enters the model, that is health-adjacent data. Fix: state that only schedule-predicted sleep opportunity is used and that no reported or measured sleep is an input.
16. LOW. Line 255 and N-22: flood entry "reaches ten" (≥10); public ISA-18.2 reproductions say "exceeds 10" (TiPS) or "≥10" (exida). The paper is entitled to its own rule but should not call ≥10 "the industry practice" without the caveat that sources differ by one alarm.

## 4. Unsupported or over-stated claims

- Line 14: Barrett [11] for physiology (see error 5); "the same finding the legislator cited [7]" inherits the mismatch.
- Line 43: AI-system definition claim without hedge (error 8); the Meeting Mediator [28] is a weak precedent for "not affect": it fed back per-person speaking time, i.e. individual behavioural data, which is the opposite of console-level aggregation.
- Line 148: "tempo and event rate are the most consistent cue to perceived arousal and activity [35]". Juslin and Laukka's meta-analysis is organised by discrete emotion, not by arousal; the sentence is an interpretation and should be labelled as one.
- Line 204: "no study establishes a larger number of monitorable streams" is an absence claim from a search the paper itself calls limited (line 287).
- Line 253: "could identify only a minority of the alarms" [21] is supported in substance (Momtahan et al. report roughly a third to a half identified) but the reference lacks volume and pages (Ergonomics 36(10):1159-1176).
- Line 313: "conveys trend well and absolute level poorly [25]" is the Queensland group's general position; Paterson et al. 2016 tested enhancements for range identification, and the ledger itself marks the numbers unverified.
- Line 251: "EEMUA 191 ... is the guide those standards were aligned with" is broadly true but uncited beyond existence.
- Line 170: "the two agree in ranking fifths-related keys as near and the tritone key as far" (Lerdahl's distance versus the chain) is asserted, not shown.
- Line 26 and line 14: Commission guidelines C(2025) 5052 and 5053 "final, 29 July 2025". The versions publicly approved on 4 and 6 February 2025 carry the numbers C(2025) 884 and C(2025) 924. This review could not confirm the July numbers; the ledger should record the relationship between the February and July documents.

## 9. Weakest paragraphs

1. Line 43 (the "three remarks"). It carries the paper's legal load and is its least careful paragraph: the AI-system definition claim is stated without the hedge the ledger says it has; the Meeting Mediator precedent points the wrong way (per-person feedback); the consent scope omits the roster index; and "the one person-related channel" contradicts the residual-risk reality of single-operator consoles.
2. Line 170 (the chain paragraph). It mixes terminology ("fifths distance" for a chain position), says "and back" where the chain continues, does not state that half the cycle is unreachable as a target, asserts agreement with Lerdahl without showing it, and, most seriously for a normative text, says "the shortest word" where the shortest word is not unique.
3. Line 350 (worked-example preamble). It says "nine alarms" against a table of eight, calls the values "illustrative" in the same breath as invoking N-26's bit-for-bit reproduction, and does not state the rounding rule, variance estimator, bin anchoring or initial condition that the following two tables silently depend on.

Honourable mention: line 14, for the Barrett misattribution, the omitted safety exception and the "identify nobody" overstatement in the companion sentence at line 26.

## 10. Verdict

The mathematics is correct: every stated property of P, L and R, the D24 structure, the LR chain, the diameter-five claim and both worked tables reproduce by independent computation. The legal architecture is sound and better than most: no biometric, no inferred person-state, roster fatigue advisory only. The paper is not yet conformant to its own N-26, because four computational conventions it relies on are unstated, and it has three internal contradictions between requirements and tables (priority input, phi in operational mode, stream registers). One citation is used for a claim it does not make (Barrett 2019), two Commission document numbers could not be confirmed, and eight references lack volume or pages. Recommendation: revise before working-group review; the fixes are local (errors 1 to 9 touch about twenty lines) and none changes the design.
