## 1. What was tested, and how

Eight tests were set by the brief and all eight were run against an artefact rather than against a claim about one. Every number below came from a command whose path is given, so that any of it can be re-run alone.

Two things should be said at the top because they shape everything after.

The first is that both papers are unusually honest about the absence of Ψ, and this review does not spend its findings restating that absence. Both declare it in their own first pages, S5 gives it a section of its own, and S6 makes it EX-0. The brief asked for the equivalent of the PRD gate's twenty-item list, which means the list of what else must exist, and section 2 supplies it. Of the forty-eight items that list contains, exactly one is Ψ.

The second is that the generator work is in better condition than either paper's own queue implies. All four scripts run from the corpus alone. The repaired `s6_three_clef.py` is what the corpus holds, at two locations, byte-identical. Every corpus table in both papers reproduces. What does not hold is the attribution of several figures to the script that did not compute them, and the claim that one defect in that script is new.

## 3. Test 2: ingest, and what class C actually does to each use case

The published position is the design's and it is not in dispute here: the best streaming diarisation error rate on DIHARD III is 19.8 per cent, the two vendors bundling diarisation with transcription are at 39.1 and 39.2 per cent, meeting-benchmark word error runs from 35 to 46 per cent, a 2025 benchmark scored with overlap included finds missed speech the dominant failure across all models, and at a fifth of speech mis-attributed a true 60 to 40 floor share is not distinguishable from an even one. No live API returns per-segment diarisation confidence, so the confidence gate has no input, which is D17.

**It kills the podcast and the panel at the arity S5 gives them.** S5 section 7.2 puts the typical arity of that use case at three to eight. Design section 4.1 states that class C does not carry a multi-party surface at all, and D18 requires class B for multi-party live participant mode. So class C does not degrade the panel, it removes the surface. S5 says the right thing in one of the three places it should be said, at the close of section 4.3, and then contradicts it twice: section 4.4's table gives class C its own column of available and degraded measures for this use case, and section 7.2's ingest row offers "A with timings, B, or C" as the options. Finding 2.

**It wounds the interview and S5 handles that passage better than any other in either paper.** At two speakers the whole Layer 1 content is latency and overlap, D17 deletes overlap outright on class C, and latency's turn boundaries become the diariser's, so one of the two informative measures is gone and the other is degraded. S5 sections 5.3 and 5.4 say exactly this, in the right place, and additionally refuse the tempting narrowing that two speakers must be the easy case, on the correct ground that the design carries no error figure broken down by speaker count and that the published rates are corpus-level and measured on multi-party material. Nothing in this passage needs changing.

**It does not reach the play script at all**, and S5 section 3.3 says so in the right place. Class A attribution is exact because the labels are given.

**It empties Expression at two speakers, and S6 does not say so anywhere.** Compose three things S6 itself holds: design section 6.3's finding that adjacency, reciprocity and floor share carry no information at two speakers beyond a single degree of freedom, EX-R3's withholding of the overlap measures, and design section 4.1's resolution floor, below which a floor-share difference renders as unmeasured. On class C at two speakers the result is that adjacency and reciprocity are constants, floor share is a single number that the 60 to 40 result puts below the floor, overlap is not computed, and latency alone remains with the diariser's boundaries. S6 section 6 describes class C as "a reduced surface that opens by saying what it cannot answer" and never says that the reduction is to nothing. The 60 to 40 figure, which is the measurement that decides the fate of the one quantity S6's own falsifier one leaves standing besides latency, appears nowhere in S6. Finding 14.

**One structural point reaches both papers.** Under the scope ruling the material is synthetic and the programme holds no class B or class C recording. The in-scope podcast, panel, interview and live exchange are all generated dialogue, which is class A. So the class B and class C content of both papers is unvalidatable within scope, not because anything forbids it but because there is no material. S6 says the honest version of this three times, at section 6 and at EX-R5. S5 says it in passing and then writes section 4 around a user who has a real multitrack and a real mix and is advised to keep the multitrack. Finding 3.

## 4. Test 3: timings, and what the play script actually has

A play script yields no latency, no overlap and no floor share in time. Design section 7.2 states the consequence and this review adopts it without modification.

**The play script has three of the five Layer 1 measures, one of them unchanged.** Speaker adjacency survives unchanged, because it comes from the turn order alone. Floor share survives in a different unit, lines or words rather than seconds. Reciprocity survives over a different window, a turn count rather than a duration. Overlap and latency do not survive at all. S5 section 3.4's table states precisely this and S5 is right to record the design's own count discrepancy, between section 6.1's "two of the five" and section 7.2's "two unavailable outright", as item S5-2 rather than choosing silently. That is the correct handling of a contradiction in a governing document and it is not a finding against S5.

**The output claim does exceed what the use case has, in three specific ways, and understates it in one.**

The first is the header. S5 section 3.5 requires it to carry "the five settings". On a script only two of the five settings have any content: the floor-share unit and window, and the reciprocity window. The overlap threshold has no overlap to threshold, the backchannel lexicon has no overlap to classify, and the latency turn-boundary rule and medium field have no timings to rule on. So the header as specified declares three settings that govern nothing on this material, which inflates the very page of caveats D37 warns this user about. The count itself is also not agreed between the two papers, which is finding 7.

The second is the resolution floor, also in the same header. There is no resolution floor on class A. Design section 4.1 defines it for class C and derives it from the deployed diariser's error rate. S5 requires a header field that has no definition, no derivation and no value on the class this use case is permanently in. Finding 23.

The third is the Φ stave. S5 section 3.6 says the use case "cannot render a Φ stave unless the dramaturg rates a state, because Φ runs only where a human has rated one, and that is Layer 2 work the dramaturg has to do". That is true and it is not the whole of it. Rendering that stave additionally needs items 27 to 34 of section 2, of which the rating instrument does not exist in any document, δ and f have no values, and the register triple is hardcoded in three files. Presenting the obstacle as work the dramaturg has to do understates it by seven items, all of them the programme's. Finding 1.

The understatement is reciprocity. S5 section 3.5's output is a graph whose node weight is the line-count floor share and whose edge weight is adjacency over the declared window. Reciprocity survives on a script in a changed window and is rendered nowhere, so of the three measures that survive, the specified output uses two. That is a gap in the specification rather than an overclaim, and it is worth closing because reciprocity over a turn count is the one surviving measure that says anything about mutuality, which is most of what a relationship network is read for.

## 5. Test 4: the unfixed numbers, exactly what each blocks and what it does not

The brief's warning is well taken and this section is written to it. On the material the scope ruling makes primary, which is class A generated dialogue and labelled script text, the unfixed numbers block considerably less than either paper implies, and the two that block most are not the two either paper emphasises.

**δ, the interpolation margin.** Blocks the mode channel entirely, and through it every Φ stave, because the interpolation weights have no divisor without it. So it blocks the interview's two Φ staves at S5 section 5.5, the live surface's two Φ staves at S5 section 6.5, and the play script's optional Φ stave at S5 section 3.6. It blocks nothing else: not the graph on any use case, not any Layer 1 measure, not Ψ, not the coverage or churn or event-density figures, and nothing at all in S6, where Φ does not run. **S5 does not name δ anywhere**, in its body, in its queue or in its list of items read from the design's queues, while specifying Φ staves as the stated output of two of its four use cases. Finding 1.

**f, the function inside the harmonic parameter.** Same reach as δ, one parameter narrower. D59 fixes the form and the constant at twenty-three and leaves f to the author. Blocks the harmonic parameter of every Φ stave, which S5 section 6.5 counts inside its own information budget as one of the six rendered parameters per stave. Blocks nothing in S6. **S5 does not name f anywhere.** Finding 1.

**The perceptual resolution of the timbre space.** Blocks any claim about how many characters the audio renderer can give distinct voices, and nothing else. It does not block the score renderer, the graph, or any Layer 1 measure. S5 handles this correctly and exactly: section 3.6 reports that at 0.80 the channel holds fourteen and at 0.90 it holds ten and that the table stops at fourteen, section 5.5 reports that two characters are immune because any resolution finer than the diameter suffices for two, and section 6.5 reports the window at (1.0898, 1.4142] with the answers at 1.0, 1.45 and 1.6. All of those reproduce from `s6_timbre_capacity.py` exactly, and this is the best-evidenced passage in either paper. It blocks nothing in S6, which correctly excludes the channel at section 8.

**The damping constant of LS-1 and D54.** Blocks only the live rendering rate. It is in the design's inventory. Its deeper problem is not the constant but the mechanism, and S5 section 6.5 is right that this is a specification problem. What S5 does not have, and what finding 9 is about, is any measurement of the redraw rate of the stave the surface exists for. **S6 omits the damping constant from section 8's list of numeric parameters the surface consumes and from EX-7**, while EX-R1 specifies a one-stave running score, which is a live rendering that LS-1 governs. Finding 21.

**The parse-confidence refusal threshold.** Blocks D8's refusal behaviour and nothing else. This is the exactness the brief asked for: a product can compute and print the three parse-confidence numbers without a threshold, and does not need one to render a graph. What it cannot do without one is refuse, which means it cannot exclude the King Lear case, which means the anthology and parse-failure handling of section 8's edge-case table has no trigger. So the threshold blocks one behaviour, that behaviour is the design's own honesty claim, and it reaches all five surfaces. Neither paper carries it in its queue: S5 section 8 has no item for it, and S6 puts it in EX-7 with three numbers that do not apply.

**The resolution floor per ingest class.** Blocks class C only. Class C is future work if real audio is ever used, on the design's own record three times over, so on the critical path the resolution floor blocks nothing at all. S5 section 4.7 nevertheless makes "a declared resolution floor as a parameter" an obligation on Ψ, and S5 section 3.5 puts the floor in a class A header. S6's EX-7 says its four numbers block "Every refusal and every blank", which is false of this one on the primary material.

**The trigger at which a below-floor difference renders as unmeasured.** Defined against the resolution floor, so it has no instance on class A either. It blocks the class C rendering and the audibility question of S6's falsifier five, and nothing on the primary material. The design calls it the numeric heart of one of its central honesty claims and that is true of class C.

**The staleness horizon.** Blocks the live capture blanking rule and nothing else. On material known in advance the score is precomputed and synchronised under D52 and D67, so there is nothing that can fall behind and nothing to blank. S6 scopes it correctly at section 6, giving it only to the live class B capture. S5 does not scope it and states blanking as a property of participant mode generally. Finding 13.

**The five Layer 1 defaults.** These are the numbers that block most, and neither paper says so. On a script exactly two of them bind, the floor-share unit and window and the reciprocity window, and S5-1 names both, correctly. On the class A panel with timings all five bind. On the interview exactly two bind, the latency turn-boundary rule with its medium field and the overlap threshold, and those two govern the only two measures the use case has, so the interview's entire Layer 1 content is determined by two numbers that nothing in either paper's queue fixes. Finding 22.

Stated compactly, and this is the useful half. **Blocked on the primary material:** every Φ stave, by δ, f, the rating instrument and the hardcoded register triple; the refusal behaviour, by the parse-confidence threshold; every Layer 1 answer, by the Layer 1 defaults, of which two bind on a script and two on an interview; and the live redraw decision, by LS-1's missing mechanism. **Not blocked on the primary material, and this is most of what the unfixed inventory looks like it blocks:** the resolution floor, the unmeasured trigger and the staleness horizon, all three of which belong to ingest classes and to a live capture that the scope ruling leaves as future work; the audible-fraction threshold and the perceptual resolution, which reach only the audio renderer's cast size; and the damping constant, which is a tuning number sitting behind a specification question that has to be answered first in any case.

## 6. Test 5: S6's backchannel ruling, what remains computable, and whether the surface can be built at two speakers

**What remains computable under EX-R3.** Four measures: floor share, speaker adjacency, latency and reciprocity. Overlap initiated and overlap received are not computed at all, and backchannel, being a parameter of the overlap row under D64, goes with them.

**What remains informative at two speakers is two quantities and one of them is a scalar.** Design section 6.3 is explicit: adjacency is deterministic, reciprocity is 1 by construction, and floor share is one number and its complement. So on the arity S6 section 10 calls "the most likely material any version of this surface is pointed at", the computable and informative set is latency, plus floor share as a single degree of freedom. S6 says this, and says it as its sharpest falsifier, which is right.

**The surface can be built at two speakers, and what S6 has not noticed is that its own arithmetic already settles the question EX-3 proposes to ask.** Two quantities on one stave is inside B4's budget of two to three. So the stave is buildable and it is under-subscribed, not over-subscribed. That has three consequences S6 does not draw.

The first is that section 9's clause three is false at this arity. It argues that "three of the five measures do not fit on the stave under any reading of B4", which is true at high arity and false at two speakers, where only two carry information and both fit. The paper asserts the oversubscription and the degeneracy in adjacent sections without composing them. Finding 6.

The second is that EX-R1's stated default, floor share and adjacency, puts a constant on the stave. Adjacency carries no information at two speakers, so a user who opens no configuration gets one degree of freedom and one constant, which is not the "defensible score rather than an empty one" clause two promises. Finding 6.

**Finding 1. The two use cases whose stated output is a Φ stave carry no Φ dependency anywhere. S5. (c) and (b). BLOCKING.**

D28 is defined against a resolution floor derived from a deployed diariser's error rate, and class A has no diariser and no floor, so D28 has no instance on a script. The rule that actually carries the obligation is design section 4.1's, that a flat score from a failed parse and a flat score from a flat conversation must never look alike, which S5 quotes in the same paragraph and which is general. Cite that and drop D28.

**Finding 17. S5 claims as new a defect its own cited verification file already records. S5. (c). Not blocking.**

**Finding 21. Both papers overstate what their unfixed numbers block, S6 by three of four in one table cell. BOTH. (b). Not blocking.**

S6's EX-7 gathers the parse-confidence threshold, the resolution floor, the unmeasured trigger and the staleness horizon and gives them the blocks column "Every refusal and every blank". **Finding 22. The interview's only two measures are governed by two settings that nothing fixes. S5. (b). BLOCKING.**

S5 section 5.4 establishes that at two speakers the whole informative Layer 1 content is latency and overlap. Latency's answer is set by the turn-boundary rule and by the medium field; overlap's answer is set by the threshold, and by whether backchannels count. None of the four has a value anywhere, and design section 10's inventory carries them only collectively as "the Layer 1 defaults". There should be an item S5-1 twin for the interview, and it should say that the medium field on a recorded interview is the one that decides whether the latency distribution is a measurement of the exchange or of the editor, which is the question S5-9 asks about podcasts and does not ask here.

**Finding 23. The class A header specifies a field that has no definition on class A. S5. (b). Not blocking.**

S5 section 3.5 requires the play script's header to carry, among other things, "the resolution floor". S5 inherits the field from design section 6.1, which lists it in the same header, so the design carries the defect too. The repair is one clause: the header states the resolution floor where the ingest class has one, and states that the class has none where it does not, which is the same distinction section 8a's four kinds already require.

## 11. Disposition

### MPN-S5, `S5-dialogue-use-cases.md`

**REVISE. Nine blocking: findings 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12 and 22.**

The paper is right about more than it is wrong about, and two of its passages are the best in the programme on their subjects: section 5.3 and 5.4 on what class C does to a two-speaker exchange, including its refusal to assume that two speakers must be the easy case, and section 3.6 and 6.5 on the timbre channel, every figure of which reproduced from the generator at its default settings. Its handling of the design's own count discrepancy at S5-2, recording the position rather than choosing the total, is the correct treatment of a contradiction in a governing document.

What returns it is a pattern rather than a list. The paper is exact about Ψ and inexact about everything else that is missing. Two of its four use cases render Φ staves and it names no Φ dependency at all, not the rating instrument, not δ, not f, not the hardcoded register triple. Its one ingest prohibition, that class C carries no multi-party surface, is stated in one place and contradicted in two. Its second use case is written around a user with real recordings, which its own scope row excludes. Its live surface's three named pieces, damping, coverage and the per-stave declaration, are all empty, and the paper offers a measurement for the first that measures something else, a figure for the second with no declared unit while a second unit already exists in its companion paper, and a default for the third whose justification is the measurement that disqualifies half of it. And participant mode, which the Understanding Lock asks for and which the design restores in full, is specified as rendering to readers the paper's own justification says are not there.

None of that requires a new argument. Every repair is a sentence or a queue item, and the nine blocking findings between them add perhaps two items to section 8 and correct five sentences. The paper does not need to be rewritten and it should not go out as it stands.

### MPN-S6, `S6-expression-aid.md`

**REVISE. Five blocking: findings 4, 5, 6, 7 and 14.**

This is the more careful of the two papers and it is the one that carries the sharper self-criticism. Section 8's statement of what the surface does not need is the most accurate dependency paragraph in either paper. Section 3 computes a premise the design had asserted and never measured, states the narrower claim the measurement supports, and prints what it fails to establish, which is exactly the right treatment. What returns it is that its three rulings do not compose. EX-R3 withholds overlap; EX-R6 then puts overlap in the sentence carried on the face of every output. And falsifier one itself is stated one ingest class short of its strength: on class C at two speakers the surface has nothing at all, and the published measurement that establishes it, the 60 to 40 result, is absent from the paper.

The repairs are small and they are all inside section 11's own table. The sentence loses a clause or EX-R3 gains a third option. And the two papers agree on whether Layer 1 carries four named settings or five before either of them goes out.
