## 3. Legal over-reach findings

- **O-1 (line 43, and ledger judgement call 3). "The obligations attached to Annex III systems apply since 2 August 2026 under Article 113."** Superseded. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI), published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and in force from 27 July 2026, defers the application of the obligations for Annex III high-risk systems to 2 December 2027 and for Annex I systems to 2 August 2028 (White & Case; Gibson Dunn; CSA research note). Article 26(7) is a deployer obligation for high-risk systems and is deferred with them. As written on 12 September 2026 the sentence is false, and the ledger's own caveat ("any later amendment ... is not reflected") shows the writer anticipated it. Art. 5 and the 2 February 2025 date are unaffected; the Omnibus adds prohibitions on non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM generation but does not touch 5(1)(f). Fix: "Under Article 113 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 the obligations attached to Annex III systems apply from 2 December 2027; Article 26(7) applies with them"; add the amending regulation to reference [8] and re-verify against the OJ text. D-10's "as a precaution ... whether or not" stands and is now better motivated.

- **O-2 (line 17). "The Commission's guidelines cite the same review the series relies on as the scientific basis of the prohibition."** Overstated. The guidelines' footnote 150 (para 241, "Emotion recognition is frequently doubted as to its effectiveness or as to its accuracy") reads "See e.g. J. Stanley, Experts Say 'Emotion Recognition' lacks Scientific Foundation, 18.7.2019, ACLU, referring to a study by L. Feldman Barrett e.a." It is one "see e.g." pointer through a secondary article, offered for the doubt about accuracy, not "as the scientific basis of the prohibition". Recital 44 gives the legislator's reason without citing anything. Fix: "the guidelines' footnote on the doubts about the accuracy of emotion recognition points, through a secondary report, to the same review".

- **O-3 (line 37). "with a prior written and motivated expert opinion"** presented as a condition of the safety exception. Para 259: any use "should be accompanied by sufficient safeguards. Such safeguards could include, for example, prior written and motivated expert opinion relating to the specific use case." It is an example of a safeguard. Fix: "accompanied by safeguards, of which the guidelines' example is a prior written and motivated expert opinion".

- **O-4 (line 31). "heart rate, EEG, voice, gaze and keystroke dynamics are biometric data for the Regulation's purposes even where they identify nobody."** Art. 3(34) still opens "personal data resulting from specific technical processing"; footnote 160 says only that the AI Act drops the GDPR's functional qualifier "which allow or confirm the unique identification". Data that relate to no identifiable person are not personal data and therefore not biometric data under 3(34) either. In a control room such signals are about an identifiable operator, so the conclusion survives; the reason given does not. Fix: "even where they do not by themselves allow unique identification, provided they are personal data about an identifiable operator, which in a manned console they are". Para 251 supports the modality list (voice, keystrokes, eye tracking, heartbeats, EEG, ECG).

- **O-5 (line 61). "A data protection impact assessment, which the GDPR requires for systematic monitoring under its Article 35."** Art. 35(3)(c) requires a DPIA for "a systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale", which a control room is not. The obligation for employee monitoring flows from Art. 35(1) (likely high risk) read with Art. 35(4): the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens' published list of processing operations requiring a DPIA (Besluit inzake lijst van verwerkingen, Stcrt. 2019, 64418) includes monitoring of employees ("controle werknemers"), and WP 248 rev.01 names "systematic monitoring" and "vulnerable data subjects (employees)" as criteria. The conclusion is right; the article-level reasoning is not. Fix the clause and add the AP list and WP 248 rev.01 to the references. The ledger already flags Art. 35 as a writer-added number.

- **O-6 (line 37 and 39). Team-level inference.** The hybrid-team example (para 254) is prohibited because the system infers emotions "from voice and imagery" of the individuals on the call. The guidelines do not address a system that reads a team's tone while inferring nothing about any individual; para 267 places crowd-level mood analysis outside 5(1)(f) precisely "because it does not infer emotions of (a concrete) natural person", and para 270 puts primarily non-employee-directed systems outside scope on an intended-purpose test with a duty to avoid screening employees. The paper's answer at line 39 ("averaging their inferred states would infer each state first") is a sound engineering argument and it is the working group's, not the Commission's. Label it so.

- **O-7 (line 55). "the Dutch and European positions close it."** WP 249 says explicit consent is "highly unlikely" to be valid; the AP said there is "over het algemeen" (in general) no free consent. Both leave a margin the paper does not. The design does not need the stronger claim; write "all but close it". Also, the NJB report states the AP's finding that the processing was unlawful; whether the AP "required" the two employers to stop, or they ceased, is not established by the secondary source read. Cite the AP's own news page (URL above, 403 to this reviewer) as the primary and the NJB as the access route.

- **O-8 (line 49). "on the Commission's reading it is a visualisation system."** Paras 40, 46 and 47 support treating a fixed, human-authored mapping as outside the definition, and the paper's two caveats are the right ones. Two further points should be recorded: the guidelines are stated by the Commission to be non-binding, with the Court of Justice the final interpreter; and para 47's dashboard example is one that "does not recommend how to improve sales", whereas the notation has an alert rule with a threshold and a flood state machine. The paper's position that an alert entry is a rendering rule and not a recommendation is defensible but is a further step of the working group's own, and D-10 rightly leaves it to the site. No change to the design; one sentence of attribution.

- **O-9 (line 57). "Article 22 ... is engaged only if the system itself triggers consequences for a person."** The test is a decision "based solely on automated processing ... which produces legal effects concerning him or her or similarly significantly affects him or her". "Triggers consequences" is looser than the text; use the statutory words.

- **O-10 (line 61). WOR completeness.** Art. 27(1) closes with "een en ander voor zover betrekking hebbende op alle of een groep van de in de onderneming werkzame personen" (a crew is a group; fine), 27(3) removes the consent requirement where the matter is already substantively regulated in a collective agreement, and 27(5) makes an unconsented decision void only if the works council invokes nullity in writing within one month. A site with a CAO clause on monitoring is a real case the paper should mention in one clause; the design's "plan for consent" is unaffected.

- **O-11 (line 55). "The AP is the designated coordinating supervisor for the Regulation's prohibitions in the Netherlands."** Not verified in this pass (the AP page was unreadable). The Dutch cabinet named the AP as coordinating supervisor for the AI Act in 2024 and the implementing act designating market surveillance authorities for Art. 5 was in draft in 2025; state the source and date or soften to "the AP has positioned itself as".

- **O-12 (line 17 and 53). "physiological data about a worker are health data under the GDPR."** Recital 35 ("the physiological or biomedical state of the data subject independent of its source") and the AP's 2016 position on movement and sleep data support the reading for the signals the paper names (HRV, EDA, EEG). No court has held that every physiological datum is health data; the paper's categorical sentence should be "are treated as health data on Recital 35 and the AP's practice".

## 10. Verdict

The paper's legal quotations are accurate: of thirty-eight quoted or closely paraphrased passages, thirty-seven match the authentic texts word for word within their ellipses, and the one miss is an attribution of Art. 9(3) words to Art. 9(2)(h). The article, recital and annex numbers all check. The lawful-by-design architecture holds under the mode check: no biometric or inferred person-state enters any operational input, adversary mode depends on no staff data, and no measured lead time, incident-rate reduction or premium effect is claimed anywhere.

Two things must be fixed before working-group review. First, the paper is out of date on the one point most likely to be checked by a reader: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 deferred the Annex III obligations to 2 December 2027 seven weeks before the paper's date, and line 43 says they have applied since 2 August 2026. Second, five sentences state as the Commission's or the law's position what is in fact the working group's inference or a harder reading than the text supports (O-2 through O-6); each is fixable in a clause, and the design does not need the stronger versions. The console-shift fatigue annotation (error 6) is a genuine design tension with D-3 and should be resolved in the invariant, not in prose. Cross-references to F-, N- and E- requirements are correct in substance with three loose parentheticals (D-3, D-12, D-14). Style is clean apart from length. Recommendation: revise lines 17, 31, 37, 39, 43, 53 to 61, 104 and 184; update reference [8], [16] and the ledger rows 8, 9, 10 and 16; re-verify Art. 113 as amended and UAVG Art. 30 in the version valid from 1 September 2026; then re-check.
