## 1. Disposition

**ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS.**

This is the strongest paper the series has produced on the measure the series has come to care about, which is whether a reader can disagree with a test rather than with a conclusion. Sixteen blocking findings were raised against it across four reviews and two revisions, and all sixteen are discharged. Nine numbered generators stand behind it, every figure reproduces from one of them, two numbers that were transcribed are now generated, one of the paper's own instruments was found to be a hardcoded whitelist and was rewritten rather than defended, and a claim the paper had made about leitmotif inversion was withdrawn as false after the function was executed rather than read. Three findings that revision 1 asserted are gone from revision 2 because they did not survive their own evidence, and each withdrawal is on the page with the reason. That is the evidential standard ARBITRATION-S3 was trying to establish, reached without the four rounds of rejection S3 needed to reach it.

The six conditions are of one kind, and naming the kind is the substance of this ruling. Every one of them is a count, a scope or a reach that is correct in the section where the work was done and wrong or absent in the section that cites it. Section 8.1 counts its repairs correctly and section 10 imports the count beside an enumeration that does not produce it. Section 6 partitions the deconstruction's findings correctly and never states the denominator the partition is against. The amendments note corrects S1 exactly at the sentence it quotes and does not reach the two other places in S1 that carry the same error. This is the series' signature failure in its last and mildest form, a claim asserted at a width its evidence does not reach, and it has migrated from the claims themselves, where three gates have now driven it out, into the places where claims are summarised. The conditions are checkable in an afternoon and none of them touches a finding.

## 2. Decision log: what each reviewer forced, and how it was resolved

## 4. The four questions put to the Arbiter

### 7. Question 3a: is S4 the right place to correct S1 revision 3, and does the amendments note discharge the obligation

**Ruling. S4 is the right place for the finding, the amendments note is the right instrument for the correction, and between them the obligation is discharged in substance and under-reaches in fact. Condition C4 closes the gap. No S1 revision 4 is required before S4 publishes, and one is required before S1 is next cited.**

The finding belongs in S4 because S4 is the only paper in the series licensed to read the implementation and the data files, and because the correction is a statement about what is in `literary_data.ts`, which is S4's subject and nobody else's. A correction of this kind cannot originate in S1: S1 would be asserting a fact about a source file with its evidence in another document, which is the arrangement three gates in this series have refused. The chain has to sit next to the claim, and in this series the chain is `s4_frame_provenance.py`.

The note is the right instrument because the series has twice established that a paper which moves a sibling's figure issues replacement text rather than leaving the sibling to be corrected from a reading of the new paper. `MPN-S2-DECISIONS` set the practice and `MPN-S3-AMENDMENTS` followed it, and the second of those is the reason S2's parser figures can be corrected from a document rather than from an argument. `MPN-S4-AMENDMENTS` is built on that model and its section 2 is better than either, because it carries the evidence table, the corroboration and the replacement paragraph together, and because it states in its own voice what the correction does not buy.

Where it under-reaches is reach, not standing. The note's Affects field names one section of S1. S1 carries the same error in two further places: the paragraph in section 11 beginning "The same holds for the library itself", which says that the 232 frames are the output of the analyser and this calculus and calls that the single most important thing in its section, and the metadata Revision note, which still describes the frames as hand-annotated. An editor updating S1 from the note as it stands would correct the quoted paragraph and leave a flat contradiction two sections later, in the sentence S1 itself marks as most important. The note also promises in its Status field that each amendment carries its replacement text, and supplies replacement text for two of the six items; the four figures of its section 3 are given as a comparison table, and two of those four are genuine corrections to a single sentence of S1 that an editor would then have to compose. Those are the two halves of condition C4.

The obligation therefore discharges at publication of S4 plus C4, and not before S1 is edited. The distinction matters for the series and I state it plainly: a note that issues replacement text discharges the obligation to correct a sibling, and it does not perform the correction. Until S1 is edited, every citation of S1 revision 3 on this point cites a paper that is wrong in a way the programme knows about. That is obligation one at entry 11 and it is the first thing the next session should do.

### 9. Question 3c: is declining to recommend a branch on S4-2 the right posture, or an evasion

**Ruling. It is the right posture, and it is the right posture only because of what section 9 does instead. Had the paper declined and left the material in section 4.5 where a reader would have to assemble it, it would have been an evasion.**

The test for an audit that declines to recommend is not whether it has an opinion. It is whether, after reading it, the person who must decide can decide. On that test this paper passes. Section 4.5 establishes what each corpus is and which one the other papers rest on. Section 2 establishes that the engine implements no psychology on the path that produced the scores, that its personality estimator has no lower half, and that its L is not the published operator, which is the whole of what the first branch would have to repair. Section 9's closing paragraph then states both branches in the form a decision needs, and it states the non-obvious cost, which is that the second branch takes S4-3 off the table and with it any study whose stimuli come from scored text at corpus scale: thirteen works at scene resolution against a possible twenty-five at line resolution. That last sentence is the sentence that makes the decision decidable, and it was not in revision 1.

The refusal is also correct on its merits, for a reason the paper gives and I endorse. What `mpn_engine` is for is a question about what the programme is for. An audit that answered it would be choosing the programme's purpose on the strength of having counted its defects, and the defect count points at the second branch while the programme's stated ambition, a listening study over scored dramatic text, points at the first. A reviewer who resolved that tension would be substituting a tidy repository for the author's research plan. The series' standing discipline, applied to eight defects in S3 and to three author decisions, is to report and assign; S4-2 is assigned, sized, gated to three downstream items, and costed on both branches, which is the whole of what the discipline asks.

I decline to make anything of this a condition. The asymmetry an exacting reader might press, that the first branch's cost is named as work and the second's as loss, is not a defect of the paper: it is what the two branches actually are.

### 10. Question 3d: is the hedging on the credential correctly placed

**Ruling. Yes, and it is the best-handled claim in the paper. No condition.**

The paper makes three claims and hedges exactly two things, and the two it hedges are the two it cannot verify. It does not hedge that a key is hard-coded at `docker-compose.yml:26`, that it was committed on 10 January 2026, or that it is present in all 36 commits since, because all three are readings of a pinned tree and are checkable by anyone with the repository. It hedges the repository's visibility, and it hedges it by attribution rather than by qualification: the repository is the one "which the programme's own working plan describes as public". That attribution is exact. The governing plan for this series says, under what is blocked on the author, "the hard-coded ElevenLabs key at `docker-compose.yml:26` in the public repo". The paper is not guessing at visibility and not asserting it; it is reporting who said so, which is the only move available to a paper that cannot see the repository settings.

It hedges liveness in the same way and then does the thing that makes the hedge safe, which is to show that the recommended action does not depend on it. "Whether the key still authenticates is not tested here and does not change the repair" appears in section 1, and section 6 repeats it, and the reason is given in both places: a credential committed to a repository that has been cloned or pushed anywhere is compromised by default, and rotation alone does not close it because the history has to be purged. The consequence clause that is conditional is written as conditional, "if the repository is public, scanners find it within minutes". The queue row instructs the reader to assume the key spent, which is a posture and not a claim, and it is the correct posture under both unresolved hedges.

The general rule this exemplifies, and which I record for the series because it has not been stated anywhere: hedge the premise you cannot verify, assert the premises you can, and then show that the action is invariant across the hedge. A paper that does the third thing has not weakened its recommendation by hedging, it has made the hedge irrelevant to it. Every other unverifiable claim in this programme should be handled this way.

## 5. Does the series close

### 11. The plan's scope for S4, discharged clause by clause

The approved plan assigns S4 four things: what the MPN Conductor does, at path and line, what it implements, what is stubbed, and what the seven scored plays show.

The first three are discharged and the third is over-delivered. Path and line are carried throughout, and the paper's readings are pinned to a named working tree that S2 section 6.5 and S3 also cite. What the Conductor implements is section 5, stated as a seven-row table against what S3 specifies with the gap named by queue item, and section 5.1 adds the three things that reach a listener and appear in no theory paper, the per-frame stave decay, the transformation that is bit-identical to another, and the register that selects nothing. What is stubbed is section 6, and the paper's most durable contribution to the programme's vocabulary is section 6.1, which separates stubbed from dead from decorative and says why the three need different words and different repairs. That distinction is not in the plan and it is worth more than the inventory the plan asked for.

The fourth clause, what the seven scored plays show, is built on a false premise, and the question put to me is how the paper handles that and whether it is enough. It is enough, and the handling is the strongest thing in the paper. The paper does not quietly reinterpret the clause and it does not answer a question the plan did not ask. It answers the clause by establishing, with a test whose rejections are printed and a second independent test that agrees with it, that there are no seven scored plays: there are seven Project Gutenberg downloads containing twenty works, six introductions, an author's preface, two licence blocks scored as drama, and one file in which the parser found no dialogue at all. It then answers the clause in the only form that survives, by saying what the rows do show, which is one formula applied to the wrong text, with a principal coordinate that is 99.4 per cent a row counter and a second coordinate that is at its floor on nearly three lines in four. It then does the three things that finish the job: it rules on what the artefact may be called in this series and reserves the word beat to the engine's column name, it issues replacement text so the four figures S1 cites from those rows can be corrected from a document, and it puts re-scoring on the queue as S4-3 with the note that a re-run will not reproduce the present files and must be treated as a new corpus rather than as a correction.

A plan clause built on a false premise is discharged by disproving the premise, reporting what is actually there, and repairing every place the premise was relied on. All three are done. I record, additionally, that the paper protects the rest of the series from its own finding in the same breath, at section 1 and again at section 8: no assertion in S1, S2 or S3 rests on these rows, and the defect is in what has been cited rather than in what has been built on. That sentence is load-bearing for the series closing at all, and it is true.

Two items of the plan's working method are not discharged and I dispose of them here rather than leave them. The plan requires a citation ledger per paper, and only `CITATION-LEDGER-S1` exists; S2, S3 and S4 have none. **I rule the ledger retired for S2, S3 and S4, and record the reason.** What the ledger was for was to attach a verification status to every bracketed citation, and the series replaced it with something stronger midway through S2: references that carry their own warrant clause, and generators that print the evidence for what they support. S4's reference [4] states which claims it covers, which it does not, and that a guard reports having skipped when the Conductor tree is absent so that its passing is not evidence that it ran; reference [5] enumerates the eleven findings it warrants and the six further readings it does not. That is the ledger, inline, at the point of use, and it survives the reader not opening a second file. The practice is retired deliberately rather than dropped silently, and the plan's Files touched list should be read accordingly. The second item, the commit to `papers-pre-publish/MPN/08_PAPERS/` with the index and link-check re-run, is not a matter for the paper and is obligation nine below.

What the four papers are, stated once so the series can be cited as what it is. There is a theory with falsification conditions and nine assertions. There is a formal apparatus that proved the collinearity forcing the A8 amendment rather than observing it. There is a mapping from state to musical material with a defined answer at the modal tie and a computed harmonic metric. And there is a working application that renders that mapping, plays it, and exports it, over thirteen dramatic works. Set against that, the series has established that the mode a listener hears is not a function of the registers, that the shipped intensity pair is two reweightings of one quantity, that the largest empirical artefact is not what it was taken to be and comes from a second program that implements no psychology at all, that no machine-learning component in the tree is trained on anything, and that nothing anywhere in the corpus was observed of any person. The programme is a well-specified proposal with a working renderer, and S4 is right that this is an honest thing to be.

Two things are worth recording about how the series got here, because they are the part that transfers.

The first is that every substantive error in four papers was one error. A claim was asserted at a width its evidence did not reach: a mode search narrower than the implementation, a parser narrower than the corpus, a whitelist narrower than the library, a comparison across an index two corpora did not share, a recheck narrower than the findings it certified. It was named at S3's gate, made a standing condition there, and it still recurred twice in this paper, in the summaries rather than in the claims. No amount of reviewing fixes it. What fixed it, each time, was an instrument made to count what it had read and to fail when the count disagreed, and the series should carry that forward as its only methodological rule: a claim reaches no further than the class its script enumerates, and the only way to know the class is to make the script say so.

The second is that this gate found less than the last one because the paper arrived having already done to itself what the gate would have done. Three claims were withdrawn in the paper's own voice with the reason on the page, one of its own generators was found to be a whitelist and rewritten rather than defended, and a finding about inversion was killed by running the function instead of reading it. A paper that withdraws its own strongest sentence is not a weaker paper. It is the only kind that can be believed when it keeps one.
