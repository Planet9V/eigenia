## 2. The four rulings

### A. The bias layer's modulation rule

Both attacks hold, they hold for different reasons, and between them they leave nothing of the rule as written.

The Skeptic's attack is the channel attack. Section 5a.4 bounds a device by the width of the band its parameter is discretised into, on the principle that a perturbation wider than a band moves the marking by itself and is therefore assignment wearing a hat. S3 section 2.1 says in terms that the output of $\Phi$ is the marking and not the velocity, that the function is piecewise constant, and that nothing about it is continuous and nothing should be claimed to be [3]. On a piecewise-constant output there is no positive perturbation that cannot move the marking. A sub-band perturbation is not a small effect, it is a categorical reassignment that fires some of the time, and S3 computes the rate in the same subsection: for a character at a uniformly random point inside a band, a step of 0.05 in trauma moves the marking one time in two at the extremes and one in three in the middle, and in the topmost band it cannot move it at all [3]. The design's own budget of 0.0333 in a 0.10 band is therefore roughly a one-in-three chance of a full step, a silent no-op otherwise, and a dead zone at fff. That is not a modulation of dynamics. It is a stochastic reassignment of the dynamic marking with a hole in it.

The Skeptic is right about the mechanism and slightly loose about the wording, and the looseness is worth correcting because the author will otherwise fix the wrong sentence. Section 5a.4 does not permit the outcome it defines as assignment. It bounds the joint effect at one step, which is a bound on a maximum and not a guarantee of a move. What collapses is not the equality of the two but the usefulness of the distinction: on a categorical output, a bounded perturbation and an assignment differ only in how often they act, never in what they do when they act. The right statement is the probabilistic one and it is worse for the design than the one the Skeptic wrote.

Section 5a.3 tries to confine the collapse to one channel, saying that on a categorical channel, mode above all, perturb has no meaning. That confinement is false on S3's own text and I checked every channel in the table. Dynamics is piecewise constant on eight markings [3, section 2.1]. Tempo is rounded to an integer beat count inside three bands and metre is a four-condition lookup with a hole in it [3, section 2.2]. Fragmentation and density are five-stage ladders at even fifths declared in advance [3, section 2.4]. Harmony is 24 discrete triads at integer chain positions under a Cayley metric [3, section 2.5]. The instrument family is categorical by construction and the three continuous timbre coordinates have no stated jump set at all, which is worse rather than better, because a bound cannot be written for them [3, section 2.6]. Every channel the layer writes to is categorical in output. BL-2 is one entry of thirty only because the design counted the categorical channels as one.

The Advocate's attack is independent and it is the marking attack. Section 4.4 states the whole of Layer 3's discipline: a model's judgement is marked as a proposal wherever it appears, it never renders without the Layer 0 material that prompted it, and it is never the only thing on screen [6]. A shift of 3.6 velocity units inside a rendered note carries no mark and is inseparable by construction from the value $\Phi$ computed. The Advocate is right that this is fatal to rule one. The Advocate is too strong on rules two and three, which are satisfied in a running score, since the Layer 0 turn is always present and the score is never alone on the screen. They are satisfied and they do no work, which is the accurate and less comfortable statement, and I have narrowed finding U2 and finding S4 to it. The Advocate's further claim, that section 4.4's cited precedent is evidence that marking fails, is wrong and I strike it: the keyword counter S4 describes carried no mark of any kind, so its misreading is evidence for marking and not against it [4].

So the rule falls, and it falls twice over. What replaces it.

The first candidate is to keep the layer on $\Phi$'s channels and find a mechanism that is both audible and markable there. There is none. Audibility on a categorical channel means crossing a boundary, crossing a boundary means a categorical change indistinguishable from $\Phi$'s own, and indistinguishability is precisely what rule one of section 4.4 forbids. The design cannot have both properties on one channel and it needs both.

The second candidate is to move the layer to the channel B4 already gives it, the harmonic relation between staves, which is also where section 6.2 puts it. That is buildable in principle and it is where ruling B lands the layer's accounting. It is not buildable today, and the reason is the thirty devices themselves. Every one of them is written as a modulation of a $\Phi$ channel: an ostinato, a pedal tone, a sforzando, a minor mode, a missing voice, an asymmetric envelope [3, section 4.2], [9]. None of them is a relation between two staves, and rewriting thirty devices for a channel that does not yet exist is authorial work of the same size as the layer. A single relation channel also cannot carry thirty distinguishable devices, let alone two at once, which is the collision problem the section already has and which gets worse rather than better when eight channels become one.

So the ruling is that the modulation rule is struck and the rendering half of the bias layer is deferred, behind one decision the author has to take and has not been asked for: which channel the layer occupies, given that it cannot occupy $\Phi$'s. D45 as written falls with the rule, because D45's stated rationale is that the modulation rule is forced by section 5a.3, and that inference is what produced the contradiction ruling B settles.

What is not deferred, and this matters, is the rest of section 5a. The detection half of the layer is untouched by any of this. A model proposing which of the thirty biases is operating on a turn, rendered as a marked proposal in text beside the Layer 0 turn it came from, satisfies all three rules of section 4.4 exactly and requires no channel at all. That is what section 5a.6's partition scores, that is what BL-5 and BL-6 test, and that is the thing the scope ruling actually bought, since a generated dialogue can be written to contain a named move on a named turn. BL-1, BL-4 and BL-7 all survive and all are authorial. Item 5 of section 10a therefore builds the detector and the test harness now and builds no renderer.

The cost, stated plainly. Revision 7's largest restoration is not restored. The author asked for the bias layer twice and what this ruling returns is half of it: the half that can be tested, without the half that can be heard. Against that, the half being deferred is the half that would have put an unmarked machine proposal inside a rendered note on the therapist's surface, which is the failure section 4.4 exists to prevent and which the Advocate found independently of the Skeptic. A layer that is audible and unattributable is worse than a layer that waits.

### B. Whether revision 7 falsifies B4

This is the most serious finding in the gate because it is internal, and it is upheld on the words. B4's claim is not that a stave carries two to three quantities. Its claim is that profile, state and bias are simultaneously legible because they occupy channels that do not compete, and it says where bias goes: bias, which lives between speakers, takes the harmonic relation between staves rather than any channel on a single stave. Its formal content adds that the bias channel is a between-stave quantity and therefore does not consume a within-stave channel. Its falsification clause names this exact failure: the between-stave channel turns out to be a within-stave channel in disguise, in which case the budget is exceeded after all [7]. S3 section 4.4 relies on that same sentence when it exempts the layer from its identifiability count, saying the layer is carried between staves rather than on one, per B4 in the register [3].

Section 5a.3 puts the layer on eight within-stave channels of a single voice. That is the disguised within-stave channel B4 names, and on the face of it revision 7 falsifies an assertion the programme holds, in one section, while section 6.2 spends the same assertion as a live budget two pages later. The Skeptic is right that a design cannot both break an assertion and spend it, and right that section 6.2 and section 5a.3 give incompatible answers about where the layer physically is, with the engine diagram siding with 5a.3.

Where I part from the Skeptic is on the remedy. He writes that the design must choose and that either choice deletes a section. That is a false dilemma and it would cost the author a great deal to act on. There is a third disposition and ruling A has already produced it. The within-stave allocation is the thing that falsifies B4, and ruling A strikes that allocation on independent grounds, because it is unbuildable. Once it is struck, nothing in the design writes a bias to a within-stave channel, and B4 is not falsified by the design at all. It is falsified by a mechanism the design no longer contains.

So B4 stands, S3 section 4.4's exemption stands, and section 6.2's treatment of the layer as a between-stave quantity is the one the document keeps. What must change is the status of section 5a.3's table. That table is not a specification of where the layer writes. It is a census of which $\Phi$ channel each of the thirty inherited and drafted devices was written for, which is a finding about the Atlas and the reference implementation, and it is a good one: it is what produces BL-2, the collision analysis and the observation that there was never going to be a free channel. Relabelled as a census it keeps all of its value and loses none of its findings. Labelled as an allocation it falsifies B4 and breaks the document.

Two consequences follow and both are real costs. First, section 5a.3's closing inference, that the layer modulates and never assigns because every channel is occupied, is no longer available, since the premise was the allocation. The modulation rule was already struck under ruling A; this removes its stated justification as well. Second, section 6.2's eighteen does not gain any bias quantities, which is what the Skeptic feared, but it loses some for a different reason, which is finding S17, and the recount is required independently.

On S17 I have narrowed the Skeptic and it is worth saying why, because the narrowing protects a conclusion he himself expects to survive. B4 counts independent state quantities carried by a stave. Section 6.2 counts six rendered parameters per voice, and three of those six carry two independent quantities between them, since dynamics is a function of trauma alone and tempo and metre are both functions of entropy alone [3]. S3 section 2.6 refuses to discharge B4 for exactly this reason and warns in its own words that the agreement between two to three and three is a coincidence of numbers [3]. So the figure eighteen must be recomputed and relabelled. What does not follow is the Skeptic's claim that D53 is computed on the wrong total. D53 requires each stave to declare two or three quantities and every reduction to carry its coverage figure. That rule depends on the total exceeding the budget, which it does on any count anyone can construct, and not on the total's value. D53 survives untouched and the author does not need to reopen it.

The cost of ruling B, stated. B4 survives, which sounds like the comfortable outcome and is not. It survives because the mechanism that would have falsified it has been struck, so the programme gains no evidence about B4 from revision 7 and B4 remains what section 10 says it is, an unmeasured estimate now carrying more weight than before. Section 6.2 is right that settling B4 is worth the study it has never justified, and that sentence is now the most load-bearing one in the section.

### C. The 31,078 rows

I verified this against the score files before ruling and it is upheld in full. Every element of it reproduces [12].

The naming first. `s6_three_clef.py` runs over the seven CSVs and asserts a total of 31,078 rows [11]. S4 section 3.5 rules on that figure in words written for this use: the figure 31,078 is right as a row count and wrong as a description, it is not a number of dramatic beats, the files are not seven plays, and no paper in this series should cite it as either, and where a later section needs to refer to the artefact it is called the seven score files or the 31,078 rows, with the word beat reserved for the engine's own column name [4]. The design's status block, section 6.2 and reference [11] all call them beats, and the script's own header does too. That is a direct breach of a ruling in a completed paper of the same series.

The substance is worse than the naming and I checked the counts myself. S4 section 3.2 establishes by a printed contents-block test that three of the seven files are anthologies: the Cherry Orchard file is the Chekhov second series and holds eight plays, the Miss Julie file is the Strindberg second series and holds five, and the Oedipus file is the whole Theban trilogy [4]. Section 6.2 presents six rows as six works. Recomputing coverage on the three files that are single plays with a real speaker set, which are A Doll's House, Hamlet and Macbeth, gives top-two shares of 69.2, 50.1 and 39.0 per cent [12]. The range over works is 39 to 69 per cent and one of three falls below half. The design's headline, between 8 and 69 per cent and on four of the six less than half, is wrong twice: at its lower anchor and in its fraction. The 8.2 per cent that carries the whole argument is the top-two share of a file holding eight plays with eight disjoint casts, and the 82 speakers beside it are eight casts counted as one.

The second contamination reproduces as well. The aggregate includes all 3,425 rows of the King Lear file, which S4 section 3.3 and the design's own section 4.1 both record as a total parse failure with 3,424 rows assigned to the non-speaker `STAGE` [4], [6]. Excluding it moves the tempo figure from 32.2 to 33.9 per cent and the metre figure from 25.7 to 26.6, with dynamics unchanged at 4.5 [12]. The magnitude is small and the principle is not, because section 4.1 mandates that the product refuse to render below a parse-confidence threshold and section 8 mandates that non-dialogue spans be marked and excluded. Section 6.2 measures its legibility budgets on a file the product it specifies would refuse, and on several hundred rows of Project Gutenberg licence that S4 found the engine scoring as a catastrophe, trauma rising to 0.80 over 268 and 269 consecutive rows [4]. The script's `assert TOTAL == 31078` makes this mandatory rather than accidental, since applying the design's own exclusion rule would fail the script, and reference [11] presents that assertion as a reproducibility guard. It is a guard that enforces the contamination and it has to be rewritten as an assertion over the retained rows.

The third element is provenance and it is the one that limits what any of these figures can mean. S4 section 2 establishes that these columns were produced by `mpn_engine`, which implements no psychology on the path that produced the scores, computing trauma as a row-position ramp plus keyword hits and entropy as a punctuation tally, and sharing no code with the Conductor the theory papers describe [4]. I confirmed the distributional consequence directly: `ENTROPY_H` takes fourteen distinct values over the whole corpus and is exactly 0.3 on 22,611 rows, 72.8 per cent, and 94.6 per cent of the 10,020 tempo-band changes have 0.3 on one side of them [12]. The event-density figures are therefore properties of one generator's output distribution and not of S3's laws, and they must be reported as such.

One sub-claim of the Skeptic's I do not adopt, and I say so because it would otherwise foreclose work the design is entitled to do. He argues that section 6.1 forbids this move in its own words, since 6.1 withdraws the corpus-generator claim on the ground that a corpus of Studio outputs is a corpus of system outputs. That rule forbids treating system outputs as evidence about the world. Applying S3's normative laws to a stream of numbers to ask how often a notational channel would change is not evidence about the world, it is a test on a distribution, and a test on a distribution is a legitimate thing to run on an artefact. The defect is that the distribution belongs to one generator, is degenerate on one of its two inputs, and is contaminated by a parse failure and two licence blocks. That is a declaration the section owes, not a prohibition it breached.

The restated position, which the author can lift into revision 8. Two staves render between 39 and 69 per cent of a single play and on one of the three below half; the figure is measured on three plays and not on six works; the 8.2 per cent is withdrawn as a coverage figure and may be reported only as the top-two share of an eight-play anthology, where it measures the anthology and not any play. ### D. The timbre capacity

The convergence failure reproduces exactly and then gets worse under a harder search, which is the strongest possible confirmation of the Skeptic's method and the weakest possible outcome for the published table.

`s6_timbre_capacity.py` optimises with 60 restarts of 1,500 steps at seed 20260913 plus $N$ [10]. Re-running the identical procedure at two other seeds and at 300 restarts of 3,000 steps reproduces the Skeptic's table cell for cell [12]. The decisive cell is $N = 5$ at seed 11111, which returns 1.4057, below the $N = 6$ value of 1.4142. That is impossible for the true optimum, because any optimal six-set contains a five-set and the max-min curve is therefore non-increasing in $N$ by construction. The search returned a value the problem forbids. The published table is not a set of optima. It is a set of lower bounds whose tightness varies with the seed, and the design prints it as a curve and reasons from its shape.

The substance survives and I strengthened the evidence for it. An exhaustive search over the sixteen corners of the DISC cube, which is exact and needs no search at all, returns 1.414214 at $N = 3, 4, 5$ and 6 and exactly 1.0 at $N = 7$ and 8 [12]. A 1,200-restart search at 4,000 steps returns 1.414214 at both $N = 5$ and $N = 6$. So the flat at the square root of two from five to six is real, the six far vertices are an optimal six, and sections 5b.1 and 5b.2, which I also reproduced, are correct and are the strongest new material in the document.

Two printed figures do not survive and one of them the Skeptic understated. The $N = 4$ entry of 1.4231 is a lower bound that rises to at least 1.4247 under 300 restarts, so the curve is not flat from four as the table implies. The $N = 7$ entry of 1.0123 is worse: my 1,200-restart search returns **1.053712**, which beats both the published figure and the Skeptic's own best of 1.0360 [12]. The drop from $N = 6$ to $N = 7$ is therefore at most 25.5 per cent on present evidence, and it falls every time somebody searches harder. D50 cites "falls 28 per cent at the seventh" as a settled figure. It is not a figure at all; it is an artefact of an unconverged search, and it must simply be deleted rather than corrected, because no value can be printed that the next search will not move.

Now the word capacity, which is the question the brief asks and which is separable from the convergence problem. Section 5b.5 concedes that the perceptual resolution $\varepsilon$ is not ours to invent and is unmeasured. The script's own inversion table shows six characters to be the answer only for $\varepsilon$ in the interval between the $N = 7$ value and the square root of two, which on my numbers is $(1.0537, 1.4142]$, a window of width 0.36 in a space of diameter 2. At $\varepsilon = 1.0$ the channel holds seven, at 1.45 it holds three, at 1.6 it holds two. What the script found, stated exactly, is that the max-min separation curve for this map has its only sharp kink between six and seven. That is a fact about packing in a rhombic dodecahedron and it is a good one. It is not a capacity, and it becomes one only if an unmeasured quantity happens to land in a window about a fifth of the width of the space, whose lower edge moves whenever the search improves.

So "six is the capacity" cannot stand and the status block's claim that [10] finds the timbre channel's capacity to be exactly six characters must go, as must D50's statement of it as a decision. What may stand, and what is worth standing, is this: the channel's best minimum separation is flat at the square root of two up to six characters and falls at the seventh, so six is the largest cast that costs nothing a five-character cast does not already cost, conditional on the perceptual resolution lying at or below the square root of two, which is unmeasured and which the experiment of section 5b.5 fixes in an afternoon. That sentence is true, it is conditional in the right place, and section 5b.5 already says the conditional four paragraphs later, which is why the status block currently contradicts its own section.

Two further requirements travel with this. The table in 5b.3 must be relabelled as lower bounds from a seeded search, the exhaustive corner result must be printed beside it since it is exact and confirms the flat, and the search must be re-run at a restart count at which the curve is non-increasing in $N$, which is the only convergence test this problem needs and which the script can assert on itself. And D51's remedy must be reconsidered under finding S11, which I uphold: keying the instrument family to the six canonical contrast directions makes the label a deterministic function of the three coordinates it labels, so it carries no information those coordinates do not already carry, and S3 section 2.6 put $F$ there precisely to hold the categorical specificities that lie on no shared dimension at all [3]. The argmax tie that section 5b.4 diagnoses is real and the diagnosis is right; the remedy solves it by deleting the channel. I strike the Skeptic's tail claim that the sole timbre bias is not a cognitive bias: CB-012 is Authority Bias, a catalogued Atlas entry in the Social domain [12]. What is true is that the device attached to it was inherited by strict name match from the implementation's Cialdini entry, which decision 8 rules a different kind of object, and that crossing is the one S3 section 5 already declares.

The cost. D50 loses its headline number and its decision form. Item 5a of section 10a loses its claim that the six canonical profiles need no further work, twice over, once here and once to Guardian finding 4. The capacity result becomes a conditional, which is what it always was, and the experiment of 5b.5 moves from being the cheapest outstanding experiment to being the one the whole section's claim now rests on.

## 4. The three rejections, argued

**Finding S14, that the collision the design keeps has the same defect as the one it strikes.** The Skeptic is right about the words. BL-7 was found by a parameter-level test, since zero-risk bias closes a chord on the harmony channel and information bias adds a countermelody on the texture channel, so the contradiction S3's prose predicts cannot arise between them. The kept collision was assessed at the channel level, since normalcy and the planning fallacy are both tabled to rhythm. The granularity is inconsistent and item 9 of the change list requires the design to state its rule.

He is wrong about the thing. His argument that the two do not meet rests on S3 section 2.2's finding that the jump sets of tempo and metre do not coincide at any boundary [3]. That is a fact about where the two parameters change as entropy moves, and it is not a fact about whether the two devices are present in the same passage. A character rated high in both biases has both devices active at once, and what the listener hears is one voice holding a metre through a disturbance while beginning a phrase at a tempo it cannot finish. That is not two effects failing to meet. It is exactly the self-contradiction the design predicted, in one voice, in one passage, and it is a better prediction than the design's own wording suggests, since it is a contradiction between stability and speed, which is the distinction S3 section 2.2 says the whole rhythm channel is about. Accepting the finding would delete a true and testable prediction and replace it with nothing. That is the trade this role exists to refuse.

**Finding G3, that the budget formula hands a full band to every single-bias channel.** The Guardian is right that dividing one band step by the number of biases returns the whole band on timbre, mode and intervals, and right that section 5a.4's own sentence about a perturbation wider than a band then condemns its own formula. What makes this a rejection rather than a narrowing is the remedy and the relation to finding S2.

The Guardian proposes a rule refinement for one-entry channels and calls the finding not blocking on that ground. Finding S2, which I uphold, shows that the defect is not a property of one-entry channels at all. On a piecewise-constant output every bounded perturbation is a categorical reassignment that fires with some probability, so the modulation-assignment distinction has no content on any channel in the table, whether it carries one bias or eight. The two remedies contradict: one patches three rows, the other strikes the mechanism. Only one survives, and it is not the patch. Adopting G3 would tell an author that seven of the eight channels are sound and three rows need a refinement, which is the opposite of true and would cost a revision to discover. The Guardian's underlying observation is preserved inside ruling A, where it belongs, as one more demonstration that the boundary the section draws does not exist.

**Finding U8, that every per-person measure now reaches the surface least able to discount one.** This is rejected on three grounds and none of them is that it is inconvenient.

The first is that revision 7 did not do what the finding attributes to it. Section 6.4 has specified Expression as Layer 1 only since revision 1, and floor share, turn count, latency, overlap and backchannel are Layer 1 measures by section 4.2's own definition. They reached Expression before revision 7 and by exactly the same silence. D32, which the finding treats as the mitigation that was removed, was a restriction on participant mode and never applied to Expression. So the finding describes a standing property of the surface and attributes it to a change.

The second is the premise. The Advocate's moment is any rendering of a conversation the user was in, and what the user sees is their own floor share beside someone else's. Under the author's ruling of 13 September 2026 the material is synthetic dialogue the programme generates, so there is no conversation the Expression reader was in. I am not entitled to set that ruling aside, and a finding whose harm depends on setting it aside is not available to me.

The third is that the residue is a limitation the document already declares. What survives when the first two grounds are applied is the claim that music is read affectively and that a line of text saying no claim is intended does not survive contact with a minor key. Section 3 of the design says this in those words, section 10 records it as the largest untested assertion in the design, and D27 as re-scoped by D42 makes listener testing a condition of $\Psi$ shipping on any surface. The Advocate cites all three and then treats the gap as newly blocking.

One thing in the finding is sound and I have carried it forward as item 45 rather than lose it: D42's test is specified as a test of $\Psi$'s affective reading and does not say whether it covers a named per-person comparison. That is a scope hole in a decision, worth a clause, and it is not a blocking defect of revision 7.

## 5. Disposition

**STOP.**

Upheld 20, upheld and narrowed 22, rejected 3, of forty-five findings ruled.

Revision 7 is not one revision from implementable and the reason is not the count. It is that three of the six restored capabilities lose their stated mechanism rather than their numbers. The bias layer's modulation rule is struck under ruling A, on two independent grounds found by two reviewers who were not looking for the same thing, and what returns in version one is the layer's detector and not its sound. The three-clef score's legibility argument is measured on an artefact that a completed paper in this series forbids describing the way this document describes it, and its headline coverage figure is wrong at its lower anchor and in its fraction, which I confirmed against the score files before ruling. The timbre channel's capacity claim is conditional on an unmeasured number in a window one fifth of the width of the space, and the search that produced its curve is demonstrably below convergence, which I reproduced and then worsened.

Against that, what stands is worth naming, because a STOP that does not say what survives tells an author to reopen everything. Sections 5b.1 and 5b.2 are correct, I reproduced every figure in them, and they are the best new material in the document. The six-vertex optimal assignment at $N = 6$ survives an exhaustive search that needs no seed at all. BL-7 is a genuine finding that reading could not have produced, and BL-1, BL-4, BL-5 and BL-6 survive ruling A intact and are the route to the first evidence this layer has ever had. The dyad-churn measurement is sound and R4's rejection is correctly reasoned. D53's rule survives every attack made on the number behind it. B4 survives, for a reason that gives the programme no comfort: the mechanism that would have falsified it has been struck, so B4 is still the unmeasured estimate section 10 says it is, now carrying more weight than before.

Two decisions belong to the author and neither can be taken by a designer or an arbiter. The first is BL-8, which channel the bias layer occupies given that it cannot occupy $\Phi$'s, because roughly half the new material follows from the answer. The second is whether to adopt MPN-NOTE-05's two recommendations, since $k_{\max} = 23$ with the absolute form closes the harmonic budget and restores A11's decomposability for the one parameter that lacks it, and the rounding recommendation removes mode from the set of channels any budget can be written for [8]. Revision 8 should not be drafted before both are answered.
