## 1. What a status in this ledger means

Four statuses are used and they are not interchangeable.

**VERIFIED** means the target was opened and the specific claim the paper cites it for was found in it. For a script, it means the script was run or its source searched for the quantity in question, and the quantity is there.

**VERIFIED, CORRECTED** means the target was opened, the claim was found, and something about the citation as the paper stated it was wrong and has been fixed in revision 2. The correction is stated in the row.

**UNSOURCED** means the paper states an external quantitative figure and no publication for it could be found, by the corpus or by an independent search. It is not a soft form of verified. Under arbitration ruling 4.7 the paper now says so on its own face at section 4.3, and the tracing is filed as work queue item S5-12, which belongs to the design because the design is where the figure originates.

**INHERITED** means the paper cites a figure that another document in this programme measured, that document has been opened, and the figure is there. The measurement is that document's and this ledger does not re-derive it.

## 2. The four external quantitative claims, which are why this ledger is blocking

Arbitration ruling 4.7 found that four external figures reach this paper through design section 4.1 and that the design carries no reference for any of them. The design's sentence, at `DESIGN-UNIFIED-FRAMEWORK-rev8.md:76`, is: "The best published streaming diarisation error rate on DIHARD III is 19.8 per cent, the two vendors bundling diarisation with transcription are at 39.1 and 39.2 per cent, meeting-benchmark word error runs from 35 to 46 per cent, and a 2025 benchmark scored with overlap included finds missed speech the dominant failure across all models."

**Three of the four are now sourced, and they are one source rather than three.** An independent search found a published streaming diarisation benchmark run on DIHARD III that reports, across all languages: pyannote API at 19.8 per cent DER, Speechmatics real-time v2 at 31.3, Deepgram Nova 3 at 39.1 and AssemblyAI Universal Streaming v3 at 39.2. That single table supplies the design's 19.8 figure and both of its "two vendors" figures at 39.1 and 39.2. The same benchmark supplies the fourth clause as well: it reports that pyannote leaves 7.71 per cent of speech unattributed while the other systems miss between 19.70 and 25.26 per cent, which is the "missed speech the dominant failure" finding, scored with overlap included. So the design's four clauses are three figures and a finding, and all four come from one benchmark [23].

**One is not sourced, and it is the one the companion paper leans on hardest.** The meeting-benchmark word error range of 35 to 46 per cent could not be traced to any publication. The nearest published material found describes multi-speaker meeting transcription scored as concatenated minimum-permutation word error rate and puts it "often in the 40 to 60 per cent range or higher" on AMI and ICSI, without attributing a figure to a system or a benchmark, which is a different metric over a different range and cannot stand in for the design's number. **It is recorded as UNSOURCED.** No citation has been invented for it, and revision 2 does not assert it as measured.

That matters beyond bookkeeping, because MPN-S6 section 6 uses this exact figure to argue that class B inherits the recognition problem unchanged. Arbitration ruling 4.7 directs both papers to mark it, and item S5-12 directs the design to trace it or mark it at source.

## 4. What this ledger does not close

One figure of the four remains unsourced, so arbitration ruling 4.7 is discharged in the manner the ruling itself directs, which is by marking it, and not by sourcing it. Whether the figure is right is unknown. If it is wrong, what moves is the design's argument that class B inherits the recognition problem unchanged from class C, and MPN-S6 section 6 moves with it. Nothing in MPN-S5's own measured content depends on it: every corpus figure in this paper comes from the seven scored plays through the four named generators, and all of those reproduced.

The other thing this ledger does not do is make the paper right. Twenty-two references verifying is a statement about provenance. It says the numbers came from where the paper says they came from. It says nothing about whether the four use cases work, and nothing at all about the caveat that governs the whole series: not one listener has been asked anything.
