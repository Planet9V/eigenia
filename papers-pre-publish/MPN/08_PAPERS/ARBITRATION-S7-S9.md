## 1. The ruling in one paragraph

All three reviewers went to the corpus rather than reasoning from the design, and what they found is the same thing three times: **the design nominated as new work three things the programme had already done, ruled, or proved impossible.** That is a failure of research, not of ambition. The subjects are right, the audiences are right, and the per-profession monograph structure is right. What has to change is what each paper claims to contribute. The Skeptic's REJECT is not adopted, because a design whose subjects hold and whose contributions are misidentified is revised rather than abandoned; his findings are adopted almost entire.

## 5. A correction to a document published yesterday

The Guardian found, and this arbiter verified independently, that `MPN-NOTE-06-persona-scaling.md` is wrong in three places.

**The sixteen DISC corners give fifteen distinct timbre images, not sixteen.** `(0,0,0,0)` and `(1,1,1,1)` map to the same point, the timbre origin, because the map has nullity one and the null direction is overall profile magnitude.

**The append-only plateau runs to N = 15, not 14.** Verified: 0.866025 holds from 8 through 15.

**There is a cliff at 16 that the note does not record.** 0.866025 falls to 0.559017, a **35.4 per cent** drop in one step, against the 13.4 per cent the note calls the worst cost in its range. The cause is that the corner set is exhausted exactly at 15 and the sixteenth persona must go into the interior.

**One consequence S9 part two inherits.** At seven personas the walk places the flat profile. A machine joining a six-human team as the seventh is assigned the neutral voice by the geometry, and a human whose profile is flat-high is timbrally indistinguishable from it.
