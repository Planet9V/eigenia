## Findings

### 1. The mapping sentence is false on any output the bias layer has touched

The user is every user, because D26 made this sentence the design's central honesty device on every surface. The moment is the instant after the bias layer ships: a dramaturg, a producer, a therapist or an Expression reader looks at the face of an output and reads either *this renders the shape of the exchange and says nothing about anyone's state* or *this renders a state a person rated*.

Revision 7 put a third writer on that output. The engine diagram in section 5 has Layer 3 modulating the shared renderers directly, and D45 confirms the bias layer perturbs parameters Φ has already set. So a Φ output carrying bias modulation is no longer only a state a person rated, and a Ψ output carrying it is no longer a rendering that says nothing about anyone's state, because a bias attribution is a claim about a speaker's cognition and nothing else. Both sentences were written in revision 3 for a two-mapping world and neither was revisited when the third writer came back. There is no third sentence, and the design gives the reader no way to tell a clean output from a modulated one.

What goes wrong is precise and it is the failure D26 was written to prevent. Two categorically different outputs look identical, and the difference is now invisible rather than one Greek letter wide. The user who is most damaged is the therapist, whose sentence is the one that says a human rated this.

**BLOCKING.**

### 2. The proposal mark is a text device and the bias layer renders as music

The user is a panel producer with Live open on generated panel material. The moment is section 6.2's third stave, which the design says is where the tension and the bias layer land. What the producer sees, or rather hears, is a sforzando, a pedal tone, an ostinato, a closing chord or a fragmenting texture, arriving against a named panellist's turn.

Section 4.4 defends Layer 3 with three rules: a proposal is marked wherever it appears, it never renders without the Layer 0 material that prompted it, and it is never the only thing on screen. Every one of those rules is written for a label on a screen. None of them can attach to a musical event. A stave cannot carry a hedge, and the second and third rules are satisfied trivially and uselessly in the musical channel, because the Layer 0 turn is always present in a running score and the score is never the only thing on screen either. The marking therefore does not do the work section 4.4 claims for the one channel section 5a actually specifies.

It is worse than unmarked. Section 5a.4's whole discipline is to make the perturbation large enough to be audible against the band it sits in, and the layer's success condition is that a listener hears the device. An audible, deliberately placed musical event attached to one person's turn is read as an assertion about that person, and the producer has no marked and unmarked pair to compare it against. The design's own precedent is against the marking rule working: section 4.4 gives as its reason for the rule that a keyword counter's output was read as a measurement through three revisions of a paper by expert readers who had the source in front of them. That is evidence the mark fails, cited in support of the mark.

**BLOCKING.**

### 3. The bias layer has no abstention and no rate, so it reads as a count per panellist

The user is the same panel producer, now in Studio, reading the arc of a sixty-minute panel rather than watching it run. The moment is the export or the scroll. What they see is a list of turns with bias names attached, over four named panellists.

Section 5a specifies thirty biases, four collisions, eight channels, a perturbation budget and a locality partition. It never specifies what a turn with no bias looks like, whether the layer may return nothing, how many of the thirty may fire on one turn, or what the expected rate is on ordinary material. Without a stated rate there is no denominator on the screen, and a reader supplies the only one available, which is the other panellists. Three confirmation-bias hits on the second panellist and none on the first is a ranking of two people by a cognitive defect, assembled by the reader out of material the design never intended as a comparison and never labelled as one.

This is the per-person comparative display of D32 arriving by a different door. D32 was voided on the ground that the people it would rank do not exist, and the argument is about the material. The mechanism above is a property of the layer and the surface, and it will do the same thing to any four names the material contains, whoever wrote them. Note also that item BL-1 leaves fourteen of the thirty devices with no stated dependence on the state, which means fourteen of them currently fire always, never, or on a rule nobody has written down. A reader counting hits per name is counting that.

**BLOCKING.**

### 4. The third stave does not carry the speakers the coverage figure is about

The user is a dramaturg with a play in Live or Studio. The moment is the two-voice reduction, and what they see is the coverage figure D53 puts on the face of the output: on the Cherry Orchard file, that the score covers 8.2 per cent of the work.

Section 6.2 tells this user what to do about it. It says the coverage measurement is the strongest argument for the third stave there is, and that the third clef is not an elegance but the only place the other half of the material can go. Four paragraphs later the same section says what the third stave actually renders, which is the relation between the two principal voices through Ψ. Those are not the same claim. The third stave carries the relation of the selected dyad, so it adds nothing whatever to coverage of the eighty other speakers in the Cherry Orchard file. The dramaturg is told the missing material has a home and it does not have one.

What goes wrong is that the user acts on the promise. They keep the third stave, believing the 92 per cent is in it, and the only place the full speaker set actually appears is the graph, which LS-2 mentions in a subordinate clause and which finding 13 shows is unspecified. The coverage figure is correct arithmetic attached to a false remedy, and the remedy is the one the section leads with.

**BLOCKING.**

### 5. The coverage figure arrives after the render, not before the choice

Same user, same play, one step earlier. D53 requires the coverage figure on the face of the output, which means the dramaturg has already ingested the work, set five Layer 1 settings, declared two or three quantities per stave under LS-3, chosen a damping constant under LS-1, selected a dyad and rendered. Only then do they learn that the thing they configured shows 8.2 per cent of their play.

A number is usable where a decision is made. This one is printed where a decision has been completed. The design has the vocabulary to handle the case and does not use it: section 8a sorts every refusal into four kinds and gives each one action, and a work whose top two speakers cover 8.2 per cent is squarely the first kind, your material, whose action is to use different material or, here, to use the graph. Nothing in section 6.2 or D53 reaches for that machinery, so a correct number sits in the one place where the user can do nothing with it except start again.

This is not blocking because the number exists and the placement is changeable without disturbing anything else in the design.

**Not blocking.**

### 6. LS-3 creates a pre-session configuration act with no budget and no default

The user is a director or a discourse researcher opening Live for the first time. The moment is before the session, because D53 requires each stave to declare its two or three quantities in advance, and LS-1 requires the damping constant to be a stated parameter rather than a hidden default.

What they meet is a choice of two or three items from eighteen, repeated per stave, made before hearing anything, by a user the design itself says is not defined by musical literacy. The eighteen are mode, dynamics, tempo, metre, texture and timbre for each of two voices, plus six interaction measures. No default set is named, no preview exists, and section 10 records that the five Layer 1 defaults, the damping constant and the staleness horizon all have no value anywhere. The design has demonstrated that it knows how to constrain this kind of act, because the PRD gives the therapist an explicit budget of ten seconds and five interactions and calls it a requirement rather than a preference. Live gets no budget at all, and revision 7 added three configuration acts to it.

Findings 10 and 37 of the earlier review, accepted as D37, were about exactly this accumulation on the dramaturg's first run. Revision 7 lengthened that page rather than shortening it.

Not blocking, because the design states LS-3 as a proposal to be tried against readers and a budget can be set without changing the architecture. It should not survive another revision unbudgeted.

**Not blocking.**

### 7. The participant-mode restoration is written into the surface and justified by the material

The user is whoever is in the room when item 6a of section 10a runs. The moment is the first class B session, which section 7.6 describes as a microphone per speaker and schedules as the first live-capture class because its attribution is trustworthy.

Section 7.4 restores every per-person measure to the shared display: floor share, turn count, mean turn length, latency before taking the floor, overlap initiated and received, backchannel given and received, degree in the adjacency graph, all attributable by name. The stated reason is a property of the material, that the people it would have ranked do not exist. The restoration itself is written as a property of the surface, with no condition on ingest class anywhere in 7.4, in D55, in D41, or in the silent-participant row of section 8, which an implementer will copy verbatim.

The design knows how to scope a capability to an ingest class, and does it twice in the same document. Section 4.2 withdraws the overlap measures on class C and calls that a capability difference between ingest classes. Section 6.2 makes the score synchronous on classes A and B and trailing on class C. Section 7.4 is the one place where a restriction was deleted rather than made conditional, and it is the place where the consequence lands on people rather than on a number. Follow the document's own build order and a microphone per speaker is sitting under a display specified to rank every person at the table by how much of the floor they took and how often they were talked over, with no clause anywhere that stops it, because the clause that would have was voided as a harm rule about a room that section 7.6 then schedules into existence.

This is not a rights argument and it does not need to be. It is the design's own rule that a capability must be scoped to the class of material that supports it, applied consistently.

**BLOCKING.**

### 8. Every per-person measure now reaches the surface least able to discount one

The user is the Expression audience, described in section 6.4 as anyone who finds conversational dynamics easier to read as sound or image than to infer from speech, with autism named. The moment is any rendering of a conversation the user was in.

Section 6.4 specifies Expression as Layer 1 only and says nothing about per-person comparative measures in either direction. Layer 1 is exactly where the restored per-person measures live, and section 7.4 has just declared all of them renderable and attributable by name in the shared renderer set that section 5 gives every surface. So the measures reach this surface by default and by silence, and the design has never asked the question for this audience. It asked it once for participants in a room, answered it with D32, and then voided the answer on a premise about the room rather than about the reader.

What the user sees is their own floor share beside someone else's, their overlap-received count, their latency before taking the floor, and their backchannel count, rendered as sound or image, on a surface whose whole purpose is that sound and image are easier for this reader to take literally than speech is. Section 3 of the design already concedes the mechanism: the codomain is music, music is read affectively, and a line of text saying no claim is intended does not survive contact with a minor key. Section 10 repeats it and calls the untested claim the largest in the design. The one mitigation the design has, D42's listener testing of Ψ as a condition of shipping on any surface, tests the affective reading of the interaction mapping. It does not test what a named per-person comparison does to this reader, and nothing else does either.

**BLOCKING.**

### 9. Expression is promised a score it has no Layer 2 input to fill

Same user, one step further in. The moment is the sentence section 6.4 now carries, which section 6.2 supplies: on generated or recorded material the score runs with the dialogue, and the surface says so.

Section 6.2 then specifies what the score is. Two staves render the two principal voices' states through Φ, and a third renders the relation through Ψ. Φ runs only where a human has rated a state, which is Layer 2. Expression is Layer 1 only, and its user has no rating instrument, no anchors and no reason to have either. So two of the three staves have no input on this surface and the document never says what happens to them.

What the user sees is undefined by the document, and the two candidate outcomes are both bad. Either the score is mostly empty, which the design's own repeated rule forbids, that a flat output from a missing input and a flat output from a flat conversation must never look alike, or the surface quietly renders something else on those staves and Expression is no longer Layer 1 only. Revision 7 restored the score and extended its sentence to this surface without checking whether this surface can fill it.

**BLOCKING.**

### 10. Participant mode has no reader on the material it is scoped to

The user is the one section 7.4 names, being the exchange's own participants reading the display while the exchange runs. The moment is version one on synthetic material.

Generated dialogue has no participants who can read anything. The mode is specified in full, with every per-person measure restored, a staleness horizon, a discontinuity rule and a place in the build order at item 6, and on the material the scope ruling makes primary it has nobody in front of it. PM-1, described as the most interesting thing the restoration makes possible, has the same shape: running the same generated dialogue through the same deterministic engine with and without a display in the loop changes nothing in the dialogue, because the dialogue is fixed and the engine is deterministic by requirement. The thing an observer effect acts on is a reader, and the experiment as written has none.

So the design has restored a fully specified per-person ranking display, scheduled it, and left the question of who reads it to be answered later by finding 7. That ordering is the problem more than the emptiness is.

Not blocking on its own, since building a mode with no current user costs the named users nothing directly. It is the reason finding 7 is blocking.

**Not blocking.**

### 11. The therapist is shown an audible fraction with no threshold, no action and no provenance

The user is the music therapist at the desk. The moment is gate G1, which requires DISC assigned per character so that the timbre channel renders, with the audible-fraction check of PRD section 7.6 computed and shown.

What the therapist sees is a ratio. PRD 7.6 says an assignment whose ratio comes out near zero is declared on the therapist's screen. Near zero has no value in either document, and neither does near one, and the design's section 10 lists the same class of missing number six times over. The therapist is shown a quantity with no threshold against which to read it, no stated action, and no place in the four-kind refusal vocabulary the design built for exactly this, so they cannot tell whether a low ratio is their material, the tool's limit, something not specified yet, or a fault.

They also cannot act on it if they read it correctly. PRD section 14 puts the assign-for-meaning against assign-for-audibility decision explicitly with the author, and the author is not in the room. The therapist holds a number whose only remedy belongs to somebody else, which inverts D38's own principle, that the person who needs to know the material changed is the one who will otherwise read a different response from a client as a change in the client.

The provenance gap is the sharpest part. Nothing on the screen says whether a profile was assigned because it describes the character or because it sits on one of the six canonical vertices, which section 5b.3 defines as two coordinates at the top of the scale and two at the bottom. A profile chosen for audibility is an extreme profile by construction, and the therapist reading it has no way to know that the extremity is a property of the channel rather than of the character. PRD section 5.2 compounds this: it binds every surface the therapist reads to a named vocabulary and the four DISC coordinates are not in that table, so the therapist is shown a ratio derived from four quantities the product does not permit itself to name to them.

**BLOCKING.**

### 12. Layer 3 is on by default and the Instrument's rule is that everything optional is off

The same therapist, at the same desk, one layer down. The moment is the first rendered passage and the explanation printed under it.

Design section 4.4 says Layer 3 is built, it is on, and it is where bias and psychometric-trait proposals live. The engine diagram routes Layer 3 straight into the shared renderers. Design section 6.3 says the Instrument shares the renderers, the state model and the explanation mechanism. PRD section 6.2 says the opposite in its own terms: the product is fully usable with every optional input switched off, it ships with all of them off by default, that binds each input separately rather than the analyser as a class, and it is a requirement rather than a preference. Neither document says which rule governs the Instrument, and the PRD does not mention Layer 3 or the bias layer anywhere.

Three things the therapist relies on fail if Layer 3 reaches the shared renderer under the design's default. PRD section 7.4 requires the explanation to name every rule that fired and every value it read, and the bias devices are rules the therapist never set and the PRD has never described. PRD section 7.5 requires determinism, and the explanation's closing sentence to a client or their advocate is that setting the same five ratings again produces the same music, which a model proposal reading text cannot promise. And PRD section 5.2's naming rule, enforced by a golden-output test precisely because the explanation is generated, has no entries for anything the bias layer would need to say.

The therapist meets this as a passage that does not sound like their ratings and an explanation that does not account for it, which is the exact situation the explanation output exists to prevent.

**BLOCKING.**

### 13. The demoted renderer is fully specified and the primary one is not specified at all

This is the test of what section 10 says about itself. Section 10 records the demotion of the score to a secondary renderer as evidence against the thesis rather than as a user-interface decision, on the ground that on the two surfaces with the most clearly specified users the graph answered their question and the music did not. D34 and D43 stand, and 10a restates that the demotion stands. Revision 7 restores the three-clef score. As statements the two are consistent, and the document is not caught contradicting itself.

What the user sees is not consistent. Section 6.2 is the longest specification of any surface in the document and all of it is about the secondary renderer: three measured legibility constraints, an event-density budget, a coverage table, a churn table, an information budget, a damping requirement, a per-stave declaration and three work items. The primary renderer for those same two surfaces gets one clause in section 6.2 saying the graph leads, one clause in LS-2 saying it carries the full speaker set, and no specification anywhere in the document. It has no legibility budget, no declared quantities, no coverage statement, no layout, no work item and no place in section 10's list of what the design does not yet have.

So the dramaturg, the director, the producer and the discourse researcher, who are the users section 10 says the graph serves, open Live and Studio and find a fully worked surface they have been told is not for them, and a one-clause mention of the thing that is. Finding 4 has just made the graph load-bearing for the coverage answer as well. And the evidence that would settle section 10's own claim is never collected: no item in section 10a puts either renderer in front of a dramaturg or a producer, and the three studies the design does schedule concern the affective reading of Ψ, timbre discrimination and B4's quantities per stave. Section 10 says the demotion is evidence against the thesis if it survives contact with real users, and revision 7 arranges no contact with any.

**BLOCKING.**

## Disposition

**REVISE.** Ten findings are blocking: 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12 and 13. Findings 5, 6 and 10 are not blocking and should not survive another revision unanswered.

The pattern across the blocking ten is one thing said three ways. Revision 7's restorations are each justified by a property of the material, which is that the subjects are synthetic, and each is written into a surface, which outlives any particular material. Section 4.2 and section 6.2 already show the document knows how to scope a capability to an ingest class and say so on the output. Sections 5a, 6.2, 7.4 and 7.6 do not, and the users who meet the consequence are the panel producer reading a bias attribution with no mark that music can carry, the dramaturg told the third stave holds the 92 per cent it does not hold, the Expression reader handed a per-person comparison on the surface least able to discount one, and the therapist shown a number they cannot act on under a default nobody has reconciled.

Blocking count: **10**.
