## 1. The question, and why it exists

Change 5 of the gate adds BL-8 to the work queue in these words: allocate a channel to the layer that $\Phi$ does not occupy, and respecify the thirty devices for it, noting that all thirty are currently written as modulations of $\Phi$ channels and that a single relation channel cannot carry thirty distinguishable devices [1]. The detection half is untouched and is built now as a marked textual proposal; what BL-8 would unblock is a musical rendering [1, ruling A].

B4 is what any answer has to survive, so it is quoted rather than summarised. Its claim: "Profile, state and bias are simultaneously legible because they are assigned to channels that do not compete: the stable profile takes timbre and register, set once per character; the moving state takes dynamics and mode; and bias, which lives between speakers, takes the harmonic relation between staves rather than any channel on a single stave." Its formal content: "Channel assignment is fixed, declared, and counted against the per-stave budget. The bias channel is a between-stave quantity and therefore does not consume a within-stave channel." Its falsification clause: "Listeners cannot recover any layer above chance. Or the layers mask each other, so that recovering one degrades recovery of another. Or the between-stave channel turns out to be a within-stave channel in disguise, in which case the budget is exceeded after all." Its constraint: "The programme's notation budget allows two to three independent state channels per stave and about 6.6 recoverable bits per character per frame" [3].

Two things follow that shape every section below. B4 bounds a stave and says nothing about how many staves a reader can follow, so an option that adds a stave leaves the region where B4 carries evidence rather than spending B4's budget. And B4's second falsification clause is masking, not overspending, so an option that occupies a channel another layer already owns fails B4 even when it consumes no within-stave state channel.

One number this note supplies to a gap the gate left open. Change 28 records that the layer does not say how many of the thirty may fire on one turn [1]. For any staved option B4 supplies the answer, which is two or three, and that cap is no more a matter of taste than the budget it comes from.

## 3. Option (a), a fourth stave

$\Phi$ does not occupy a stave that does not exist, and this is the only candidate where test 1 is answered by construction rather than by a search. Section 6.2's score is two $\Phi$ staves and one $\Psi$ stave, and nothing in S3 sections 2.1 to 2.6 or section 3 assigns a parameter to a fourth [2], [4]. The question is what it costs to add one and what it carries once added.

What it carries is computed rather than estimated. Taking the thirty as the census now stands, which is the fourteen inherited devices with BL-1's drafted coordinates and S3's sixteen drafted mappings [5], [11], and applying the rule of section 8, sixteen can be stated on a dedicated stave without altering a note $\Phi$ wrote and fourteen cannot [9]. Of the sixteen, ten need only a single melodic line and six need a stave that carries simultaneous pitches. The distinguishability the gate doubts has two answers at two time scales. Sequentially, all sixteen are distinct gestures an analyst can tell apart. Simultaneously, the stave carries two or three quantities under B4 and no more, so at most three may fire at once, which is where the missing per-turn cap comes from.

Against B4 the option is honest and uncomfortable in the same breath. It spends none of the within-stave budget, since it adds a stave rather than crowding one, and it does not touch the between-stave relation, so none of B4's three falsification clauses fires on it. What it does instead is leave the region where B4 has anything to say. B4 is a per-stave estimate that section 6.2 already spends at three staves, eighteen quantities wanted against six to nine available [4]. A fourth stave takes the available count to eight or twelve, and the quantity that actually governs, which is how many simultaneous staves a reader can follow, is measured nowhere. Section 6.2 already states the risk at three, that three staves may read as three voices [4], and a fourth compounds a risk the design has stated and not measured.

The proposal mark is where this option is strongest, and the argument is structural rather than aesthetic. Ruling A kills modulation because a shift of 3.6 velocity units inside a rendered note is inseparable by construction from the value $\Phi$ computed [1]. On an exclusively occupied stave the separation is total: every mark on it came from the layer, so the stave is the boundary, and a stave carries a name, a brace and a label, which are text devices attached to musical objects. Rules two and three of section 4.4 are satisfied in a running score for the reason ruling A gives [1], [4]. Rule one, the one that killed modulation, is satisfiable here and on no option that shares a channel with $\Phi$.

Three things are unspecified before it could be built. The per-turn cap and the abstention rule, which change 28 already owes and which the design owns [1]. Whether the stave is one per rendered voice or one shared, which B3 forces rather than taste, since B3 indexes dyadic biases by speaker, other and turn and individual biases by speaker and turn [3]: a shared stave cannot attribute an individual bias to one of two speakers without an attribution device nobody has specified, and one stave per voice makes the score five staves. And the fourteen respecifications, which are authorial and which section 8 counts.

## 4. Option (b), the between-stave relation channel, discarded

This is the option B4's own claim names and S3 section 4.4 leans on, and it fails three tests. It is discarded, and two of the reasons are findings rather than restatements.

The first is that $\Phi$ occupies it completely, by determination rather than by writing. Two rendered staves have a vertical relation that is a function of the two staves and of nothing else. Each stave's pitches come from the registers through the leitmotif transformation, its key from the register thresholds and its chord quality from tension [2, sections 2.3 and 3]. Given two characters' states, the interval between the staves is fixed, and to move it something must move a note on one of them, which is a within-stave write. That is not an analogy for B4's third falsification clause, it is that clause: the between-stave channel turns out to be a within-stave channel in disguise [3].

The second is that the channel already has a claimant inside S3. Section 5, on the influence layer, says in its own words that where a bias modulates a character's own stave, an influence principle modulates the relation between two staves, which is exactly the channel B4 reserves for what happens between speakers and which no bias mapping in section 4 uses [2]. So section 4.4 claims the relation channel for the bias layer and section 5 assigns it to the influence layer and denies that any bias mapping uses it, in one paper. The six influence devices have the structure the channel wants, an ordered pair with a source and a target, and the bias devices do not. Putting the bias layer there masks the influence layer, which is B4's second falsification clause.

The third is capacity, and it is a number from the register rather than an impression. B3 partitions the thirty into dyadic and individual and expects the dyadic members to fall in the Atlas's Social domain [3]. The completed Atlas distributes as Perception 8, Decision 10, Social 6 and Memory 6 [9], [11], so at most six of the thirty are between-speaker quantities and the other twenty-four cannot be sited between staves without falsifying B3 as well as B4. Of those six, five have devices needing three or more voices rather than two, being groupthink, the halo effect, in-group bias, the bandwagon effect and fundamental attribution [9]. Exactly one, CB-012, authority, is statable as a relation between two parties, its device is a timbre device the influence layer already carries under its own identifier, and BL-1 flags it as the one row whose channel and coordinate are disjoint [5]. So the channel carries one of the thirty as written and six as an upper bound after respecification. The gate's doubt is correct and the true figure is far worse than thirty against one.

## 5. Option (c), articulation and voicing

Test 1 has a two-part answer and the second part is why this option is live rather than obvious. $\Phi$ as S3 specifies it does not occupy articulation, voicing or spacing: sections 2.1 to 2.6 specify six parameters and section 3's reach table lists dynamics, density, fragmentation, harmonic position, tempo, metre, mode and timbre and nothing else [2]. The words articulation, staccato, legato, tenuto, marcato, voicing, spacing and registral appear in S3 only inside bias and influence device descriptions, and in S1, S2, S4, the PRD and the assertions register they do not appear at all. That enumeration covers those six documents and the design; it does not cover the arbitrations, the reviews, the listening pack or the sister series.

The implementation is where the risk lives. `lookupArticulation` returns sforzando above trauma 0.7 and marcato above trauma 0.5, both before the bias loop is entered, so articulation in the shipped code is read from trauma, which is $\Phi$'s own input [10], and BL-1 reports the same gate [5]. Of the eleven entries in the implementation's ARTICULATION category, two are keyed to DISC coordinates, four to Big Five factors, two to emotion, one to the dark triad, one to a physics dimension and one to the McKenney-Lacan isomorphism, and none to the cognitive-bias dimension [9], [10]. DISC is what $\Phi$ drives timbre from, so articulation as built is a second reader of the profile, and S3 section 2.6 leaves $F$ and its selection rule unfixed [2], which is the space a later revision would fill here. So $\Phi$ does not occupy it today and plausibly would later, and BL-2 says the same of voicing in its own words, that an author who intends to specify voicing later should strike rather than remap [6].

Capacity is small and countable. The calculus declares an articulation type of five values, the notation renderer maps six glyphs, and the intersection is four [10]. Voicing as BL-2's Option A states it is one axis, open and high against close and low, which is two values and at most four if spacing is graded [6]. One articulation attaches to one note, so simultaneity is one value per voice. Of the thirty as written, six are articulation or voicing devices by their own text, being the sforzando, the asymmetric envelope, the short and long note contrast, the incompatible tuning or articulation of two groups, the sustained dissonance against the passing consonance, and the complete triad against the richer incomplete voicing, and BL-2's remapped framing device makes seven [6], [9]. One of the seven, in-group bias, needs two groups and is out. So five sit here naturally and the remaining twenty-four would have to be compressed into an alphabet of four to six values that cannot distinguish them.

Against B4 the cost turns on one ambiguous word. B4's claim gives the stable profile "timbre and register, set once per character" [3]. On the musical reading, which the rest of the sentence supports since every other term in it is a musical channel, B4 has already assigned registral disposition to the profile, and a bias that moves it degrades recovery of the profile, which is B4's masking clause. On the Lacanian reading it has not. One clause settles it and the debt is the author's.

The proposal mark is satisfiable by a different mechanism from option (a)'s. Articulation and voicing print per note, and notation has a convention for editorial matter, which is to print what an editor proposed in brackets or reduced size. Nothing in this programme's reference set establishes that convention, so it is offered as a convention and not a finding. If it holds, rule one of section 4.4 is satisfied at the note rather than at the channel, which is a weaker guarantee than exclusive occupancy, because a per-note mark can be lost in transcription, export or audio.

## 6. Option (d), spatialisation, discarded

No parameter in S3 uses azimuth and the words pan, panning, stereo and spatial appear nowhere in S1, S2, S3, S4, the PRD, the assertions register or the design, on the same enumeration as section 5 [2], [3], [4]. So test 1 passes on the papers and fails on the implementation, where `MPNSynthesizer` routes every sampler to one of five panners by instrument family, at minus 0.6, plus 0.5, minus 0.2 and centre, four distinct positions for five families, and the family is $\Phi$'s $F$ taken as the argmax of DISC [2, section 2.6], [10]. A character's position is therefore already a function of a $\Phi$ output. The actor picker adds a third writer, a per-actor pan from minus 100 to plus 100 the user sets [10].

Test 4 kills it outright. The primary Live artefact is a notated running score [4, section 6.2] and a spatial position is not notatable, so a device written here is invisible on the score renderer and present only in audio. A heard position carries no mark and nothing attaches text to it, so the layer would be exactly the unmarked machine proposal ruling A refused [1]. Capacity is unmeasurable today for the reason section 5b.5 gives for timbre, and ruling D has just shown what happens when a capacity is asserted before its resolution is measured [1]. Discarded.

## 7. Option (e), no musical channel

The layer remains what the gate built, a Layer 3 detector whose output is a marked textual proposal beside the Layer 0 turn that prompted it [1, ruling A], [4, section 4.4]. Test 1 does not apply, since no channel is occupied. Test 2 is answered at thirty of thirty, because a textual proposal names the bias and distinguishability is a property of the name rather than of a channel, and the gate's own arithmetic supports this: twenty-two of the thirty are frame-local and scored per turn, eight are historical and scored as passage pairs, and BL-2's Option A would move framing from the first class to the second, making the partition twenty-one and nine [1], [6], [8]. Test 3 costs nothing, since nothing is consumed and B4 is untouched. Test 4 is satisfied by construction, which is the whole of the arbitration's case for the detector.

Test 5 is where the option earns its place rather than winning by default. Two things are unspecified and both are already owed: the abstention rule, meaning what a turn with no bias looks like and whether the layer may return nothing, and the firing rate [1, change 28]. Both debts land on the design, and neither needs a channel or the author. BL-1's search turns up a candidate prior for the second, an activation severity from four to nine out of ten carried by sixteen of the thirty Atlas entries and dropped by the five-field projection [5], [11].

What argues for the null is not caution. It is that the thirty devices rest on no evidence at all, which S3 section 4.4 states plainly and section 10 of the design repeats, that no listener has ever been asked whether any of them carries what it claims to carry [2], [4]. BL-5 and BL-6 would produce the first evidence this layer has ever had, and neither needs a channel: BL-5 is two passages of one score and an analyst, and BL-6 scores planted moves against a generator [4], [8]. Allocating a channel now spends fourteen respecifications, at minimum, on thirty devices whose audibility is unmeasured, which is the shape of the error ruling D catches on the timbre table.

What argues against it is real. The author asked for the bias layer twice, and the framing sentence of section 6.2, that the third stave is where the tension and the bias layer land, becomes a sentence about tension alone [4]. B1's audibility stays untested for as long as the null holds, since a layer that renders nothing produces no listener evidence about itself. The null is a decision not to learn something, and it is defensible only while the cheaper evidence is being gathered elsewhere.

## 8. How many of the thirty survive translation

The arbitration says all thirty are currently written as modulations of $\Phi$ channels [1]. That is true of the census column and false of the device text on more than half of them, and the difference decides what BL-8 costs.

The rule applied, stated so that any row can be disagreed with. A device survives translation to a channel the layer occupies exclusively if it can be stated there without altering a parameter $\Phi$ determines and without referring to material a $\Phi$ stave states. Four flags disqualify a row: it needs a soloist against a group or three or more voices; it sets a parameter a score holds globally and $\Phi$ owns, meaning metre, mode, key or tempo; it is defined against a motif or phrase $\Phi$ wrote; or it needs simultaneous pitches, which disqualifies it from a single line but not from a stave that carries chords. Applied to the thirty as the census now stands, the counts are nine, four, one and six, leaving ten that need only a single line [9].

So on a dedicated stave that carries chords, sixteen survive translation unchanged and fourteen must be respecified. On a single line, ten survive and twenty must be respecified. On the relation channel, one survives and twenty-four are excluded by B3 before any respecification is attempted. On articulation and voicing, five survive and one more arrives already respecified from BL-2.

Two consequences the author should see. The sixteen that survive a dedicated stave are self-contained gestures, an ostinato, a sforzando, a mirror inversion, a pedal tone, a perfect cadence, which never needed a $\Phi$ value at all: what made them modulations was the census assigning each a channel, not anything in the device. And the fourteen that fail are not a random fourteen. Nine fail because they are ensemble devices, and five of those nine are in the Social domain, which is where B3 expects the dyadic biases to be. The layer's most structurally interesting third is the third no single added channel can carry.

## 9. The recommendation, and what it costs

**Option (e)**, with one amendment: the word permanently should be replaced by a stated reopening condition, which is the return of a detection rate from BL-5 and BL-6. The reason is not that (a) fails a test. It passes all five, and it is the only candidate that does. The reason is ordering. A rendering of thirty devices costs fourteen respecifications and a fourth line on a surface the design already measures as oversubscribed by a factor of two at B4's ceiling, and it buys a channel for devices whose audibility no listener has ever been asked about. The same programme has just spent a gate discovering that its timbre capacity was a search artefact conditional on an unmeasured resolution [1, ruling D]. Building a renderer before the detector has returned a number would repeat that at four times the size.

What it costs. The author asked for this layer twice and gets no sound from it in version one, which is a second deferral of the same ask and should be recorded as such. Section 6.2 loses half its framing sentence for the duration. B1 stays untested and the thirty devices stay at the status S3 gives them, proposals nobody has heard. The detector's output is text, so the layer meant to be the most musical thing in the design is the one thing on the surface that is not music, and a reader of Live will notice.

What it forecloses. Structurally, nothing: option (a) remains available at the same price later, and probably at a lower one, because a detection rate would tell the author which devices are worth respecifying. What it does foreclose is any listening evidence about the layer for as long as it holds, which is why the reopening condition matters and should be written into the queue rather than left as an intention.

If the author overrules this and wants a channel in version one, the recommendation is **option (a) and not option (c)**, for one reason that outweighs option (c)'s lower cost. Option (c) sits on a surface the implementation already reads from trauma and DISC and that S3 has not yet specified, so it is the option most likely to be reclaimed by the next revision of S3, and BL-2 says so in its own words about voicing [6]. A channel a later specification reclaims is worse than no channel, because its devices are respecified twice.

## 10. What the author is actually deciding

Four decisions, in the order they unblock each other, and none needs the others settled first.

The first is whether the bias layer renders at all in version one. That is a yes or no, the recommendation is no, and everything else in this note is conditional on it. A no costs the framing sentence of section 6.2 and a second deferral of an ask the author has made twice; it costs no structure, because option (a) stays open.

The second, if the answer is yes, is a fourth stave against articulation and voicing. The fourth stave carries sixteen of the thirty unchanged, satisfies section 4.4's mark by exclusive occupancy, and leaves the region where B4 has evidence. Articulation and voicing carries five, satisfies the mark per note by a convention this programme has not established, and risks being reclaimed by a later S3.

The third is one clause on B4, worth taking whichever way the first two go because it is owed anyway. B4 gives the stable profile "timbre and register, set once per character" [3]. Whether register there is the musical register or one of Lacan's three decides whether the voicing half of option (c) collides with the profile channel, and nothing else.

The fourth is the reopening condition. If the null is adopted, what result from BL-5 or BL-6 puts BL-8 back on the queue. A detection rate above chance on planted moves is the obvious trigger, and this note does not set the threshold, because it is the same kind of number as the audible-fraction threshold change 35 already puts in section 10's inventory [1].

Two things this note does not decide. It does not touch BL-2, whose recommendation stands on its own terms and whose remapped device is the one row already written in a shape a channel could take [6]. And it does not settle whether the influence layer keeps the relation channel S3 section 5 gives it, which section 4 raises only far enough to show that the bias layer cannot have it.
