## 0. What this paper is, and what it is not

**This is a paper about a tool.** The tool renders the shape of an exchange. The question it asks throughout is whether the tool serves the person using it, and the answer in several places is that it does not yet, for reasons that are stated as specifications rather than as cautions.

**It makes no claim about autism.** It makes no claim about anyone's abilities and no claim that anyone needs help. The author raised autism as a possible application of decomposing dialogue into relationship networks expressed mathematically, visually and musically, and confirmed in the Understanding Lock that it is framed as a non-clinical communication and expression aid [5]. Section 6.4 of the design writes the audience as anyone who finds conversational dynamics easier to read as sound or image than to infer from speech. That is a statement about a preference for a medium, which is the only kind of statement this paper is entitled to make about its reader, and it is the same kind of statement one makes about a musician who reads a score faster than a description of a score.

**And the standing caveat, which this paper is written under rather than around.** No listener has been asked anything, and every empirical claim in this programme is untested. Everything the programme holds it produced itself: 31,078 scored rows and 232 annotated frames, both outputs of the system under study. The listening pack is built, blinded and published, and it has been sent to nobody. Two earlier listener studies exist and neither is citable, their stimuli having come from an unseeded generator, and section 2 reports both [1], [3]. **On this surface that is not a statement about confidence. It is a shipping condition.** D42 makes listener testing of Ψ a condition of Ψ shipping anywhere; section 2 sets out the four things the test must establish here and says that the fourth is a gate; and Ψ itself has no specification at all [5]. **So what the user meets until the test exists is nothing.** There is no Expression surface to open, because the mapping that would drive it has not been written and the test that would license it has not been run, and an empty interval is the honest form of that rather than a provisional build with a sentence of text on its face. If one were shipped ahead of the test, what the user would meet is a musical rendering of their own exchange, carrying most of the informational load on the one surface where the score is not the secondary renderer, with nothing between it and a reading as a verdict on their own conduct but a line of text that section 2 has already said does not survive contact with a minor key.

**Where this paper cites evidence from the autism literature it does so to constrain the tool.** That evidence, carried here through the author's own analysis document [13], answers three questions and no others: what an existing listener figure was measured in and therefore what it licenses, what the renderer has to declare about its own acoustic output, and who has to be in the room when the tool's own tests are designed. None of it is used to characterise the person at the keyboard, in either direction. This paper does not claim a deficit and it does not claim an affinity. The author's analysis is explicit on the second point and it is worth repeating where an implementer will read it: music is the modality where the measured difference is smallest, which is not the same claim as music being a preserved strength, and the defensible sentence is the first one [13].

**The test the author set is the right test and this paper is written to it.** Write it as you would write a paper about a tool for professional musicians. The user knows what they want, the tool either serves it or it does not, and the paper's job is to say which. A sentence here that would read as condescending to the person using the tool is an error in this paper and not a caution to that person, and where such a sentence would be needed to defend a design the design is wrong and is changed instead.

**What this paper is not.** It is not a review, it is not a clinical document and it contains no regulatory, legal, privacy, consent or compliance content, all of which fall with the premise that this framework captures real conversations, which it does not [5]. It does not amend S1 to S4. It does not rule on the Instrument, which is MPN-PRD-01's subject and keeps its own gates [4], [5]. And it does not specify Ψ, which nobody has specified, a fact section 8 states rather than works around.

**The user and the question.** This surface's reader is anyone who finds the shape of a conversation easier to read as sound or image than to infer from the speech itself, and the question they bring is: *what actually happened between us, in the shape of the exchange rather than in what either of us was like?* Draft 1 had no such sentence, and the companion paper opens each of its four use cases with one [18], [19]. Its absence is why three of this paper's rulings could be made without anything catching them: a ruling that cannot be weighed against a task is a preference with a number on it. Every ruling in sections 3 to 9 is answerable against the question above, and section 11's table now records, for each, what the ruling costs the question and what it buys it. Where a ruling costs the question something and this paper takes it anyway, the ruling says so in its own row rather than in a footnote.

## 1. What the tool shows, which is a technical fact and not a disclaimer

**Expression is Layer 1 only** [5]. That sentence has been in the design since revision 1 and it is the whole of the claims boundary, so it is worth spelling out as a chain rather than quoting as a label.

Layer 0 is the transcript: turns, timings, speakers, with parse confidence reported as a first-class output and a refusal below a declared threshold [5]. Layer 1 is the five observed measures over that transcript, which are floor share, speaker adjacency, overlap, latency and reciprocity, four of them carrying a named setting that changes the answer and is shown on every output [5]. The count is five and not six; backchannel is a parameter of the overlap row rather than a measure the design ever defined, which is D64 [5]. Layer 2 is a human being's rating, recorded as a judgement. Layer 3 is a machine's proposal, marked as a proposal wherever it appears.

**Φ, the state mapping, runs only where a state exists, and a state exists only where a human has rated one** [5]. That is the withdrawal of section 2 of the design, and it is not a policy that could be relaxed: the derivation of a rated quantity from turn structure was shown false and withdrawn, and Φ's domain is the nine-component state that S3 specifies [3]. Expression has no rating instrument, its user has no reason to have one, and nothing on this surface produces a state. **So Φ does not run here, and the tool renders nothing about anybody's mind.** That is not a promise the product makes. It is a consequence of the layer boundary, and it can be checked by asking what the input to Φ would be and finding that there is none.

**What the tool renders is therefore a function of four things: who spoke, when, for how long, and after whom.** Ψ, the interaction mapping, takes the Layer 1 measures and renders the shape of the exchange. That sentence is a statement of what $\Psi$ is for and not of what it does, because $\Psi$ has not been written; section 8 states what is missing and this section's claims about $\Psi$ are constraints on it rather than descriptions of it. The claims boundary above, that $\Phi$ does not run here, is of the other kind and can be checked, because the input to $\Phi$ is absent and can be shown to be. It is a different function from Φ with a different domain, and the two are never conflated, which is D3 [5].

**Three consequences follow that are worth having explicitly.**

**The theory's vocabulary never arrives here.** The engine is named for a psychometric calculus whose state variables carry a Lacanian provenance, and the author's own analysis concludes that the vocabulary belongs in the theory and drama papers, where it is accurate and where it is an asset, and not in a user-facing layer [13]. On this surface that conclusion needs no enforcement, because no part of Φ runs: there are no registers, no trauma coordinate and no entropy coordinate in anything Expression computes or renders. The split the analysis asks for is a fact of the layer boundary on this surface rather than a rule someone has to remember, and the design's mark on the face of every output says the operative half in the reader's language: this renders the shape of the exchange and says nothing about anyone's state [5].

**The epistemic risk the analysis names is not available here.** The analysis identifies the risk specific to this family of tools as epistemic overwrite: a person handed a confident-looking external representation of their own state, produced by an unvalidated mapping, may take the representation as the answer, and the analysis is careful to present that as the author's inference rather than as a cited finding [13]. The condition of that risk is that a representation of a state is produced. On Expression none is, so the **claim** is closed. The conjunction is not. The risk as this programme's own analysis states it has three parts: a confident-looking external representation about the person, an unvalidated mapping, and the reader taking the representation as the answer. This surface does produce a representation about the reader, through a mapping that has no specification and no listener test, and section 2's conduct reading is the same structure with *conduct* in place of *state*. What holds this surface to Layer 1 is a rule, stated at section 9 clause five, that there is no setting that turns $\Phi$ on and that a build with a toggle is a different surface. A rule is the right form here, because the failure mode is gradual rather than sudden and a gradual failure is caught by a check rather than by a crash. It is care of a kind that can be audited, and it is not construction. This paper has also enumerated exactly one path by which a state representation could enter the surface, a Layer 2 rating arriving by configuration, so the claim above reaches that path and no further; three other objects here carry a quantity about the reader and have not been enumerated against the conjunction, being the numeric readout, the graph and the surface sentence, and EX-14 is that enumeration. This programme's own rule is that a negative claim reaches no further than the class its enumeration covers. **That is the reason this section is load-bearing.** It is also the reason the surface must never acquire a Layer 2 input by configuration: section 9 states what happens if one ever arrives.

**And what remains is a different problem, which is the next section's.** A rendering that says nothing about a mind can still be read as a verdict on conduct, because it is rendered in music and music is read affectively whatever the mapping intends. Layer 1 only closes the claim. It does not close the reading.

**What the tool cannot show, stated here rather than discovered.** Ψ is content-blind, so two people who agree and two people in a polite deadlock have the same Layer 1 signature and render the same, which the design records as a known limit [5]. Adjacency is adjacency and never response, because nothing in a transcript marks an addressee, and the renderer's directed arrow is a default rather than a claim, which is D35 as the Arbiter narrowed it [5], [8]. So the tool can say who spoke after whom and cannot say who was answering whom, and a user who wants the second is asking the transcript for something that is not in it. On a script, two of the five measures are unavailable outright, floor share degrades to a line or word count, and reciprocity's window becomes a turn count rather than a duration [5].

**Layer 3 reaches this surface in no version.** Expression is Layer 1 only, so the question is closed before it opens, but it is worth recording what the answer would be if it were not, because the author's framing for the Live surface names bias as something the display shows. Under the author's decision of 14 September 2026 the bias layer occupies no musical channel at all: it is a Layer 3 detector whose output is a marked textual proposal beside the Layer 0 turn that prompted it, and it renders no sound and no notation on any surface [9]. Running `s6_bias_layer.py` on 14 September 2026 confirms the layer's state: thirty entries, thirty now naming a state coordinate with three flagged as the author's to settle, eighteen signatures, eight collisions involving twenty of the thirty, and a work queue whose last line records that the layer still has no representation for a turn carrying no bias, no abstention rule and no per-turn cap, which is why it has no surface anywhere [12]. **So a version of Expression that shows bias does not exist to be asked for, and would show text if it did.** That answer is conditional and the condition is stated: the channel allocation was taken as the null with a reopening condition rather than a permanence clause, and it reopens on a detection rate from the two pilot studies, so a musical bias rendering is barred by a decision that evidence can revisit rather than by the layer boundary that bars Φ here [9].

## 2. The unavoidable problem, and what the listener test must establish for this surface

**Ψ makes no psychological claim and Ψ's codomain is music, which is read affectively by construction.** The design states the conflict in those terms and refuses to resolve it by wording: a user whose conversation renders harsh or thin will read that as a verdict on the conversation and on themselves, and a line of text saying no claim is intended does not survive contact with a minor key [5]. That sentence is not softened here and nothing in this paper depends on its being softened.

**The evidence the programme already holds.** MPN-PRD-01 section 7.2 carries a systematic review of the major-minor dichotomy which finds the major-happy and minor-sad association robust, developing from 58 per cent correct at age four to 92 per cent in adults, and replicated in Japanese and Chinese samples [4], [15]. The same review finds that major mode does not induce greater happiness than minor in a group of people with minimal exposure to Western music, and that about 70 per cent of listeners performed near chance when asked to classify rapid tone-scrambles, the authors reading the association as a psychoacoustic predisposition that becomes an established cultural connotation through exposure [4], [15]. **Both halves matter here.** The association is strong in the adult listeners it was measured in, which is why the design treats it as a real hazard rather than a theoretical one; and it is exposure-conditioned rather than universal, which is why the figure cannot simply be carried to a different set of ears.

**What the programme does not hold is any usable listener evidence of its own.** S1 reports the only two listener studies in the corpus and disqualifies both: one gave twenty-four participants a mean appropriateness rating of 4.2 on a five-point scale and tested an assignment that no shipped module implements, and the second is a null result at forty-eight participants, p = 0.72, effect size 0.08, sitting in a document that presents the system as validated. Both used stimuli from an unseeded generator and are not reproducible [1]. S3 states the same thing about the bias layer's devices without qualification: no listener has ever been asked whether any of the thirty carries what it claims to carry [3]. **This surface therefore begins with nothing to borrow.**

**D42 makes listener testing of Ψ a condition of shipping on every surface, and what varies by surface is the required result rather than whether the test is run** [5]. This section states the required result here.

**Why the required result is strictest on this surface, for a reason that is about the layout and not about the reader.** The design demotes the score to a secondary renderer on Studio and Live, where the graph leads and the score sits beside it, and records that demotion as evidence against the thesis rather than as a user-interface decision [5]. Expression is the surface where that demotion does not apply, because sound and image are what the audience is defined by wanting. **So Expression is the one surface where the musical rendering is not one channel among several but carries most of the informational load.** An unintended affective reading of Ψ is, on Studio, a distortion of one of three things on the screen, and on Expression it is a distortion of the output. That was stated in draft 1 as an arithmetic property of what is on the screen and it is not one. Expression's screen carries three things, as Studio's does: the interaction stave, the graph that section 5 ruling three assigns the by-name attribution to, and the numeric readout. What differs is not the count but the configuration a given reader is in. For a reader who takes the sound and not the image, and design section 6.4 writes this audience as people who find conversational dynamics easier to read as sound **or** image, the score is the whole of what they read, and an unintended affective reading of $\Psi$ is then a distortion of their entire output rather than of one panel of three. That is a claim about a configuration and not a property of the surface, and how this audience divides between the two has not been measured, which is EX-13. The conclusion below survives on that narrower ground; what does not survive is the ranking of this surface above Studio as a matter of arithmetic and it would be equally true of a producer, an analyst or the author sitting in front of the same layout.

**The test must establish four things, and the fourth is the one that decides whether the surface ships.**

**One, the object of the inference.** Present a listener with a Ψ rendering of an exchange and ask what they take it to be about. The score is the object, not the pleasantness. A listener who says the music sounded harsh has reported the stimulus. A listener who says one of those people was hostile, or that the conversation went badly for someone, has drawn the inference the mapping claims not to license, and that report is the failure the test exists to detect. The question must be open rather than forced, because a forced choice between named readings supplies the reading the test is trying to measure.

**Two, the conduct reading specifically.** The residue the arbitration left standing when it rejected finding U8 is that music is read affectively and a line of text does not survive contact with a minor key [6]. The sharp form of that on this surface is not a reading about a state, which section 1 has closed, but a reading about conduct: whether a listener told that a rendering is of an exchange they took part in hears it as an evaluation of how they behaved in it. The material under test stays synthetic under the scope ruling, so the condition is supplied by the framing given to the listener rather than by the material, and the comparison is between listeners told the exchange is theirs and listeners told it is not. If the framing moves the reading, the surface's sentence is doing no work and section 7's sentence has to be rewritten before anything ships.

**Three, whose ears.** The 92 per cent is a general-population adult figure [4], [15]. Three things in the author's analysis bear on whether it transfers to the listeners this surface is designed with, and they do not point the same way [13]. Allen, Davis and Hill found physiological responsiveness to music intact while verbal report of the response was reduced, with the gap fully mediated by alexithymia, which is the shape of a warrant for a non-verbal channel. Kinnaird's review puts alexithymia at 49.93 per cent against 4.89 per cent, described there as common rather than universal. And Suslow and Kersting found that alexithymia degrades the perception of emotion in music as well, which points the other way. **The honest position is that the existing evidence does not settle the direction and cannot be cited for either answer**, which is why the test has to be run on this surface's own audience rather than inherited from the review. The author's analysis records the participatory position that instruments validated in general populations are not valid for autistic adults without adaptation and retesting, and that position applies to a listening instrument exactly as it applies to a questionnaire [13]. This is a sampling requirement on a test and nothing more is claimed from it.

**And the purpose of the test is not in doubt and should be stated in the pack.** It is to find out what the mapping communicates. A mapping read as a verdict is a defective mapping, and the defect is in Ψ's codomain choices. No result of this test is ever a finding about a listener, and the pack says so in the same plain register the programme's existing therapist pack uses, which tells its participants the system's defects before asking them anything and invites the answer that the whole idea is misconceived [17].

**Four, the required result and what failing it costs.** Listener testing is a condition of shipping, so the result is a gate rather than an input to a judgement [5]. For this surface the required result is that the open-inference test of item one returns readings about the exchange and not about the people in it, at a rate the author fixes before the test is run rather than after, and that the framing manipulation of item two does not move that rate. **If Ψ fails, Ψ does not ship on this surface.** The available responses are to respecify Ψ's codomain choices and retest, or to ship the visual renderer alone.

**The second of those could be a real product rather than a consolation, and two things have to be true before that sentence can be made.** The graph has no specification anywhere in this programme, which is EX-10. And nothing in this paper, in the design or in the implementation order puts a visual rendering in front of anybody and asks what they take it to be about: D42 and D27 are listener tests and a graph has no listeners, and item 9's comparison asks which renderer answered a user's question, which is a usefulness test rather than an inference test. So the fallback offered here is at present the one object in the chain that is neither specified nor tested, and section 10's falsifier three, which says the surface does not ship if the visual renderer carries the same reading, names a condition that no instrument in this programme can currently evaluate. EX-17 is what makes this a product. The graph carries the full speaker set whether or not the score does, which is the design's own sentence about what carries the material a two-stave reduction drops [5], and the audience section 6.4 names is defined by sound **or** image. A surface that renders the shape of an exchange as a graph, with its settings and its refusals intact, is the thing this paper describes minus one renderer. It would also be the honest outcome of a failed test rather than a retreat from it.

**One more required result belongs to this test and is easy to lose.** Design section 4.1 requires a below-floor difference to be rendered as **unmeasured**, visibly distinct from any rendering of an even exchange, and worded as *this recording cannot tell you*, which is D28 and which the design calls a central honesty claim [5], [8]. If the musical channel cannot carry that distinction audibly, the rule does not hold in audio, and it then holds in the visual and textual channels alone, which means the musical renderer is not self-sufficient on this surface. The test must ask. Nobody has.

**Two populations are in play in this section and draft 1 used one phrase for both.** The **audience**, as section 0 defines it and as design section 6.4 writes it, is anyone who finds conversational dynamics easier to read as sound or image, which is a statement about a preferred medium and is the only kind of statement this paper is entitled to make about its reader. The **sample** required result three needs is a different question: whether the general-population result transfers is a question about listeners whose response to the affective channel may differ, and the findings cited above are about one such group. So the gate is not run on the audience and it is not meant to be. It is run on the sample most likely to produce a null, which is the conservative choice, and a positive result on that sample is evidence about the audience while a null on it is a reason to look at the audience rather than a verdict on it. Nothing in this paragraph defines the audience by anything but preference, and nothing in it says who this surface is for. Stating the sampling frame properly is EX-16.

## 3. The settings problem, and what this surface does instead

**Visibility is not intelligibility.** Showing a user a control reading *floor share unit: seconds, words or turns* hands them the power to change the answer and no basis on which to choose. That is D30 and the design accepts it [5], [8]. The obvious repair, which is to remove the control, is the wrong one: a tool that hides the decision that changes the answer is worse than one that exposes it, and removing a control from this audience and not from another would be exactly the condescension this paper refuses. **The user keeps every control. What the user gains is a basis.**

**The premise had never been computed, so this paper computes it.** The design asserts that the three units genuinely disagree about who dominated a conversation [5]. `s6_floor_share_units.py` [14] measures the disagreement on the only material the programme holds, which is the seven score files, under the design's own exclusions: King Lear refused under section 4.1, the non-speaker token dropped under section 8's non-dialogue rule, and the three anthology files printed below the line because a top-two share over a file of several plays with disjoint casts measures the anthology [5], [10].

| Score file | Top two by turns | Top two by words | Coverage, turns | Coverage, words | Speakers at 1 per cent or more | Changing rank between the units |
|:---|:---|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A Doll's House | Nora, Helmer | Nora, Helmer | 64.6 | 71.0 | 7 | 4 |
| Hamlet | Hamlet, **Horatio** | Hamlet, **the King** | 40.4 | 53.2 | 18 | 17 |
| Macbeth | Macbeth, Lady Macbeth | Macbeth, Lady Macbeth | 30.0 | 42.3 | 22 | 17 |

**What that establishes.** The two computable units disagree, and on one of the three single plays they disagree about which speaker is second, which is precisely the pair a two-stave reduction renders. They disagree about how much of the work that pair covers by 6.4, 12.8 and 12.3 points. So below the single busiest voice, D30's premise holds on this material and is now a measurement rather than an assertion.

**What it does not establish, which is the half a reader will assume.** On all three single plays the single busiest speaker is the same under both units. The disagreement is about the order below the top, not about who dominated. **The design's sentence is therefore stronger than the material supports and this paper states the narrower claim**: the units agree about who spoke most and disagree about almost everything after that. Two files do change their top speaker, and both are anthologies: the Chekhov file, where Irina leads by turns and Lubov by words, and the Strindberg file, where Maurice leads by turns and Adolphe by words and falls from first to fourth. An anthology figure is not a coverage figure for any play, so neither bears on the claim above [5], [10].

**Three limits travel with the table and none of them is decorative.** Seconds is not computable on this material at all, because a script yields no timings, which is section 7.2 [5]; and seconds is the unit most unlike the other two, since a speaker with few long turns and a speaker with many short ones are exactly the pair it separates, so every number above is a lower bound. A row in these files is a line rather than a turn, so the turn column merges consecutive rows carrying the same speaker, which is the nearest approximation the material allows. And none of the seven files is a conversation and none is a moderated exchange, which is change 19's standing limit on every corpus figure in this programme [6]. These figures are new here, they are not among those `VERIFICATION-2026-09-14.txt` confirms, and the coverage column is a different computation from the verified 69.2, 50.1 and 39.0 per cent, which count rows and retain the non-speaker token [10].

**What the surface does, ruled.** Each setting appears with a sentence saying what it changes, and the sentence names the consequence on the output in front of the user rather than the definition of the unit. *Floor share is counted in seconds. Counted in turns instead, this exchange reads as 53 to 47 rather than 40 to 60, and the stave's floor-share line inverts. The Hamlet instance, *counted in turns instead, the second voice on this stave is Horatio and not the King*, is the same form drawn from a multi-stave surface and is given here as the general pattern rather than as an instance this surface can produce, because section 9 rules Expression a one-stave surface with no principal-voice selection.* That form is the only one that supplies a basis, because the definition of a unit is not a reason to prefer it and the effect on this exchange is. It costs a recomputation under the alternative setting, which is cheap on material known in advance and is bounded by D36's requirement that the selection be reversible without re-ingest [5].

**The default is justified by the material rather than chosen.** Where turn onsets and offsets exist the unit is seconds; where they do not, floor share is a different measure and is labelled one, being a word or line count under section 7.2's rule that a script analysis and a podcast analysis are not the same measurement and must not be placed side by side in one report [5]. So the user is not asked to pick a unit out of the air on first use: the ingest fixes the default and the sentence beside it says what the alternative would do. The numeric defaults themselves are among the parameters the design leaves unfixed, which section 10 records and section 8 of this paper repeats [5].

**And where the units disagree about the answer, the disagreement is content rather than a warning.** A conversation whose ordering is stable across units and one whose ordering inverts are different conversations, and the second fact is worth rendering. Hamlet is the case: a reader told only that the two principal voices are Hamlet and Horatio has been told something that a change of unit reverses, and a reader told that the pair depends on the unit has been told something true about the exchange. This is stated as a property of the material, in the same voice as any other measure, and never as an apology for the tool.

## 4. The backchannel problem, ruled

**This is the failure most likely to do real harm on this surface and the design names it exactly.** A raw overlap detector scores the most supportive listener as the most aggressive interrupter, and the design's mitigation at Layer 1 is that the backchannel lexicon is declared and editable [5]. Editing a word list is an expert act. On the surface whose audience most needs the mitigation, the mitigation would not exist, which is D30's second half [5], [8]. Section 6.4 leaves the disposition as a disjunction: the lexicon is either right by default for the deployed language, or the overlap measures are withheld as they are on class C [5].

**The first branch is not available, and the reason is in the design's own text rather than in a preference.** Section 4.2 records that gap length and overlap tolerance vary by language **and by speech community**, that a fixed threshold will therefore systematically score some communities as more interruptive, and that the defaults are declared rather than universal [5]. That record is about a threshold and not about a word list, and taken as the ground for this ruling it proves too much: it would withdraw the overlap threshold on every surface in this programme, and the latency turn-boundary rule with it, because gap length is exactly what latency measures, and latency is the one measure section 10's falsifier one leaves standing here [19]. The ground for this ruling is the second reason, which is this programme's own rather than an inherited sentence, and it is stated below. What the design's record does establish, and it is enough for the narrower point, is that the most a list can be is right for one community inside one language, which is not what the first branch claims. **So the branch fails on the design's own evidence and the second branch is what the surface does.**

**Ruled: the overlap-derived measures are withheld by default on this surface.** They are released only where a lexicon has been established for the deployed language and speech community by the same standard the programme applies to any other measured quantity, and establishing one is a programme item rather than a user task. Until then, overlap initiated and overlap received are **not computed at all**, which is the disposition section 4.2 already specifies for class C, adopted here for an independent reason [5].

**Not computed is the operative phrase and it is chosen against two weaker options.** A measure computed and greyed is a measure the user will read. A measure computed and rendered as zero is worse, because zero is a finding. The design's own rule is that a flat output from a missing input and a flat output from a flat conversation must never look alike, and it applies here word for word [5].

**What the surface shows in the withheld case, specified.**

**It says so before it says anything else.** The analysis opens with the sentence, ahead of the content rather than in a footnote, in the same position class C uses and for the same reason the design gives: interruption is the dynamic most users are trying to read, and silent omission lets a reader conclude that nobody talked over anybody [5]. The wording follows D29's pattern and names the cause rather than the symptom: *this analysis cannot tell you whether anyone talked over anyone, because no backchannel lexicon has been established for the way this group talks. A lexicon built for the language is not enough on its own: the design's own record is that what counts as talking over someone varies between communities inside a language, and EX-2 is the item that establishes one.*

**It is sorted as the third of the four kinds, which is not specified yet.** Section 8a requires every refusal, suppression and blank to state which of four kinds it is and the one action available, because undifferentiated they all read as a fault [5]. This is not **your material**, which would send the user to find different material to no purpose, and it is not **we broke**, which would send them to rebuild a recording setup for nothing. It is **not specified yet**, whose stated action is that there is nothing to do today and whose obligation is to name the item it waits on, which is EX-2 in section 11.

**The other three measures render normally.** A withheld overlap does not blank the surface, and the sentence above is a statement about one measure and not about the analysis.

**And the cost is recorded rather than softened.** Overlap is very likely the thing the user came for; the design says as much for class C and the reasoning carries. Withholding it is the expensive answer. It is the right one because the failure it prevents is not an absence but a false positive that looks like a measurement, and because the object that would produce that false positive is a word list. **S4's finding is that a keyword counter's output was read as a measurement through three revisions of a paper** [2], [5]. A backchannel lexicon is a keyword counter. Shipping one as a default, on the surface with the least apparatus around its output, is that finding scheduled to happen again, and the one thing this programme has learned at first hand is what that costs.

## 5. What the per-person measures do here, ruled conservatively

**The restoration and the mismatch.** Design section 7.4 restores every per-person measure in full, floor share, turn count, mean turn length, latency before taking the floor, overlap initiated and received, backchannel given and received, and degree in the adjacency graph, and, on surfaces where a Layer 2 rating exists, each person's own $\Phi$ rendering, which does not arise here, all attributable by name on a shared display, and the stated reason is that the people it would rank do not exist [5]. The User Advocate found that this reaches the surface least placed to discount it, the gate rejected the finding as U8 on three grounds, and one part of it survived the rejection and was carried forward: **D42's listener test covers the affective reading of the interaction mapping and does not cover a named per-person comparison, and nothing else does either** [6], [7].

The gate's three grounds are correct and this paper does not reopen them. Revision 7 did not create the situation, since these have been Layer 1 measures reaching a Layer 1 surface since revision 1; the Advocate's harm required a conversation the reader took part in, which the scope ruling excludes; and the residue is a limitation the design already declares [6]. **What the gate did not rule on is the shape of the restoration**, and the Advocate's cross-cutting observation about revision 7 is the accurate one: each restoration is justified by a property of the material, which is that the subjects are synthetic, and each is written into a surface, which outlives any particular material [7]. That is a mismatch of scope between a justification and a rule, and it is the kind of mismatch the design knows how to avoid, having scoped capabilities to an ingest class twice in the same document [5], [7].

**Ruling one. The restoration is scoped to the material and not to the surface.** Expression renders per-person measures where the material is class A material with no person in it, which is generated dialogue and script text alike, the design's own class table putting them in one row because in both the speakers are characters somebody wrote, which is the condition the justification actually names. Written that way the rule states its own reason, it needs no rewrite if the material ever changes, and it follows the document's existing practice at section 4.2 and section 6.2 rather than departing from it [5].

**Ruling two. Within that, the surface renders the measures and not a ranking.** The distinction is exact rather than a hedge. A per-person measure is a quantity of the exchange indexed by speaker: A held the floor for 61 per cent of it, B's median latency before taking the floor was 0.4 seconds. A ranking is a derived ordering over people, presented as the object of interest: a sort, a leaderboard, a most-dominant label, a per-person composite. **The first is in Ψ's domain and the second is not.** A ranking is a new quantity that no paper in this series specifies, that D42's test does not cover, and about which the programme holds no evidence of any kind. It is therefore not rendered, and the reason given on the surface is the true one, that it is unspecified and untested, rather than a protective one, which would be false and would be the condescension this paper refuses. The section 3 result bears directly on it: on Hamlet the second-ranked voice changes with the unit, so an ordering below the top depends on the setting as much as on the exchange, and rendering that ordering as a result would be rendering the setting as a result.

**Ruling three, REVERSED by the author's decision of 15 September 2026 [21]. The by-name attribution is carried on the stave, and the graph and the numeric readout carry it as well.** A persona here may be an actor, a character in a play, a speaker in a podcast or any persona a score carries, and a score carries several of them, added and removed as the material requires. The reasoning below was written for the refusal and is kept because it states the cost the permission now incurs, which is real: D42's listener test is scoped to the affective reading of the interaction mapping and does not cover a named per-person comparison, so what goes on the stave goes there **untested**, on the channel where an affective reading lives, attached to a person by name. It is carried as a labelled untested permission, which is what MPN-S5 already does for the panel producer [18], and EX-4's first branch, extending D42 to cover it, is now a test to run rather than a choice to make. The exchange-level form the paragraph below describes remains available and is no longer the only form. This is the conservative half and it costs something, so the reasoning is given in full. Ψ's stave renders shape: how concentrated the floor was, how the turn order alternated, the latency profile, the reciprocity of the window. Those are exchange-level quantities derived from the per-person measures, and they are what the design's own division of labour assigns to the score, the graph being what carries the full speaker set whether or not the score does [5]. The named comparison, A against B, is the one case D42's test is scoped not to cover, and a stave is exactly where an affective reading lives. **So the named comparison is not put on the musical channel until a test covers it**, and it reopens automatically when D42's scope is extended, which is EX-4 in section 11. What this costs is real and is named here rather than discovered: a user who wants to hear their own floor share as a line against somebody else's does not get that, and it is a thing a user might reasonably want.

**Ruling four, which is the limit of the conservatism.** **At two speakers the two objects coincide and the distinction does not do any work.** With two speakers a per-person measure and its complement are one number: a floor share of 61 per cent for one speaker is a floor share of 39 for the other, and the same holds for turn count, mean turn length and latency, while degree in the adjacency graph is the constant 1 for both. So the readout does not present measures from which a ranking could be derived; the ordering is the whole content of the measure and it is read in one glance. The rule above therefore governs the stave at every arity and governs the readout only above two speakers, where a sort, a composite or a most-dominant label is a thing a surface could choose to build and this one does not. Stating the rule without this clause would make it a rule that forbids nothing on the arity section 10 calls most likely, while its cost is paid in full.

None of this withholds a number the surface has. Five of the nine per-person measures are available to the user as a number with its setting and its window beside it, being floor share, turn count, mean turn length, latency before taking the floor and degree in the adjacency graph. The other four are the overlap-derived ones and they are not computed at all, for the reason section 4 gives, which is not a judgement about what this reader can handle but a judgement about what a word list can measure. That is the one place this paper does hold something back, and it is held back from every user of every surface on the same ground, not from this one, and the graph carries every speaker. **Withholding the numbers from the person who asked for them would be the condescension**; withholding a rendering whose reading has never been tested is the same rule the design applies to everything else it has not tested. **That sentence held for one day.** The arbitration recorded at its decision 9.2 that MPN-S5 permits the panel producer a named per-person comparison, untested and labelled, on the same scope hole in D42 that this ruling cited, and that the two papers could not both be asserting a uniformity the other contradicted [18], [19]. The author settled it on 15 September in the direction of permission, on both surfaces and on the stave [21]. So this ruling no longer withholds the comparison; it records that the comparison is untested, and the labelling is what carries that. The sentence this ruling used to carry, which claimed the rule, and it applies on Studio and Live equally. The asymmetry between surfaces is in the layout argument of section 2 and nowhere else.

## 6. The live case, and which case this user is actually in

**The partition is whether the material is known in advance, and not the ingest class.** Revision 7 scoped the running score by class and the gate corrected it, because generated and recorded material are known in full before a single note is rendered, so the score is computed ahead and played against the dialogue, which is what a film score is [5], [6]. **The user of this surface is in that case.** On generated material, which is the material the scope ruling makes primary, the Ψ stave is synchronous, and the author's picture of a score running with the dialogue is deliverable as soon as Ψ exists.

**One rule governs the precomputation and it is not a formality.** Every quantity rendered at a turn is a function of that turn and of turns before it and of nothing after it, which is D66; whole-work precomputation is a convenience of the implementation and not a licence to see forward, and a quantity that genuinely needs the whole work, such as a normalisation over total speaking time, is labelled a whole-work quantity and is not rendered as a running one [5]. On a surface whose point is to render an exchange as it unfolds, a measure at turn ten that knew about turn five hundred would not be rendering the exchange.

**The live capture case, which is a different case and is not built.** Class B is a microphone per speaker. Its speaker error is near none, because attribution is the channel, and it still has to be recognised as it arrives: meeting-benchmark word error of 35 to 46 per cent is a recognition figure, a figure the design states without a source and which MPN-S5's citation ledger records as unsourced after an independent search failed to trace it [18], [20] and survives the move to one channel per speaker unchanged [5]. The split inside the live case is therefore between what needs the words and what does not. Floor share, adjacency, latency, overlap and reciprocity are computed from channel timings and need no words at all, so they are synchronous on class B. What trails is anything requiring the words, which on the Live surface means every Layer 3 output and any overlap classified as a backchannel [5].

**On Expression that split has an unusual consequence and it is worth stating.** Layer 3 does not reach this surface at all, and section 4 withholds the backchannel classification by default. **So the only two word-dependent quantities the surface could have had are both absent, and Expression's Ψ stave on class B is fully synchronous.** If a lexicon is ever established under EX-2, the backchannel classification becomes the one thing on this surface that trails, and the surface says which half is which on the face of the output rather than in a document, which is section 6.2's rule [5].

**Class C, a single room microphone, is last and carries three things.** The overlap-derived measures are not computed at all, because diarisation is worst exactly at overlap and many pipelines assign an overlapped span to one speaker, which deletes an interruption rather than mis-scoring it [5]. A resolution floor derived from the deployed diariser's error rate, below which differences are not reported as numbers and are rendered as unmeasured rather than as even [5]. And an opening sentence saying what the recording cannot tell you, ahead of the content [5]. A panel of five on one microphone is precisely the material those error rates are measured on, so class C carries no multi-party surface [5].

**And the honest headline.** Both live capture classes are future work if real audio is ever used, which is section 4.1's record, section 7.6's sequencing and item 6a of the implementation order, all saying the same thing three times because it is the thing an implementer will otherwise miss [5]. Live capture is sequenced by measured error rather than by permission, and it gains the programme nothing until Ψ exists. **So the sentence for this surface is: the user is in the synchronous case, the trailing case is not built, and the surface describes itself as a running score rendered against the dialogue rather than as captioning**, which is D67 [5].

**What each case gives the user, so the difference is visible before they choose material.** Material known in advance gives the whole surface at once, synchronous, every measure at the resolution its ingest supports, with the causal-window rule holding and whole-work quantities labelled. A live class B capture gives the same stave synchronously, minus what section 4 withholds, plus two rules that exist because a delayed feedback loop is worse frozen than blank: the display blanks after a declared staleness horizon and says it is stale rather than holding the last frame, and it marks a discontinuity when the speaker set changes mid-session rather than restating prior measures [5]. A live class C capture gives a reduced surface that opens by saying what it cannot answer.

## 7. What it is for, in one sentence

**The design requires the sentence and does not supply it** [5], [8]. It is supplied here.

> **This is a picture of the conversation's shape, who held the floor, who followed whom, and, where the material carries timings, how fast anyone came in, and, where a backchannel lexicon exists for the deployed community under EX-2, who came in over whom, and it is not a picture of anyone in it.**

**Four things about it are deliberate.** It names the measures in the words a person would use rather than in the words section 4.2 uses, so a reader knows what the picture is of before they read it. It carries the content of the design's mapping mark, which is that this renders the shape of the exchange and says nothing about anyone's state, in the reader's language rather than as a Greek letter, which is D26 [5]. Its claims boundary is the final clause and it is phrased as information rather than as an assurance, because an assurance invites the question it is answering. And it is one sentence, carried on the surface itself, in the same type as everything else: not in a modal, not behind an icon, not in a footer.

**The negative test is the one that matters and it is easy to apply.** If the sentence reads as reassurance, it is wrong. If it reads as an apology for what the tool cannot do, it is wrong. If a user would be irritated to be told it twice, it is the right length and in the wrong place, and it moves rather than shrinks.

**Two operational notes.** The sentence is translated and not transliterated, because D26 makes the mark a sentence in the reader's language rather than a fixed string [5]. And on a live capture it gains a second clause and only there: the stave trails by seconds, it blanks when it falls behind, and it is not to be relied on in the moment [5]. On material known in advance that clause is false and is absent, which section 6 has just settled.

**The paper's first test on this sentence is about tone, and a sentence can be calm and still describe a product the surface does not have.** The second test is that every quantity the sentence names is a quantity the surface computes in the configuration the reader is looking at. Draft 1's sentence failed the second test on two of the four quantities it named, which is why the version above is a stem with two conditional clauses rather than a fixed string, and D26 already has the sentence translated rather than transliterated, so the conditionality is a property of a mechanism the design specifies rather than a new one.

Draft 1 did not cite MPN-S5 anywhere and therefore neither adopted nor refused any of the four, which is how one paper came to hold a position about another that the other had not answered [19].

And item 9, both renderers in front of real users, which section 10 of the design says decides the thesis and which no earlier revision scheduled.

**What this surface does not need, said so that nobody builds it for this surface.** Item 5, Layer 3 and the bias detector, which Layer 1 only excludes and which renders nothing anywhere in any case [9], [12]. Item 5a, the timbre channel, which is a Φ channel fed by an assigned DISC profile and therefore has no input here; the design also records that it is not live today and depends on item 2's rating store and item 4's audio path [5]. Items 6 and 6a only if live capture is wanted, on the sequencing section 6 gives. And item 8, the Instrument, which is a separate surface with its own gates [4], [5].

**The numeric parameters this surface consumes and the design leaves unfixed** [5]: the parse-confidence refusal threshold, the resolution floor per ingest class, the Layer 1 defaults, the trigger at which a below-floor difference renders as unmeasured, which the design calls the numeric heart of one of its central honesty claims, and the staleness horizon if live capture is ever built. None is set here and none can be set without the material or the test that fixes it.

**One gap this paper adds rather than inherits.** The design measures three legibility budgets, event density, coverage and dyad churn, all three on the seven score files, and the one of the three that measures how often a stave changes measures Φ channels on one generator's output distribution [5], [10]. **Nothing equivalent has ever been measured for a Ψ stave, because Ψ does not exist**, so Expression has no legibility budget at all, neither measured nor estimated. The one budget that does reach it is B4's, and it reaches it harder than it reaches Live: B4 puts a stave at two to three independent quantities, three staves therefore at six to nine, and Expression has one stave wanting five interaction quantities [1], [5], [16]. Section 9 does that arithmetic.

**Three items this paper generates that are not in any existing queue.** The listener test of section 2, with its four required results and its sampling requirement. The backchannel lexicon question of section 4, which is a piece of authorial and community work rather than a piece of engineering. And the acoustic declaration, which is next.

**The acoustic declaration, which is a specification of the output and not a concession.** This surface emits audio. Because the audio is generated from parameters rather than selected from recordings, its acoustic properties can be **declared and guaranteed** rather than checked after the fact: a ceiling on level, a bound on the rate of dynamic change, a stated frequency ceiling, a predictable form, and a stop control that is instantaneous and costs nothing, meaning no re-ingest, no re-render and no loss of position, which is D36's principle applied to the audio path [5], [13]. The author's analysis identifies this as the one genuinely technical contribution available in the domain, notes that a recording cannot offer it and a generator can prove it, and records the prevalence figures that make stating the bounds worth the engineering rather than leaving them implicit: hyperacusis at 41.4 per cent currently and 60.6 per cent over a lifetime in a sample of 13,093, with half to seventy per cent reporting decreased sound tolerance at some point, and hyperacusis and misophonia requiring different mitigations [13]. **The design consequence is about declaring, not about limiting.** A user who cannot state the bounds of what a tool will play cannot decide whether to use it, which is true of a studio monitoring chain and is true here, and the bounds are parameters the user sets rather than a policy the tool applies. The reason to record it in this paper rather than nowhere is scheduling: the audio path is built at item 4 for Studio, and an audio path built without declared bounds is an audio path that gets rebuilt.

**And the item that governs how the rest are done.** The author's analysis records the participatory position that a tool for this population designed without the population will be judged on that alone, and that Kenny's survey of 3,470 people found 61 per cent of autistic adults preferring identity-first terms, with professionals the outlier group and the authors' own conclusion being to ask rather than to mandate [13]. Two consequences are small and concrete. The listener test of section 2 and the setting sentences of section 3 are designed with autistic co-researchers from the start, because both are instruments and both fail if the wrong people write them. And this paper uses identity-first language on the survey's finding, while noting that asking is the rule the survey's authors drew and that a product with a user in front of it can ask.

Any of them that carries weight in a later revision should be verified first.

## 9. The staves, how many there are, and where finding U9 lands

**The contradiction, stated as the gate states it.** Design section 6.4 promises this surface the running score, section 6.2 specifies that score as two Φ staves and one Ψ stave, Φ runs only where a human has rated a state, and Expression is Layer 1 only. **So two of the three staves have no input here.** The gate upheld that as finding U9 and set the disjunction: either Expression renders the Ψ stave alone and says so, or it is no longer Layer 1 only [6], [7].

**Ruled, and it is the first branch.** Expression renders **interaction staves only and no state staves**, which is D70 [5]. This paper adopts it and states the five operational consequences the decision implies, because the decision on its own does not settle a layout.

**RESTATED 15 September 2026 by the author, and the restatement is larger than it looks [21].** Draft 1 and revision 2 read D70 as making Expression a **one-stave** surface. That reading was an artefact of the reference layout rather than a consequence of the decision: design section 6.2 specifies a score of two Φ staves and one Ψ stave, which is a two-speaker layout, and removing the two Φ staves from it leaves one. The author's decision of 15 September states that a score carries **several personas, added and removed as the material requires**, a persona being an actor, a character in a play, a speaker in a podcast or any persona a score carries, and permits a named per-person comparison on a stave. Per-person Layer 1 measures are indexed by speaker. So the layout is **one interaction stave per persona**, N of them, and still no state staves, because $\Phi$ still does not run here and the reason is unchanged: no Layer 2 rating reaches this surface. What D70 rules out is the state stave. It never ruled on how many interaction staves there are, and this paper had been reading a count off a reference layout.

**One, the layout is an N-stave layout with no state staves.** One interaction stave per persona, and the count is a property of the material rather than of the surface. It never draws an empty $\Phi$ stave and it never silently omits a stave from a layout the reader has been told the shape of. Adding or removing a persona changes the stave count, which is a **speaker-set discontinuity** under D53 and takes its marked seam; what it is not is a relabel, because the append-only rule of `MPN-NOTE-06-persona-scaling.md` places a new persona at the point farthest from every persona already on the page and **never moves an existing one**, at a cost of at most 13.4 per cent of timbre separation across the whole range from two personas to fourteen [22]. Removal costs nothing and repacks nothing. So D36's refusal of a mid-session relabel does not bite on a cast change, and that is a computed result rather than an assurance. Those were the two candidate outcomes the Advocate named and both are bad for the same reason: the design's repeated rule is that a flat output from a missing input and a flat output from a flat conversation must never look alike [5], [7].

**Two, the per-stave declaration reduces.** LS-3 requires each stave to carry a declared two or three quantities, chosen before the session and shown on the output, with a stated default set, a one-screen budget and a preselected default, which is D69 [5]. That default set is written for a three-stave surface: dynamics and tempo on each Φ stave, floor share and adjacency on the Ψ stave. **On Expression the declaration is the Ψ stave's half of it**, and D69's default for that half is floor share and adjacency, which are the two Layer 1 measures that need neither timings nor an overlap detector, and are therefore the two that survive the largest number of ingest classes in some form. They do not never blank: on class C a below-floor floor share renders as unmeasured under D28 and adjacency is degraded by attribution error, and at two speakers both are degenerate [5]. **That default is inherited from a three-stave layout and it cannot stand here**, for the reason section 10's falsifier one gives: at two speakers, which section 10 calls the most likely material this surface will meet, adjacency is deterministic and floor share is one number and its complement, so the inherited default fills the surface's only stave with the two measures that carry no information and omits latency, which is the one that does not. A user who opens no configuration would get one degree of freedom and one constant, which is not a defensible score. Re-deriving the pair is EX-11 and it is the author's; latency plus floor share is the candidate this paper would put forward, with adjacency reserved for arity above two.

**Three, the budget, recomputed for N personas, and the recomputation reverses what revision 2 said.** In B4's unit, which counts independent quantities carried by a stave, Expression has **N staves against a budget of 2N to 3N**, and each persona's stave wants that persona's six Layer 1 measures: floor share, degree in the adjacency graph, overlap, latency, reciprocity and backchannel. B4 as amended on 15 September is a per-stave claim with no between-stave escape, so the budget applies stave by stave and the cast size does not enter it [21].

What is informative per stave depends on the arity and on whether EX-R3's withholding is in force.

| Arity | Withheld under EX-R3 | Constant at that arity | Informative per stave | Budget per stave | Verdict |
|:---|:---|:---|---:|:---|:---|
| Two personas | overlap, backchannel | degree, reciprocity | **2**, floor share and latency | 2 to 3 | **Fits, exactly** |
| Two personas, lexicon established | none | degree, reciprocity | 4 | 2 to 3 | Oversubscribed 1.3x to 2x |
| Above two | overlap, backchannel | none | 4 | 2 to 3 | Oversubscribed 1.3x to 2x |
| Above two, lexicon established | none | none | 6 | 2 to 3 | Oversubscribed 2x to 3x |

**At two speakers each persona's stave carries floor share and latency, which is two quantities inside a budget of two to three.** It fits, exactly, with nothing displaced and nothing left over. That pair is precisely the default the arbitration put forward at its decision 9.4 and left open, so the restatement does not make that decision harder; it makes the candidate answer the only one the arithmetic admits at the arity this surface will mostly see [19].

**Revision 2 said the opposite and it was wrong for a stated reason.** It said the stave at two speakers was asked to carry latency and one scalar and was therefore **under**-subscribed, and it reached that by reading floor share as a single degree of freedom shared between the speakers. That is true of one Ψ stave carrying the relation. It is false of per-persona staves: persona A's floor share is a number on A's stave and persona B's is a number on B's, and the fact that they sum to one is a relation between two staves rather than an emptiness in either. The same correction applies to degree and reciprocity in the other direction, which are genuinely constant at two and are therefore the two the table above withholds.

Above two personas the older reading returns and D53's rule that a surface must choose is not a formality: four of the six do not fit on a stave under the generous reading of B4 and two do not fit under the mean one. Section 5's ruling is what says where the rest go, which is the graph and the numeric readout, and under the author's decision of 15 September the named comparison reaches the stave as well [21]. The paragraph that stood here in revision 2 argued the opposite and is superseded by the table above and by the correction beneath it. It ran: the oversubscription per stave is unchanged and the surface's total headroom is a third of Live's, so at least two of the five measures do not fit under any reading of B4; and at two speakers the arithmetic inverts, the stave is asked to carry latency and one scalar, and the stave is then under-subscribed rather than over-subscribed so the problem is the opposite one, which is not that the surface must choose but that it has almost nothing to choose from. Both halves of that arithmetic were in draft 1, in adjacent sections, and neither was composed with the other.

**Four, the generator still disagrees with the design about the count, by one, and the one is the backchannel.** Running `s6_three_clef.py` on 14 September 2026 reproduces every corpus figure the design cites, asserts the exclusion correctly at 31,078 rows read, 3,425 excluded and 27,653 retained, and prints its information budget as **eighteen quantities against six to nine, listing backchannel as a sixth Ψ quantity** [11]. The design settles Layer 1 at five measures under D64 and the rendered-parameter total at seventeen under D65, and the verification file records the script's budget section as unchanged [5], [10]. **So change 20 landed in the document and not in the script**, and the single quantity they disagree about is the one section 4 of this paper withholds. It is a small defect and it is exactly the kind that this programme has twice found to be load-bearing, so it is EX-8 in the queue rather than a footnote.

**Five, and this is the clause that keeps the ruling from decaying.** If a Layer 2 rating ever reaches this surface, Expression stops being Layer 1 only, which is a change to the surface and not a configuration of it [5]. The operational form: **there is no setting that turns Φ on.** A build with a toggle for it is a different surface, needs a different claims boundary, needs a different sentence on its face under section 7, and reopens the epistemic question section 1 closed. The reason for stating it in this form rather than as a preference is that the failure mode is gradual: a surface that acquires one rating field, then a default, then a rendering, will arrive at a state mapping without anybody having decided to build one.

## 10. What would falsify the whole surface

**Named here so that the programme meets these rather than discovers them, in the order in which each becomes answerable.**

**One, and it is answerable before Ψ exists. The measure set may be empty at the size this surface will mostly see.** Design section 6.3 records that at two speakers adjacency is deterministic, reciprocity is 1 by construction, and floor share is one number and its complement, so **three of the five Layer 1 measures carry no information at that size**, and latency and overlap are the two that still do [5]. Section 4 of this paper withholds the overlap-derived measures by default. **That leaves latency, plus floor share as a single number.** A two-person conversation is the most likely material any version of this surface is pointed at, so on its most likely input the surface renders one informative measure and one ratio. If that cannot be shown to carry something a reader recognises, **Expression is a one-number instrument and should be described as one**, and the honest form of it is a much smaller product. This is the sharpest falsifier in this paper, it needs no listener panel and no Ψ, and it should be answered before item 7 is scheduled rather than after.

**Two. Ψ may not recover the structure the generator put in.** Item 1 supplies a ground truth and item 3 validates against it [5]. A renderer of a measurement that does not measure is a decoration, whatever it sounds like. The accuracy target is not set and setting it is ordinary work once item 1 exists.

**Three. The listener test of section 2 may return that the rendering is read as an evaluation of the people in the exchange, and no respecification of Ψ's codomain may remove the reading.** Then Ψ does not ship on this surface. If the visual renderer is then shown to carry the same reading, the surface does not ship at all, and the correct response is to say so rather than to add a disclaimer, since the design has already ruled that the disclaimer does not survive contact with a minor key [5].

**Four. Neither renderer may answer the question a user came with.** Item 9 puts the graph and the score in front of real users on the same material and asks which answered their question, and section 10 of the design says that decides the thesis of section 1 [5]. Expression is the surface with the most to lose from that test, because it is the surface where the score is not the secondary renderer.

**Five. The unmeasured rendering may be unbuildable in the musical channel.** If Ψ cannot make a below-floor difference audibly distinct from an even exchange, then D28 holds in the visual and textual channels only, the musical renderer is not self-sufficient here, and a design that ships audio alone on this surface would be shipping a channel that cannot express one of the two things the design calls central to its honesty [5], [8].

**Six, and it is the one that would be discovered latest and cost most.** The backchannel question of section 4 may have no answer that a programme this size can reach. If a lexicon cannot be established for even one language and community to the standard any other measured quantity is held to, then the overlap measures are withheld permanently rather than provisionally, falsifier one bites at full strength, and the surface is a latency instrument. That is not a reason to lower the standard for the lexicon. It is a reason to attempt the lexicon early, because the answer changes what the surface is.

**What the restatement of 15 September does to this falsifier, stated before the class C composition because it changes the premise.** This falsifier was written against a **one-stave** surface carrying the relation, where floor share is one degree of freedom shared between two speakers and the stave therefore holds one number and one constant. Under the author's restatement the surface is one interaction stave **per persona**, so floor share is a number on each persona's own stave and the fact that two of them sum to one is a relation between staves rather than an emptiness in either [21]. Recomputed at two personas with EX-R3 in force, each stave carries floor share and latency, degree and reciprocity being the constants and overlap and backchannel being withheld: **two quantities inside a budget of two to three, which fits exactly.** So the falsifier's arity half is answered rather than confirmed, and answered in the surface's favour. What survives of it, and it is the sharper half, is the class C composition below, which is about error rather than about arity and is untouched by the restatement.

**Composed with class C, the surface has nothing.** The degeneracies above are a property of the arity and they hold on every ingest class. On class C two further things happen. EX-R3's withholding is already in force, so the overlap-derived measures are absent for a reason that has nothing to do with arity. And the attribution error attenuates the one remaining scalar: modelling a fraction $e$ of speech assigned to the wrong speaker symmetrically, the observed gap between two speakers is $(1-2e)$ times the true gap, so at the best published streaming diarisation error rate of 19.8 per cent a true 60/40 reads as 56.0/43.9, and at the 39.1 and 39.2 per cent of the two vendors that bundle diarisation with transcription it reads as 52.2/47.8. Inverted, a 60/40 reading off a bundled vendor's output means a true split of about 95.9 to 4.1, which is one speaker holding the floor for nineteen parts in twenty [20]. The design states this as "at a fifth of speech mis-attributed, a true 60/40 floor share is not distinguishable from an even one", which is comfortably true at the vendor rates and **too strong at the 19.8 per cent figure it cites in the same sentence**, where the surviving gap is twelve points and no resolution floor in this programme is set that high [5]. Latency on class C carries the recognition error on top of the attribution error, and the design's figure for that, a meeting-benchmark word error of 35 to 46 per cent, is recorded as unsourced in MPN-S5's citation ledger [18]. So the quantitative basis for the latency half of this composition does not exist in this programme today. The conclusion does not depend on which of the two rates is right: at two speakers on class C this surface has one attenuated scalar and a latency measure whose error is unquantified, and that is a surface with nothing above its floor. Class C is future work on the design's own record three times over, and this falsifier is why that record should not be relaxed for this surface.
