## Why REVISE

Findings 1 through 8 are not covered by the design's admitted gaps, and six of them land on the audiences the design itself identifies as least able to defend against them.

## Findings

| # | Finding | Disposition |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 1 | Ψ and Φ are the user-facing vocabulary and no named user can decode them. Two categorically different outputs differ by one Greek letter | **Accepted.** D26: the mark is a sentence in the reader's language |
| 2 | Ψ has no psychological claim on paper and an unavoidable one in the ear, because its codomain is music and music is read affectively | **Accepted.** D27, re-scoped by the Arbiter to every surface using Ψ, not Expression alone |
| 3 | "Not distinguishable" reads as "even" to the audience least able to tell the difference between absence of evidence and evidence of absence | **Accepted.** D28: rendered as unmeasured, visibly distinct |
| 4 | Class C removes interruption entirely, which is the dynamic the Expression user came for, and silence about it reads as a finding of none | **Accepted.** D29: class C opens by saying what it cannot answer |
| 5 | The five settings are visible to the Expression user and not decidable by them; the backchannel lexicon mitigation is an expert act the audience cannot perform | **Accepted.** D30 |
| 6 | Sixteen correct refusals, undifferentiated, read as a broken product. Four kinds must be distinguished | **Accepted.** D31 |
| 7 | Participant mode would display a culturally biased per-person ranking live to a room, landing on the most junior and the non-native speaker | **Accepted.** D32, **re-scoped by the Arbiter**: per-person measures visible to that person alone, never on the shared display |
| 8 | The silent participant's only refusal is vetoing the meeting before knowing how the display will feel | **Accepted.** D33: withdrawal at any point, node dropped without naming who |
| 9 | The three-clef score is the wrong representation for most named users, and no renderer is named primary per surface | **Accepted.** D34, with the Arbiter's note that this is evidence against the thesis and belongs in section 10 |
| 10 | The dramaturg's first run is the design's most degraded output: twelve pieces of epistemic chrome above two measures | **Accepted.** D37 |
| 11 | The therapist learns their stimulus changed from a release note | **Accepted.** D38: the stimulus version is on the therapist's screen |
| 12 | "Adjacency, never response" is undone the moment the renderer draws a directed arrow | **Accepted.** D35, **narrowed by the Arbiter** to a renderer default; direction is retained in the data |
| 13 | Dyad selection asks the user for the answer they came for | **Accepted.** D36: reversible without re-ingest |
| 14 | Correcting an obvious diarisation error is punished, so the rational response is to leave it in | **Accepted.** Section 8a: the refusal names post-session re-derivation as where relabelling belongs |
| 15 | The captioning expectation survives the withdrawal of the word, because nothing replaced it | **Accepted.** Section 6.4 carries a plain statement of what the surface is for |
