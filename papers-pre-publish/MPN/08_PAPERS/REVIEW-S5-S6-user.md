## Findings

### 1. Every refusal, every overflow and both fallbacks route to the graph, and neither paper records that the graph has no specification

**Tagged BOTH.**

The person is all six of them at once, which is why this is first. The dramaturg reducing Macbeth, told at the point of selection that two staves carry 39.0 per cent of the play, is sorted into the refusal kind **your material**, and section 8a's stated action for that kind, quoted approvingly by MPN-S5 section 3.5, is to use the graph, which carries the full speaker set whether or not the score does. The dramaturg who wants the coalitions is sent to a signed-edge structure at Layer 2, drawn where the nodes are. The panel producer, whose question MPN-S5 section 4.5 says is mostly about the full panel, is given a graph because the score cannot carry one. The reader of the live score, whose surface is oversubscribed on either count of the information budget, is told in section 6.5 that each stave declares two or three quantities and **the rest are in the graph**, which on the rendered-parameter count is eleven to fourteen of seventeen. The Expression user in MPN-S6 section 5 is told that the by-name attribution is carried by the graph and the numeric readout rather than by the stave, and in section 2 that if the listener test fails, shipping the visual renderer alone is **a real product and is not a consolation**.

What goes wrong is that the graph has no specification anywhere in this programme. The earlier Advocate review of revision 7 established this as finding 13 and it was blocking: section 6.2 is the longest surface specification in the design and all of it is about the secondary renderer, while the primary one gets one clause saying it leads and one clause saying it carries the full speaker set, with no layout, no legibility budget, no declared quantities, no coverage statement, no work item and no entry in section 10's list of what the design does not yet have. The arbitration upheld the finding and answered it with change 44, which schedules item 9, a test of which renderer answered the user's question. Scheduling a comparison is not specifying the thing compared. Revision 8 still has no graph specification, and I checked section 10 for one: the inventory of what the design does not yet have runs to eleven paragraphs and the graph is in none of them.

So both papers spend the whole of their remedial budget on an unspecified object and neither says so. MPN-S5 mentions the graph thirty times, names it the leading renderer on all four use cases, makes it the answer to the coverage refusal, the coalition question, the panel's shape and the information budget's overflow, and never once records that nobody has written down what it draws. MPN-S6 makes it the destination of the per-person comparison it takes off the stave and the entire product that survives a failed listener test. A reader of either paper closes it believing the graph is the mature half of the design. It is the undescribed half.

The consequence for each person is concrete rather than rhetorical. The dramaturg told to use the graph cannot be told what the graph will show for forty-nine speakers, because no legibility budget has ever been measured or estimated for it, whereas three have been measured for the score. The producer is told the busiest speaker's position is visible in the graph rather than inferred, which is a claim about a layout nobody has chosen. The Expression user is offered a graph-only product as the honest outcome of a failed test, and there is nothing to hand them.

MPN-S5 section 8 adds nine work items and none of them is the graph. MPN-S6 adds nine and none of them is the graph. Both papers are entitled to say the specification is the design's to write; neither is entitled to route six users' only remaining action into it without naming the gap.

**BLOCKING on both papers.**

### 2. The declared default set is the two measures that carry no information at the arity both papers call most likely

**Tagged BOTH.**

The person is the reader who opens the live score or the Expression surface and changes nothing, which is the person D69 was written for. The moment is the first render. What they see, under D69 as MPN-S5 section 6.4 quotes it, is dynamics and tempo on each state stave and **floor share and adjacency** on the interaction stave, preselected, defensible, chosen because those two are the Layer 1 measures available on every ingest class and therefore the two that never blank. MPN-S6 section 9 adopts the same pair for Expression's single stave and repeats the same justification word for word.

Four sections later, MPN-S6 section 10 states the falsifier it calls the sharpest in the paper: at two speakers adjacency is deterministic, reciprocity is 1 by construction and floor share is one number and its complement, so three of the five measures carry no information at that size, and with the overlap measures withheld by section 4 **that leaves latency**. MPN-S5 section 5.4 states the same degeneracy in a table and calls the composition of it with section 3.3 the sharpest fact in that paper. Both papers also say two speakers is the likely case: MPN-S6 section 10 says a two-person conversation is the most likely material any version of this surface is pointed at, and MPN-S5 gives an entire use case to it.

So on the arity both papers name as most likely, the preselected default renders the two quantities that are constants and omits the one that is not. The reader who never opens the configuration is not given a defensible score. They are given a score that cannot vary, on a surface whose whole premise is that a person can watch it move. And the design's central honesty rule, repeated in section 4.1, in section 4.2 and three times in MPN-S6, is that a flat output from a degenerate input and a flat output from a flat conversation must never look alike. The stated default breaks that rule at N equals two by construction, in the one configuration the design guarantees every user will meet at least once.

MPN-S6 section 9 is where this is worst, because Expression has one stave and its default fills both of its two slots with the uninformative pair, leaving no room for latency at all unless the user opens a configuration the paper says they may never open. The paper had the answer in its own section 10 and did not carry it four sections back.

Neither paper can rewrite D69, and neither has to. What both owe is the sentence that the default set is stated for the multi-party case and is degenerate at two speakers, and an item saying who fixes it. MPN-S6's EX-3 is the nearest thing and it asks a different question.

**BLOCKING on both papers.**

### 3. MPN-S6 rules that no number is withheld and withholds a measure four sections earlier, and its own test decides against the withholding

**Tagged S6.**

The person is the Expression user who wants to know how often their turns and the other speaker's turns overlapped. The moment is the opening of the analysis. What they see, under EX-R3, is a sentence ahead of the content saying the analysis cannot tell them whether anyone talked over anyone, and a surface on which the overlap-derived measures are not computed at all.

Section 5, ruling four, says this: **None of this withholds a number. Every per-person measure is available to the user as a number with its setting and its window beside it.** And, one sentence later, **Withholding the numbers from the person who asked for them would be the condescension.** Section 4 withholds two of them, overlap initiated and overlap received, which section 5 lists by name in its own catalogue of the restored per-person measures. The paper's most emphatic statement of its own standard is contradicted by its own longest ruling, four sections apart, and neither section acknowledges the other.

That is the drafting defect. The substantive one is that the paper's stated test decides against section 4 and the paper does not run it. Section 4's argument is that a backchannel lexicon is a word list, that a word list cannot be right for a speech community it was not built for, that S4's own finding is a keyword counter read as a measurement through three revisions of a paper, and that shipping one as a default is that finding scheduled to happen again. Every step of that is about the **classification** of an overlap as supportive or interruptive. It is not about the overlap. Section 6 of the same paper states the split precisely: floor share, adjacency, latency, overlap and reciprocity are computed from channel timings and need no words at all, and what needs the words is any overlap classified as a backchannel. So the defective object is the label and the withheld object is the timing.

The programme already has a practice for exactly this and section 1 of MPN-S6 quotes it: adjacency is reported as adjacency and never as response, because nothing in a transcript marks an addressee. The design did not withhold adjacency because a reader would take an arrow for an answer. It renamed the quantity, defaulted the renderer to undirected, labelled the edge "speaks next", and kept the number. Section 4 reaches for withholding where the document's own precedent reaches for naming, and it does not say why the precedent does not apply.

I am not entitled to redesign, so I will state the finding rather than the remedy. The finding is that the user loses a computable timing quantity in order to prevent a misreading of a classification that is not computed, that the paper's own ruling four calls that outcome condescension, and that the paper never puts the two rulings in the same paragraph. The user who asks why they cannot see how often two people spoke at once is owed an answer, and the answer currently available to them is a sentence about a word list that the withheld measure does not consume.

**BLOCKING.**

### 4. The line between a measure and a ranking is not perceivable at two speakers, so the user pays the whole cost of the rule and gets none of its protection

**Tagged S6.**

The person is an Expression user comparing themselves with the other speaker. The moment is the numeric readout beside the stave. What they see is: floor share, them 61 per cent and the other speaker 39; median latency before taking the floor, them 0.4 seconds and the other speaker 1.2; turn count, mean turn length, degree in the adjacency graph, each as two numbers. What they do not see, under ruling three, is any of that on the stave, because the named comparison is the one case D42's listener test is scoped not to cover.

Section 5 draws the line exactly and, on its own terms, correctly. A per-person measure is a quantity of the exchange indexed by speaker; a ranking is a derived ordering over people presented as the object of interest, a sort, a leaderboard, a most-dominant label, a per-person composite. The first is in the mapping's domain and the second is not.

At two speakers the two objects are the same object. Two numbers over two people is a complete ordering, it is exhaustive, it needs no sort and no composite, and it is read in one glance. There is no presentation of a per-person measure at N equals two that is not also a presentation of the ranking, because the ranking has one bit in it and the measure already carries that bit. The line is one a designer can state and not one a reader can perceive, and the paper's own material says this is the surface's likely arity: section 10 calls a two-person conversation the most likely material any version of this surface is pointed at.

So the rule is vacuous exactly where the surface lives. What is not vacuous is its cost, and section 5 names that cost honestly: a user who wants to hear their own floor share as a line against somebody else's does not get that, and it is a thing a user might reasonably want. The user therefore pays the whole price of ruling three and receives none of the protection ruling two claims, and the paper presents the combination as conservative.

It is worse than neutral, because of where the two channels sit. The stave is the channel with the apparatus: the mapping sentence on its face, the declared settings, the window, the refusal vocabulary, the unmeasured rendering. The numeric readout is the channel with none of that. Ruling three moves the comparison from the channel that carries the caveats to the channel that does not, and calls the move conservative. At two speakers, and with the graph drawing one edge, the readout is where the user actually meets the comparison, unmediated.

**BLOCKING.**

### 5. The same untested named comparison is permitted on the producer's stave and refused on the Expression user's, and MPN-S6 asserts there is no asymmetry

**Tagged BOTH.**

The people are the panel producer and the Expression user, and the finding exists because the two papers were written in parallel and disagree.

MPN-S5 section 4.6 tells the producer this: the design carries per-person measures in full, including a named comparison on a shared display; the listener test of D42 covers the affective reading of the interaction mapping and does not cover a named per-person comparison; so **a named comparison is permitted, is not tested, and is recorded as untested**. The producer gets it, labelled.

MPN-S6 section 5, ruling three, tells the Expression user this: the named comparison is the one case D42's test is scoped not to cover, a stave is exactly where an affective reading lives, so the named comparison is not put on the musical channel until a test covers it. The Expression user does not get it.

Ruling four then says, of that withholding, that it is **the same rule the design applies to everything else it has not tested, and it applies on Studio and Live equally**, and that the asymmetry between surfaces is in the layout argument of section 2 and nowhere else. On the evidence of its companion paper, written to the same design by the same programme on the same day, that sentence is false. The rule does not apply on Studio and Live equally; MPN-S5 permits on Studio and Live precisely what MPN-S6 forbids on Expression, and cites the same scope hole in the same decision as its reason for permitting it.

Read this as the user MPN-S6 says it is written for. The same untested rendering is shown to a producer, who is told it is untested, and withheld from me, who am told nothing is being withheld from me. The paper's stated standard is that a sentence which would read as condescending to the person using the tool is an error in the paper and not a caution to that person, and that where such a sentence is needed to defend a design the design is wrong and is changed instead. This is not a sentence that reads as condescending. It is a rule that is condescending in its extension, arrived at with the best intentions, and defended with a claim of symmetry that the companion paper falsifies.

One of the two papers is wrong and I am not entitled to say which. Either the untested named comparison belongs on the stave for everybody with its untested label, which is MPN-S5's position and is consistent with ruling four's own principle, or it belongs on nobody's stave until EX-4 closes, which is MPN-S6's position and would require MPN-S5 section 4.6 to be rewritten. What is not available is the present state, in which the audience the programme has taken most care over is the only audience that loses the rendering.

**BLOCKING on both papers.**

### 6. MPN-S5's remedy for two speakers is unavailable on the surface it says its obligations reach unchanged, and MPN-S6 ranks the answer fourth

**Tagged BOTH.**

The person is the Expression user in the two-person case, which both papers say is the case they will mostly be in. The moment is the stave.

MPN-S5 section 7.3 collects four obligations on the interaction mapping and section 7.4 says, of MPN-S6, that **the four obligations of section 7.3 therefore reach MPN-S6 unchanged, and the partiality obligation reaches it hardest**. The obligation that actually reaches hardest is the third, non-degeneracy at two, and MPN-S5 states its two admissible answers: either the mapping at two speakers is a declared function of **latency and overlap alone**, with the other three documented as carrying no information at that size, or the mapping declares two speakers outside its domain and the surface is served by the state mapping, Layer 2 and Layer 3 with no interaction stave at all.

Neither answer survives the trip to Expression, and MPN-S5 does not check. The first answer names overlap, and EX-R3 withholds overlap on this surface by default, so what MPN-S5 offers as a two-argument function arrives as a one-argument function of latency. The second answer removes the interaction stave, and EX-R1 rules that Expression renders the interaction stave **alone**, so removing it removes the surface. The dyad reduction obligation and the partiality obligation both survive the trip; the one the interview use case was written to establish does not, and it is the one that decides what this user sees.

MPN-S6 gets there on its own and deserves credit for it. Section 10, falsifier one, says that what is left is latency plus floor share as a single number, that if that cannot be shown to carry something a reader recognises then **Expression is a one-number instrument and should be described as one**, that the honest form of it is a much smaller product, that the question needs no listener panel and no interaction mapping, and that **it should be answered before item 7 is scheduled rather than after**. That paragraph is the best thing in either paper.

The queue then puts it fourth. EX-3 sits below EX-0, EX-1 and EX-2 in section 11's table, with the status "Open, and it is the cheapest thing on this list", and its stated block is "Whether item 7 is worth scheduling". Section 10 says answer it first; section 11 lists it fourth; nothing reconciles the two. A reader working through the queue in order reaches the sharpest and cheapest question about whether this surface is worth building after three items, one of which is a research programme and one of which is community work that falsifier six says may have no answer.

So the user in the most likely case meets a stave carrying one moving quantity, neither paper says that in the place where the surface is specified, and the cheapest experiment that would establish it is scheduled behind the most expensive things in the programme.

**BLOCKING on both papers.**

### 7. The withheld-overlap sentence names the wrong unit, and the refusal kind promises a queue position the paper's own falsifier says may not exist

**Tagged S6.**

The person is the Expression user meeting the gap where the overlap measures should be. The moment is the opening line of the analysis. What they see, written out in section 4, is: *this analysis cannot tell you whether anyone talked over anyone, because no backchannel lexicon has been established for this language.*

Two things in one sentence mislead the person reading it, and both are contradicted by the paragraph above it.

The first is the unit. The paragraph that rules the withholding says the first branch of section 6.4's disjunction is unavailable because **a word list cannot be right for a language, because a language is not the unit the variation is defined over**, the design's own record being that gap length and overlap tolerance vary by language and by speech community. The user-facing sentence then names the language. A user reading it concludes that the wait is for somebody to do English, or Portuguese, and that when that lands they get their measure. Under section 4's own rule they will not, unless the work is done for their speech community, and the paper's EX-2 says so: establish a lexicon for one deployed language **and speech community**. The sentence the user reads understates the wait by exactly the distinction the section was written to make.

The second is the kind. Section 4 sorts the refusal as the third of section 8a's four, **not specified yet**, whose stated action is that there is nothing to do today and whose obligation is to name the item it waits on. The sorting is defensible and the naming is done. What the kind carries to a reader, though, is a queue position: not yet means later. Section 10, falsifier six, says the thing this user is waiting for may have no answer a programme this size can reach, that in that case the overlap measures are withheld **permanently rather than provisionally**, and that the surface is then a latency instrument. The refusal vocabulary has no kind for a refusal that is permanent for this user and open for the programme, and the paper knows the distinction matters and does not surface it. A user who waits is spending something, and the paper has information about the odds that the surface does not pass on.

Both defects are cheap to repair and both land on the one sentence this user reads first, which is why the finding is blocking rather than editorial.

**BLOCKING.**

### 8. The shipping gate's listener sample is defined by diagnosis and the surface's audience is defined by preference

**Tagged S6.**

The person is anyone in the audience section 6.4 of the design names, which MPN-S6 section 0 quotes and endorses: anyone who finds conversational dynamics easier to read as sound or image than to infer from speech. The moment is section 2's required result three, whose heading is "whose ears", which is the gate that decides whether this surface ships at all.

Section 0 is emphatic and it is right. The audience is defined by a preference for a medium, which the paper calls the only kind of statement it is entitled to make about its reader, and it likens that to a musician who reads a score faster than a description of one. It makes no claim about autism, no claim about anyone's abilities, and no claim that anyone needs help.

Section 2 then defines the sample for the shipping gate by diagnosis. It argues that the general-population figure of 92 per cent may not transfer to **the listeners this surface is designed with**, and it establishes that with three findings about autistic adults and alexithymia, concluding that the test has to be run on this surface's own audience rather than inherited from the review. The slide is in the phrase "this surface's own audience", which section 0 defined by preference and section 2 uses to mean the population the author raised.

Read this as the user. The tool is for anyone who prefers this medium; the experiment that decides whether the tool exists samples me by my diagnosis. That is designing at rather than with, and it arrives in the paper that sets designing with as its own standard and discharges it properly elsewhere: EX-6 puts autistic co-researchers on both instruments from the start, which is the right item and is not what this finding is about.

There is a second consequence and it is the one a careful reader of the programme will raise. Section 0 says the paper makes no claim about autism, and section 2 makes the shipping gate for the whole surface conditional on a result measured in a sample defined by autism. A gate keyed to a population is a claim about that population, whichever way the result comes out. If the sample is drawn that way, the paper owes the sentence saying why a preference-defined audience is tested through a diagnosis-defined sample, and what the result would mean if the two groups diverge.

The evidence section 2 assembles is careful and its conclusion, that the existing literature does not settle the direction and cannot be cited for either answer, is the right conclusion. That is a reason to span the audience in the sample, not a reason to redefine the audience.

**BLOCKING.**

### 9. MPN-S6 never states its user's question, so the test the paper sets itself cannot be applied to the paper's own rulings

**Tagged S6.**

The person is the user of the Expression surface. The moment is any moment, which is the finding.

MPN-S5 opens every one of its four use cases with a subsection headed "The user and the question", and each one states a question in the user's own words: show me the shape of this text; who actually held the floor against who seemed to; how is the asymmetry carried and where does it slip; can I read the shape of this while it is happening. Every subsequent ruling in that paper can be checked against the question, and MPN-S5 does check, which is why its section 3.6 and section 4.6 are worth reading.

MPN-S6 has no such subsection and no such sentence. The nearest it comes is the audience description it inherits from the design, which is a statement about a preferred medium and not a question, and section 7's surface sentence, which says what the picture is of and not what anyone wants from it. Between them, sections 3, 4, 5, 6 and 9 rule on seven things and none of the rulings can be tested against a task, because no task is written down.

The consequence shows up in the rulings themselves and explains three other findings in this review. Ruling three takes the named comparison off the stave and says, correctly, that a user might reasonably want it; with no stated task there is no way to weigh what that costs against what it buys, so the paper weighs it against nothing and calls the result conservative. Section 4 withholds a measure the paper says is very likely the thing the user came for, and "very likely" is doing the work a stated question would have done. Section 9 fixes a default set without asking what the user is trying to see. Section 10's falsifier one is the one place where a task is implicitly present, in the phrase "something a reader recognises", and it is the sharpest paragraph in the paper for exactly that reason.

The paper's own test is whether the tool serves the person using it, written as one would write a paper about a tool for professional musicians, where the user knows what they want and the tool either serves it or it does not. A paper written to that test has to say what the user wants. This one says what the tool renders, what it withholds and what it declares, seven times over, without once saying what anybody is trying to do with it.

**BLOCKING.**

### 10. The listener gate covers the musical renderer alone, and the fallback offered on failure is the ungated one

**Tagged S6.**

The person is the Expression user whose conversation renders harsh. The moment the brief asks about is what this user meets until the listener test exists, and the answer is creditable: nothing, because D42 makes listener testing a condition of the interaction mapping shipping on any surface, item 7 of the implementation order carries the testing of D27 with it, and EX-1 records that the test cannot even start before EX-0 gives it a stimulus. The user does not meet a harsh rendering before the test, because the user meets no rendering before the test. Both papers say this plainly and MPN-S6 says it three times. That part of the brief returns a clean answer and the paper deserves it.

What the gate does not cover is the other renderer, and MPN-S6 leans its entire fallback on it. Section 2 says that if the mapping fails the test, the available responses are to respecify its codomain choices and retest, or to ship the visual renderer alone, and that **the second of those is a real product and is not a consolation**. Section 10, falsifier three, then concedes the hole in one clause: if the visual renderer is then shown to carry the same reading, the surface does not ship at all. Shown by what? No test in this paper, in the design or in the implementation order puts the visual rendering in front of anybody to ask what they take it to be about. D42 and D27 are listener tests, and a graph has no listeners. Item 9 asks which renderer answered a user's question, which is a usefulness test and not an inference test.

So the position the user is in is this. The channel with the affective hazard is gated, correctly and at some cost. The channel with no gate is the one the surface falls back to when the gate bites, and it renders the same unvalidated mapping over the same measures with the same people's names attached. A node sized by floor share, sitting beside a much smaller node carrying the other speaker's name, is read evaluatively by anybody, and nothing in either paper says otherwise or proposes to find out. Finding 1 above compounds this, because the ungated fallback is also the unspecified one.

Section 2 is entitled to prefer the visual renderer. It is not entitled to call it a real product while its own section 10 makes its readability an open falsifier and no item anywhere proposes to close it.

**BLOCKING.**

### 11. The producer reaches the clash at step seven and finds nothing there, and the quantity that would have got them there is already on the screen

**Tagged S5.**

The person is the panel producer. The moment is the one the brief names: they want to see the clash between two panellists, and dyad selection is a user act under D10.

Walk it as they walk it. They choose their material, and if what they kept is the published mix they are in class C. They ingest, and Layer 0 computes parse confidence and refuses below a threshold that has no value in any document. If they are in class C they read the opening sentence saying the analysis cannot tell them whether anyone talked over anyone. They accept or set five Layer 1 settings whose defaults are also unfixed. They read the header: the mapping sentence, three parse-confidence numbers, the ingest class, the five settings, the resolution floor and the window. The graph appears, nodes weighted by floor share, edges undirected and labelled "speaks next". That is six steps and it is everything MPN-S5 says the producer gets today. To see the clash they must take the seventh step and select a dyad, and the third stave that would render the relation of the pair they select runs through a mapping that does not exist. So the clash is at step seven and step seven is empty.

Set aside the emptiness, which section 2 declares honestly and which will not be permanent. The finding is about step seven itself. MPN-S5 section 4.5 is the strongest measured passage in the paper: the busiest speaker sits in 10.2 to 87.0 per cent of active pairs, so an automatic active-dyad rule renders the dominant voice against everyone else for most of the runtime and discards the clash the producer came for, and that is why the reduction is a user act and not an automatic one, with its justification now a number rather than an argument. Every word of that is right about what the automatic rule loses. Not one word of it says how the producer performs the act it protects.

On a panel of five there are ten pairs, on a panel of six there are fifteen, and the producer is given no ordering over them, no preview, no summary and no rule. They guess, render, look, and guess again, and D36's reversibility without re-ingest is what makes the guessing affordable rather than what makes it unnecessary. The paper notes in section 4.5 that on a panel of five or six the coverage figure is the producer's decision rather than the engine's, which is the same observation arriving as a reassurance.

The sharp part is that the quantity which would support the act is already computed and already on the screen. Adjacency count over the declared window is the edge weight of the graph the same section describes, and a pair that clashes is a pair with weight the producer did not expect. Section 4.5 draws the graph, states that the busiest speaker's position in it is visible rather than inferred, and never connects the edge weights to the selection the next paragraph makes the user responsible for. D10 protects the user from an automatic choice and leaves them without an informed one, and MPN-S5 is the paper whose job was to notice.

**BLOCKING.**

### 12. The live score has one chooser and many readers, and the one-click reversal is the act the design rejects when it is a relabel

**Tagged S5.**

The person is whoever reads the live score, and in participant mode MPN-S5 section 6.1 says that is the exchange's own participants rather than an analyst. The moment is the per-stave declaration, and the brief's question is who chooses, when, and what happens if they choose wrong.

The answer the paper gives, in section 6.4, is D69's: the surface has a stated default set, the default is preselected, the user is asked once on one screen before the session, every alternative states what it displaces rather than being offered as a free addition, and the selection is reversible without re-ingest under D36 **so the cost of choosing wrongly is a click**. As an answer about one person at one screen that is good, and it is a real improvement on what the earlier Advocate review met in revision 7.

It is an answer about one person and the surface has several. Section 6.1 restores both modes in full and says the participant mode renders the same surface to the exchange's own participants. So there is a chooser and there are readers, and the paper never separates them. The reader who did not choose sees the declared quantities on the output, which is right and is D53 working, and sees nothing of what was displaced, because "states what it displaces" is a property of the configuration screen and the configuration screen belongs to the chooser. Eleven to fourteen of seventeen rendered parameters are absent from the surface the reader is looking at, and the reader is told the names of the two or three that are present and nothing about the absent ones. That is a different epistemic position from the chooser's and the design has no vocabulary for it, because section 8a's four kinds are about refusals and this is not a refusal.

The second half is sharper, because it is a rule colliding with a rule. "The cost of choosing wrongly is a click" is a sentence about a running surface. A reader has been tracking two lines for four minutes; the chooser clicks; the lines now carry different quantities. Nothing in the design or in MPN-S5 says whether that is permitted mid-session, whether it marks anything, or what the reader sees at the seam. What the design does say, in section 8a and repeated in MPN-S5 section 6.6, is that mid-session speaker relabelling is **rejected in session** because a relabel silently rewrites floor share for the whole elapsed window, that the session is marked discontinuous when the speaker set changes, and that the governing technical rule is that a stale or unsound reading must not be indistinguishable from a sound one. A mid-session change of which quantities a stave carries is the same object as a relabel: it silently rewrites what the reader has been tracking, and the reader has no way to tell the new line from the old one. The design refuses one and D36 advertises the other as a convenience, and MPN-S5 quotes D36's convenience into a section about a running surface without noticing that the surface is running.

What the chooser does about a wrong choice is a click. What a reader does about someone else's click is nothing, and the paper does not know they are there.

**BLOCKING.**

### 13. The interview's two rated staves need a rating instrument that does not exist, and the queue names a store

**Tagged S5.**

The person is the interviewer reviewing their own work, or the researcher reading a set of interviews. The moment is section 5.5, headed "What the output actually is". What they read is: two state staves and one interaction stave, coverage 100 per cent by construction, the selection problem absent, and on the state staves **a state a person rated, with the sentence saying so on the face of the output**.

The person who rates it is this user, and nothing in this programme tells them what to rate or against what. Layer 2 is a human judgement, and the design's section 4.3 says it has known properties and can be tested for inter-rater agreement. What it does not have anywhere is an instrument for a reader of interviews: no gradient definitions written for anybody but a therapist, no anchors, no scale, no worked example, and no statement of what a rating of a person in an interview even ranges over. The rating apparatus this programme owns belongs to MPN-PRD-01, it is Bruscia's profiles and gradients as adapted for a clinical session, its own gate G0 is the anchors, and MPN-S5 section 7.4 correctly says the Instrument is a peer whose rules do not travel.

MPN-S5 handles this well for the dramaturg and not for the interviewer. Section 3.6 says flatly that the state stave cannot render unless the dramaturg rates a state, that the mapping runs only where a human has rated one, and that this is **Layer 2 work the dramaturg has to do**. Section 5.5 says none of that. It presents the two state staves as the output, in the present tense, with the mapping sentence attached, and the reader takes them as available. The correction, such as it is, arrives in section 5.7 as a dependency on **item 2's rating store**, and a store is a database. The interviewer does not lack somewhere to put a rating. They lack the thing that would let them make one.

This matters more here than in any other use case in the paper, and section 5.6 is where MPN-S5 proves it: the interview's central question, whether an interviewee is leading or refusing and whether an interviewer is guiding or dominating, is the Leader against Resister distinction, the derivation of it from turn structure is withdrawn under D4, and it is therefore **a Layer 2 question by ruling rather than by present limitation**. So the one thing this user came for is reachable only through an instrument nobody has written, and the paper says the rating is the answer rather than a supplement to one without saying that the answer has no form yet.

Section 8 adds nine items and none of them is a rating instrument for a non-clinical rater.

**BLOCKING.**

### 14. "What the output actually is" mixes what ships with what needs $\Psi$, in the present tense, in the section each user reads first

**Tagged S5. Not blocking.**

This is the finding the brief asks for on MPN-S5, about where a reader first forms a false impression that something works, and the verdict is favourable to the paper and needs saying that way.

The honesty arrives **before** the impression, decisively and by design. Section 2 is titled "The honest frame: $\Psi$ is not yet a function", it is the second section of nine, it says in bold in its first line that every use case in the paper describes a surface whose interaction mapping does not exist, it says there is no specified mapping, no acceptance criterion and no listener evidence, and it then says explicitly that this is stated once at the front and will not be repeated as a hedge in each section. The diagram in section 7.1 labels the mapping NOT YET SPECIFIED in capitals inside the node. Each use case closes with a section headed what it cannot do and a section headed what must be built first. A reader who reads this paper end to end cannot come away thinking the interaction mapping exists, and the decision not to re-hedge is a defensible piece of writing rather than a concealment.

The residual risk is local and it is in one heading repeated four times. Each use case's section 5 is headed **What the output actually is** and is written in the present indicative, and in two of the four it mixes an output that ships today with one that does not, without a mark between them. Section 3.5 is the clearest case, and it is the dramaturg's. Its first half describes the graph, which needs no mapping, which section 3.2 says is why Studio ships first, and which is true today. Its second half describes the coverage figure that appears where the two principal voices are chosen, which is a step in producing a score, and a score cannot be produced. The 39.0 per cent is in the second half. So the number the brief asks me to walk to is real, verified, correct, and attached to a selection the dramaturg cannot make for as long as item 3 is open, and the paragraph gives the reader nothing to separate the two halves by. The correction arrives one subsection later, at the top of section 3.6: it cannot render a score today, because $\Psi$ does not exist. Section 5.5 has the same shape, presenting three staves of which two need a rating instrument and one needs the mapping.

That is not blocking, because the frame is early, unhedged and unmistakable, and because the fix is a tense and a subheading rather than an argument. It should not survive to a second revision, because the four sections headed "What the output actually is" are the sections a use-case paper's readers will read first and quote from.

**Not blocking.**

### 15. MPN-S6's chain of consequence and its rulings table read as settled behaviour, and the correction is one sentence in a paragraph headed what this paper is not

**Tagged S6. Not blocking.**

The same question, asked of MPN-S6, returns a narrower answer.

The honesty is there and it is **before**, but only just. Section 0's last paragraph, in a passage headed "What this paper is not", closes with the clause that the paper does not specify $\Psi$, which nobody has specified, a fact section 8 states rather than works around. Section 8 then states it properly and in bold: no function, no acceptance criterion, no stated codomain, no channel count, no jump set and no discretisation. EX-0 heads the queue with "Nothing on this surface exists until this does". So a reader who reads to the end is not misled.

The impression forms earlier and in two places. Section 1 is the paper's load-bearing argument and it is a chain of present-tense consequence: Layer 1 is the five measures, the state mapping does not run here, what the tool renders is a function of who spoke, when, for how long and after whom, and the interaction mapping takes the Layer 1 measures and renders the shape of the exchange. Every link of that is a statement about a working object, and the section's whole rhetorical force, which it earns, is that the claims boundary holds **by construction and not by care**. The construction argument is sound for the state mapping and vacuous for the interaction mapping, because you cannot show that a function that does not exist is safely bounded, and the section never says which half it is arguing. Section 11's table then lands seven rulings in the imperative present, and a reader scanning the table takes seven settled behaviours.

It is not blocking because section 0 does carry the sentence and section 8 carries it properly. It would cost one clause in section 1 to say that the chain describes the mapping's obligations rather than its behaviour, and the paper is better than this everywhere else.

**Not blocking.**

### 16. The output header is written for the worst ingest class and shown on the best

**Tagged S5. Not blocking.**

The person is the dramaturg on day one, and the moment is the one D37 describes and MPN-S5 section 3.5 repeats: a page of caveats above a line count. What sits above the graph is the mapping sentence, three parse-confidence numbers, the ingest class, five settings, the resolution floor and the window.

Two of those cannot bind on this user's material. The resolution floor is defined in section 4.1 of the design as a minimum reportable difference **derived from the deployed diariser's error rate**, and class A labelled text has no diariser and a speaker error of none. Two of the five settings, the overlap threshold with its backchannel parameter and the latency turn-boundary rule with its medium, govern measures that section 3.4 has just established are unavailable outright on a script. So the dramaturg reads a header in which roughly half the declarations describe machinery that did not run.

It is, though, the exact accumulation D37 exists to name, made worse by items that carry no information for this user, on the surface the design ships first, for the user the design ships it for. ### 17. Participant mode's unlocking sentence is the sentence that empties it

**Tagged S5. Not blocking.**

The person is whoever reads the live score in participant mode. The moment is section 6.1, where MPN-S5 says both modes are built as locked, that the participant mode renders the same surface while the exchange is running to the exchange's own participants, and that the design restores the participant clause in full **because the restrictions two earlier revisions placed on it rested on a premise about real people in a real room and on generated material there is nobody in the room**.

The sentence that unlocks the mode is the sentence that empties it. Generated dialogue has participants who cannot read anything, so on the material the scope ruling makes primary the participant mode is fully specified, scheduled at item 6, and has nobody in front of it. The earlier Advocate review made this as finding 10, ruled it not blocking, and said the ordering was the problem more than the emptiness. MPN-S5 reproduces the restoration and the justification and does not reproduce the observation, and its section 6.6 offers DC-1, whose reader is a generator and which the paper correctly says measures display conditioning of a generator rather than an observer effect on people.

### 18. Ruling one conditions on generated dialogue where its own argument conditions on there being no person in the material

**Tagged S6. Not blocking.**

The person is an Expression user working on a play script, which the design's own ingest table puts in class A alongside generated dialogue, on the ground that the labels are given.

Section 5, ruling one, is a good ruling and the reasoning behind it is the sharpest cross-cutting observation in the earlier Advocate review: each restoration in revision 7 is justified by a property of the material and written into a surface, which outlives any particular material, so the restoration should be scoped to the material instead. The ruling then writes the scope as **class A generated dialogue with no person in it**, calling that the condition the justification actually names.

It is not the condition the justification names. The justification is that there is no person in the material. A play script has no person in it either; its speakers are characters somebody wrote, which is exactly what a generated dialogue's speakers are, and the design's class table puts them in the same row for the same reason. So as written, ruling one withholds every per-person measure from an Expression user reading a script, for no reason that survives its own argument, and it does so on the surface where the paper's other ruling says withholding numbers would be the condescension.

Small, real, and a one-clause repair: scope the ruling to material with no person in it, which is what the paper means and what makes the rule state its own reason, which is the virtue ruling one claims for itself.

**Not blocking.**

### 19. The one worked setting sentence is drawn from a surface Expression does not have

**Tagged S6. Not blocking.**

The person is the Expression user meeting a setting for the first time. The moment is EX-R2 in practice, and what they see is the paper's only worked example of the form: *Floor share is counted in seconds. Counted in turns instead, the second voice on this stave is Horatio and not the King.*

The form is right and section 3 is the best-argued section in the paper. It keeps every control, refuses to remove one on the grounds that removing a control from this audience and not from another would be the condescension the paper refuses, computes the premise that had never been computed, states the narrower claim the measurement supports rather than the design's broader one, and rules that where the units disagree about the ordering the disagreement is content rather than a warning. All of that is the paper doing exactly what its section 0 sets out to do.

The example is drawn from somewhere else. "The second voice on this stave" presupposes a two-voice selection and a multi-stave layout, and section 9 has just ruled that Expression is a **one-stave** surface with no state staves and no principal-voice selection at all. On Expression the consequence of a unit change is not which voice occupies a stave; it is what the interaction stave's floor share quantity is computed from, and no sentence in the paper shows what that reads like. So the user meets a sentence form whose only instance is impossible on their surface, and an implementer copying the instance will build the wrong thing.

The generalisation is easy and the section has the material for it, since its own table gives the coverage differences of 6.4, 12.8 and 12.3 points, which are exchange-level quantities and are the right shape for a one-stave surface.

**Not blocking.**

## What both papers get right, recorded so a revision does not lose it

Four things should survive every future draft of MPN-S5. Section 2 is the cleanest statement of an absent central mechanism I have read in this programme, and its decision to say it once at the front rather than as a per-section hedge is correct writing. Section 6.5's provenance paragraph refuses to explain a measurement with a mechanism the corpus refutes, deletes the explanation rather than hedging it, keeps the measurement, and says what survives, which is the discipline the whole series has been trying to learn. Section 6.6's sentence that three staves may read as three voices and that a reader who can read music will read the third stave's pitch as pitch, with the observation that this risk is in the author's picture rather than in the implementation of it, is the single most useful sentence in the paper for the person who has to decide what to build. And section 7.3's collection of four obligations turns the specification of the interaction mapping from an open job into a bounded one, which is what the paper claims for itself and is true.

Three things should survive in MPN-S6. Section 3's refusal to remove a control, with the reasoning that removing one from this audience and not from another would itself be the condescension, is the paper meeting its own standard at the point where it costs something. Section 10, falsifier one, is the sharpest self-directed question anywhere in this programme and should be answered before anything else in that queue. And EX-R7, which specifies acoustic bounds as **parameters the user sets rather than a policy the tool applies**, with an instantaneous and costless stop, is the model for how this whole surface should be written: it hands the user the control, states the guarantee, and makes no claim about why the user might want it.

## Dispositions

### MPN-S5

**REVISE.** Seven findings are blocking: 1, 2, 5, 6, 11, 12 and 13. Findings 14, 16 and 17 are not blocking and should not survive another revision unanswered.

The pattern across the blocking seven is one thing. MPN-S5 is scrupulous about what each surface cannot do and inattentive about what each user does next. It measures what an automatic dyad rule would discard and does not say how the producer selects instead. It measures what two staves fail to cover and sends the dramaturg to a renderer nobody has specified. It rules that the interview's central question is a Layer 2 question and names a rating store where the user needs a rating instrument. It quotes a one-click reversal into a section about a surface that is running, in front of readers who did not click. The paper's honesty about mechanisms is genuinely better than the design's; its attention to the moment after the honest sentence is not yet as good.

Blocking count: **7**.

### MPN-S6

**REVISE.** Ten findings are blocking: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10. Findings 15, 18 and 19 are not blocking and should not survive another revision unanswered.

MPN-S6 sets itself the right test and passes it in its sentences. I found no sentence in the paper that reads as condescending to the person using the tool, and section 3 and EX-R7 are the paper meeting the test where meeting it costs something. The test fails in the paper's extensions rather than in its prose, and in both directions the brief anticipated. It over-corrects where it withholds a computable timing measure to prevent a misreading of a classification that is not computed, four sections after ruling that withholding a number would be the condescension. It over-corrects where it takes a named comparison off the stave that the companion paper grants a producer, and then asserts that no such asymmetry exists. It under-corrects where it draws a line between a measure and a ranking that a designer can state and a two-person reader cannot perceive, leaving the user with the cost and none of the protection. And it redefines its own audience by diagnosis at the one point where the definition decides whether the surface ships.

Under all four sits the omission finding 9 names. The paper rules seven times without ever writing down what the user is trying to do, and a rule that has no stated purpose to serve can only be weighed against caution. That is how a paper written by people who refuse condescension arrives at four rulings that read as protective, and it is repairable, because section 10 already contains the question the rest of the paper needed.

Blocking count: **10**.
