# Listening pack, version 2: a page to keep beside you

One page. Read it before you start, or keep it open while you work. Nothing here
is on the pack itself except the timings.

## What the pack asks

You pick which of three descriptions is closest to how you will be listening,
and the page shows you the parts for that path. It changes which parts you see,
not what the sounds are.

The three descriptions are three professions, not three seats. Other people in
your profession are working through the same page, and how many answer on any
of the three paths is not fixed and is not balanced by design. Your answers are
read beside theirs, and beside the other professions' only where the question
put to both was the same.

| Path | Parts | About |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Composer or music director | A, B, C, and a short close | 30 minutes |
| Music therapist | A, D, the instrument, and a short close | 25 minutes |
| Researcher or methodologist | B, C, D, the method, and a short close | 25 minutes |

| Part | What it asks | About |
|:---|:---|:---|
| A | Seven items on one melody. Rank six of them, then say which you would use for each of four situations | 8 to 12 minutes |
| B | Seven items on one melody. Mark each as a scale or as out of tune, name anything that sounds the same, and answer one question about what you would rather hear | 6 to 8 minutes |
| C | Ten items, each a chord, a gap, and another chord. Rate how far the second travelled from the first | 5 to 7 minutes |
| D | Eight items, each a tone, a gap, and another tone. Same or different, and how sure. Then six tones played once each | 6 to 8 minutes |
| The instrument | Questions about the tool rather than about sound. Therapists only | 5 to 8 minutes |
| The method | The four claims, what would falsify each, and what you would require. Researchers only | 5 to 8 minutes |
| Close | One box for anything the questions got wrong, and how you want to be credited | 2 to 3 minutes |

## The order, and the one rule

Work down the page in the order it gives you. The order is fixed and it is the
same for everybody, which is the only reason two people's answers can be
compared at all.

**Do not revise an earlier part after hearing a later one.** A later part will
sometimes make you think you now understand an earlier one. That feeling is not
new information about what you heard, and an answer edited in its light is worth
less than the answer you gave first. If you want to say that you changed your
mind, say it in the last box and leave the earlier answer alone.

Listen before you reason. Report what you hear. If two things sound the same to
you, say so. If they sound different, say that. Neither answer is worth more
than the other.

Every question has an honest way to say the question cannot be answered, and
choosing it is a real answer rather than a failure to finish. You are not being
told what any of the items are, and you are not being told how any of them were
built. That is deliberate and it holds until after your answers are back.

## Before you start

Use headphones, or speakers you trust. Set the volume on the first item and then
leave it alone for the rest of the pack. Work in one sitting if you can.

Fill in the four boxes at the top, including what you are listening on. They are
part of the answer rather than paperwork.

## If something goes wrong

**A sound will not play.** Wait a moment and press play again; each item loads
only when you ask for it. If one item will not play at all, leave its questions
blank, note the label in the last box, and carry on. Do not guess in place of an
item you could not hear.

**You have to stop partway.** Close the tab. Your draft is kept in that browser
on that machine, so open the same link in the same browser and it will still be
there. It does not travel to another machine or another browser, so finish on
the one you started on.

**The download button does not work.** Use the copy button beside it, which puts
the whole file on your clipboard, and paste it into an email. A copied file is
worth exactly as much as a downloaded one.

**Your answers look like they are gone.** The counter at the bottom shows how
many answers are stored. If it reads zero on a page you have already worked on,
the browser has cleared the draft, which happens with private windows and with
some settings that clear site data on close. Do not try to reconstruct it from
memory; say so and start again in a normal window.

**A question assumes something false, or cannot be answered honestly as
written.** Answer it with the option that says you cannot tell, and then write
what was wrong with it in the last box. That box is the most useful one on the
page.

**Something interrupts you in the middle of a part.** Finish the item you are on,
then take the break between parts rather than inside one.

## When you are done

Press the download button, or copy and paste. That single text file is the whole
of what comes back. Send it however you like. There is nothing else to return,
no account to close, and nothing left running.

Answers come back over weeks rather than all at once. A file that arrives after
others have been read is filed, scored and counted exactly like the first one
in, and the analysis is run again over everything that has arrived. Sending
late costs the answer nothing.
